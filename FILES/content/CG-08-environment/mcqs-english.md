# CG-08: Environment & Ecology — 35 MCQs (English)

## Micro-Topic 1: Natural Disaster & Coastal Management
**MT-ENV-M5-5-008**

1. **Which of the following describes the zone of transition between a river and the sea, characterized by high productivity and mixing of salt and fresh water?**
   A) Lagoon
   B) Estuary
   C) Archipelagos
   D) Peninsula
   **Answer: B**
   **Explanation: An estuary is a semi-enclosed coastal body of water with one or more rivers or streams flowing into it, and with a free connection to the open sea.**

2. **Mangroves are often called halophytes because of their ability to:**
   A) Live in extremely cold temperatures
   B) Flourish in highly saline environments
   C) Survive in desert-like conditions
   D) Grow without sunlight
   **Answer: B**
   **Explanation: Halophytes are salt-tolerant plants that can survive in high-salinity coastal environments like mangrove swamps.**

3. **The "Montreux Record" under the Ramsar Convention is maintained for:**
   A) Identifying critical tiger habitats
   B) Documenting polluted Himalayan rivers
   C) Listing wetlands of international importance facing ecological threats
   D) Tracking the migration of Siberian Cranes
   **Answer: C**
   **Explanation: The Montreux Record is a register of wetland sites on the List of Wetlands of International Importance where changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring, or are likely to occur.**

4. **Coral bleaching is primarily caused by:**
   A) Increase in coral polyps population
   B) Excessive nutrient runoff (Eutrophication)
   C) Rise in ocean temperatures causing loss of Zooxanthellae
   D) Industrial extraction of coral stones
   **Answer: C**
   **Explanation: Coral bleaching occurs when coral polyps expel the algae (zooxanthellae) that live in their tissues, usually triggered by stressors such as increased sea surface temperatures.**

5. **Which adaptive feature allows mangrove trees to breathe in anoxic (oxygen-poor) waterlogged soils?**
   A) Prop roots
   B) Stilt roots
   C) Pneumatophores
   D) Tap roots
   **Answer: C**
   **Explanation: Pneumatophores are specialized "breathing roots" that grow upwards out of the soil to facilitate gas exchange in anaerobic sediment.**

6. **What is the significance of the 2004 Tsunami on coastal ecosystems in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands?**
   A) Expansion of coral reef areas
   B) Massive destruction of mangrove forests and siltation of reefs
   C) Elimination of all invasive species in the region
   D) Increase in freshwater levels in coastal aquifers
   **Answer: B**
   **Explanation: The 2004 Tsunami caused extensive physical damage to mangroves and deposited massive amounts of silt on coral reefs, leading to their degradation.**

7. **Under the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification in India, which category represents ecologically sensitive areas like mangroves and coral reefs?**
   A) CRZ-I
   B) CRZ-II
   C) CRZ-III
   D) CRZ-IV
   **Answer: A**
   **Explanation: CRZ-I includes ecologically sensitive areas (like mangroves, coral reefs, and sand dunes) where no new construction is permitted except for specific critical infrastructure.**

## Micro-Topic 2: Solid Waste & Pollution Management
**MT-ENV-M5-5-007**

8. **In the process of Eutrophication, the depletion of oxygen in a water body is measured by which parameter?**
   A) pH level
   B) Turbidity
   C) Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD)
   D) Salinity
   **Answer: C**
   **Explanation: High BOD indicates that a large amount of oxygen is being consumed by microorganisms to decompose organic matter, leading to oxygen depletion.**

9. **"Red Tides" are periodic algal blooms caused by which of the following organisms?**
   A) Diatoms
   B) Blue-green algae
   C) Dinoflagellates
   D) Fungi
   **Answer: C**
   **Explanation: Red tides are caused by a bloom of dinoflagellates, which can discolor water and produce toxins harmful to marine life.**

10. **The process of thermal decomposition of organic material at high temperatures in the absolute absence of oxygen is known as:**
    A) Incineration
    B) Pyrolysis
    C) Gasification
    D) Vermicomposting
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Pyrolysis is the chemical decomposition of organic materials by heating in the absence of oxygen or any other reagents.**

11. **Which of the following hazardous substances is most commonly associated with E-Waste and causes damage to the human nervous system?**
    A) Iron
    B) Carbon
    C) Lead
    D) Sodium
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: Lead is a major component of electronic waste (found in solder and glass of monitors) and is a potent neurotoxin.**

12. **The "Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016" in India emphasized the concept of 'Extended Producer Responsibility' (EPR). This primarily applies to:**
    A) Municipalities
    B) Farmers
    C) Brand owners and manufacturers of packaging waste
    D) Individual householders
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: EPR shifting the responsibility for the post-consumer stage of a product's life cycle onto the producers.**

13. **Which method is considered most effective for managing non-biodegradable and hazardous hospital waste?**
    A) Direct landfilling
    B) Open burning
    C) Incineration at high temperatures
    D) Composting
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: Incineration is preferred for biomedical and certain hazardous wastes to ensure complete destruction of pathogens and toxins.**

14. **What happens to the Dissolved Oxygen (DO) levels in a lake when an Algal Bloom occurs?**
    A) They increase significantly
    B) They remain stable
    C) They decrease as the algae decompose
    D) They fall only during the daytime
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: When the massive amounts of algae from a bloom die and decompose, the process consumes nearly all available oxygen in the water.**

## Micro-Topic 3: Biodiversity (Hotspots, National Parks, Tiger Reserves)

15. **To be identified as a "Biodiversity Hotspot", a region must contain at least how many endemic species of vascular plants?**
    A) 500
    B) 1,500
    C) 5,000
    D) 10,000
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Norman Myers defined hotspots based on two criteria: 1500+ endemic vascular plants and >70% loss of primary vegetation.**

16. **Which of the following is NOT one of the 34 globally recognized Biodiversity Hotspots located in India?**
    A) Himalaya
    B) Indo-Burma
    C) Deccan Plateau
    D) Western Ghats
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: While Deccan Plateau is a distinct biogeographical zone, it is not listed as a global Biodiversity Hotspot.**

17. **Which type of protected area in India allows for the most controlled human activities (like collection of forest produce)?**
    A) National Park
    B) Wildlife Sanctuary
    C) Biosphere Reserve
    D) Reserved Forest
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Wildlife Sanctuaries allow some human activities under regulation, whereas National Parks strictly prohibit all human interference.**

18. **The Dachigam National Park in Jammu & Kashmir is famous for the conservation of which endangered species?**
    A) Red Panda
    B) Hangul (Kashmir Stag)
    C) Snow Leopard
    D) Barasingha
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Dachigam is the last major refuge for the Hangul (Cervus affinis hanglu).**

19. **The conservation of biodiversity outside its natural habitat, such as in Botanical Gardens or Seed Banks, is known as:**
    A) In-situ conservation
    B) Ex-situ conservation
    C) Buffer zone management
    D) Restoration ecology
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Ex-situ conservation involves the removal of species from their natural environment to a controlled environment for protection.**

20. **India's Project Tiger (1973) was launched from which National Park?**
    A) Corbett National Park
    B) Kaziranga National Park
    C) Kanha National Park
    D) Bandipur National Park
    **Answer: A**
    **Explanation: Project Tiger was initiated in Jim Corbett National Park, Uttarakhand.**

21. **The concept of "Biosphere Reserves" was introduced by which UN organization?**
    A) UNEP
    B) UNESCO
    C) UNDP
    D) FAO
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: The Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme was launched by UNESCO in 1971.**

## Micro-Topic 4: Climate Change (Paris Agreement, NDCs, Carbon Markets)

22. **The 2015 Paris Agreement aims to limit the increase in the global average temperature to well below:**
    A) 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels
    B) 2.0°C above pre-industrial levels
    C) 3.0°C above pre-industrial levels
    D) 5.0°C above pre-industrial levels
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: The Agreement sets a target of "well below 2.0°C", while pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.**

23. **India's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) include a target to reduce the emission intensity of its GDP by _____ by 2030 (relative to 2005 levels).**
    A) 10-15%
    B) 33-35%
    C) 50-55%
    D) 100%
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: One of India's key NDC commitments is a 33-35% reduction in GDP emission intensity by 2030.**

24. **The Montreal Protocol (1987) is specifically designed to phase out the production and consumption of:**
    A) Greenhouse gases like Methane
    B) Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS)
    C) Toxic chemical pesticides
    D) Radioactive wastes
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Montreal Protocol is a global treaty to protect the ozone layer by phasing out ODS like CFCs.**

25. **Which article of the Paris Agreement provides for the establishment of a mechanism to contribute to the mitigation of greenhouse gas emissions and support sustainable development through Carbon Markets?**
    A) Article 2
    B) Article 4
    C) Article 6
    D) Article 12
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: Article 6 covers international cooperation, including carbon markets (ITMOs).**

26. **Which of the following is a greenhouse gas with the highest Global Warming Potential (GWP) over a 100-year timeframe?**
    A) Carbon Dioxide
    B) Methane
    C) Nitrous Oxide
    D) Sulfur Hexafluoride (SF6)
    **Answer: D**
    **Explanation: SF6 is extremely potent, with a GWP of over 23,000 compared to CO2.**

27. **The Kyoto Protocol's "Clean Development Mechanism" (CDM) allowed developed countries to meet their emission reduction targets by:**
    A) Buying credits from other developed countries
    B) Investing in emission-reduction projects in developing countries
    C) Trading carbon with private companies only
    D) Reducing internal carbon taxes
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: CDM allows Annex I countries to earn CER credits by funding projects in non-Annex I (developing) countries.**

28. **In the context of climate change, what does the term "Carbon Sink" refer to?**
    A) A type of industrial furnace
    B) A natural or artificial reservoir that absorbs more carbon than it releases
    C) A location where carbon is extracted from mines
    D) An area with high industrial emissions
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Forests and Oceans are classic examples of carbon sinks as they absorb CO2 from the atmosphere.**

## Micro-Topic 5: Environmental Laws (EPA, WPA, Forest Rights)

29. **Under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, animals listed in which Schedule are considered "Vermin" and can be hunted under specific conditions?**
    A) Schedule I
    B) Schedule II
    C) Schedule IV
    D) Schedule V
    **Answer: D**
    **Explanation: Schedule V lists animals (like common crows, fruit bats) that can be declared vermin and hunted.**

30. **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 is often called the "Umbrella Act" because:**
    A) It only covers air and water pollution
    B) It provides a framework for coordinating activities of various central and state authorities under different laws
    C) It was passed during a heavy monsoon season
    D) It applies only to metropolitan cities
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: It bridges the gaps and provides overall powers to the Central Government to protect the environment where other acts might be specific.**

31. **Which Act in India resulted from the 1992 Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) and established the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)?**
    A) Forest Act, 1927
    B) Biological Diversity Act, 2002
    C) Environment Act, 1986
    D) Wildlife Act, 1972
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: India enacted the Biological Diversity Act in 2002 to fulfill its obligations under the CBD.**

32. **Under the Forest Rights Act (2006), which body is the final authority to initiate the process for determining the nature and extent of individual or community forest rights?**
    A) District Magistrate
    B) State Forest Department
    C) Gram Sabha
    D) Ministry of Tribal Affairs
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: The Gram Sabha is the first and final factual authority to initiate the process of recognition of rights under FRA.**

33. **The National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established in India under the NGT Act of:**
    A) 2000
    B) 2005
    C) 2010
    D) 2014
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: The NGT was established in 2010 for effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to environmental protection.**

34. **Scheduled VI of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 was added to provide protection to:**
    A) Endangered birds only
    B) Specified plants (e.g., Pitcher plant, Blue Vanda)
    C) Marine mammals
    D) Invertebrates
    **Answer: B**
    **Explanation: Schedule VI specifically lists plants for which cultivation and collection are restricted.**

35. **The "Polluter Pays Principle" and "Sustainable Development" were legally integrated into the Indian environmental jurisprudence primarily through which Act?**
    A) Air Act, 1981
    B) Water Act, 1974
    C) National Green Tribunal Act, 2010
    D) Forest Act, 1927
    **Answer: C**
    **Explanation: The NGT Act explicitly mentions these principles to be applied by the Tribunal while deciding cases.**
