# CG-08: Environment & Ecology — English Notes

## Subject Overview
Environment and Ecology is a interdisciplinary subject that integrates biological, physical, and geographical aspects with human policy and sustainable development. The core focuses on ecosystem dynamics, biodiversity conservation, climate change mitigation, and environmental legislation.

---

## 1. Natural Disaster & Coastal Management (MT-ENV-M5-5-008)
### Estuaries and Wetlands
*   **Estuaries**: The transitional zone (ecotone) between fresh water (rivers) and sea water. They are highly productive because they receive nutrients from both land and sea. In India, estuaries are mostly on the East Coast.
*   **Wetlands**: Areas of marsh, fen, peatland, or water (natural or artificial, permanent or temporary).
    *   **Ramsar Convention (1971)**: Treaty for conservation and sustainable use of wetlands. India is a signatory.
    *   **Keoladeo Ghana National Park (Rajasthan)** and **Chilika Lake (Odisha)** were the first Ramsar sites in India.
    *   **Montreux Record**: A register of wetland sites where changes in ecological character have occurred (currently India has Keoladeo and Loktak Lake).

### Mangroves and Coral Reefs
*   **Mangroves**: Salt-tolerant (halophytes) shrubs and trees found in tropical and subtropical intertidal regions.
    *   **Adaptations**: Pneumatophores (breathing roots), Stilt roots (for stability), and Viviparity (seeds germinate while still attached to the parent).
    *   **India**: Sunderbans (West Bengal) is the largest single block of tidal halophytic mangrove forest in the world.
*   **Coral Reefs**: Undergrowth of calcium carbonate built by coral polyps living in symbiosis with Zooxanthellae (algae).
    *   **Threats**: Coral Bleaching (loss of algae due to higher sea temperatures, sedimentation, and pollution).
    *   **India's Reefs**: Gulf of Mannar, Lakshadweep (atolls), Andaman & Nicobar, and Gulf of Kutch.

## 2. Solid Waste & Pollution Management (MT-ENV-M5-5-007)
### Eutrophication and Algal Blooms
*   **Eutrophication**: Nutrient enrichment (Nitrogen, Phosphorus) of water bodies, often from urban runoff and fertilizers.
    *   **Impact**: Leads to massive growth of algae (Algal Bloom) which depletes Dissolved Oxygen (DO) and increases Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD), causing fish kills.
*   **Red Tides**: Discoloration of water by certain dinoflagellates (algae). They can be toxic and cause massive mortality in marine life.

### Waste Management
*   **Municipal Solid Waste (MSW)**: Includes household waste and commercial refuse.
*   **Treatment Methods**:
    *   **Incineration**: High-temperature combustion; reduces volume but releases dioxins.
    *   **Pyrolysis**: Thermal decomposition in the absence of oxygen.
    *   **Composting**: Biological decomposition of organic matter.
*   **E-Waste**: Discarded electronic devices. Contains heavy metals like Lead, Mercury, and Cadmium.
    *   **Regulations**: E-Waste (Management) Rules focus on Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR).

## 3. Biodiversity (Hotspots, National Parks, Tiger Reserves)
### Biodiversity Hotspots
*   First proposed by **Norman Myers (1988)**.
*   **Criteria**: Must contain at least 1,500 species of vascular plants as endemics (> 0.5% of world’s total) and have lost at least 70% of its original habitat.
*   **India's Hotspots**:
    1.  **Himalaya** (Entire Indian Himalayan region).
    2.  **Indo-Burma** (North-Eastern India except Assam and parts of AP).
    3.  **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka**.
    4.  **Sundaland** (Nicobar Islands).

### Protected Area Network (In-Situ)
*   **National Parks (NP)**: Areas with high conservation value where human activity (like grazing) is strictly prohibited. (e.g., Dachigam for Hangul, Kaziranga for Rhino).
*   **Wildlife Sanctuaries**: Limited human activity is permitted.
*   **Biosphere Reserves**: Large areas subdivided into Core (Protected), Buffer (Managed research), and Transition zones (Human settlement). India has 18 BRs (12 in UNESCO MAB Programme).
*   **Tiger Reserves**: Conserved under **Project Tiger (1973)**. **NTCA** (National Tiger Conservation Authority) is the statutory body.

## 4. Climate Change (Paris Agreement, NDCs, Carbon Markets)
### International Agreements
*   **Montreal Protocol (1987)**: Aimed at phasing out Ozone Depleting Substances (ODS) like CFCs.
*   **UNFCCC & Kyoto Protocol**: Targeted Greenhouse Gas (GHG) reductions.
*   **Paris Agreement (COP 21, 2015)**: A landmark agreement to keep global temperature rise "well below 2 degrees Celsius" above pre-industrial levels.
    *   **NDCs**: Nationally Determined Contributions. India's goals include reducing emission intensity of GDP by 33-35% and achieving 40% non-fossil fuel capacity by 2030.

### Carbon Markets & Mechanisms
*   **Carbon Credit**: Permit representing one tonne of CO2 removed or not emitted.
*   **Article 6 (Paris Agreement)**: Provides for international cooperation and carbon markets (6.2 - Bilateral, 6.4 - Centralized UN mechanism).

## 5. Environmental Laws (EPA, WPA, Forest Rights)
### Key Legislation in India
*   **Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972**:
    *   Established protection for listed species.
    *   **Schedules**: Schedule I & II provide absolute protection. Schedule V covers vermin (e.g., crows, fruit bats) that can be hunted. Schedule VI covers specified plants.
*   **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**:
    *   Passed after the Bhopal Gas Tragedy. Known as the **"Umbrella Act"** as it gives wide powers to the Central Government to bridge gaps between other laws.
*   **Biological Diversity Act, 2002**:
    *   Implements the CBD. 3-tier structure: **NBA** (National), **SBB** (State), and **BMC** (Local).
*   **Forest Rights Act, 2006 (FRA)**:
    *   Recognizes and vests forest rights in Scheduled Tribes and Traditional Forest Dwellers. The **Gram Sabha** is the final authority to decide the nature and extent of rights.
