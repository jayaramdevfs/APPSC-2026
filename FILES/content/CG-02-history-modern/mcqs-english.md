## 5. MT-HIST-M2-5-001 — National Movement

### Q1. With reference to the early phase of the Indian National Congress (1885-1905), consider the following statements:
1. The early Congress leaders adopted the method of 'politics of prayers' through petitions to the government.
2. They demanded a greater voice for Indians in the administration and legislature.
3. They consistently advocated for complete independence (Purna Swaraj) from British rule.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2, and 3

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The early phase of the INC (Moderates) focused on constitutional reforms and a greater voice in administration. They did not demand complete independence; the demand for Purna Swaraj was raised much later in 1929 at the Lahore session.

**Cognitive Level:** Understanding

### Q2. Consider the following pairs regarding leaders and their associated factions:
1. Dadabhai Naoroji - Radical 
2. Bipin Chandra Pal - Moderate
3. Bal Gangadhar Tilak - Radical

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
A. None of the pairs
B. Only one pair
C. Only two pairs
D. All three pairs

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Dadabhai Naoroji was a Moderate leader who criticized the economic drain of India. Bipin Chandra Pal was a Radical (Extremist) leader. Bal Gangadhar Tilak was a Radical leader ("Freedom is my birthright"). Thus, only the 3rd pair is correctly matched.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Cognitive Level:** Remembering
**Question Type:** MATCH
**Micro-Topic-ID:** MT-HIST-M2-5-001

### Q3. Regarding the Partition of Bengal (1905), consider the following statements:
1. Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal arguing it was for 'administrative convenience'.
2. The real motive was to curtail the influence of Bengali politicians and split the Bengali people.
3. The anti-partition movement was confined only to the Bengal province and did not spread elsewhere.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2, and 3

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The real motive was to divide Bengali nationalism. The resistance (Swadeshi movement) spread beyond Bengal, notably to deltaic Andhra, where it was known as the Vandemataram Movement. Hence, statement 3 is incorrect.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Cognitive Level:** Analyzing
**Question Type:** STMT
**Micro-Topic-ID:** MT-HIST-M2-5-001

### Q4. Which of the following best describes the fundamental difference in the methodology of the 'Moderates' and the 'Radicals' in the early National Movement?
A. The Moderates believed in armed rebellion while the Radicals believed in non-violent petitions.
B. The Moderates focused on creating public awareness through speeches and petitions, whereas the Radicals emphasized mass mobilization, strikes, and self-reliance.
C. The Moderates wanted the immediate end of British rule, while the Radicals wanted dominion status.
D. The Moderates focused on social reform, while the Radicals exclusively focused on political independence without any mass protests.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Moderates believed the British would accept just demands if they were educated through petitions and resolutions. The Radicals criticized this 'politics of prayer' and argued that people must rely on their own strength, mass mobilization, and strikes (boycott of foreign goods) to achieve swaraj.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Cognitive Level:** Analyzing
**Question Type:** ELIM
**Micro-Topic-ID:** MT-HIST-M2-5-001
**Source:** NCERT Class 8, Bipan Chandra

### Q7. Consider the events during the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922) and their consequences on the National Movement:
1. Students left government-controlled schools and colleges.
2. Lawyers such as Motilal Nehru and C.R. Das gave up their practices.
3. The movement was uniformly non-violent across all regions until its logical conclusion.
4. It led to a massive boycott of British titles and foreign cloth.

Which of the following combinations correctly eliminates the false statements?
A. 1, 2, and 3 are true
B. 1, 2, and 4 are true
C. 1, 3, and 4 are true
D. 2, 3, and 4 are true

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Non-Cooperation Movement involved resigning from government jobs, boycotting schools, and surrendering titles. However, it was not uniformly non-violent, as demonstrated by the Chauri Chaura incident in February 1922, which prompted Gandhi to abruptly call off the movement. Hence, statement 3 is incorrect.
**Difficulty:** Very Hard
**Cognitive Level:** Synthesizing
**Question Type:** ANALYTICAL
**Micro-Topic-ID:** MT-HIST-M2-5-001
**Source:** NCERT Class 8, NCERT Class 12
