## 5. MT-HIST-M2-5-001 — National Movement
**Source Covering this Topic:** NCERT Class 8 (Ch: The Making of the National Movement: 1870s-1947), NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History III), Bipan Chandra (Chapters 1-12).

### Timeline & Framework
* **1885:** Formation of Indian National Congress (INC) at Bombay.
* **1885-1905:** Moderate Phase of the Congress. Focus on constitutional reforms.
* **1905:** Partition of Bengal by Lord Curzon. Swadeshi movement begins.
* **1906:** Formation of the All India Muslim League at Dacca.
* **1907:** Surat Split between Moderates and Extremists (Radicals).
* **1915:** Mahatma Gandhi arrives in India from South Africa.
* **1919:** Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh Massacre.
* **1920-1922:** Non-Cooperation Movement and Khilafat Agitation.
* **1930:** Civil Disobedience Movement (Dandi March).
* **1942:** Quit India Movement.
* **1947:** Independence and Partition of India.

### Content Sections
* **Early Political Associations:** Following the Arms Act and Vernacular Press Act (1878) and the Ilbert Bill controversy (1883), early political organizations culminated in the Indian National Congress (1885), founded by 72 delegates with A.O. Hume.
* **Moderates vs. Radicals:** Moderates (Naoroji, Gokhale) demanded a greater voice for Indians in the government through petitions. Radicals (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal) criticized the 'politics of prayers' and emphasized self-reliance and constructive work.
* **Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi:** Divided in 1905 for 'administrative convenience', but mainly to weaken Bengali nationalism. This triggered the Swadeshi Movement (Vande Mataram movement in deltaic Andhra).
* **Arrival of Gandhi & Mass Nationalism:** Gandhi led successful local satyagrahas in Champaran, Kheda, and Ahmedabad before launching the nationwide Rowlatt Satyagraha in 1919.
* **Non-Cooperation to Quit India:** The struggle evolved systematically. Non-cooperation was called off due to Chauri Chaura (1922). Purna Swaraj was declared in 1929. The Dandi March (1930) broke the salt law. The 1942 Quit India Movement called for an immediate British withdrawal.

### Thematic Summary
* **Economic Critique:** Early nationalists like Dadabhai Naoroji formulated the "drain of wealth" theory, explaining exactly how British policies systematically impoverished India.
* **Social Base:** The movement expanded from the English-educated elite in the late 19th century to include peasants, tribals, women, and the working class by the 1920s under Gandhi's leadership.

### Prelims Revision Points
* **First INC President:** W.C. Bonnerjee (1885).
* **"Freedom is my birthright and I shall have it":** Bal Gangadhar Tilak.
* **Partition of Bengal Annulled:** 1911.
* **Rowlatt Act (1919):** Curbed freedom of expression and strengthened police powers.
* **Chauri Chaura (1922):** Led to the withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement.
* **Salt March (1930):** Covered 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi.
