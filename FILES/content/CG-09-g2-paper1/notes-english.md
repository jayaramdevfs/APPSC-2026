## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-001 — President: Election, Powers & Emergency Powers

### 1. Election of the President
The President of India is elected indirectly by an Electoral College comprising:
- **Elected members** of both Houses of Parliament
- **Elected members** of State Legislative Assemblies
- **Elected members** of Legislative Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry (70th Amendment, 1992)

**Value of Votes:**
- MLA vote value = (Total population of state / Total elected MLAs) × (1/1000)
- MP vote value = Total value of all MLA votes / Total elected MPs
- **Single Transferable Vote** with proportional representation system is used.

### 2. Qualifications & Term
- Must be a citizen of India, completed 35 years of age
- Qualified for Lok Sabha membership
- Must not hold any office of profit
- **Term:** 5 years from date of assuming office; eligible for re-election

### 3. Powers of the President
**Executive Powers:**
- All executive actions of the Union are taken in the President's name (Article 53)
- Appoints the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, Attorney General, CAG, Governors, Election Commissioners, UPSC members
- Administers Union Territories through appointed administrators

**Legislative Powers:**
- Summons, prorogues Parliament; dissolves Lok Sabha
- Nominates 12 members to Rajya Sabha (Art 80) and 2 Anglo-Indian members to Lok Sabha (discontinued by 104th Amendment)
- Gives assent to Bills; can withhold assent or return (except Money Bills)
- Issues Ordinances when Parliament is not in session (Article 123)

**Judicial Powers:**
- Appoints judges of Supreme Court and High Courts
- Power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions (Article 72)

### 4. Emergency Powers
- **National Emergency** (Article 352): On grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion
- **President's Rule** (Article 356): Failure of constitutional machinery in states
- **Financial Emergency** (Article 360): Threat to financial stability of India — never invoked

### 5. Veto Powers
- **Absolute Veto:** Withholding assent to a Bill
- **Suspensive Veto:** Returning a Bill for reconsideration (not applicable to Money Bills)
- **Pocket Veto:** Taking no action on a Bill indefinitely (e.g., Indian Post Office Amendment Bill, 1986)

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II1-003 — Salient Features of the Indian Constitution

### 1. Longest Written Constitution
- Originally had 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules
- Currently has ~470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules
- Drawn from multiple sources: British, US, Irish, Canadian, Australian, German, Soviet, French, South African, Japanese constitutions

### 2. Blend of Rigidity and Flexibility
- **Flexible provisions:** Amended by simple majority (e.g., creation of new states)
- **Semi-rigid:** Amended by special majority of Parliament (2/3rds of members present and voting + more than 50% of total membership)
- **Rigid provisions:** Require special majority + ratification by half of state legislatures (e.g., federal provisions)

### 3. Federal System with Unitary Bias
- Division of powers (Seventh Schedule: Union, State, Concurrent Lists)
- Single Citizenship, Integrated Judiciary, All-India Services → Unitary features
- "Indestructible Union of destructible States" — Centre can alter state boundaries

### 4. Parliamentary Form of Government
- Executive responsible to the Legislature
- Collective responsibility of Council of Ministers to Lok Sabha
- Dual executive: President (nominal) and Prime Minister (real)
- Fusion of legislative and executive organs

### 5. Independent Judiciary with Judicial Review
- Supreme Court and High Courts are independent of executive and legislature
- Power of judicial review (Articles 13, 32, 226)
- Basic Structure Doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973)

### 6. Fundamental Rights and DPSPs
- Part III: Fundamental Rights (justiciable, enforceable in court)
- Part IV: Directive Principles (non-justiciable, guiding principles for governance)
- Part IV-A: Fundamental Duties (added by 42nd Amendment, 1976)

### 7. Secular State
- No state religion; equal treatment to all religions
- Articles 25–28 guarantee freedom of religion
- 42nd Amendment explicitly added "secular" to the Preamble

### 8. Universal Adult Franchise
- Every citizen above 18 years (reduced from 21 by 61st Amendment, 1988) has the right to vote
- No discrimination on basis of caste, religion, sex, race

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-004 — Supreme Court: Composition, Jurisdiction & Judicial Review

### 1. Composition
- **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** + other judges (currently 34, including CJI)
- Appointed by the President after consultation with CJI and other senior judges
- **Collegium System:** CJI + 4 senior-most judges recommend appointments (Second and Third Judges Cases)
- Retire at age 65; can be removed only by impeachment (proved misbehaviour or incapacity)

### 2. Jurisdiction
**Original Jurisdiction (Article 131):**
- Disputes between Centre and State(s), or between two or more States
- Must involve a question of law or fact on which the existence of a legal right depends

**Appellate Jurisdiction:**
- Constitutional matters (Article 132)
- Civil matters (Article 133)
- Criminal matters (Article 134)
- Special Leave Petition (Article 136) — appeals from any court/tribunal in India

**Writ Jurisdiction (Article 32):**
- For enforcement of Fundamental Rights
- Five writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto

**Advisory Jurisdiction (Article 143):**
- President may seek SC's opinion on any question of law or fact of public importance

### 3. Judicial Review
- Power to examine constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders
- Article 13: Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are void
- **Basic Structure Doctrine:** Parliament cannot amend the Constitution to destroy its basic structure

### 4. Supreme Court as Court of Record (Article 129)
- Its judgments are recorded for perpetual memory and testimony
- Has power to punish for contempt of itself

### 5. Seat and Proceedings
- Sits in New Delhi (Article 130); CJI may establish circuit benches with President's approval
- Minimum quorum: 5 judges for constitutional cases; other cases as per rules

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II7-002 — Election Commission of India

### 1. Constitutional Provisions
- Article 324: Superintendence, direction and control of elections vested in the Election Commission
- Covers elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and offices of President and Vice-President
- Does NOT cover elections to Panchayats and Municipalities (State Election Commissions handle these)

### 2. Composition
- Originally a single-member body; made multi-member in 1989 (reverted), then again in 1993
- Currently: **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)** + 2 Election Commissioners
- All appointed by the President but CEC can only be removed like a SC judge (impeachment)
- Other ECs can be removed only on recommendation of CEC

### 3. Functions
- **Electoral Rolls:** Preparation, revision and maintenance
- **Conduct of Elections:** Scheduling, notification, polling, counting
- **Recognition of Political Parties:** Granting national/state party status based on performance criteria
- **Model Code of Conduct:** Enforcing rules during elections
- **Delimitation Commission:** Works in coordination for constituency boundaries
- **Election Expenditure Monitoring:** Setting limits and monitoring spending

### 4. Powers
- Can postpone or cancel elections in case of violence or booth capturing
- Can disqualify candidates on grounds of corrupt practices
- Advisory jurisdiction on post-election disqualification of sitting members
- Power to decide disputes arising out of party symbol allocation

### 5. Landmark Reforms
- **EVM (Electronic Voting Machines):** Phased introduction since 1998; universal from 2004
- **VVPAT:** Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail introduced for transparency
- **NOTA:** None of the Above option introduced in 2013 (PUCL case)
- **Voter ID (EPIC):** Electoral Photo Identity Card made mandatory

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II6-001 — Centre-State Relations: Legislative, Administrative & Financial

### 1. Legislative Relations (Articles 245–255)
**Territorial Jurisdiction:**
- Parliament can make laws for the whole or any part of India
- State Legislature can make laws for the whole or any part of the State

**Distribution of Legislative Subjects (Seventh Schedule):**
- Union List: 97+ subjects (Defence, Banking, Foreign Affairs)
- State List: 66 subjects (Police, Public Health, Agriculture)
- Concurrent List: 47+ subjects (Education, Forests, Marriage)
- Residuary subjects vest in the Union (Article 248)

**Parliamentary Legislation in State Field:**
- During National Emergency (Article 250)
- When Rajya Sabha passes resolution under Article 249
- To implement international treaties (Article 253)
- When states request (Article 252)
- During President's Rule (Article 356)

### 2. Administrative Relations (Articles 256–263)
- Executive power of Centre extends to whole of India for Union List matters
- States must comply with Union laws and not impede Union's executive power
- **All-India Services (Article 312):** IAS, IPS, IFoS created by Rajya Sabha resolution
- **Inter-State Council (Article 263):** Advisory body to investigate and advise on Centre-State disputes
- **Zonal Councils:** Statutory bodies under States Reorganisation Act, 1956

### 3. Financial Relations (Articles 268–293)
**Tax Distribution:**
- Taxes levied and collected by Centre but assigned to States (e.g., stamp duties)
- Taxes levied by Centre but collected and appropriated by States (now abolished; replaced by GST)
- Taxes levied and collected by Centre but shared with States (e.g., Income Tax, Corporation Tax)
- **GST (101st Amendment, 2016):** Subsumed multiple indirect taxes; GST Council recommends rates

**Grants-in-Aid:**
- Statutory grants (Article 275): Recommended by Finance Commission
- Discretionary grants (Article 282): For any public purpose

**Finance Commission (Article 280):**
- Constituted every 5 years by the President
- Recommends distribution of net proceeds of taxes between Centre and States
- Recommends measures to augment state revenue

### 4. Key Commissions on Centre-State Relations
- **Sarkaria Commission (1983):** Recommended strengthening of Inter-State Council, restraint on Article 356
- **Punchhi Commission (2007):** Recommended guidelines for Governor's appointment, Centre-State consultation before deploying forces
- **Rajamannar Committee (1969):** Tamil Nadu-appointed; recommended more autonomy for states
