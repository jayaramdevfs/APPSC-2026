## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-001 — President (7 Questions)

1. **Question:** The President of India is elected by:
   - A) Direct election by all citizens
   - B) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies
   - C) All members of both Houses of Parliament and State Legislatures
   - D) Elected members of Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies only
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The Electoral College for the President consists of elected (not nominated) members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (Article 54). Nominated members and members of State Legislative Councils do not participate.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

2. **Question:** Which of the following statements regarding the President's ordinance-making power is/are correct?
   - 1. An Ordinance can be promulgated only when at least one House of Parliament is not in session
   - 2. An Ordinance must be laid before both Houses when Parliament reassembles
   - 3. An Ordinance ceases to operate after 6 weeks from the reassembly of Parliament unless approved
   - A) 1 and 2 only
   - B) 2 and 3 only
   - C) 1 and 3 only
   - D) All of the above
   - **Answer:** D
   - **Explanation:** Under Article 123, the President can promulgate ordinances when either House is not in session. The ordinance must be laid before Parliament and ceases to operate 6 weeks after reassembly if not approved. All three statements are correct.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

3. **Question:** The 'Pocket Veto' of the President refers to:
   - A) Returning a Bill to Parliament for reconsideration
   - B) Withholding assent to a Bill indefinitely without any time limit
   - C) Seeking the opinion of the Supreme Court on a Bill
   - D) Giving assent to a Bill with conditions attached
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Unlike the American President, the Indian President has no constitutional time limit to act on a Bill. By indefinitely withholding assent without returning it, the President exercises a 'Pocket Veto' (e.g., Indian Post Office Amendment Bill, 1986 by President Zail Singh).
   - **Difficulty:** Hard

4. **Question:** Which article empowers the President to grant pardons, reprieves, and remissions of punishment?
   - A) Article 71
   - B) Article 72
   - C) Article 74
   - D) Article 75
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Article 72 grants the President the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, or remissions of punishment, or to suspend, remit, or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence under Union law, including court martial cases and death sentences.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

5. **Question:** Financial Emergency under Article 360 of the Indian Constitution:
   - A) Has been proclaimed twice in India's history
   - B) Requires approval of Rajya Sabha within two months
   - C) Has never been proclaimed in India
   - D) Can only be proclaimed during war
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** Financial Emergency (Article 360), which can be proclaimed when the financial stability or credit of India is threatened, has never been invoked since the Constitution came into force in 1950.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

6. **Question:** During President's Rule under Article 356, which of the following is/are true?
   - 1. State Legislature is dissolved or suspended
   - 2. Parliament exercises the power of the State Legislature
   - 3. The State Governor assumes executive powers on behalf of the President
   - A) 1 only
   - B) 1 and 2 only
   - C) 2 and 3 only
   - D) All of the above
   - **Answer:** D
   - **Explanation:** Under Article 356, the President can assume all executive functions, dissolve or suspend the State Legislature (which then cannot sit), and Parliament assumes state legislative powers. The Governor acts on behalf of the President.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

7. **Question:** The President can nominate how many members to the Rajya Sabha?
   - A) 2
   - B) 10
   - C) 12
   - D) 14
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** Under Article 80(1)(a), the President can nominate 12 members to the Rajya Sabha who have distinguished themselves in literature, science, art, and social service.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II1-003 — Salient Features of the Constitution (7 Questions)

8. **Question:** The Indian Constitution borrows the concept of Directive Principles of State Policy from the Constitution of:
   - A) United States of America
   - B) United Kingdom
   - C) Ireland
   - D) Canada
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** The DPSPs in Part IV of the Indian Constitution are inspired by the Irish Constitution, which in turn was influenced by the Spanish Constitution.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

9. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT a federal feature of the Indian Constitution?
   - A) Written Constitution
   - B) Division of powers between Centre and States
   - C) Single Citizenship
   - D) Independent Judiciary
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** Single Citizenship is a unitary feature. In a true federation like the USA, citizens have dual citizenship (national + state). India has only one (Indian) citizenship.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

10. **Question:** The provision of 'Single Integrated Judiciary' in India means:
    - A) There is only one court in India
    - B) The Supreme Court is the only appellate court
    - C) The judiciary forms a single hierarchy with the Supreme Court at the apex
    - D) High Courts have no independent power
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Unlike the USA which has separate federal and state court systems, India has a single integrated judicial hierarchy – Supreme Court → High Courts → Subordinate Courts – where all levels enforce both central and state laws.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

11. **Question:** Which amendment added the words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble of the Indian Constitution?
    - A) 42nd Amendment, 1976
    - B) 44th Amendment, 1978
    - C) 73rd Amendment, 1992
    - D) 86th Amendment, 2002
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 (during Indira Gandhi's government) added the words 'Socialist,' 'Secular,' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

12. **Question:** Which of the following features make the Indian Constitution rigid?
    - 1. Amendment by special majority in Parliament
    - 2. Ratification by half of state legislatures for certain amendments
    - 3. Presidential assent required for all amendments
    - A) 1 only
    - B) 1 and 2 only
    - C) 2 and 3 only
    - D) All of the above
    - **Answer:** D
    - **Explanation:** The rigidity comes from requiring special majority (2/3rds present and voting + absolute majority), plus ratification by half the states for some amendments, and mandatory Presidential assent for all amendments.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

13. **Question:** The concept of 'Fundamental Duties' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from:
    - A) The Japanese Constitution
    - B) The German Constitution
    - C) The Soviet Union (USSR) Constitution
    - D) The French Constitution
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A, Article 51A) were added by the 42nd Amendment based on the Sardar Swaran Singh Committee recommendation and inspired by the Constitution of the former Soviet Union.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

14. **Question:** The Indian Constitution provides for a 'Parliamentary form of government' which means:
    - 1. The executive is responsible to the legislature
    - 2. The President is the real head of state
    - 3. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
    - A) 1 and 3 only
    - B) 2 and 3 only
    - C) 1 only
    - D) All of the above
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** In a Parliamentary system, the executive (Council of Ministers) is responsible to the Legislature (Lok Sabha). The President is the nominal head while the Prime Minister is the real executive. Statement 2 is incorrect — the President is the nominal head.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-004 — Supreme Court (7 Questions)

15. **Question:** The Supreme Court of India was inaugurated on:
    - A) 26 January 1950
    - B) 28 January 1950
    - C) 26 November 1949
    - D) 15 August 1947
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Supreme Court of India was inaugurated on 28 January 1950 (two days after the Constitution came into force), replacing the Federal Court of India established under the Government of India Act, 1935.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

16. **Question:** Under which article can the Supreme Court issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
    - A) Article 32
    - B) Article 131
    - C) Article 136
    - D) Article 226
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** Article 32 empowers the SC to issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 the 'heart and soul of the Constitution.' Article 226 is for High Courts.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

17. **Question:** The 'Collegium System' for the appointment of Supreme Court judges was established through:
    - A) A constitutional amendment
    - B) An Act of Parliament
    - C) Judicial interpretation (Supreme Court judgments)
    - D) A Presidential order
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The Collegium System evolved through the Second Judges Case (1993) and Third Judges Case (1998), where the SC interpreted Article 124 to establish that the CJI along with 4 senior-most judges form the collegium for appointments.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

18. **Question:** Special Leave Petition (SLP) can be filed in the Supreme Court under:
    - A) Article 132
    - B) Article 134
    - C) Article 136
    - D) Article 143
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Article 136 grants the SC discretionary power to grant special leave to appeal from any judgment, decree, order, or sentence of any court or tribunal in India. This is a vast power making the SC the ultimate appellate authority.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

19. **Question:** The retirement age of a Supreme Court judge is:
    - A) 60 years
    - B) 62 years
    - C) 65 years
    - D) No fixed retirement age
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Supreme Court judges retire at 65 years of age (Article 124). High Court judges retire at 62 years. This distinction is important for competitive exams.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

20. **Question:** The Supreme Court's Advisory Jurisdiction is provided under:
    - A) Article 131
    - B) Article 137
    - C) Article 141
    - D) Article 143
    - **Answer:** D
    - **Explanation:** Under Article 143, the President can refer any question of law or fact of public importance to the SC for its opinion. The SC may report its opinion. This is advisory and not binding on the President.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

21. **Question:** A Supreme Court judge can be removed from office by:
    - A) The President on his own
    - B) The Chief Justice of India
    - C) An address by both Houses of Parliament on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity
    - D) A resolution of the Lok Sabha alone
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Under Article 124(4), a SC judge can be removed only by an order of the President passed after an address by each House of Parliament supported by a special majority on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity. This is called 'impeachment.'
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II7-002 — Election Commission of India (7 Questions)

22. **Question:** Article 324 of the Indian Constitution vests the superintendence and control of elections in:
    - A) The President
    - B) The Parliament
    - C) The Election Commission of India
    - D) The Supreme Court
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Article 324 vests the Election Commission with the power of superintendence, direction and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and offices of President and Vice-President.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

23. **Question:** The Chief Election Commissioner can be removed from office in the same manner as:
    - A) The President
    - B) The Vice-President
    - C) A Judge of the Supreme Court
    - D) The Prime Minister
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The CEC enjoys the same security of tenure as a Supreme Court judge and can only be removed through impeachment — by an address of both Houses of Parliament on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

24. **Question:** NOTA (None of the Above) option in Indian elections was introduced following the Supreme Court judgment in:
    - A) Lily Thomas case (2013)
    - B) PUCL v. Union of India (2013)
    - C) Kuldip Nayar case (2006)
    - D) Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** In the PUCL v. Union of India case (2013), the Supreme Court directed the Election Commission to provide a NOTA option in EVMs, giving voters the right to reject all candidates.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

25. **Question:** Which of the following elections are NOT conducted by the Election Commission of India?
    - A) Elections to the State Legislative Assembly
    - B) Elections to the Rajya Sabha
    - C) Elections to Panchayats and Municipalities
    - D) Election of the Vice-President
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Elections to Panchayats and Municipalities are conducted by State Election Commissions (Article 243-K and 243-ZA), not the Election Commission of India.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

26. **Question:** The Election Commission became a multi-member body in its present form in:
    - A) 1989
    - B) 1991
    - C) 1993
    - D) 2000
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** While it was briefly made multi-member in 1989 (reverted in 1990), the current three-member structure (CEC + 2 ECs) was established in 1993 through the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Conditions of Service) Act amendment.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

27. **Question:** Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) were used for the first time on a universal basis in all constituencies in:
    - A) 1998 General Elections
    - B) 2004 General Elections
    - C) 2009 General Elections
    - D) 2014 General Elections
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** While EVMs were introduced in selected constituencies from 1998 onwards (first in Kerala bye-elections in 1982 on a trial basis), they were used universally across all constituencies for the first time in the 2004 General Elections.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

28. **Question:** The Model Code of Conduct comes into effect:
    - A) From the date the election schedule is announced
    - B) 45 days before polling
    - C) From the date of notification
    - D) One month before the election date
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** The Model Code of Conduct comes into force immediately upon the announcement of the election schedule by the Election Commission and remains in force until the completion of the election process.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II6-001 — Centre-State Relations (7 Questions)

29. **Question:** The Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution contains:
    - A) Languages recognized by the Constitution
    - B) Three lists dividing legislative subjects between Centre and States
    - C) The forms of oaths or affirmations
    - D) Provisions relating to anti-defection
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Seventh Schedule contains three lists: Union List, State List, and Concurrent List, which distribute legislative subjects between the Parliament and State Legislatures.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

30. **Question:** In case of conflict between a Central law and a State law on a subject in the Concurrent List:
    - A) The State law prevails
    - B) The Central law prevails
    - C) The President decides which law prevails
    - D) Both laws are void
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Under Article 254, if there is a conflict between Central and State legislation on a Concurrent List subject, the Central law prevails and the State law becomes void to the extent of repugnancy. However, if the State law has received Presidential assent, it may prevail in that State.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

31. **Question:** The Finance Commission is constituted by the President under:
    - A) Article 270
    - B) Article 275
    - C) Article 280
    - D) Article 293
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Article 280 mandates the President to constitute a Finance Commission every five years (or earlier) to recommend the distribution of net proceeds of taxes between the Centre and States.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

32. **Question:** Residuary powers of legislation belong to:
    - A) State Legislatures
    - B) Parliament (Union)
    - C) Both Centre and States equally
    - D) The President
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Under Article 248, the Parliament has exclusive power to make any law on a matter not enumerated in the Concurrent List or the State List. Entry 97 of the Union List further reinforces this residuary power with the Union.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

33. **Question:** The Sarkaria Commission was set up to examine:
    - A) Judicial reforms in India
    - B) Electoral reforms
    - C) Centre-State relations
    - D) Police reforms
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The Sarkaria Commission, established in 1983 under Justice R.S. Sarkaria, examined and recommended changes in Centre-State relations. It submitted its report in 1988 with 247 recommendations including restraint on Article 356, strengthening Inter-State Council, etc.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

34. **Question:** The Inter-State Council is constituted under which Article?
    - A) Article 255
    - B) Article 258
    - C) Article 263
    - D) Article 280
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Article 263 empowers the President to establish an Inter-State Council to investigate and advise on inter-state disputes and to make recommendations for better coordination of policy and action. It was established in 1990 on the recommendation of the Sarkaria Commission.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

35. **Question:** GST (Goods and Services Tax) was introduced by which Constitutional Amendment?
    - A) 99th Amendment
    - B) 100th Amendment
    - C) 101st Amendment
    - D) 103rd Amendment
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 introduced GST in India, establishing the GST Council (Article 279A) to recommend rates, exemptions and other matters related to GST. It subsumed multiple Central and State indirect taxes.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium
