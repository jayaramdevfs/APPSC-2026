# Disaster Management

> *The 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami killed ~10,000 people on AP's coast alone. It also created India's disaster management legal framework — and AP built one of the world's best cyclone preparedness systems in response.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims GS | G2 Screening | G1 Mains Paper 3 | G2 Paper 2 & 3
**Sources:** Disaster Management Act 2005, NDMA Guidelines, Sendai Framework, INCOIS Reports

## The Big Picture

Disaster management in India was transformed by two catastrophic events: the **2001 Bhuj Earthquake** (20,000+ deaths, total institutional collapse of response) and the **2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami** (which killed ~2.3 lakh people across 14 countries, with ~10,000+ in India, including AP's coast). The Disaster Management Act 2005 — enacted directly in response — created India's entire institutional framework for disasters, from NDMA to NDRF.

For APPSC candidates, the AP angle is significant: AP's coastline faces the worst cyclone risk in India, and AP has developed one of the world's most effective cyclone preparedness systems. The 2019 Fani Cyclone evacuation (Odisha) is the global benchmark; AP's preparedness is modelled on similar lines.

## 1. Disaster — Definition & Classification

**Definition (Disaster Management Act, 2005):**
A disaster is a catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence arising from natural or man-made causes which results in substantial loss of life, human suffering, or damage to property.

### Classification

| Category | Types |
|---|---|
| **Natural — Hydro-Meteorological** | Floods, Cyclones, Droughts, Heat Waves, Cloudbursts |
| **Natural — Geological** | Earthquakes, Landslides, Tsunamis, Volcanic eruptions |
| **Natural — Biological** | Epidemics (COVID-19, Nipah, Dengue), Pandemics, Locust attacks |
| **Man-Made — Industrial/Chemical** | Bhopal Gas 1984, CBRN (Chemical-Biological-Radiological-Nuclear) |
| **Man-Made — Conflict** | Terrorism, riots |
| **Man-Made — Infrastructure** | Bridge collapse, building collapse, industrial accidents |

## 2. Legal & Institutional Framework

### Disaster Management Act, 2005

The **apex legislation** for disaster management in India, enacted after the 2004 Tsunami. It created:

| Body | Chair | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **NDMA** (National Disaster Management Authority) | **Prime Minister** | Sets national policies and guidelines; coordinates response |
| **SDMA** (State Disaster Management Authority) | **Chief Minister** | State-level policies and plans |
| **DDMA** (District Disaster Management Authority) | **District Collector / DM** | District-level planning and coordination |
| **NDRF** (National Disaster Response Force) | — | **16 battalions** of specialised disaster response; under NDMA |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** **NDMA is chaired by the PRIME MINISTER**, not the Home Minister. The **National Executive Committee (NEC)** — which coordinates central ministries for disaster response — is chaired by the **Home Secretary**. Questions frequently swap these two.

### Key Supporting Bodies

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **NEC** (National Executive Committee) | Chaired by **Home Secretary**; coordinates disaster response across ministries |
| **NDRF** | 16 battalions; rapid specialised response (rescue, evacuation, medical); permanent deployment across country |
| **SDRF** (State Disaster Response Force) | State-level counterpart to NDRF |
| **IMD** (India Meteorological Department) | Weather forecasting, cyclone tracking, heat wave warnings |
| **INCOIS** (Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services) | **Tsunami early warning**; HQ **Hyderabad**; 24×7 monitoring |

## 3. Disaster Management Cycle

The DM cycle has 6 phases — prevention and mitigation happen BEFORE disasters; the rest after:

| Phase | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Prevention** | Eliminating risk entirely | Flood plain zoning laws; no construction in risky zones |
| **Mitigation** | Reducing impact | Cyclone shelters; earthquake-resistant construction codes |
| **Preparedness** | Training, stockpiling, early warning | Mock drills; NDRF training; IMD forecasting systems |
| **Response** | Immediate action during disaster | NDRF rescue; evacuation; emergency relief |
| **Recovery** | Restoring essential services (short-term) | Shelter, food, water, temporary power restoration |
| **Reconstruction** | Long-term rebuilding | **"Build Back Better"** — rebuild more resilient than before |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** "**Build Back Better**" is the international standard for post-disaster reconstruction — don't just rebuild what was there, but build it safer, more resilient, and more sustainable. This is Priority 4 of the Sendai Framework. India's post-Fani (2019) reconstruction in Odisha is cited globally as an example of successful Build Back Better in action.

## 4. AP and Disaster Vulnerability

### Cyclone Profile (AP's Primary Disaster Risk)

AP has **one of the most cyclone-prone coastlines in India** — the warm waters of the Bay of Bengal consistently generate severe cyclones that track towards the AP-Odisha coast:

| Cyclone | Year | Impact on AP |
|---|---|---|
| **Hudhud** | **2014** | Hit **Visakhapatnam** directly; Category 5; massive urban destruction; ~100 deaths (but 3.6 lakh evacuated) |
| **Phailin** | 2013 | Odisha/AP border; one of strongest ever; excellent evacuation saved thousands |
| **Nivar/Burevi** | 2020 | AP coast; moderate impact |

- **Cyclone Shelters**: AP has constructed thousands of multi-purpose cyclone shelters (used as schools/community halls in normal times)
- **Coastal warning system**: Doppler radars at Visakhapatnam, Machilipatnam; IMD 4-stage warning

### Earthquake Seismic Zones (BIS — Bureau of Indian Standards)

| Zone | Risk Level | AP Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Zone V | Very High | J&K, Uttarakhand, NE India, Gujarat (Kutch) |
| Zone IV | High | Delhi, Bihar, parts of Himachal |
| **Zone III** | **Moderate** | **Most of AP falls here** |
| Zone II | Low | South AP (Rayalaseema interior), Deccan plateau |

### AP Heat Wave Vulnerability

- AP and Telangana are among the **most heat wave-prone states** in India
- IMD defines heat wave as 4.5–6.4°C above normal (or 45°C+ in plains)
- AP cities (Vizag, Guntur, Nellore) have developed **City Heat Action Plans**

## 5. Major Historical Disasters in India

| Disaster | Year | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Latur Earthquake** | 1993 | 10,000+ deaths; Maharashtra; triggered building code reforms |
| **Orissa Super Cyclone** | 1999 | 10,000+ deaths; worst cyclone in India's recorded history |
| **Bhuj Earthquake** | 2001 | **20,000+ deaths**; Gujarat; triggered Disaster Management Act |
| **2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami** | 2004 | 9.1 Richter; 2.3 lakh deaths; AP coast heavily hit; triggered India's NDMA creation |
| **2013 Uttarakhand Floods** | 2013 | Cloudbursts + glacial lake outburst; 5,000+ deaths |
| **2019 Fani Cyclone** | 2019 | Category 5; Odisha; ~1.2 million evacuated in 48 hrs — **global benchmark for cyclone evacuation** |

## 6. International Framework — Sendai Framework (2015–2030)

- **Adopted**: March 2015 in **Sendai, Japan** at World Conference on DRR
- **Successor to**: Hyogo Framework for Action (2005–2015)
- **Duration**: 2015–2030

### Four Priorities for Action

1. Understanding disaster risk
2. Strengthening disaster risk governance
3. Investing in disaster risk reduction for resilience
4. Enhancing preparedness and **"Build Back Better"**

### Seven Global Targets

Reduce: mortality, affected people, economic losses, critical infrastructure damage
Increase: national/local DRR strategies, international cooperation, access to early warning systems

| System | Body | AP Relevance |
|---|---|---|

| **Flood Forecasting** | **CWC** (Central Water Commission) | Real-time river gauge data; Godavari/Krishna monitoring |
| **Heat Wave Warning** | **IMD** | AP Heat Action Plans in major cities |
