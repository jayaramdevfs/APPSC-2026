## The Big Picture

An ecosystem is nature's accounting system — everything flows in cycles, energy moves in one direction (from sun to heat), and every organism has a role. Disrupting these cycles, whether through deforestation, pollution, or climate change, sends shockwaves through the whole system. India, as one of the world's most biodiverse nations, has both an extraordinary natural inheritance and an extraordinary responsibility.

For APPSC, ecosystem questions test the science (food chains, nutrient cycles, 10% rule), while biodiversity questions test the policy (hotspots, Ramsar sites, conservation projects, international treaties). AP has several high-frequency biodiversity facts: Nagarjunasagar Tiger Reserve (largest in India), Coringa mangroves, Pulicat Lake, and Nelapattu bird sanctuary.

## 1. Ecosystem — Fundamentals

### Definition

An **ecosystem** is a functional unit of nature where living organisms (biotic components) interact with each other and with their non-living environment (abiotic components) through energy flow and nutrient cycling.

### Components

- **Biotic**: Producers (plants, algae), Primary Consumers (herbivores), Secondary Consumers (carnivores), Decomposers (fungi, bacteria)
- **Abiotic**: Temperature, water, light, soil, minerals, pH

### Energy Flow

- **Unidirectional**: Energy flows from Sun → Producers → Consumers → Decomposers; it is never recycled
- **10% Rule (Lindeman's Law)**: Only 10% of energy at one trophic level passes to the next; **90% is lost as heat**

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Energy flow in ecosystems is **unidirectional and non-recyclable**. Nutrients (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus) cycle between biotic and abiotic components — they ARE recycled. Energy is NOT recycled. This is a fundamental distinction tested frequently.

### Food Chain vs. Food Web

| Concept | Description |
|---|---|
| **Food Chain** | Linear sequence: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle |
| **Food Web** | Interconnected network of multiple food chains (more realistic; adds stability) |
| **Trophic Level** | Position in the food chain (Level 1 = Producers, Level 2 = Herbivores, etc.) |

## 2. Biogeochemical Cycles

### Carbon Cycle

- **Fixation**: Plants absorb CO₂ through photosynthesis
- **Release**: Respiration, combustion, decomposition
- **Carbon Sinks**: Oceans, forests, soil — absorb more CO₂ than they release
- **Problem**: Burning fossil fuels releases carbon stored for millions of years → excess CO₂ → climate change

### Nitrogen Cycle

- **N₂ (78% of atmosphere)** is unusable directly by plants
- **Nitrogen Fixation**: Bacteria convert N₂ to ammonia — *Rhizobium* in legume root nodules, *Azotobacter* free-living
- Steps: **Nitrogen Fixation → Nitrification → Assimilation → Ammonification → Denitrification**

### Phosphorus Cycle

- **No gaseous phase** — purely sedimentary cycle (rocks → soil → organisms → back to rocks)
- Critical for DNA, ATP, and cell membranes
- Main reservoir: rocks and ocean sediments

## 3. India's Biodiversity

### India's Status

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Megadiverse Country** | One of **17 Megadiverse Countries** (holds 70%+ of world's biodiversity despite being 2% of area) |
| **Species richness** | ~45,000 plant species, ~91,000 animal species |
| **Endemism** | ~33% of India's plants are endemic (found nowhere else) |

### India's 4 Biodiversity Hotspots

| Hotspot | Coverage |
|---|---|
| **Western Ghats & Sri Lanka** | Western Ghats mountain range; one of world's best studied hotspots |
| **Eastern Himalayas** (Himalaya Hotspot) | Includes Darjeeling, Sikkim, Arunachal |
| **Indo-Burma** | Northeast India — most understudied hotspot |
| **Sundaland** | Andaman & Nicobar Islands |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** India has **4 biodiversity hotspots**, not 2. Many candidates confuse this because Western Ghats and Eastern Himalayas are the two most discussed. But Indo-Burma (Northeast) and Sundaland (A&N Islands) are equally valid — and often appear as correct/incorrect options in MCQs.

## 4. Threats to Biodiversity — HIPPO

| Letter | Threat | India Example |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | **Habitat Destruction** | Deforestation, urbanisation, wetland drainage |
| **I** | **Invasive Species** | Lantana camara (invades forests), Water Hyacinth (chokes waterways) |
| **P** | **Pollution** | Air, water, soil, plastic — all categories |
| **P** | **Population** | Human population pressure on natural habitats |
| **O** | **Overexploitation** | Overfishing, poaching (tigers, elephants, rhinos) |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** **Habitat destruction is the #1 threat to biodiversity globally** — responsible for more species loss than all other factors combined. In India, 65% of natural habitats have been converted or heavily modified. The remaining forests are increasingly fragmented — isolated forest patches cannot support viable tiger populations, which need territories of 100–400 sq km.

### IUCN Red List Categories

| Category | Code |
|---|---|
| Extinct | EX |
| Extinct in the Wild | EW |
| Critically Endangered | **CR** |
| Endangered | **EN** |
| Vulnerable | **VU** |
| Near Threatened | NT |
| Least Concern | LC |

**India's Threatened Species:**
- **CR**: Great Indian Bustard, Gharial, Malabar large-spotted civet
- **EN**: Bengal Tiger, Asiatic Lion, One-horned Rhinoceros, Snow Leopard
- **VU**: Indian Elephant, Dhole (Wild Dog), Sloth Bear

## 5. Conservation Strategies

### In-situ Conservation (conservation in natural habitat)

| Category | Count (India) | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **National Parks** | **106** | No human activity permitted inside |
| **Wildlife Sanctuaries** | **567** | Limited human activities permitted |
| **Biosphere Reserves** | **18** (UNESCO recognised) | Core zone (strict) + Buffer zone + Transition zone |
| **Tiger Reserves** | **55** | Under Project Tiger (1973) |

### Ex-situ Conservation (conservation outside natural habitat)

- Zoos, Botanical Gardens, Seed Banks, Gene Banks
- **National Gene Bank**: NBPGR (National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources), New Delhi — stores genetic material of India's crop varieties

### Key Conservation Projects

| Project | Year | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Tiger** | **1973** | 55 Tiger Reserves; **3,167 tigers** (2022 census — highest ever); India = **70% of world's tigers** |
| **Project Elephant** | **1992** | 33 Elephant Reserves; India = **60% of Asia's elephants** |
| **Project Crocodile** | **1975** | Mugger, Saltwater, **Gharial** (critically endangered; Chambal river) |

## 7. International Biodiversity Agreements

| Agreement | Year | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| **CBD** (Convention on Biological Diversity) | **1992** (Rio Earth Summit) | Biodiversity conservation, sustainable use, access & benefit-sharing |
| **Cartagena Protocol** | 2000 | Safe transfer and handling of Living Modified Organisms (LMOs/GMOs) across borders |
| **Nagoya Protocol** | 2010 | Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) — countries must share benefits from using other nations' genetic resources |
| **CITES** | **1973** | Trade in endangered species; Appendix I (banned) / II (controlled) / III |
| **Ramsar Convention** | **1971** | Protection of internationally important wetlands |

### Ramsar Sites — India

- India has **75 Ramsar sites** (2023) — **highest number in the world**
- **Montreux Record** (threatened Ramsar sites in India): **Loktak Lake** (Manipur) and **Keoladeo Ghana** (Rajasthan)
- **AP Ramsar Sites**: Kolleru Lake, Coringa Wildlife Sanctuary, Pulicat Lake
