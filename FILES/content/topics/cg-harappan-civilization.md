# Indus Valley / Harappan Civilization

> *The Harappans built the world's first planned cities with underground drains, standardized bricks, and public baths — 4,500 years ago. Then they vanished so completely that their civilization was unknown until 1921.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims Ancient History | G2 Screening History | G1 Mains Paper 2 | G2 Paper 1
**Sources:** R.S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past* (Oxford, 2005), Chapter 10

---

## The Big Picture

The Harappan civilization (c. 2500–1900 BCE) was the world's largest Bronze Age civilization by area — 1.3 million sq km, larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined. It stretched from Baluchistan to Meerut, from the Makran coast to the foot of the Siwaliks. Yet when it collapsed around 1700 BCE, it left no named kings, no deciphered texts, and no successor state. The civilization was completely forgotten until archaeologists found it in 1921.

What makes Harappa remarkable is not military power (no evidence of a warrior king class) but **urban planning** — standardized bricks, underground drainage, covered street drains, and public sanitation of a sophistication not seen again in India for thousands of years. For APPSC, the key facts are: discovery timeline, important sites and their specific claims-to-fame, urban planning features, and the decline debate.

## 1. Discovery and Excavation

| Milestone | Year | Person / Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Harappa noticed | 1853 | British General **Cunningham** — not properly excavated |
| **Harappa excavated** | **1921** | **Daya Ram Sahni** — first systematic excavation |
| **Mohenjo-daro excavated** | **1921** | **R.D. Banerjee** |
| Brought to world attention | **1931** | **John Marshall** — published landmark study as DG of ASI |
| Mohenjo-daro further work | 1938 | Ernest Mackay |
| Stratigraphic excavation | 1946 | **Mortimer Wheeler** — introduced modern methods |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** The first site **excavated** was **Harappa** (1921, Daya Ram Sahni) — NOT Mohenjo-daro. Mohenjo-daro was excavated simultaneously by R.D. Banerjee. Questions often imply Mohenjo-daro was "first" because it is more famous. It was not.

## 2. Geographic Extent

- **Area**: ~1,299,600 sq km — larger than Egypt or Mesopotamia
- **Sites**: ~2,800 total; ~1,022 in mature phase (406 in Pakistan, **616 in India**)

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Most Harappan sites are in **India** (616), not Pakistan (406). The civilization was not confined to the Indus valley — it extended deep into Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Haryana.

### Key Sites and Their Specific Claims

| Site | Location | Key Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Harappa** | Punjab, Pakistan (Ravi river, left bank) | First excavated site; gave civilization its name; massive granaries |
| **Mohenjo-daro** | Sindh, Pakistan (Indus river, right bank) | **Largest city** (500 ha); Great Bath; largest granary |
| **Lothal** | Gujarat | **Earliest tidal dock** in the world; earliest **rice** evidence (~1800 BCE) |
| **Kalibangan** | Rajasthan (Ghaggar river) | **Earliest evidence of ploughing** (criss-cross ploughed field preserved) |
| **Dholavira** | Gujarat (Rann of Kutch) | **Only stone-built citadel** (all others are brick); elaborate water management |
| **Rakhigarhi** | Haryana | **Largest Harappan site in India** (~250 ha); DNA studies done here |
| **Sutkagendor** | Makran coast, Baluchistan | Westernmost Harappan site; likely a trading post |
| **Banawali** | Haryana (Ghaggar-Hakra plain) | Mature phase city; ploughing evidence |
| **Chanhu-daro** | Sindh | Bead-making + seal-making industry |

## 3. Town Planning — The Defining Achievement

### City Layout

Every major Harappan city had:
- **Citadel** (raised western mound, walled): Public buildings — Great Bath, granary
- **Lower Town** (eastern, larger): Residential and commercial areas
- **Grid streets**: Main streets ran N-S or E-W; corner houses had rounded corners for cart traffic

### The Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro)

- **Dimensions**: 11.88 m × 7.01 m × 2.43 m deep
- Floor and walls: **Kiln-fired bricks + bitumen** (waterproofing)
- Steps from both ends; surrounded by corridors and rooms
- Purpose: Almost certainly **ritual bathing for religious purification** — the world's earliest known public water tank

### Granaries

- Mohenjo-daro granary: **45.71 m × 15.23 m** — massive storage structure
- Harappa: Six granaries near the river (for loading/unloading)
- Grain = primary wealth; granaries near citadel = **state-controlled taxation in kind**

### Drainage System

The Harappan drainage system was the **most sophisticated of the ancient world**:
- Every street had an underground covered drain
- Houses had their own bathrooms and latrines
- Waste connected to street drains via small channels
- Soak pits at intervals; removable brick covers for cleaning

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The Harappan **drainage system** has no parallel in the ancient world. Egypt and Mesopotamia built grand monuments — pyramids, ziggurats. The Harappans built sewers. This tells us something profound about their priorities: health and cleanliness mattered as much (or more) as monumental grandeur. This city-planning obsession — uniform bricks, grid streets, covered drains — implies strong **central authority** or deep cultural uniformity across 1.3 million sq km.

### Bricks

- **Kiln-fired bricks** — unique to Harappa; Egypt and Mesopotamia used **sun-dried mud bricks**
- Standardized size ratio: **4:2:1** (length:width:thickness) across the entire civilization
- Uniform bricks over 1.3 million sq km = evidence of centralized authority or deep cultural coherence

## 4. Agriculture and Economy

**Main crops**: Wheat and barley (staples); also rai, peas, sesamum, mustard
**Cotton**: Harappans were the **first people in the world to grow and spin cotton**. Greeks called the region "Sindon" (from Sindh) after this discovery.
**Rice**: First evidence at **Lothal** (~1800 BCE)
**Plough**: Used; Kalibangan has direct criss-cross field evidence

**Animals**: Oxen (for plough/cart), buffaloes, goats, sheep, pigs; **humped bull (zebu)** most common on seals (religious/cultural significance)

**Horse**: Absent from mature Harappan phase. No unambiguous horse bones, no horse-drawn chariots, no spoked wheels. This contrasts sharply with the Vedic culture (horse-obsessed). This contrast is the central evidence that Harappan ≠ Vedic Aryans.

## 5. Crafts and Technology

| Craft | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Bronze** | Copper (from **Khetri mines, Rajasthan**) + Tin (from Afghanistan); tools, weapons |
| **Bronze Dancing Girl** | From Mohenjo-daro; 11 cm tall; casual contrapposto pose; iconic ancient Indian art |
| **Steatite Seals** | ~2,000 found; most common animal = **unicorn** (single-horned bull-like animal); also buffalo, tiger, rhino, elephant; lines of undeciphered script |
| **Bead Making** | Carnelian (Gujarat), lapis lazuli (Afghanistan), gold, silver, faience; extraordinarily fine drilling |
| **Pottery** | Wheel-made; kiln-fired; characteristic red/pink with black paintings — geometric designs, animals, trees; remarkably uniform across the civilization |
| **Cotton Textiles** | Spindle whorls found everywhere; cotton weaving widespread domestic activity |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Pashupati Seal** from Mohenjo-daro shows a seated yogic figure surrounded by four animals (elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, buffalo) + two deer below. Some scholars identify this as a **proto-Shiva / Pashupati** figure. R.S. Sharma is cautious — connection is plausible but not proven. However, this is the most-tested religious artifact of the Harappan civilization.

## 6. Trade and Commerce

**Internal trade**: Enabled by **standardized weights** (binary system: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64... then multiples of 160); uniform across 1.3 million sq km

**External trade**: With Mesopotamia
- Mesopotamian records (~3rd millennium BCE) refer to "**Meluha**" = the Harappan region
- "**Dilmun**" = modern Bahrain = intermediary trading post
- Exported: Carnelian beads, lapis lazuli, copper, ivory, pearls, fine textiles

**Lothal dockyard**: Earliest purpose-built **tidal dock** in the world — ships entered at high tide, loaded/unloaded as tide receded

**No coins**: Trade was barter, but standardized weights ensured fairness

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The weights based on **multiples of 16** may have persisted for 4,500 years: the Indian monetary system used **16 annas = 1 rupee** right until decimalization in 1957. The traditional 16 seers = 1 maund, 16 chhatanks = 1 seer — all based on the same 16-multiple system. Whether this is direct continuity or coincidence is debated, but the parallel is striking.

## 7. Religion

| Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Thousands of female terracotta figurines with headdress, plant from embryo | **Mother Goddess** worship — earth/fertility deity |
| Pashupati Seal (seated yogi + 4 animals) | Possible **proto-Shiva** figure |
| Pipal tree (sacred fig) on seals and pottery | **Tree worship** — pipal sacred in later Hinduism and Buddhism (Bodhi tree) |
| Polished stone conical objects | Possible **linga** (Shaiva symbol) worship |
| Small objects worn on body | **Amulets** — belief in supernatural forces |

---

## 8. Script and Polity

**Script**: ~4,000 inscribed objects found; average inscription = 5 signs; ~250–400 distinct signs; runs **right to left**; **NOT deciphered** — no bilingual inscription (like Rosetta Stone) to crack it; NOT alphabetical (logo-syllabic system)

**Polity**: Unknown (undeciphered script). R.S. Sharma's inference: governed by **merchant oligarchy** — no temples, no palaces found (unlike Egypt/Mesopotamia); granaries + standardized weights + trade infrastructure = merchant class priorities, not warrior king priorities.

## 9. Decline (~1900–1700 BCE)

Three main causes (R.S. Sharma):

1. **Environmental change**: The **Ghaggar-Hakra river (Sarasvati)** dried up ~1700 BCE when Yamuna + Sutlej changed course due to tectonic shifts → hundreds of settlements lost water → mass migration east and south
2. **Trade collapse**: Rise of Elamite kingdom (~2000 BCE) disrupted Mesopotamian trade routes; Mesopotamian records stop mentioning "Meluha" after ~1900 BCE
3. **Soil exhaustion**: Intensive farming + deforestation for brick kilns may have degraded the agricultural base

**Aryan Invasion Theory: REJECTED** by R.S. Sharma — the archaeological evidence for violent catastrophic destruction at most sites is unconvincing. Skeletons in upper Mohenjo-daro layers are a late deposit, not evidence of conquest.

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Harappan civilization ≠ Vedic/Aryan civilization. Evidence: (1) The mature Harappan phase ended ~1900 BCE; Rig Veda composed ~1500–1200 BCE — gap of 400+ years. (2) No horses in Harappan (horse central to Vedic culture). (3) No spoked wheels in Harappan (spoked chariot wheels central to Vedic warfare). These three facts together conclusively separate Harappan and Vedic civilizations.
