## 1. The Universe and the Solar System
Based on GC Leong (Ch 1) and NCERT 6 (Ch 1)

### The Universe
- **Galaxies**: Stars occur in clusters called galaxies or nebulas. Each may contain as many as 100 million stars.
- **Milky Way**: Our own galaxy, contains about 100,000 million stars.
- **Distance**: Measured in light years. Light travels at 186,000 miles/sec.
    - Sun to Earth: ~8 minutes.
    - Moon to Earth: ~1 second.

### The Solar System
- **Composition**: The Sun and nine planets (Pluto recently downgraded but mentioned in sources).
- **The Sun**: Surface temperature ~6,000°C; Interior ~20 million°C. 300,000 times bigger than Earth.
- **Planets**:
    - **Mercury**: Smallest, closest (36 million miles). Year = 88 days.
    - **Venus**: "Earth's twin" (size/mass/density).
    - **Earth**: Only planet with life. Natural satellite = Moon (238,900 miles away; revolves once in 27 days).
    - **Mars**: "Red Planet"; potential for plant life.
    - **Jupiter**: Largest; gas giant (Hydrogen, Helium, Methane). 12 satellites.
    - **Saturn**: Second largest; three rings; 9 satellites.
    - **Uranus**: Blue-green disc; 50x bigger than Earth; orbits **clockwise** (East to West).
    - **Neptune & Pluto**: Extreme remoteness; Pluto's year = 247 Earth years.

### The Earth's Movements
- **Rotation**: West to East once in 24 hours. Causes **Day and Night**.
- **Revolution**: On an elliptical orbit at 18.5 miles/sec. One cycle = 365 1/4 days (Leap year every 4 years).
- **Inclination**: Axis inclined at **66 1/2°** to the ecliptic plane. Causes **Seasons** and varying lengths of day/night.
    - **Summer Solstice (21 June)**: Sun overhead at Tropic of Cancer (23 1/2°N). Longest day in Northern Hemisphere.
    - **Winter Solstice (22 Dec)**: Sun overhead at Tropic of Capricorn (23 1/2°S). Longest day in Southern Hemisphere.
    - **Equinoxes (21 March & 23 Sept)**: "Equal nights"; sun overhead at Equator.

## 2. Geomorphology: The Earth's Crust & Landforms
Based on GC Leong (Ch 2, 3) and NCERT 11 (Unit II)

### Internal Structure of the Earth
1. **The Crust (Lithosphere)**:
    - **Upper Crust (SIAL)**: Granite rocks; Silica + Aluminium. Forms continents (Density ~2.7).
    - **Lower Crust (SIMA)**: Basaltic rocks; Silica + Magnesium. Forms ocean floors (Density ~3.0).
2. **The Mantle (Mesosphere)**: ~2,900 km thick; mainly olivine.
3. **The Core (Barysphere/Nife)**: ~3,500 km radius; Nickel + Iron (Nife). High temperature and pressure.

### Classification of Rocks
- **Igneous Rocks**: Formed by cooling of magma. **Crystalline**; no fossils.
    - *Plutonic*: Cool deep inside (Granite).
    - *Volcanic*: Cool on surface (Basalt).
- **Sedimentary Rocks**: Formed by accumulation of sediments. **Stratified**; contain fossils.
    - *Mechanically formed*: Sandstone, Shale.
    - *Organically formed*: Limestone, Coal.
    - *Chemically formed*: Gypsum, Potash.
- **Metamorphic Rocks**: Formed by heat/pressure on existing rocks.
    - Clay → Slate; Limestone → Marble; Sandstone → Quartzite; Granite → Gneiss; Coal → Graphite.

### Earth Movements & Landforms
- **Orogenic (Mountain building)**:
    - **Fold Mountains**: Caused by compression (Himalayas, Rockies, Andes).
    - **Block Mountains**: Caused by faulting/tension. "Horsts" (up-lifted) and "Graben" (Rift Valleys). Example: Rhine Rift Valley, Vosges.
- **Epeirogenic (Continental building)**: Large-scale uplift or subsidence.

## 3. Vulcanism and Earthquakes
Based on GC Leong (Ch 3)

### Volcanic Features
- **Intrusive**: Cools underground.
    - *Sills*: Horizontal sheets.
    - *Dykes*: Vertical intrusions.
    - *Laccoliths*: Dome-shaped.
    - *Batholiths*: Massive granite cores of mountains.
- **Extrusive**: Erupts on surface.
    - *Active*: Frequent eruptions (Etna, Stromboli - "Lighthouse of Mediterranean").
    - *Dormant*: Quiet but might erupt (Vesuvius, Fujiyama).
    - *Extinct*: No historic eruptions (Kilimanjaro).

### Earthquakes
- **Focus**: Origin point inside Earth.
- **Epicenter**: Point on surface directly above focus.
- **Measurement**: 
    - *Richter Scale*: Measures Magnitude (Energy).
    - *Mercalli Scale*: Measures Intensity (Damage).
- **Distribution**: 
    - Circum-Pacific Belt ("Ring of Fire"): 70% of earthquakes/volcanoes.
    - Alpine-Himalayan Belt.

## 4. Oceanography: The Relief of Oceans
Based on GC Leong (Ch 12)

### Ocean Floor Divisions
1. **Continental Shelf**: Shallow (up to 200m depth); widest in Arctic Ocean. Source of fish and minerals (Petroleum).
2. **Continental Slope**: Steep drop (2° to 5°).
3. **Deep Sea Plain (Abyssal Plain)**: 3,000–6,000m deep; covers 2/3rd of ocean floor.
4. **Ocean Deeps/Trenches**: Deepest parts. **Mariana Trench** (Pacific) is deepest (~11,022m).

### Ocean Currents
- **Warm Currents**: Flow from Equator to Poles (Gulf Stream, Kuroshio).
- **Cold Currents**: Flow from Poles to Equator (Labrador, Oyashio).
- **Indian Ocean Circulation**: Unique due to **Monsoon reversal**.

## 5. The Atmosphere
Based on GC Leong (Ch 13)

### Layers of Atmosphere
1. **Troposphere**: Lowest (0–16km). Contains 75% of air. **Weather phenomena** occur here. Temperature decreases with height (Lapse Rate: 6.5°C/km).
2. **Stratosphere**: Contains **Ozone layer** (absorbs UV). Ideal for jet flying (no clouds).
3. **Mesosphere**: Temperature drops to -100°C. Meteors burn up here.
4. **Thermosphere (Ionosphere)**: Contains ions; reflects radio waves back to Earth.
5. **Exosphere**: Outer space.

## 6. Indian Physiography & AP Angle
Based on NCERT 11 (Unit II)

### Major Physiographic Divisions of India
1. **The Northern Mountains**: Himalayas (Young Fold Mountains).
2. **The Northern Plains**: Gange-Brahmaputra alluvial plains.
3. **The Peninsular Plateau**: Oldest landmass (Gondwanaland).
4. **The Coastal Plains**: Western (narrow) & Eastern (wide, delta-rich).
5. **The Islands**: Andaman & Nicobar (volcanic), Lakshadweep (coral).

### Andhra Pradesh Angle (Physical)
- **Coastal Andhra**: Part of the **Eastern Coastal Plains**. Known for the fertile **Krishna-Godavari Deltas**.
- **Rayalaseema**: Part of the **Deccan Plateau**. Characterized by semi-arid conditions and "Cuddapah rocks" (sedimentary/metamorphic rich in minerals).
- **Eastern Ghats**: Run through AP as broken hills (Nallamala, Erramala, Seshachalam). Highest peak in AP: **Jindhagada** (Araku Valley).

## Quick Revision Points
- **Earth's Core**: Nife (Nickel-Iron).
- **Sial vs Sima**: Silica-Alumina (Continents) vs Silica-Magnesium (Oceans).
- **Fold Mountains**: Compression; Block Mountains: Faulting.
- **Mariana Trench**: Deepest ocean point (~11km).
- **Troposphere**: All weather activity; Stratosphere: Ozone layer.
- **Standard Meridian of India**: 82°30' E (passes through Kakinada, AP).
- **AP Highest Peak**: Jindhagada (1690m).
