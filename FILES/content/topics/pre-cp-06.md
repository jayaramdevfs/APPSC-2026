# India's Foreign Policy & International Relations

> *India chose Non-Alignment in 1947 not out of weakness but out of strategic wisdom — staying independent let India court both superpowers while refusing to be bound by either.*

## The Big Picture

India's foreign policy is unique because of where India sits: the world's most populous democracy, a nuclear power, a civilization-state with 5,000 years of history, surrounded by difficult neighbors (Pakistan, China), and positioned between great power rivalries (USA vs. China).

The first 40 years were defined by **Non-Alignment** — keeping India out of Cold War alliances. The 1991 economic crisis forced a pivot: India had to engage the world economy. Since then, India has become progressively more active — deepening ties with the USA, joining plurilateral groupings (QUAD, SCO, BRICS, G20), and asserting influence in the Indo-Pacific.

For APPSC, foreign policy questions test: the foundational principles, key doctrines by name and year, India's position in major international organizations, nuclear policy, and AP-specific international linkages.

## 1. Foundational Pillars of India's Foreign Policy

India's foreign policy at independence rested on five principles:

1. **Non-Alignment**: Staying independent of Cold War military blocs. India was a founding member of the **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)** — established at the **1961 Belgrade Conference** with Nehru, Nasser (Egypt), and Tito (Yugoslavia) as key architects.

2. **Panchsheel (Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence)**: Signed with China in **1954** as part of the **India-China Agreement on Tibet Trade**. The five principles:
   - Mutual respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty
   - Mutual non-aggression
   - Mutual non-interference in internal affairs
   - Equality and mutual benefit
   - Peaceful coexistence

3. **Anti-Colonialism**: Active support for independence movements worldwide — Africa, Asia, Latin America.

4. **Respect for International Law and the UN Charter**: India as a rule-based international order proponent.

5. **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** ("The world is one family"): Ancient Sanskrit concept; India's G20 Presidency (2023) theme.

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **NAM (Non-Aligned Movement)** — Founded **1961**, **Belgrade**. India's contribution through Nehru's concept of **"Panch Shila"** shaped the movement. Today, NAM has 120 member states — the largest group of states outside formal alliances. But its relevance in the post-Cold War era is debated.

## 2. India's Key Foreign Policy Doctrines

| Doctrine / Initiative | Year | Core Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Gujral Doctrine** | 1997 | India should make **non-reciprocal concessions** to smaller South Asian neighbors — give without expecting equal return. Pakistan was excluded. Proposed by I.K. Gujral (PM 1997–98). |
| **Look East Policy** | 1991 | Engagement with **ASEAN** and East Asia; economic integration. Launched by Narasimha Rao government. |
| **Act East Policy** | 2014 | Upgraded version of Look East under Modi; more strategic + security dimension; deeper engagement. |
| **Neighbourhood First** | 2014 | Modi government's stated priority: India's immediate neighbors (SAARC) come first in diplomacy, aid, and cooperation. |
| **SAGAR** | 2015 | **Security and Growth for All in the Region** — India's Indian Ocean strategy; maritime security, trade, anti-piracy. |
| **Indo-Pacific Vision** | 2018+ | Free, Open, Inclusive Indo-Pacific — part of QUAD framework; implicitly counters China's maritime expansion. |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The shift from **Look East** to **Act East** (2014) is not just semantic. Look East was about economic integration with ASEAN. Act East adds **security cooperation** — India's concern about China's growing influence in Southeast Asia and the Indian Ocean required a more assertive engagement posture.

## 3. India and the United Nations

| Aspect | India's Position |
|---|---|
| **UN Membership** | **Founding member** (1945); signed the UN Charter at San Francisco |
| **UN Security Council** | India is NOT a permanent member. P5 = USA, UK, France, Russia, China. India has been a **non-permanent member 8 times** (most recently 2021–22) |
| **UNSC Reform** | India leads the **G4 group** (with Japan, Germany, Brazil) seeking permanent UNSC seats. Opposed by "Coffee Club" (Pakistan, South Korea, Italy, etc.) |
| **UN Peacekeeping** | India is consistently one of the **largest contributors** of troops to UN peacekeeping missions worldwide |
| **Specialized Agencies** | India is active in WHO, UNESCO, ILO, FAO, WIPO, IAEA |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** India was elected to the UN Human Rights Council for 2022–24 — another active multilateral engagement. India's bid for UNSC permanent membership is a recurring foreign policy objective across all governments.

## 4. Nuclear Policy

India is a declared nuclear weapons state — it conducted its first nuclear test (**Pokhran-I / Smiling Buddha**) in **1974**, and its second (**Pokhran-II / Operation Shakti**) in **May 1998**.

| Aspect | India's Position |
|---|---|
| **Nuclear Doctrine** | **No First Use (NFU)**: India will not use nuclear weapons first. But if attacked by nuclear, biological, or chemical weapons, India reserves the right to **massive retaliation** |
| **CTBT** | India has **NOT** signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty |
| **NPT** | India has **NOT** signed the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty — argues it is discriminatory (creates two classes of nations) |
| **Indo-US Nuclear Deal** | Signed in **2008** (123 Agreement); allowed India to access civilian nuclear technology and fuel from the international market while keeping its weapons program outside IAEA full-scope safeguards |
| **NSG** | India is seeking membership of the Nuclear Suppliers Group — opposed by China |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's position — nuclear weapons state that refuses NPT — is strategically rational but diplomatically costly. The **2008 Indo-US nuclear deal** essentially legitimized India's status by allowing civilian nuclear cooperation without demanding India join the NPT. This was a major strategic achievement. The 1998 tests led to sanctions by USA and others — all eventually lifted.

## 5. Key Regional and International Groupings

| Organisation | Members | HQ | India's Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SAARC** | 8 (South Asia) | Kathmandu | Founding member; largely stalled due to India-Pakistan tensions; last summit 2014 |
| **BIMSTEC** | 7 (Bay of Bengal rim) | Dhaka | Active alternative to SAARC; India's preferred South Asian grouping |
| **SCO** | China, Russia, India, Pakistan + others | Beijing | Full member since **2017** (Astana Summit) |
| **BRICS** | Brazil, Russia, India, China, S. Africa + 5 new members (2024) | Rotating | Founding member; expanded at Johannesburg Summit 2023 |
| **G20** | 20 major economies + EU | Rotating | India hosted G20 in **2023** (New Delhi Declaration) |
| **QUAD** | India, USA, Japan, Australia | Informal | Focus on Indo-Pacific maritime security; revived 2017 |
| **ASEAN** | 10 Southeast Asian countries | Jakarta | India is a **Dialogue Partner**, not member; FTA with ASEAN |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** BRICS expanded at the **Johannesburg Summit 2023** — new members (effective January 2024): Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Argentina (Argentina later declined). This is the most recent major BRICS development.

## 7. AP-Specific International Linkages

Andhra Pradesh has a unique position in India's external engagement:

- **Visakhapatnam** is a major port for India's trade with Southeast Asia under Act East Policy — deepwater harbor, naval base, submarine construction
- **Krishnapatnam Port** (Nellore) serves as a strategic hub for container trade with East and Southeast Asia
- **AP's Blue Economy Initiative**: Aligned with India's SAGAR doctrine — AP contributes 30% of India's fish output and 31% of shrimp exports
- **Telugu NRI Diaspora**: Among India's largest diaspora communities in the USA (~1.5 million), UK, Australia, and Gulf states — significant remittance flows and diplomatic leverage
- **Amaravati-Singapore**: AP government signed agreements with Singapore for urban development of the new capital region
