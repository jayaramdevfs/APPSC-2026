# National Income, Poverty & Employment

> *A country's development story is told in three numbers: how much it produces, how many people remain poor, and how many are employed. India's numbers on all three have improved — but not fast enough, or evenly enough.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims Economy | G2 Screening Economy | G1 Mains Paper 3 | G2 Paper 2
**Sources:** NCERT Class 11 & 12 Economics, PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey), UNDP Human Development Reports

---

## The Big Picture

India's economic growth story since 1991 is remarkable by any standard — GDP grew from $300 billion to $3.7 trillion in 30 years. Poverty declined from ~45% to under 20%. Life expectancy rose from 50 to 70 years. Yet the country ranks 134th on the Human Development Index — lower than many countries with smaller economies.

This gap between GDP growth and human development is the central question of Indian development economics. For APPSC, understanding this gap means knowing the specific measurement systems: how we measure GDP, how we define poverty, and what the labour market actually looks like for India's 500 million workers.

## 1. Measuring National Income — Three Methods

The **National Statistical Office (NSO)**, under MoSPI, is responsible for India's national accounts. (NSO is the renamed Central Statistics Office / CSO.)

| Method | Approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Product/Output Method** | Sum of value added by each sector | Avoids double-counting by counting only value-added |
| **Income Method** | Sum of all factor incomes (wages + rent + interest + profit) | Measures the distribution side |
| **Expenditure Method** | GDP = C + I + G + (X − M) | Measures demand side; C = consumption, I = investment, G = govt spending, X−M = net exports |

**Real vs. Nominal GDP:**
- **Nominal GDP** (current prices): Includes inflation — can rise even if output is flat
- **Real GDP** (constant prices, base year 2011–12): Pure volume of output growth
- **GDP Deflator** = (Nominal / Real) × 100 — broader price index than CPI (covers all goods, not just consumer basket)

> 📌 **Exam Note:** When the government announces **7% GDP growth**, it means **real GDP growth** — inflation-adjusted. Nominal GDP growth would be higher (real growth + inflation). APPSC questions sometimes test this distinction.

## 2. Human Development — Beyond GDP

GDP alone doesn't tell whether people's lives are improving. The **Human Development Index (HDI)** provides a composite view.

**Published by**: UNDP in the annual Human Development Report.

**Three Dimensions, Four Indicators:**
- **Health**: Life expectancy at birth
- **Education**: Mean years of schooling + Expected years of schooling
- **Standard of Living**: GNI per capita (PPP)

**HDI Ranges:**
- Very High: > 0.800 | High: 0.700–0.799 | Medium: 0.550–0.699 | Low: < 0.550

**India's Position:**
- India's HDI rank (2023/24 Report): **134 out of 193** — **Medium HDI** category
- India's score: ~0.644

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's HDI rank (134th) is much lower than its GDP rank (5th largest economy). This gap reflects persistent inequalities in health, education, and income distribution. Countries like Sri Lanka, Bhutan, and Bangladesh rank higher on HDI despite having smaller economies. Per capita income alone doesn't guarantee human development — it requires investment in public services.

## 3. Poverty in India

### Key Concepts

- **Absolute Poverty**: Unable to meet basic needs (food, clothing, shelter) — measured against a poverty line
- **Relative Poverty**: Being poor compared to others in society — measured by income distribution
- **Poverty Line**: Monthly consumption expenditure below which a person is considered poor

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** India's official poverty line is based on **monthly consumption expenditure (MPCE)**, NOT income. This is important — India's poor often don't have regular income (seasonal workers, daily laborers), so consumption better captures actual welfare.

### Poverty Line Committees

| Committee | Year | Rural (₹/month) | Urban (₹/month) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lakdawala** | 1993 | Based on calorie norms | — | First modern poverty line |
| **Tendulkar** | 2009 | **₹816** | **₹1,000** | Official until 2014; 2011–12 prices |
| **Rangarajan** | 2014 | **₹972** | **₹1,407** | Revised upward; not officially adopted |

### Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)

Published by **UNDP + OPHI (Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative)**. Goes beyond income to capture deprivation across:
- **3 dimensions**: Health, Education, Living Standards
- **10 indicators**: Nutrition, child mortality, schooling, electricity, cooking fuel, sanitation, water, housing, assets

**India's MPI progress**: Reduced from 55.1% (2005–06) to ~14.96% (2022–23) — 415 million people lifted out of multidimensional poverty in 15 years.

## 4. Key Poverty Alleviation Schemes

| Scheme | Year | Focus | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MGNREGS** | 2005 | Rural employment | 100 days guaranteed; legal right |
| **PM Awas Yojana** | 2015 | Housing for poor | Urban + Rural variants |
| **PM Jan Dhan Yojana** | 2014 | Financial inclusion | Zero-balance bank accounts; over 50 crore accounts |
| **PM Kisan Samman Nidhi** | 2019 | Farmer income | ₹6,000/year direct transfer |
| **Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY)** | 2018 | Health insurance | ₹5 lakh/family/year; 50 crore beneficiaries |
| **PMJJBY** | 2015 | Life insurance | ₹2 lakh cover at ₹436/year premium |
| **PMSBY** | 2015 | Accident insurance | ₹2 lakh cover at ₹20/year premium |

## 5. Employment and Labour Market

### Types of Workers (Census Classification)

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Main Workers** | Worked for **183+ days** in a year |
| **Marginal Workers** | Worked for **<183 days** |
| **Non-Workers** | Did not participate in economic activity |

### Key Labour Force Indicators (PLFS — Periodic Labour Force Survey by NSO)

| Indicator | Definition |
|---|---|
| **LFPR** (Labour Force Participation Rate) | % of working-age population in the labour force (employed + seeking work) |
| **WPR** (Worker Population Ratio) | Employed persons as % of total population |
| **UR** (Unemployment Rate) | Unemployed (seeking work) as % of Labour Force |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** India's female **LFPR** is significantly lower than male — particularly in urban areas. Urban female LFPR is around 25% versus 75% for men. This reflects a "missing women from workforce" phenomenon driven by social norms, safety concerns, and the nature of available work.

### Unemployment Data

- India's overall unemployment rate (2024 approx.): **~7–8%** (CMIE estimates)
- **Youth unemployment (15–24 age group)**: Much higher — ~20%+
- India's biggest challenge: **Informal Economy** — over **90% of India's workforce** works without formal contracts, social security, or job protections

### Labour Law Consolidation — Four Codes (2019–20)

India consolidated 44 central labour laws into four codes (not yet fully implemented):
1. **Code on Wages** (replaces Minimum Wages Act 1948, Payment of Wages Act, etc.)
2. **Code on Industrial Relations** (replaces Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Trade Unions Act)
3. **Code on Social Security** (replaces EPF, ESI, BOCW Acts)
4. **Code on Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions**

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **four labour codes** represent the most significant labour law reform since independence. The stated goal: expand formal employment by making hiring and firing easier and reducing compliance burden on employers. Critics argue this weakens worker protections. Implementation has been delayed because states must also pass their own versions.

**National Income Chain:**
- CSO/NSO (under MoSPI) measures national accounts
- Three methods: Output, Income, Expenditure
- Real GDP uses base year **2011–12**
- Expenditure formula: **C + I + G + (X−M)**

**HDI:**
- Published by: **UNDP**
- Three dimensions: Health (life expectancy) + Education (schooling) + Income (GNI per capita)
- India's rank: **134 / 193** (Medium HDI, ~0.644)

- Official poverty line: Based on **consumption expenditure** (not income)
- Tendulkar Committee: Rural **₹816**, Urban **₹1,000** (2011–12 prices)
- MPI: India reduced poverty from **55% (2005–06) to ~15% (2022–23)**

**Labour Market:**
- PLFS: Periodic Labour Force Survey by NSO
- Key metrics: LFPR, WPR, UR
- Informal economy: **90%+** of India's workforce
- Four Labour Codes: consolidated 44 laws into 4 (2019–20; not yet fully in force)

- PM Jan Dhan: **2014** (financial inclusion)
