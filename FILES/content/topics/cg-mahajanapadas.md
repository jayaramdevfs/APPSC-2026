# Mahajanapadas & Rise of Magadha

> *The Mahajanapadas arose because iron ploughs created agricultural surplus, and agricultural surplus created states that needed armies, and armies needed money, and money came from towns and trade. It all started with iron.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims Ancient History | G2 Screening History | G1 Mains Paper 2 | G2 Paper 1
**Sources:** R.S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past* (Oxford, 2005), Chapters 15–18

## The Big Picture

The Mahajanapadas (c. 600–300 BCE) represent one of the most consequential transformations in Indian history: the change from tribal pastoral societies into territorial kingdoms, driven by iron technology and surplus agriculture. The period also saw the Second Urbanization (the first was Harappan), the birth of punch-marked coins, the rise of Buddhism and Jainism, and the eventual emergence of India's first empire — the Mauryan — out of Magadha.

For APPSC, the key knowledge areas are: the sixteen mahajanapadas and their capitals, the four dominant states, the Magadha dynasties in sequence (Haryanka → Shishunaga → Nanda → Maurya), the reasons for Magadha's success, and the Iranian and Macedonian invasions.

---

## 1. Conditions for the Rise of Mahajanapadas

Four material conditions matured together around the 6th century BCE:

1. **Iron + Agriculture**: Iron ploughshares enabled clearing of heavy jungle and cultivation of hard soils → agricultural surplus → state revenue for armies
2. **Iron Weapons**: Stronger military capability → power projection over wider territories
3. **Territorial Identity**: Shift from *jana* (tribal loyalty) to *janapada* (territorial loyalty) — kings could claim allegiance from all inhabitants of a region
4. **Second Urbanization**: Surplus agriculture fed towns → trade → taxable commerce → state finance

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **material sequence** is critical to understand: Iron tools → agricultural surplus → state revenue → professional armies → territorial expansion → mahajanapadas. This is not just a list of facts — it's a causal chain. Without iron, no surplus; without surplus, no state; without state, no mahajanapadas. Magadha controlled the best iron deposits (near Rajgir) — which is exactly why it eventually dominated all others.

## 2. The Sixteen Mahajanapadas

Pali Buddhist texts list 16 states (~5th century BCE). Most were in the upper and mid-Gangetic plains, north of the Vindhyas.

**Four Most Powerful**: **Magadha, Koshala, Vatsa, Avanti**

### Key States and Capitals

| Mahajanapada | Capital | Modern Location | Key Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Magadha** | Rajgir → **Pataliputra** | Bihar (Patna/Gaya) | Founded India's first empire; best iron; strongest |
| **Koshala** | **Shravasti** (= Sahet-Mahet) | Eastern UP (Gonda/Bahraich borders) | Included Shakya republic; Ayodhya (but 5th century BC settlement) |
| **Vajji** | **Vaishali** (= Basarh) | Tirhut, N Bihar | **Republican oligarchy**; dominant clan = **Licchhavi**; 7,707 Licchhavi rajas |
| **Kashi** | **Varanasi** | UP | Initially most powerful; eventually absorbed by Koshala |
| **Vatsa** | **Kaushambi** (near Allahabad) | UP | Kuru clan shifted from Hastinapur |
| **Avanti** | **Ujjain** (north) / Mahishmati (south) | Malwa, MP | Iron working; Magadha's toughest rival — took 100 years to conquer |
| **Anga** | Champa | Monghyr/Bhagalpur, Bihar | Absorbed early by Magadha |
| **Mallas** | **Kushinara** (= Kasia, Deoria) | Eastern UP | Republican; **Buddha died at Kushinara** |
| **Gandhara** | Taxila | Pakistan/Afghanistan | Iranian then Alexander's target |
| **Kamboja** | — | Pamir, Central Asia (Tajikistan) | Connected to Pataliputra by *uttarapatha* (northern high road) |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **Shakyas of Kapilavastu** (= Piprahwa, Basti district, UP) — Gautama Buddha's clan — were a tribe within the Koshala territory. **Lumbini** (Buddha's birthplace) is 15 km from Piprahwa, in Nepal. Confirmed by an **Ashokan inscription** at Lumbini.

## 3. Second Urbanization — Material Life

The Mahajanapadas arose alongside a major material transformation:

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **NBPW (Northern Black Polished Ware)** | Lustrous, highly fired black pottery; ~700 sites; 7th–5th centuries BC; marker of Second Urbanization |
| **Punch-marked coins** | First coins in India — silver and copper; marks include hills, trees, fish, elephants; earliest hoards from eastern UP and Magadha |
| **Rice transplantation** | Began in Buddha's age (*shali* = transplanted rice) |
| **Tax on produce** | **1/6 (*bhaga*)** of produce; *bali* became compulsory |
| *Gahapati* | New social class — rich peasant/householder who owned land and hired labour |
| **20 towns** | Pali text lists 20 towns in age of Buddha — Kaushambi, Shravasti, Varanasi, Vaishali, Rajgriha |
| **18 guilds** | *Shrenis* of merchants and artisans at Pataliputra |

## 4. Jainism and Buddhism

### Vardhamana Mahavira (Jainism)

- Born **540 BC**; died **468 BC** at **Pavapuri** (near Rajgir) at age 72
- 24th *tirthankara*; preached in Magadha region

**Five Doctrines**: Ahimsa (non-violence), No lying, No stealing, No hoarding, Continence (5th added by Mahavira; first 4 from predecessor Parshva)

- *Ahimsa* supreme → agriculture prohibited (involves killing organisms) → **Jainas became traders and merchants**
- *Triratna*: Right knowledge + Right faith + Right action
- Two sects: **Shvetambara** (white-robed) and **Digambara** (naked) — split after famine in Magadha (~200 years after Mahavira)

### Gautama Buddha (Buddhism)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Born | **567 BC**, **Lumbini**, Nepal — Shakya kshatriya family |
| Enlightenment | Age 35 at **Bodh-Gaya** under pipal tree |
| First Sermon | **Sarnath, Varanasi** (Dhammachakkapavattana — "turning of the wheel of dharma") |
| Death | **487 BC** at **Kusinagara** (= Kasia, Deoria, eastern UP), age 80 |

**Core Doctrine**: World full of sorrows → desires cause suffering → conquer desires → nirvana (liberation)
**Eightfold Path**: Right observation, determination, speech, action, livelihood, effort, awareness, concern
**Three Jewels**: Buddha + Dhamma + Sangha (monastery/community)

**Why Buddhism Spread**:
- Did not recognize caste — admitted women AND shudras to the Sangha
- Used **Pali** (language of ordinary people)
- Appealed to Magadha (placed outside Aryavarta — looked down on by orthodox Brahmins)
- Ashoka's patronage → spread to Central Asia, West Asia, Sri Lanka

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Both Mahavira and Buddha were contemporaries and both preached in the **Magadha region** in the 5th–6th centuries BCE. Both rejected Brahminical authority. Both used Prakrit/Pali instead of Sanskrit. Both drew support from the new trading classes (*gahapati*) who resented Brahmin domination. This parallel is not coincidental — both arose as responses to the same social tensions created by the varna system and surplus agriculture.

## 5. Magadha Dynasties — The Road to Empire

### Haryanka Dynasty

**Bimbisara (544–492 BCE)**
- Established Magadha's dominance; contemporary of Mahavira + Buddha
- Conquered **Anga**; placed son Ajatashatru as viceroy at Champa
- **Three wives** (diplomatic marriages): (1) Koshalan princess (dowry = Kashi village, revenue 100,000 coins); (2) **Chellana** (Licchhavi princess) → gave birth to Ajatashatru; (3) Madra clan daughter (Punjab)
- Capital: **Rajgir (Girivraja)** — surrounded by 5 hills; stone walls closed openings → impregnable
- Sent physician **Jivaka** to Ujjain when Avanti's king fell ill — diplomatic genius
- Ruled ~52 years; **killed by son Ajatashatru**

**Ajatashatru (492–460 BCE)** — High watermark of Haryanka dynasty
- War with Koshala: Won; Koshalan king gave daughter in marriage + ceded Kashi
- War with **Vaishali (Licchhavis)**: Lasted **16 years** — their republican fortifications were immensely strong
- New weapons: **Catapult** (hurling stones) + **Mace-chariot** (mass casualties in open field)
- Added Kashi + Vaishali to Magadhan empire

**Udayin (460–444 BCE)**
- Built fort at confluence of **Ganges and Son** at Patna → founded **Pataliputra**
- This decision made Pataliputra India's capital for centuries

### Shishunaga Dynasty (~412–344 BCE)
- Greatest achievement: Destroyed **Avanti** (Ujjain) — ended the 100-year Magadha–Avanti rivalry
- Temporarily moved capital to Vaishali

### Nanda Dynasty (~344–322 BCE)
- **Mahapadma Nanda**: Title ***ekarat*** (sole sovereign; destroyer of all ruling princes)
- Conquered **Kalinga** — took the *jina* image as victory trophy
- Army: **200,000 infantry, 60,000 cavalry, 3,000–6,000 war elephants**
- **Alexander dared not advance east** — his army refused to fight this force
- Later Nandas proved unpopular → overthrown by Chandragupta Maurya (322 BCE)

### Maurya Dynasty (322–185 BCE) — India's First Empire

| Ruler | Period | Key Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| **Chandragupta Maurya** | 322–298 BCE | Defeated Nandas; defeated Seleucus; unified north India into first empire |
| **Bindusara** | 298–272 BCE | Expanded south |
| **Ashoka** | 272–232 BCE | Kalinga War (261 BCE); Buddhist conversion; Dhamma propagation; rock/pillar edicts |

## 6. Causes of Magadha's Success

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Iron deposits** | Near **Rajgir** (earliest capital) → superior weapons. Avanti also had iron (eastern MP mines near Ujjain) — which is why Avanti was Magadha's toughest rival for 100 years |
| **Strategic capitals** | Rajgir = 5 hills → impregnable; **Pataliputra** = *jaladurga* (water fort) at Ganges + Gandak + Son confluence |
| **River transport** | Ganges = highway for commerce and military movement; boat building easy; power projected east and west |
| **Fertile alluvium** | Heavy rainfall + cleared jungle alluvium = immensely productive rice-growing land; huge taxable surplus |
| **Elephant warfare** | Eastern India supplied war elephants; 6,000 under Nandas; elephants stormed fortresses and crossed marshy terrain impassable to cavalry |
| **Unorthodox social character** | Magadha was outside orthodox Aryavarta (Kiratas + Magadhas = recently Vedicized) → more dynamic and expansionist than kingdoms long under Vedic orthodoxy |

## 7. Iranian and Macedonian Invasions

### Iranian (Achaemenian) Invasion

- NW India in 6th century BCE: fragmented principalities (Kambojas, Gandharas) with no unifying power
- **Darius I** (Achaemenian king of Iran) invaded NW India in **516 BCE**
- Annexed Punjab (west of Indus) + Sindh → India became **20th satrapy** of the Iranian empire
- Indian satrapy paid tribute of **360 talents of gold = one-third of Iran's entire Asian revenue**
- Impact: Introduction of **Kharoshthi script** by Iranian scribes (used in NW India 3rd BC – 3rd AD; written **right to left**)

### Alexander's Invasion (326 BCE)

- Entered through **Khyber Pass**, 326 BCE
- Defeated **Porus (Paurava)** on the **Jhelum** river
- Stayed **19 months** (326–325 BCE); army refused to advance further east (feared Nanda empire's 200,000 infantry + elephants)
- Alexander died in Babylon, **323 BCE**

**Impact of Alexander's invasion**:
- Opened India–Greek contact → Greek accounts (Megasthenes) describe Indian society
- Fragmented NW principalities → Chandragupta Maurya exploited the power vacuum to unify north India
- **Gandhara art** (Greco-Buddhist blend) emerged in subsequent centuries
