# Social Issues in India

## The Big Picture

India's social fabric is held together by the Constitution's promise of equality and dignity — but the lived reality has always been more complex. Caste, religion, gender, and regional identity are not abstract concepts in India. They shape elections, drive policy, trigger violence, and determine life outcomes.

For APPSC candidates, social issues questions test two things: (1) do you understand the concept well enough to distinguish casteism from communalism from regionalism, and (2) do you know the specific legislation — the Article number, the Act year, the Commission name. Both are necessary. Neither alone is sufficient.

The pattern: define the issue → explain its political and social impact → cite the constitutional/legal response → give one current example from India or AP.

## 1. The Three Political Divides — Casteism, Communalism, Regionalism

These three form a recurring cluster in APPSC questions. Each represents a different axis along which Indian society fragments, and each has distinct causes, consequences, and constitutional responses.

### Casteism

**Definition:** The blind loyalty to one's own caste group that leads to prejudice, discrimination, and political mobilization based on caste identity rather than merit or national interest.

India's caste system pre-dates any known political structure. The Constitution abolished untouchability (Article 17) and created reservations to remedy centuries of exclusion. But caste has adapted — it now operates as a voting bloc, a social capital network, and in some areas, still as an instrument of oppression.

**Key Developments:**
- **Mandal Commission (1979)**: Recommended 27% reservation for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in central government jobs. Implementation by V.P. Singh's government in 1990 triggered nationwide protests and political realignment.
- **Indra Sawhney Case (1992)**: Supreme Court upheld OBC reservation but capped total reservation at 50%.
- **Atrocities Act (1989)**: Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act provides specialized courts and stringent penalties for crimes against Dalits and tribals.

> 📌 **Exam Note:** "Casteism in politics and politicization of caste" — this phrase from NCERT describes how the relationship is **two-way**. Politicians mobilize caste for votes (casteism in politics), and communities use political leverage to advance caste interests (politicization of caste). Both are problems.

### Communalism

**Definition:** An ideology that assumes all members of one religious group have identical political, economic, and social interests — and that these interests are opposed to those of other religious groups.

Communalism reduces individuals to religious identities. It feeds on competition for resources, historical grievances, and political manipulation. In India, communal tensions have periodically erupted into violence — the most significant recent examples being the 1984 anti-Sikh riots in Delhi (following Indira Gandhi's assassination) and the 2002 Gujarat riots.

**India's Constitutional Response — Secularism:**
- India adopted the word "Secular" in the Preamble through the **42nd Amendment (1976)**
- The Indian interpretation of secularism is **Sarva Dharma Sambhava** — equal respect for all religions (distinct from Western secularism's strict separation of church and state)
- Articles 25–28 guarantee freedom of religion; Article 15 prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The Indian state does not keep out of religion — it regulates religion, funds religious institutions, and mandates social reform (like banning untouchability). This makes Indian secularism unique: it's not religion-blind, it's religion-equal. This is the nuance that APPSC Mains tests.

### Regionalism

**Definition:** Excessive affinity and loyalty to a particular state or region, to the point where regional interest is prioritized over national unity.

Regionalism is not inherently negative — demands for recognition of cultural and linguistic identity are legitimate. The problem arises when regional sentiment becomes exclusionary ("sons of the soil" movements, anti-migrant agitation) or threatens national integrity (separatist movements).

**Causes:** Economic disparity between regions, linguistic identity, historical grievances about resource sharing (river water disputes), perceived neglect by the Centre.

**Key Example:** The formation of **Telangana (2014)** is the most recent and APPSC-specific example. The Telangana movement was driven by grievances over economic underdevelopment relative to coastal Andhra, water rights to the Godavari and Krishna rivers, and cultural/linguistic distinctiveness. It is the 29th state of India.

**Positive aspect of regionalism**: Regional aspirations led to the **States Reorganisation Act (1956)**, which reorganized states on linguistic lines — generally seen as a success for national integration through accommodation of diversity.

## 2. Crime Against Women

Women in India face a persistent pattern of gender-based violence. APPSC questions test the legislation and constitutional provisions, not just awareness of the problem.

### Key Crimes and Their Legal Framework

| Crime | Relevant Law | Key Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Domestic Violence** | Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act (PWDVA), **2005** | Covers physical, sexual, verbal, emotional, and economic abuse; provides Protection Officers and Shelter Homes |
| **Sexual Harassment at Work** | Vishakha Guidelines (1997 SC judgment) → codified as POSH Act, **2013** | Mandatory Internal Complaints Committee in workplaces with 10+ employees |
| **Dowry Deaths** | Section 304B, IPC | Death within 7 years of marriage related to dowry harassment; presumption of guilt on husband/in-laws |
| **Acid Attacks** | Sections 326A & 326B, IPC (amended 2013) | Stringent punishment; mandatory free medical treatment to victims |
| **Rape** | Section 376, IPC (Criminal Law Amendment Act, **2013** — post Nirbhaya) | Broadened definition of rape; mandatory fast-track courts |
| **Human Trafficking** | Section 370, IPC | Addresses trafficking for sexual exploitation, forced labour |

**Key Institutions:**
- **National Commission for Women (NCW)**: Statutory body (1992) to review legal safeguards for women
- **Women Helpline**: 181 (state) / 1091 (police)

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **POSH Act (2013)** full name: "Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition, and Redressal) Act." It requires ALL organizations with 10+ employees to have an **Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)**. Companies outside the formal sector use a **Local Complaints Committee (LCC)** at the district level.

## 3. Child Labour and Child Abuse

### Child Labour — The Legal Framework

**Definition (UNICEF):** Work that deprives children of childhood, potential, and dignity — harmful to their physical and mental development.

- **Constitutional Provision**: **Article 24** prohibits employment of children below 14 years in factories, mines, or hazardous employment
- **Article 21A** (86th Amendment, 2002): Right to free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14
- **Child Labour Act, 1986** (amended **2016**): Prohibited child labour (below 14) in ALL occupations (earlier only hazardous ones). Children aged 14–18 cannot work in hazardous occupations.

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The 2016 amendment to the Child Labour Act was criticized because it allows children to work in "family enterprises" — including farms and domestic work — which many argue is a loophole that perpetuates child labour in agricultural and household settings. This tension between law and enforcement is a common Mains discussion point.

### Child Abuse — POCSO Act

- **POCSO (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences) Act, 2012**: Comprehensive law protecting children (under 18) from sexual assault, sexual harassment, and pornography
- Key features: Gender-neutral (boys can be victims too), mandatory reporting by anyone who knows of such offence, special courts for speedy trial, child-friendly recording of evidence

## 4. Youth Unrest and Unemployment

India has the world's largest youth population — approximately 600 million people below 25 years. This is a demographic dividend, but also a potential crisis if not channeled productively.

**Root Causes of Youth Unrest:**
- Unemployment and underemployment — formal job creation has not kept pace with the workforce entering the labour market
- Mismatch between educational qualifications and job market requirements
- Regional disparities — rural youth have fewer opportunities than urban
- Political exploitation of youth grievances

**Major Agitations (Examples):**
- **Reservation agitations**: Maratha, Jat, Kapu (AP) — dominant castes demanding OBC status, driven by economic distress
- **Anti-corruption movement**: Anna Hazare's Jan Lokpal movement (2011) — mass youth participation
- **Student union conflicts**: Common in universities; often politicized

**Government Response:**
- **Skill India Mission**: Vocational training to bridge the skills gap; target of skilling 400 million by 2022
- **National Youth Policy (2014)**: Framework focusing on education, employability, entrepreneurship, health, and civic engagement
- **PM Mudra Yojana**: Credit for micro-enterprises to promote self-employment

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Kapu agitation in AP (2016)** was significant — Kapu community (a dominant agricultural caste) demanded BC reservation. It led to violence in Bhimavaram. This is the AP-specific example for reservation/youth unrest questions.

## Quick Revision — High-Frequency APPSC Facts

**Three Divides:**
- **Casteism**: Blind loyalty to caste → Mandal Commission (1979) → 27% OBC reservation (1990)
- **Communalism**: Religion as political identity → 42nd Amendment added "Secular" to Preamble → Sarva Dharma Sambhava
- **Regionalism**: Region over nation → Telangana (2014) is prime APPSC example

**Key Articles:**
- Article **17**: Abolition of untouchability
- Article **24**: No child labour under 14 in factories/mines
- Article **21A**: Right to Education (6–14 years); 86th Amendment
- Article **15(3)**: Special provisions for women and children

**Key Legislation (Years Matter):**
- PWDVA: **2005** (Domestic Violence)
- POCSO: **2012** (Child Sexual Offences)
- POSH: **2013** (Sexual Harassment at Workplace)
- Child Labour Amendment: **2016** (all occupations covered)
