## 1. The Advent of Europeans & EIC Expansion

### Order of Arrival
1. **Portuguese** (1498): Vasco da Gama reached Calicut (Zamorin). Capital shifted from Cochin to Goa (1530). Introduced printing press and tobacco to India.
2. **Dutch** (1605): Formed VOC. Established first factory at Masulipatnam (Andhra) in 1605. Primarily focused on Indonesian spice trade. Eliminated by British at Battle of Bedara (1559).
3. **English** (1600): East India Company formed by royal charter. First factory at Surat (1613). Forts: St. George (Madras), William (Calcutta), Bombay acquired from Portuguese as dowry.
4. **Danes** (1616): Tranquebar (TN), Serampore (Bengal). Sold settlements to British in 1845.
5. **French** (1664): French East India Company (state-sponsored by Colbert). First factory at Surat. Pondicherry was headquarters.

### British Conquest of India
- **Anglo-French Rivalry (Carnatic Wars, 1746–1763)**: Ended with French confined to Pondicherry after the Battle of Wandiwash (1760).
- **Conquest of Bengal**:
  - **Battle of Plassey (1757)**: Robert Clive defeated Nawab Siraj-ud-Daula (betrayed by Mir Jafar). EIC gained massive financial resources.
  - **Battle of Buxar (1764)**: Hector Munro defeated combined forces of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula (Awadh), and Shah Alam II (Mughal). EIC gained **Diwani** (revenue rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.
- **Mysore Wars (1767–1799)**: Four wars. Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan fought fiercely (Tipu died defending Srirangapatna in 1799). Arthur Wellesley played a key role in the final victory.
- **Maratha Wars (1775–1818)**: Three wars. Internal divisions among Maratha chiefs (Peshwa, Sindhia, Holkar) led to their defeat. Peshwaship abolished.
- **Sikh Wars (1845–1849)**: Punjab annexed by Lord Dalhousie after the death of Ranjit Singh.

## 2. EIC Administration & Economic Impact

### Land Revenue Systems
| System | Introduced By | Regions Covered | Key Feature |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Permanent Settlement (Zamindari)** | Cornwallis (1793) | Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Northern Madras | Zamindars made owners of land; revenue fixed permanently. Created a loyal absentee landlord class. |
| **Ryotwari System** | Reed & Munro (1820) | Madras, Bombay, parts of Assam | Direct settlement with individual cultivator (ryot). High tax rates (50%+); strict cash collection led to rural indebtedness. |
| **Mahalwari System** | Holt Mackenzie (1822) | NWFP, Central Provinces, Punjab | Settlement with the village community (Mahal) as a whole. |

### Economic Drain & Impact
- **De-industrialization**: Indian handloom industry ruined due to cheap machine-made British textiles and discriminatory tariffs.
- **Commercialization of Agriculture**: Forced cultivation of cash crops (indigo, cotton, opium, tea) instead of food crops, leading to frequent famines.
- **Drain of Wealth Theory**: Propounded by **Dadabhai Naoroji** in his book *"Poverty and Un-British Rule in India"*. Indignantly highlighted 'Home Charges' paid to Britain.

## 3. The Revolt of 1857

### Causes
- **Political**: Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse (annexed Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur); Annexation of Awadh (1856) on grounds of misgovernance.
- **Socio-Religious**: Interference in customs (Abolition of Sati 1829, Widow Remarriage Act 1856); fear of forced Christian conversion.
- **Military (Trigger)**: Introduction of Enfield rifle cartridges, supposedly greased with beef and pig fat.

### Centers & Leaders
- **Delhi**: Bahadur Shah Zafar (nominal), General Bakht Khan (real).
- **Kanpur**: Nana Sahib, Tantia Tope.
- **Lucknow**: Begum Hazrat Mahal.
- **Jhansi**: Rani Laxmibai.
- **Bihar (Arrah)**: Kunwar Singh (an octogenarian Zamindar).
- **Bareilly**: Khan Bahadur Khan.

### Aftermath & Government of India Act 1858
- EIC rule abolished; India transferred directly to the British Crown.
- Governor-General designated as **Viceroy** (Lord Canning became the first).
- Policy of annexation abandoned to appease Indian princes.
- Army reorganized (Peel Commission) to limit Indian soldiers, heavily skewing weapons and artillery command to Europeans.

## 4. Socio-Religious Reform Movements

- **Brahmo Samaj (1828)**: Founded by **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (Father of Modern India). Advocated monotheism, abolished Sati. Later led by Debendranath Tagore and Keshab Chandra Sen.
- **Arya Samaj (1875)**: **Swami Dayananda Saraswati**. Call to "Go back to Vedas". Discarded idol worship, untouchability. Started Shuddhi movement. Book: *Satyarth Prakash*.
- **Ramakrishna Mission (1897)**: **Swami Vivekananda**. Preached practical Vedanta, service to humanity is service to God.
- **Prarthana Samaj (1867)**: Founded by Atmaram Pandurang in Bombay. Prominent leader: MG Ranade.
- **Aligarh Movement**: **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan**. Promoted modern Western education among Muslims. Founded MAO College (later AMU).
- **Satyashodhak Samaj (1873)**: **Jyotiba Phule**. Fought against caste oppression in Maharashtra. Book: *Gulamgiri*.
- **Self-Respect Movement**: **E.V. Ramasamy Naicker (Periyar)** in Tamil Nadu.

## 5. Indian National Movement (Phase I: 1885–1915)

### Indian National Congress (1885)
- Founded by A.O. Hume in Bombay (W.C. Bonnerjee was 1st President). First session had 72 delegates.
- **Moderates (1885–1905)**: Leaders like Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta. Believed in constitutional methods (Pray, Petition, Protest).
- **Extremists (1905–1919)**: Lal, Bal, Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal), Aurobindo Ghosh. Demanded *Swaraj*. Tilak: "Swaraj is my birthright".

### Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi Movement (1905)
- Curzon partitioned Bengal ostensibly for administrative convenience, but actually to divide Hindus and Muslims.
- Anti-partition movement birthed the **Swadeshi and Boycott Movement**. Burning of foreign clothes; rise of indigenous industries.
- *Surat Split (1907)*: Congress formally split into Moderates and Extremists.

### Revolutionary Terrorism
- Reaction to heavy repression. Key acts:
  - Abhinav Bharat Society (V.D. Savarkar).
  - Anushilan Samiti (Bengal).
  - Ghadar Party (1913, San Francisco) led by Lala Hardayal.
  - Alipore Bomb Case (Khudiram Bose, Prafulla Chaki).

## 6. The Gandhian Era (1915–1947)

### Early Campaigns
- Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915.
- **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)**: First civil disobedience against Tinkathia system (indigo cultivation) in Bihar.
- **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)**: First hunger strike for plague bonus.
- **Kheda Satyagraha (1918)**: First non-cooperation movement against tax collection during crop failure.

### Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)
- Triggered by **Rowlatt Act** (1919) and the resultant **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre** (April 13, 1919).
- Combined with the **Khilafat Movement** (Ali brothers) to cement Hindu-Muslim unity.
- Sudden withdrawal by Gandhi after the **Chauri Chaura incident** (Feb 1922) where a mob burnt a police station killing 22 policemen.
- Led to formation of **Swaraj Party** (C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru) to fight elections.

### Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934)
- Catalyst: **Simon Commission (1927)** which had no Indian members (Black Flag protests: "Simon Go Back", death of Lala Lajpat Rai).
- 1929 Lahore Session (Pres: J.L. Nehru) declared **Purna Swaraj** (Complete Independence) as the goal.
- Triggered by **Dandi March (March 12, 1930)** — Gandhi walked to Dandi to break the salt law.
- Suspended temporarily by **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)** so Gandhi could attend the 2nd Round Table Conference. Resumed later but suppressed by 1934.

### The Final Phase & Partition
- **Government of India Act 1935**: Provided for provincial autonomy. Congress formed ministries in 1937 but resigned in 1939 protesting India's forced entry into WW2.
- **August Offer (1940) & Cripps Mission (1942)**: British tried to secure Indian support holding out dominion status; both rejected.
- **Quit India Movement (1942)**: August 8, Gowalia Tank, Bombay. Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die"**. Entire top leadership arrested immediately. Leaderless, spontaneous nationwide revolts.
- **INA (Indian National Army)**: Formed by Mohan Singh, led effectively by **Subhas Chandra Bose** ("Give me blood, I will give you freedom").
- **Cabinet Mission (1946)**: Proposed an undivided India with a weak center. Interim government formed.
- **Mountbatten Plan (June 3, 1947)**: Officially partitioned the subcontinent into India and Pakistan.
- India became free on **August 15, 1947**.

## 7. Crucial Constitutional Acts
1. **Regulating Act (1773)**: Created post of Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings). Supreme Court at Calcutta.
2. **Charter Act (1813)**: Ended EIC monopoly over India trade (except tea and trade with China). ₹1 lakh allocated for education.
3. **Charter Act (1833)**: GG of Bengal became Governor-General of India (William Bentinck). EIC became purely administrative.
4. **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)**: Introduced **separate electorates for Muslims**.
5. **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919)**: Introduced **Dyarchy** at the provinces (Reserved and Transferred subjects). Bicameral legislature at the center.
6. **Government of India Act (1935)**: Replaced provincial dyarchy with provincial autonomy; proposed an All-India Federation (which never materialized).

## 8. AP / Andhra Regional Context (Important)
- **First English Factory**: Masulipatnam (1611).
- **Vandemataram Movement**: Vigorous in Rajahmundry (led by Bipin Chandra Pal's tour, Rajahmundry Arts College strike by student Markandeyulu).
- **Rampa Rebellion (1922-24)**: Led by **Alluri Sitarama Raju** against the Madras Forest Act in the Godavari agency area.
- **Non-Cooperation Movement**: Chirala-Perala movement (led by Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya), Palnadu forest satyagraha.
- **Language Movement**: Formation of Andhra Mahasabha to push for a separate linguistic state out of the Madras Presidency.
- **National Flag**: Designed by **Pingali Venkayya** (a native of Machilipatnam).

## 9. Prelims Revision Points (Quick Recall / Exam Traps)

1. **Trap**: Portuguese were the **first to arrive** (1498) and the **last to leave** India (Goa was liberated in 1961).
2. The Dutch first factory was in Masulipatnam (1605), not Surat.
3. Dual Government in Bengal was started by Robert Clive and abolished by Warren Hastings.
4. Permanent Settlement: Cornwallis. Ryotwari: Thomas Munro. Mahalwari: Holt Mackenzie.
5. Sati Abolition (1829) was enacted by Lord William Bentinck with efforts from Raja Ram Mohan Roy.
6. **Trap**: The INC was founded during the viceroyalty of Lord Dufferin (1885), NOT Curzon.
7. Partition of Bengal (1905) = Lord Curzon. Re-unification (1911) = Lord Hardinge.
8. Muslim League formed in 1906 (Dhaka) by Aga Khan and Nawab Salimullah.
9. "Swaraj is my birthright" = B.G. Tilak.
10. Gandhi's first Satyagraha in India = Champaran (1917) for indigo farmers. First hunger strike = Ahmedabad mill strike (1918).
11. Rowlatt Act (1919) = "No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal".
12. Swaraj Party established by C.R. Das (President) and Motilal Nehru (Secretary) after NCM suspension.
13. Complete Independence (Purna Swaraj) resolution passed at 1929 Lahore session.
14. Separate electorates introduced in 1909. Dyarchy in provinces in 1919. Provincial autonomy in 1935.
15. Do or Die = Gandhi (Quit India, 1942). Dilli Chalo = Subhas Bose.
16. **Trap**: Subhas Chandra Bose resigned from INC presidency in 1939 (Tripuri session) and formed the **Forward Bloc**.
