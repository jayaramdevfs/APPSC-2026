# Vedic Age (c. 1500 – 500 BCE)

> *The Rig Vedic Aryans mentioned the word "horse" 215 times and the word "territory" zero times. By the later Vedic period, the horse-driven pastoralists had become settled farmers — and territory mattered more than tribe.*

**Sources:** R.S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past* (Oxford, 2005), Chapters 11–13

## The Big Picture

The Vedic Age produced India's oldest texts — but understanding them requires knowing who wrote them and when they came from. The Aryans were not a race; they were a linguistic community of Indo-European speakers who migrated from Central Asia into the northwest Indian subcontinent around 1500 BCE. They brought with them the horse, the spoked chariot, fire worship, and the soma ritual — all absent from Harappan civilization — confirming that they were a distinct cultural group that arrived after Harappan civilization had already declined.

The Vedic period has two phases: the **Early (Rig) Vedic phase** (~1500–1000 BCE) of pastoral tribal people living in the northwest, and the **Later Vedic phase** (~1000–500 BCE) of settled farmers who expanded into the Gangetic plains, developed territorial kingdoms, and hardened the caste system. Understanding the shift between these two phases is the central question of the Vedic Age for APPSC.

## 1. Who Were the Aryans?

- **"Aryan" is linguistic, not racial**: It describes speakers of Indo-European languages. In the Rig Veda, *arya* occurs 36 times and denotes a cultural community, NOT a race.
- **Origin**: Central Asia (Andronovo culture + BMAC region, 1900–1500 BCE)
- **Evidence of Central Asian origin**:
  - Genetic marker M17: 40% in Central Asian steppes; 35% in Hindi-speaking Delhi population
  - Flora/fauna in Rig Veda: temperate conditions, birch trees; NO tiger, NO rhinoceros (tropical animals) — text composed when Aryans were in northwest, not tropical India
  - Soma plant (*ephedra*) found at Togolok-21 site in Margiana, Turkmenistan

### The Horse Question

- The horse was the **defining cultural marker** of Aryan civilization:
  - *Asva* (horse) in Rig Veda: **215 times**; *go* (cow): 176 times; *vrsabha* (bull): 170 times
  - Earliest horse in South Asia: **Pirak complex, Baluchistan (~1700 BCE)**
  - Horse + spoked wheel chariots appear in **Gandhara grave culture (Swat valley) ~1400 BCE**
  - Horse completely **absent from mature Harappan sites** (Harappa excavations 1986–95; Dholavira)
- **Harappan toy carts: solid wheels** (not spoked). Spoked wheels NOT found at Harappa/Mohenjo-daro.

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **horse** is the single most important piece of evidence separating Harappan and Vedic civilizations. The Rig Veda is saturated with horses — the *ashvamedha* (horse sacrifice), horse-drawn chariots, the word *asva* 215 times. The Harappan civilization shows zero convincing horse bones in extensive excavations. A civilization that used horses as centrally as the Rig Veda describes would leave abundant horse remains. The conclusion: Harappan ≠ Vedic Aryan. They were different peoples, different times.

## 2. Early Vedic / Rig Vedic Phase (~1500 – 1000 BCE)

### Geography and Archaeological Marker

- Lived in "Land of Seven Rivers" — eastern Afghanistan, NWFP, Punjab, western UP
- Archaeological marker: **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)** + late Harappan pottery at **Bhagwanpura** (Haryana); dated 1600–1000 BCE
- Did NOT live in cities — lived in fortified mud settlements

### Economy — Pastoral People

- **Primarily pastoral**: *gavishthi* (word for war) = "search for cows"
- Cattle = primary wealth; gifts to priests = cows (never land)
- Agriculture secondary; plough mentioned; wheat, barley known
- The cow appears 176 times in Rig Veda — but cow was wealth, not sacred yet

### Political Organization — Tribal

| Term | Meaning | Rig Vedic Context |
|---|---|---|
| *Jana* | Tribe | 275 times in Rig Veda; primary unit of loyalty |
| *Janapada* | Territory | **NOT MENTIONED ONCE** in Rig Veda |
| *Rajan* | Tribal chief | Hereditary but NOT unlimited power; worked with assemblies |
| *Sabha* | Tribal assembly | Women attended in Rig Vedic times |
| *Samiti* | Tribal assembly | Public deliberative body |
| *Vidatha* | Assembly | Disappeared in later Vedic period |
| *Purohita* | Chief priest | Most important royal functionary; Vasishtha + Vishvamitra were key |
| *Bali* | Voluntary tribute | Offered to rajan by kinsmen |
| *Gavishthi* | "Search for cows" | The Rig Vedic word for WAR |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The word *janapada* (territory) does NOT appear even once in the Rig Veda. The entire political vocabulary is tribal, not territorial. This is a crucial distinction — Rig Vedic people felt loyalty to their *jana* (tribe), not to any geographic territory. Territorial identity emerged only in the Later Vedic period.

**Key Conflict**: The **Battle of Ten Kings (Dasharajna)** — fought on river Parushni (= Ravi). Bharata clan vs. 10 chiefs (5 Aryan, 5 non-Aryan). Bharatas won → merged with Purus → became Kurus. India is named **Bharatavarsha** after the Bharata tribe.

### Society — Still Largely Egalitarian

- Kinship = basis of society; family = large patriarchal joint unit
- **Shudras appear only in Book 10** of Rig Veda (the LATEST addition) — varna system not yet hardened
- Women attended sabha and vidatha; women could offer sacrifices; marriageable age ~16–17; widow remarriage mentioned
- Society "still tribal and egalitarian" — gifts = cows, not land; no territorial property

### Religion — Nature Worship, Prayer-Based

| God | Hymns | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Indra** | **250** (most) | Rain god + warlord; *Purandara* = destroyer of dwelling units |
| **Agni** | **200** (second) | Fire god; intermediary between humans and gods |
| **Varuna** | Third position | Water; upholds natural order |
| **Soma** | — | God of plants; ritual intoxicating drink |

- Worship = pragmatic (asking for cattle, children, wealth, health) — NOT for spiritual liberation
- No idols; prayer-based; Agni as messenger of offerings to gods

## 3. Later Vedic Phase (~1000 – 500 BCE)

### Sources and Archaeological Marker

**Texts**: Sama Veda (music), Yajur Veda (rituals), Atharva Veda (charms/spells), Brahmanas (ritual prose)
**Archaeological marker**: **PGW (Painted Grey Ware) culture** — ~700 sites in upper Gangetic basin; sites used iron weapons alongside this pottery

### Geographic Expansion

- Expanded from Punjab over entire western UP (Ganga-Yamuna doab)
- **Kuru-Panchala people** established capital at **Hastinapur** (Meerut district) — the Mahabharata capital
- **Battle of Bharata** (Mahabharata) fought ~**950 BCE** on upper doab; Kauravas vs. Pandavas; nearly destroyed Kuru clan
- After flood, remnants moved to **Kaushambi** near Allahabad
- By ~500 BCE: expanded to Koshala (eastern UP) and Videha (north Bihar)

### Iron Technology

- Iron first appears in Gandhara area ~**1000 BCE**
- Commonly used in western UP from ~**800 BCE**
- Iron in later Vedic texts = ***shyama* or *krishna ayas*** (black metal)
- Iron axe cleared forests; iron weapons expanded Aryan power east

### From Tribe to Kingdom — Political Transformation

| Early Vedic | Later Vedic |
|---|---|
| Tribal chief (rajan) | Territorial king (*rashtra*) |
| Voluntary tribute (*bali*) | Compulsory tribute; *sangrihitri* (tax collector) |
| Vidatha, sabha, samiti all active | Vidatha disappeared; sabha/samiti controlled by nobles |
| Women in assemblies | Women excluded from sabha |
| *Jana* = primary loyalty | *Janapada* = territory; new concept *rashtra* + *rajya* |

**Three New Royal Rituals** (demonstrate territorial sovereignty):
1. **Rajasuya**: Royal consecration sacrifice — confers supreme power
2. **Ashvamedha**: Horse sacrifice — signifies control over territory through which horse roams uninterrupted for one year
3. **Vajapeya**: Chariot race — royal chariot made to "win" against kinsmen

### Varna System Hardens

| Varna | Role | Later Vedic Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmin** | Priests | Rose dramatically; monopolized sacrifice knowledge; most powerful class |
| **Kshatriya** (Rajanya) | Warriors | Second; sometimes clashed with brahmins |
| **Vaishya** | Farmers, cattle-breeders, artisans | **Only tribute payers** in later Vedic times |
| **Shudra** | Servants of the conquered | Denied *upanayana* (sacred thread); denied Vedic education; "to be beaten at will" (Aitareya Brahmana) |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **Vaishyas were the ONLY tribute-payers** in later Vedic society. Brahmins and Kshatriyas lived on the tributes extracted from Vaishyas. The process by which the original *vis* (clan members) were converted into tribute-paying *vaishyas* was "long and protracted." This is the Vedic origin of taxation — and of India's deep tradition of rural surplus extraction by non-producing elites.

**New Social Institutions:**
- ***Gotra***: Clan lineage; **gotra exogamy** = no marriage within same gotra
- ***Upanayana***: Sacred thread ceremony — denied to shudras and women
- ***Ashrama* system**: 4 stages of life (brahmachari → grihastha → vanaprastha → sannyasin); first 3 well-defined, 4th not yet established

### Religion — Sacrifice-Based

- Indra and Agni lost importance; **Prajapati** (Creator) became supreme
- **Rudra** (proto-Shiva, god of animals) and **Vishnu** (preserver) became important
- Worship shifted from prayer to **sacrifice (*yajna*)** — elaborate, animal-killing rituals
- 240,000 cows given as *dakshina* in one rajasuya sacrifice — enormous wealth concentration
- **Idols** (idol worship) begin to appear for the first time
