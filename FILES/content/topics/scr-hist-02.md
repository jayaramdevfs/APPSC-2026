## 1. Early Medieval Period (~8th to 12th Century)

### The Tripartite Struggle
- Fought for control over **Kanauj** (symbol of sovereignty in North India)
- Involved three great empires:
  1. **Gurjara-Pratiharas** (Western India & Malwa)
  2. **Palas** (Eastern India / Bengal)
  3. **Rashtrakutas** (Deccan)
- **Rashtrakutas** (founded by Dantidurga; capital: Malkhed) were the most powerful and longest-lasting among the three.
- Kailashnath Temple at Ellora built by Rashtrakuta king Krishna I.

### The Chola Empire (9th–13th Century)
- Founded by **Vijayalaya** (captured Thanjavur from Mutharaiyars).
- **Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE)**: Built the Brihadeshwara Temple (Thanjavur); expanded empire by sea (Sri Lanka, Maldives).
- **Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE)**: Conquered up to the Ganga establishing "Gangaikonda Cholapuram"; naval expedition to Sriwijaya (Southeast Asia).
- **Administration**:
  - Entire empire divided into Mandalam (province) → Valanadu → Nadu (district) → Kurram (villages).
  - **Local Self-Government**: Highly developed. Village assemblies called *Ur* (general assembly) and *Sabha/Mahasabha* (brahmin settlements).
  - *Uttaramerur Inscription* (Parantaka I) gives details of election to local committees.
- **Agrarian Expansion**: Network of canals from Kaveri. Mention of *Brahmadeya* (tax-free land grants to brahmins) and *Devadana* (grants to temples).

## 2. The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE)

### Dynasties of the Sultanate
| Dynasty | Period | Key Rulers |
|---------|--------|------------|
| **Slave (Mamluk)** | 1206–1290 | Qutbuddin Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia, Balban |
| **Khalji** | 1290–1320 | Jalaluddin, Alauddin Khalji |
| **Tughlaq** | 1320–1414 | Ghiyasuddin, Muhammad bin Tughlaq, Firoz Shah |
| **Sayyid** | 1414–1451 | Khizr Khan |
| **Lodi** | 1451–1526 | Bahlul, Sikandar, Ibrahim Lodi |

### Slave Dynasty (Mamluks)
- **Qutbuddin Aibak**: Founder; known as 'Lakh Baksh' (giver of lakhs); started Qutb Minar.
- **Iltutmish**: Real founder of Delhi Sultanate; shifted capital from Lahore to Delhi; saved India from Genghis Khan by not giving shelter to Khwarezm Shah; introduced **Iqta system** (land grants in lieu of salary) and **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** (Corps of Forty).
- **Razia Sultan**: Only woman ruler of Delhi; opposed by Turkish nobles.
- **Balban**: Destroyed the 'Forty'; introduced Persian customs like **Sijda** (prostration) and **Paibos** (kissing the monarch's feet); policy of "Blood and Iron" to restore crown prestige.

### Khalji Dynasty
- **Alauddin Khalji**: First Turkish Sultan to cross Vindhyas and conquer Deccan (led by Malik Kafur).
- Maintained a large permanent standing army (paid in cash).
- **Military Reforms**: Introduced *Dagh* (branding of horses) and *Chehra* (descriptive roll of soldiers) to end corruption.
- **Market Control Policy**: Set up three markets in Delhi (food grains, costly cloth/horses/slaves, cattle/slaves) under strict control of an officer called *Shahna-i-Mandi*. Fixed prices to afford his massive army on low pay.

### Tughlaq Dynasty
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq (MBT)**: Known for his controversial experiments:
  1. **Transfer of Capital**: Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri) to control Deccan; failed.
  2. **Token Currency**: Introduced copper/bronze coins at par with silver *tanka*; failed due to massive forgery.
  3. Khurasan & Karachi Expeditions; Taxation in Doab (during famine time).
  - Famous traveler **Ibn Battuta** (Morocco) visited his court.
- **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**: Built extensive **canal network** (Yamuna, Sutlej); established new cities (Firozabad, Hisar, Jaunpur); set up *Diwan-i-Khairat* (for poor orphans/widows) and *Diwan-i-Bandagan* (department of slaves). Imposed Jizya strictly, even on Brahmins.

### Lodi Dynasty
- **Sikandar Lodi**: Founded the city of Agra (1504); measured land using *Gaj-i-Sikandari*.
- **Ibrahim Lodi**: Defeated by Babur in the **First Battle of Panipat (1526)**, marking the end of the Sultanate.

### Sultanate Administration & Architecture
- **Central Admin**: Sultan (head) → *Wazir* (finance) → *Ariz-i-Mumalik* (military) → *Qazi* (justice).
- **Iqta System**: Empire divided into tracts (iqtas) given to nobles (Muqtis/Walis) who maintained law & order and supplied troops from collected revenues.
- **Architecture**: 
  - True Arch and Dome introduced by Turks.
  - Alai Darwaza (Alauddin Khalji) - first true arch.
  - Use of red sandstone and mortar.

## 3. Vijayanagara & Bahmani Kingdoms (14th–16th Century)

### Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 CE)
- Founded by **Harihara and Bukka** (Sangama dynasty) on the banks of Tungabhadra.
- Four dynasties: Sangama → Saluva → Tuluva → Aravidu.
- **Krishnadeva Raya (1509–1529 CE)**: Greatest ruler (Tuluva dynasty). Mentioned by Babur in *Baburnama* as the most powerful ruler of India. Authored *Amuktamalyada* (Telugu).
  - His court had **Ashtadiggajas** (eight great Telugu poets, including Allasani Peddana).
- **Amaranayaka System**: Military commanders (Nayakas) were given territories (Amaram) to govern and maintain troops for the king (similar to Iqta).
- **Architecture**: Virupaksha Temple, Vittalaswami Temple (Hampi). Distinct *Kalyana Mandapa* features.
- Visited by foreign travelers: *Nicolo Conti* (Italy), *Abdur Razzaq* (Persia), *Domingo Paes* (Portugal).
- **Battle of Talikota (1565)**: Vijayanagara defeated by allied Deccan Sultanates; city of Hampi destroyed.

### Bahmani Kingdom (1347–1527 CE)
- Founded by Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah.
- Prime Minister **Mahmud Gawan** implemented key reforms; expanded the empire.
- Disintegrated into five Deccan Sultanates: 
  1. Bijapur (Adil Shahis)
  2. Golconda (Qutb Shahis)
  3. Ahmadnagar (Nizam Shahis)
  4. Bidar (Barid Shahis)
  5. Berar (Imad Shahis).

## 4. The Mughal Empire (1526–1707 CE)

### Founders: Babur & Humayun
- **Babur**: Won First Battle of Panipat (1526) against Ibrahim Lodi (use of artillery). Wrote autobiography *Tuzuk-i-Baburi* in Turkish.
- **Humayun**: Defeated twice by Sher Shah Suri (Battle of Chausa 1539, Kannauj 1540); exiled to Iran; regained Delhi in 1555 but died falling from library stairs.

### Sur Interregnum (1540–1555 CE)
- **Sher Shah Suri**: Built the Grand Trunk Road (Sonargaon to Peshawar); introduced the silver coin **Rupiya** (precursor to modern Rupee); excellent land revenue system which became the basis for Akbar's system.

### Akbar (1556–1605 CE)
- Defeated Hemu in Second Battle of Panipat (1556) with Bairam Khan.
- **Mansabdari System**: Every official given a rank (Mansab) determining salary and military responsibilities. Composed of two numbers: *Zat* (personal rank/salary) and *Sawar* (cavalrymen to maintain).
- **Land Revenue (Zabti/Bandobast System)**: Orchestrated by Todar Mal. Land surveyed, categorized (Polaj, Parauti, Chachar, Banjar), and tax fixed at 1/3rd of the average produce of 10 years (Dahsala).
- **Religious Policy**: Abolished Jizya (1564) and pilgrimage tax. Promoted **Sulh-i-Kul** (universal peace). Established *Ibadat Khana* (Hall of Prayer, 1575) at Fatehpur Sikri. Propounded a new syncretic order **Din-i-Ilahi**.
- **Nine Gems (Navaratnas)**: Included Birbal, Todar Mal, Tansen, Abul Fazl.

### Jahangir & Shah Jahan
- **Jahangir**: Executed 5th Sikh Guru Arjan Dev (revolt of Prince Khusrau); wife Nur Jahan practically ran the admin; painting reached its zenith. Allowed English (Thomas Roe, William Hawkins) to set up factories.
- **Shah Jahan**: Architecture reached its absolute peak (Taj Mahal, Jama Masjid, Red Fort). Fought wars in Deccan.

### Aurangzeb (1658–1707 CE)
- Defeated his brothers (Dara Shikoh) in war of succession.
- Reign saw maximum expansion of Mughal empire, completely annexing Deccan (Bijapur 1686, Golconda 1687).
- Re-imposed Jizya (1679). Constant wars with Marathas (Shivaji), Sikhs (executed 9th Guru Tegh Bahadur), and Rajputs drained the treasury ("Deccan ulcer" ruined him).

## 5. Bhakti & Sufi Movements

### Bhakti Movement
- **Alvars & Nayanars**: Began in South India (Tamil Nadu) around 7th-9th centuries. Alvars (devotees of Vishnu), Nayanars (devotees of Shiva). Casteless movement. *Nalayira Divyaprabandham* (Alvar compilation).
- **Advaita & Vishishtadvaita**: 
  - *Shankaracharya* (8th C.): Advaita (non-dualism).
  - *Ramanuja* (11th C.): Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism).
- **Virashaiva Movement**: Started by Basavanna (12th C.) in Karnataka; vehemently opposed caste and ritualism.
- **North Indian Saints**:
  - *Ramananda*: Brought Bhakti to North.
  - *Kabir*: Weaver; rejected orthodox Islam and Hinduism; *dohes* preserved in Bijak and Guru Granth Sahib.
  - *Guru Nanak*: Nirguna bhakti; founded Sikhism.
  - *Mirabai*: Rajput princess, devotee of Krishna.
  - *Chaitanya Mahaprabhu*: Bengal; popularized Kirtan.
  - *Tulsidas*: Awadhi version of Ramayana (*Ramcharitmanas*).

### Sufism
- Islamic mysticism focusing on love and devotion to God.
- Organized into *Silsilas* (orders). Most important in India:
  - **Chishti Silsila**: Founded in India by Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti (Ajmer). Nizamuddin Auliya (Delhi). Adopted yogic practices, popular among poor, held musical gatherings (*Sama*).
  - **Suhrawardi Silsila**: Prominent in Punjab/Multan; accepted state patronage (unlike Chishtis).

## 6. Significance for APPSC (Andhra Links)
- **Kakatiya Dynasty (Warangal, ~12th-14th c.)**: Contemporary of early Delhi Sultanate. Rani Rudramadevi was a prominent ruler. Marco Polo visited Kakatiya kingdom (Motupalli port). Prataparudra was defeated by Delhi Sultanate forces (Alauddin Khalji, then Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq).
- **Qutb Shahis of Golconda**: Emerged after Bahmani breakup. Capital at Golconda, later Hyderabad. Founded Hyderabad city and built Charminar (Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah). Patronized Telugu literature (Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah was called 'Malkibharama'). Annexed by Aurangzeb in 1687.
- **Vijayanagara**: Southern Andhra regions (Rayalaseema) were the core of the empire. Key architecture (Lepakshi temple - Veerbhadra, Nandi statue) is located in Anantapur, AP.

## 7. Thematic Understanding (For Mains)

- **Centralization vs. Decentralization**: Delhi Sultans and Mughals continuously struggled to centralize power against autonomous regional chiefs and *Zamindars*. The *Iqta* and *Mansabdari* systems were administrative tools to prevent nobles from becoming hereditary, though eventually both degenerated leading to imperial collapse.
- **Cultural Syncretism**: The Medieval period was not merely a clash of Hinduism and Islam but a profound synthesis (Indo-Islamic architecture, Urdu language origins, Sufi-Bhakti intellectual exchange, Mughal paintings blending Persian/Indian styles).
- **Economic Integration**: Growth of inner markets; standard currencies (Tanka, Rupiya); extensive road networks boosted trade, fueling the highly monetized economy under Akbar and Shah Jahan.

## 8. Prelims Revision Points (Quick Recall / Exam Traps)

1. **Trap**: Iltutmish introduced the Iqta system in India, NOT Alauddin Khalji or Akbar.
2. Market Control Policy = Alauddin Khalji (officer: Shahna-i-Mandi).
3. Transfer of Capital + Token Currency = Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
4. **Trap**: Firoz Shah Tughlaq levied Jizya on Brahmins, who were previously exempt.
5. First Battle of Panipat (1526) = Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi. Second Battle (1556) = Akbar vs Hemu.
6. Mansabdari system parts: *Zat* (personal rank) and *Sawar* (cavalrymen). Introduced by Akbar.
7. Rupiya was introduced by Sher Shah Suri, NOT Akbar.
8. **Trap**: Chola village assemblies were *Ur* (non-brahmin) and *Sabha* (brahmin), explicitly detailed in the Uttaramerur inscription.
9. Amaranayaka system (Vijayanagara) = Similar to Iqta system of Delhi Sultanate.
10. Ashtadiggajas = Krishnadeva Raya's court (Telugu poets); Navaratnas = Akbar's and Chandragupta II's court.
11. Battle of Talikota (1565) — destroyed Vijayanagara; fought at Rakshasi-Tangadi.
12. Alvars = Vishnu devotees; Nayanars = Shiva devotees.
13. Chishtis avoided state patronage; Suhrawardis accepted state patronage and wealth.
14. Kakatiya capital = Orugallu (Warangal). Marco Polo visited Motupalli port during Rudramadevi's reign.
15. Dahsala / Zabti land revenue system was devised by Todar Mal under Akbar.
