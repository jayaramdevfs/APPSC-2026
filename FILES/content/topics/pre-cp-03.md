# Constitutional Authorities & Governance

> *The Constitution distributes power across three tiers — Union, State, Local — and within each tier across Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary. Understanding who has what power, and why, is the core of Indian Polity.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims Unit 4 | G2 Paper 1 Section B | G1 Mains Paper 3
**Sources:** Constitution of India (Parts V, VI, IX, IXA), NCERT Class 11 (Executive & Legislature), M. Laxmikanth

## The Big Picture

India's constitutional architecture is a three-tier government: Union at the top, States in the middle, and Local Bodies (Panchayats and Urban Local Bodies) at the base. Within each tier, power is divided between an Executive (that governs), a Legislature (that makes laws and oversees the executive), and a Judiciary (that interprets laws and resolves disputes).

The key to this topic is understanding the **formal vs. real** executive. In India's parliamentary system, the President and Governor are the *formal* (constitutional) heads — they act on advice. The Prime Minister and Chief Minister are the *real* executive heads — they wield actual power. This distinction generates a large share of APPSC questions.

## 1. The Union Executive (Articles 52–75)

### The President of India (Art 52–62)

The President is the constitutional head of the Union — the formal executive. All executive actions of the Government of India are taken in the President's name.

**Election:**
- Indirectly elected by an Electoral College
- Electoral College = elected members of Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha + State Legislative Assemblies
- Proportional representation with single transferable vote

**Powers:**
| Type | Powers |
|---|---|
| **Executive** | Appoints PM, Council of Ministers, Governors, CAG, CEC, Chief Justice; Supreme Commander of Armed Forces |
| **Legislative** | Summons and prorogues Parliament; dissolves Lok Sabha; addresses joint sitting; gives assent to bills |
| **Judicial** | Pardoning powers under **Article 72**: pardon, reprieve, respite, remit, commute (covers courts-martial and death sentences) |
| **Emergency** | National Emergency (Art 352), President's Rule (Art 356), Financial Emergency (Art 360) |

**Veto Powers (Three Types):**
- **Absolute Veto**: Withhold assent to a bill (non-Money, non-constitutional amendment bills)
- **Suspensive Veto**: Returns a bill for reconsideration; but if Parliament repasses it, President MUST assent
- **Pocket Veto**: No time limit mentioned for ordinary bills — President can keep the bill pending indefinitely

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **Article 74** states the President "shall act in accordance with such advice" of the Council of Ministers — this was strengthened by the **44th Amendment (1978)** which added that the President *may* return advice for reconsideration *once*, but must act on the reconsidered advice. This makes it clear the President cannot refuse to act on CoM advice.

### The Prime Minister & Council of Ministers (Art 74–75)

The PM is the real head of the Union Executive. The Council of Ministers (CoM) is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha — meaning if the CoM loses a confidence vote, all must resign.

- **Article 74**: CoM to aid and advise President; President shall act on this advice
- **Article 75**: PM appointed by President; other ministers appointed by President on PM's advice
- **Collective Responsibility** (Art 75(3)): CoM is collectively responsible to Lok Sabha
- **Individual Responsibility**: Each minister is individually responsible to Parliament for their ministry

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **PM is the "keystone of the Cabinet arch"** (Ivor Jennings). All key decisions flow through the PM. The President cannot dismiss the PM as long as the PM commands Lok Sabha majority. But the PM can effectively dismiss any minister by advising the President to withdraw their portfolio.

## 2. The State Executive (Articles 153–167)

### The Governor (Art 153–161)

The Governor is the constitutional head of the State — appointed by the President, not elected.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Appointment** | By President (effectively Central government's choice) |
| **Tenure** | 5 years — but holds office "during the pleasure of the President" (can be removed anytime) |
| **Role** | Constitutional head of state + Central government's agent in the state |
| **Discretionary Powers** | Wider than President's: can reserve a bill for President's consideration; can appoint CM when no clear majority; can send reports to Centre leading to Art 356 |

### The Chief Minister (Art 163–164)

The CM is the real head of state government. The Council of Ministers at the state level is collectively responsible to the State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha).

- **Article 163**: Council of Ministers to aid and advise Governor
- **Article 164**: CM appointed by Governor; other ministers on CM's advice
- **Collective Responsibility** to State Vidhan Sabha

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The Governor has **more discretionary power** than the President. The Constitution explicitly gives Governors discretionary powers in certain situations (e.g., Article 163(2) — when the Governor acts at their discretion, the Governor's decision is final). The President has almost no discretionary power. This is a key distinction.

## 3. Union Parliament (Articles 79–122)

Parliament = **President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha**

### Rajya Sabha (Council of States)

- **Permanent body** — never dissolved; 1/3rd members retire every 2 years
- **Maximum strength**: 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated by President for contributions to art, literature, science, social service)
- Currently **245 members**
- Chairman: Vice-President of India (ex-officio)
- **Special Powers**: Can pass resolution under Art 249 to authorize Parliament to legislate on State List

### Lok Sabha (House of the People)

- **Directly elected** by universal adult franchise
- Maximum strength: **550** (530 from states + 20 from UTs); Currently 543
- **5-year term** (unless dissolved earlier)
- Speaker: Elected by LS members; decides if a bill is a **Money Bill** (final authority)
- Government must maintain majority in LS — this is where confidence votes and no-confidence motions occur

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** Only the **Lok Sabha** matters for the government's survival. The Rajya Sabha cannot move a no-confidence motion. Money Bills (budget, taxation) originate only in Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha's power over them is limited to 14 days' delay. This is why controlling Lok Sabha = controlling government.

### Types of Bills

| Type | Definition | Rajya Sabha's Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary Bill** | On subjects other than money | Equal powers with Lok Sabha; joint sitting resolves deadlock |
| **Money Bill** (Art 110) | Only about taxation, borrowing, appropriation | RS can only suggest amendments (not binding); 14 days delay only |
| **Financial Bill** | Contains money + other provisions | Treated as ordinary bill |
| **Constitutional Amendment Bill** | To amend the Constitution | Special majority (2/3rd of members present + voting, plus majority of total membership); some need state ratification |

## 4. State Legislature (Articles 168–212)

- **Unicameral**: Most states — only Vidhan Sabha (lower house)
- **Bicameral** (with Vidhan Parishad/Legislative Council): Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, UP, Telangana, **Andhra Pradesh**

**Andhra Pradesh:**
- AP re-established its Legislative Council in **2007** (after being abolished in 1985)
- After the AP Reorganisation Act 2014, the new AP continues to have a bicameral legislature
- **AP Vidhan Sabha**: 175 members

## 5. Local Government — 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)

The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments gave constitutional status to local self-government — arguably the most significant democratization since independence.

### Panchayati Raj (73rd Amendment) — Rural

- Added **Part IX** and the **11th Schedule** (29 subjects for Panchayats)
- **Three-tier structure**: Gram Panchayat (village) → Panchayat Samiti (block/intermediate) → Zila Parishad (district)
- **State Election Commission**: Conduct Panchayat elections
- **State Finance Commission**: Every 5 years, to recommend devolution of funds to PRIs
- **Reservation**: Minimum **1/3rd seats for women** (many states including AP have increased to 50%)
- Reservation for SC/ST proportional to their population

### Urban Local Bodies (74th Amendment) — Urban

- Added **Part IXA** and the **12th Schedule** (18 subjects for Municipalities)
- Types: Nagar Panchayat (transitional town), Municipal Council (smaller urban area), Municipal Corporation (large city)
- Same reservation provisions: minimum 1/3rd for women
- **Ward Committees** mandatory in cities with population above 3 lakh

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Articles **243D** (Panchayats) and **243T** (Urban Bodies) mandate the **1/3rd reservation for women** in local bodies. This is the constitutional provision. The 108th Constitutional Amendment Bill (Women's Reservation Bill) for 1/3rd reservation in Parliament was passed as the **Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023** — mandating 33% reservation in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, operative after the next delimitation exercise.

## Quick Revision — High-Frequency APPSC Facts

**Articles (Union Executive):**
- **Art 52**: President of India
- **Art 72**: President's pardoning power (includes death sentences)
- **Art 74**: CoM to aid and advise President (mandatory)
- **Art 75(3)**: Collective responsibility of CoM to Lok Sabha

**Articles (State Executive):**
- **Art 153**: Governor for each state
- **Art 163**: CoM to aid and advise Governor
- **Art 164(2)**: CM and CoM collectively responsible to Vidhan Sabha

**Parliament Numbers:**
- Rajya Sabha max: **250** (238 elected + 12 nominated); current 245
- Lok Sabha max: **550** (currently 543 elected)
- Speaker decides: **Money Bill** status (Article 110)

**73rd/74th Amendments:**
- **73rd (1992)**: Panchayats → Part IX, 11th Schedule (29 subjects)
- **74th (1992)**: Municipalities → Part IXA, 12th Schedule (18 subjects)
- Women's reservation in local bodies: minimum **1/3rd** (Art 243D, 243T)
