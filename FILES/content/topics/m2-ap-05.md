# Andhra Pradesh 1956–2014 & Bifurcation

> *In 1969, students burned themselves alive demanding a separate Telangana. In 2014, sixty-two years after Potti Sriramulu's death created one state, another act of Parliament divided it. The story of AP is one of linguistic unity colliding with regional identity — again and again.*

**Covers:** G2 Paper 1 (AP History) | G1 Mains Paper 2 (AP History & Culture) | G2 Paper 3 (AP Polity/Governance)
**Sources:** Srikrishna Committee Report (2011); APPSC G2 Syllabus; AP Reorganisation Act 2014

## The Big Picture

From 1956 to 2014, the history of Andhra Pradesh is essentially two parallel stories running simultaneously. Story one: AP's political evolution — Congress dominance, NTR's revolutionary TDP, YSR's populism. Story two: the Telangana movement — the unending demand for a separate state by the people of the 10 Telangana districts, rooted in grievances about water, employment, and representation.

For APPSC, the key facts are: the 1969 Telangana agitation (Six-Point Formula), the 1972 Jai Andhra movement, the formation of TDP in 1982, the 2009 Srikrishna Committee, the AP Reorganisation Act 2014, and the specific features of residual AP (Amaravati capital, Polavaram project).

## 1. AP Politics 1956–1983: Congress Dominance

### First Phase (1956–1978)

AP was a Congress stronghold for its first two decades:

| Chief Minister | Party | Period | Notable |
|----------------|-------|--------|---------|
| N. Sanjiva Reddy | Congress | 1956–60 | First CM of unified AP |
| D. Sanjeevaiah | Congress | 1960–62 | First Dalit CM of AP |
| N. Sanjiva Reddy | Congress | 1962–64 | Second term; later became President of India (1977) |
| K. Brahmananda Reddy | Congress | 1964–71 | Longest serving early CM |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **P.V. Narasimha Rao** was Chief Minister of AP from 1971–73 before he became the Prime Minister of India who launched economic liberalization (1991). His AP tenure and his national legacy are both high-frequency APPSC facts.

## 2. The Telangana Agitation of 1969

### Background: Grievances

The Gentlemen's Agreement of 1956 promised Telangana protection in employment and development funds. By the late 1960s, Telugu politicians from coastal Andhra dominated the state government and violated the Mulki Rules (residence-based employment protection for Telangana):

**Key Telangana grievances:**
- Coastal Andhra settlers occupying government jobs in Hyderabad (violating Mulki Rules)
- Development funds meant for Telangana diverted to Andhra projects
- Krishna-Tungabhadra irrigation projects primarily benefiting Andhra farmers
- Dominance of Andhra-origin people in education, commerce, and bureaucracy

### The 1969 Agitation

| Event | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| January 1969 | Students at Osmania University began agitation for **Telangana Rashtra** (separate state) |
| JAC formed | **Joint Action Committee** of students became the organizing force |
| Spread | Agitation spread to 10 Telangana districts; government employees joined; multiple self-immolations |
| Death toll | Over **300 deaths** (mostly self-immolations and police firing) |
| Indira Gandhi's response | Used Article 356 + political management to defuse the agitation |

**Six-Point Formula (1973):**
Indira Gandhi imposed the Six-Point Formula (also called the **Mulki Rules Enforcement**) to address Telangana grievances:
1. Zone-based employment allocation (employees to serve in their region)
2. Educational institutions to follow domicile rules
3. Establishment of Regional Committee with budget oversight
4. Multi-zone examination boards for services
5. Regional Planning Board for Telangana
6. Mulki Rules to be strictly enforced

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The Six-Point Formula was a compromise — it acknowledged Telangana's grievances while refusing to bifurcate AP. But it created a new problem: Andhra people (particularly from coastal areas) now felt *they* were being discriminated against — triggering the **Jai Andhra movement** of 1972–73.

---

## 3. The Jai Andhra Movement (1972–73)

The Six-Point Formula's strict zone-system alarmed Andhra settlers in Hyderabad who feared they would lose jobs. In 1972, a **Jai Andhra** (Long Live Andhra) agitation erupted — demanding either reversal of the formula or separation of Andhra from Telangana (ironically asking for what Telangana also wanted).

The crisis was severe: AP was temporarily under President's Rule. Indira Gandhi's government imposed a negotiated compromise, and the agitation subsided by 1974. But it revealed the deep structural tension built into the Andhra–Telangana merger.

## 4. NTR and the Telugu Desam Party (1982–1995)

### Formation of TDP

N.T. Rama Rao (**NTR**) — legendary Telugu film star (over 300 films) — entered politics and founded the **Telugu Desam Party (TDP)** on **March 29, 1982**, with the slogan of Telugu self-respect (*Telugu vari atmagoravam*).

| Milestone | Date | Detail |
|-----------|------|--------|
| TDP founded | March 29, 1982 | Founded by N.T. Rama Rao; headquartered Hyderabad |
| 1983 elections | January 1983 | TDP won 202/294 seats — landslide; Congress routed |
| NTR became CM | January 9, 1983 | Ended 26 years of Congress rule in AP |
| **Nadendla crisis** | August 1984 | Governor Ramlal swore in Nadendla Bhaskara Rao (TDP dissident) as CM — constitutional crisis |
| NTR restored | September 1984 | NTR proved majority; Governor recalled; Indira Gandhi's proxy dismissed |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **Nadendla episode (1984)** was one of India's most brazen misuses of gubernatorial power. NTR was abroad (in the US for bypass surgery) when Ramlal installed a dissident. NTR returned, organized MLAs in Bengaluru, proved majority — Governor was recalled. The episode reinforced the constitutional norm that governors cannot install minorities without a floor test.

**NTR's policies (1983–89, 1994–95):**
- Mid-day meal scheme (anganwadi + schools)
- Rs. 2-per-kg rice scheme
- Rural employment (forerunner to MGNREGS)
- Computerization of government offices

### Chandrababu Naidu Era (1995–2004)

**N. Chandrababu Naidu** displaced his father-in-law NTR in 1995 and became CM. He transformed AP's reputation:

| Achievement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Vision 2020 | Long-term development plan; attracted IT investments |
| HITEC City | Hyderabad became India's 2nd IT hub after Bengaluru |
| e-Government | Early computerization of land records (Bhoomi), online services |
| World Bank loans | Structural adjustment programs; fiscal discipline |

NTR died on **January 18, 1996** — eight months after being displaced.

## 5. YSR Congress Era and the Second Telangana Agitation

### Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy (YSR) — 2004–2009

YSR (Congress) won the 2004 elections by building a rainbow coalition. His tenure:
- **Rajiv Gandhi Urban Housing Scheme** and **Indiramma** housing for rural poor
- **Arogyasri** health insurance scheme (2007) — India's first state health insurance for BPL families
- **NREGS implementation** — AP was top performer
- **Fee reimbursement** for SC/ST/BC students
- Supported big industrial investments (Polavaram, Thermal plants)

**YSR died on September 2, 2009** in a helicopter crash near Nallamala hills while on a flood-relief tour.

### TRS and the Telangana Movement 2009

**Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS)** was formed by **K. Chandrashekar Rao (KCR)** on **April 27, 2001**.

| Event | Date | Detail |
|-------|------|--------|
| TRS formed | April 27, 2001 | KCR's party with singular goal: separate Telangana |
| TRS–UPA alliance | 2004 | TRS joined UPA; KCR got Union Cabinet berth |
| TRS pulled out | 2006 | Withdrew from UPA when PM didn't act on Telangana |
| KCR fast | November 29, 2009 | KCR began fast-unto-death at Khammam; sparked renewed agitation after YSR's death |
| Chidambaram's announcement | December 9, 2009 | Home Minister P. Chidambaram announced commencement of Telangana state formation process — sparked Andhra counter-agitation |
| Announcement withdrawn | December 23, 2009 | Government suspended the process due to protests from Andhra/Rayalaseema MPs and MLAs |

## 6. Srikrishna Committee (2010) — The Six Options

The government appointed a committee under retired Supreme Court Chief Justice **B.N. Srikrishna** in February 2010 to study the situation.

**Committee submitted report: December 30, 2010**

The Srikrishna Committee gave **6 options**:

| Option | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Maintain status quo — keep AP united |
| 2 | Bifurcate AP into Seemandhra and Telangana; Hyderabad as UT |
| **3 (recommended as best)** | Keep AP united with strengthened Telangana Regional Committee and statutory guarantees |
| 4 | Divide AP into smaller states (more than 2) |
| 5 | Bifurcate based on districts, not current boundaries |
| **6 (recommended as second-best)** | Bifurcate into Seemandhra and Telangana; Hyderabad as shared capital |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Srikrishna Committee's own recommendation was Option 3 (keep AP united with strong guarantees)**. The government ultimately went with a variant of **Option 6** (bifurcation) — contradicting the Committee's top recommendation. This is a frequently tested fact.

## 7. AP Bifurcation — The 2014 Act

### Timeline of Bifurcation

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| July 30, 2013 | Cabinet decision to bifurcate AP |
| February 18, 2014 | AP Reorganisation Bill passed in Lok Sabha amid tears, protests, pepper spray in Parliament |
| February 20, 2014 | Passed in Rajya Sabha |
| March 1, 2014 | Presidential assent |
| **June 2, 2014** | **Telangana became India's 29th state** |

### The Two New States

| Feature | **Telangana** | **Residual Andhra Pradesh** |
|---------|--------------|---------------------------|
| Formation date | June 2, 2014 | June 2, 2014 |
| Districts (at formation) | 10 | 13 |
| Capital | **Hyderabad** | **Amaravati** (new capital being built) |
| First CM | K. Chandrashekar Rao (KCR) | N. Chandrababu Naidu |
| Hyderabad status | Capital of Telangana | Shared capital for **10 years** |
| Shared capital end | — | June 2, 2024 |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** **Telangana is India's 29th state**, NOT the 28th. The count is sometimes confused. Jharkhand (28th) was formed in 2000. Telangana (29th) in 2014. Andrapradesh is NOT a new state — it is the *residual* AP continuing with modified boundaries.

### Features of the AP Reorganisation Act 2014

1. **Hyderabad as shared capital** for 10 years (until June 2, 2024) — both states use the same capital city
2. **Amaravati** designated as new capital for residual AP
3. **Special Category Status** promised for residual AP (later denied by Centre — major political controversy)
4. **Polavaram Project** declared a National Project — Centre to fund and execute
5. Bifurcation of **APSRTC, AP High Court** (Hyderabad HC for Telangana; new HC at Amaravati for AP)
6. Central institutions to be continued in both states

## 8. Residual AP After 2014 — Key Facts

### Polavaram National Project

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Location | West Godavari district; across Godavari river |
| Purpose | Irrigation (7.2 lakh acres), drinking water (23 districts), hydropower (960 MW), flood control |
| National Project status | Declared **National Project by Centre** in 2014 Reorganisation Act |
| Key benefit | Water to drought-prone Rayalaseema and northern coastal AP |

### AP Capital — Amaravati

- Located on the south bank of **Krishna river** in Guntur district
- Greenfield capital — land pooling from farmers of 27 villages
- Capital Region Development Authority (CRDA) set up
- **First capital to be built entirely through voluntary land pooling** — global experiment

### Key Economic Projects

| Project | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **APSEZ** (AP Special Economic Zone) | Kakinada; biggest SEZ in AP |
| **PCPIR** (Petroleum, Chemicals and Petrochemicals Investment Region) | Visakhapatnam–Kakinada corridor |
| **AP Fiber Grid** | State broadband infrastructure project |
| **Vizag–Chennai Industrial Corridor** | Under National Industrial Corridor Program |
