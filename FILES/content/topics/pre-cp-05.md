# Rights Issues — Fundamental Rights, Human Rights & Protective Laws

## 1. Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)
The Constitution guarantees six categories of Fundamental Rights enforceable against the State.

| Article(s) | Right | Key Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 14 | Equality before Law | Also includes "equal protection of the laws" — a positive right. |
| 15 | Prohibition of Discrimination | On grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. Art 15(3) allows special provisions for women & children. |
| 17 | Abolition of Untouchability | An absolute right — no exceptions. |
| 19 | Six Freedoms | Includes freedom of speech & expression, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession. |
| 21 | Right to Life & Personal Liberty | Most expansive FR — includes right to livelihood, education, health, privacy (Puttaswamy case 2017). |
| 21A | Right to Education | Inserted by 86th Amendment (2002). Free & compulsory education for 6–14 years. |
| 32 | Right to Constitutional Remedies | Dr. Ambedkar called it the "Heart & Soul" of the Constitution. |

> 💡 **EXAM TRAP**: Right to Property was a Fundamental Right under Art 31 but was removed by the **44th Amendment (1978)** and made a **legal right** under Art 300A.

## 2. National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

- **Established**: **12 October 1993** under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.
- **Chairperson**: A retired Chief Justice of India.
- **Composition**: Chairperson + 4 Members (retired CJI / SC Judges + 2 others with knowledge of Human Rights).
- **Nature**: A statutory, quasi-judicial body — NOT a constitutional body.
- **Limitation**: Cannot investigate matters older than **1 year** from the date of the alleged violation.

> 💡 **EXAM TRAP**: NHRC's recommendations are **not binding** on the government. It can only recommend, not enforce.

## 3. POCSO Act (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences), 2012

- **Full Form**: Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012.
- **Purpose**: Provides a comprehensive framework to protect children from sexual abuse and exploitation.
- **Child Definition**: Any person below the **age of 18 years**.
- **Key Features**:
  - Establishes child-friendly court procedures.
  - Mandates Special Courts for speedy trials.
  - Places the **burden of proof on the accused** (reversal of the standard principle).
  - Mandatory reporting — any person aware of an offence must report it to the police.

## 4. SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989

- **Official Name**: The Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989.
- **Purpose**: Prevents atrocities and discrimination against SCs and STs, and provides for Special Courts.
- **Key 2018 Amendment** (Post *Subhash Kashinath Mahajan* Case):
  - The SC's ruling (no automatic arrest, prior sanction required) was overturned.
  - The **2018 Amendment** restored the original provision allowing immediate arrest without approval.
- **Nodal Ministry**: Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment.

---

## 5. Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005

- **Applies to**: All constitutional authorities (Executive, Legislature, Judiciary) and bodies substantially financed by government.
- **Key Timeframe**: Information must be provided within **30 days** (48 hours for matters of life/liberty).
- **CIC (Central Information Commission)**: The appellate authority at the national level.
- **Exempt Organisations**: Intelligence and security agencies (listed in the 2nd Schedule) are exempt, except on corruption & human rights violations.

## 6. Writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, etc.)

| Writ | Literal Meaning | Used Against | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Habeas Corpus** | "You may have the body" | Any authority (private too) | Release of an illegally detained person |
| **Mandamus** | "We command" | Public authority | Compels performance of a public duty |
| **Prohibition** | Forbid | Lower courts/tribunals | Prevents excess of jurisdiction |
| **Certiorari** | "To be certified" | Lower courts/tribunals | Quashes illegal orders |
| **Quo Warranto** | "By what authority" | Public office holders | Questions authority to hold a public office |

> 💡 **EXAM TRAP**: Habeas Corpus can be issued against **private individuals** too, unlike most other writs which only lie against public authorities.
