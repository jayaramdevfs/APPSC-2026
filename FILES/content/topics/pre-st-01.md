# Science & Technology

> *From Aryabhata in 1975 to Chandrayaan-3 in 2023, India's science story is about building national capacity — and AP sits at the heart of it, from DRDO labs in Visakhapatnam to nuclear fuel in Kadapa.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims S&T | G2 Screening S&T | G1 Mains Paper 3 | G2 Paper 2 & 3
**Sources:** NCERT Class 10 Science, PIB Science & Technology releases, ISRO/DRDO Annual Reports

## The Big Picture

India's science and technology story since independence is about converting a colonial economy — where science was imported — into a country that builds its own rockets, develops its own nuclear bombs, and now manufactures its own aircraft carriers. Each phase of this transformation was deliberate policy: Nehru's "temples of modern India" PSU model → DRDO's self-reliance drive → post-1991 IT boom → post-2014 Make in India and semiconductor ambitions.

For APPSC, S&T questions test both factual recall (year of missions, who founded what) and conceptual understanding (how does Bt Cotton work, what is India's nuclear doctrine). The AP angle appears in defence (Visakhapatnam Naval base), nuclear energy (Tummalapalle uranium), and digital infrastructure (APSDDC, AP Fiber Grid).

## 1. Space Technology — ISRO

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | **1969** by Dr. **Vikram Sarabhai** (Father of Indian Space Programme) |
| **Headquarters** | **Bengaluru** |
| **Current Chairman** | V. Narayanan (2024) |
| **Launch Sites** | SDSC (SHAR), Sriharikota, AP — primary launch site |

> 📌 **AP Angle:** **SDSC-SHAR (Satish Dhawan Space Centre)** in Sriharikota, Nellore district, is India's only operational spaceport. Every ISRO mission launches from AP soil. This is a high-frequency APPSC fact.

### Major ISRO Missions

| Mission | Year | Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| **Aryabhata** | 1975 | India's first satellite |
| **SLV-3** | 1980 | First indigenous launch vehicle (built by APJ Abdul Kalam) |
| **Chandrayaan-1** | 2008 | Discovered water molecules on the Moon (data confirmed 2009) |
| **Mangalyaan (MOM)** | 2013 | First interplanetary mission; Mars orbit in first attempt — cheapest in history |
| **Chandrayaan-3** | **2023** | Soft landing near Moon's **south pole** — **India is the FIRST country to do so** |
| **Aditya-L1** | 2023 | India's first solar observatory; positioned at **L1 Lagrange point** |
| **GSLV Mk-III / LVM-3** | — | Heavy-lift launch vehicle; also used commercially (OneWeb launches) |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** Chandrayaan-3's south pole landing (23 August 2023) was historic because every previous soft landing had been near the equator — the south pole has permanently shadowed craters that may contain water ice. India succeeded where Russia (Luna-25, crashed 4 days earlier) failed. This makes India the 4th country to achieve a lunar soft landing and the FIRST to reach the south pole.

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** Chandrayaan-1 **launched** in 2008 but the water discovery was published in 2009. Questions sometimes state "discovered water in 2008" — technically the launch year was 2008; data confirming water was 2009.

## 2. Defence Technology

### Key Institutions

| Body | Function |
|---|---|
| **DRDO** | Defence Research & Development Organisation; ~50+ labs; HQ New Delhi |
| **HAL** (Hindustan Aeronautics Limited) | Indigenous aircraft manufacturing |
| **BEL** (Bharat Electronics Limited) | Defence electronics |
| **OFB → DPSU** | Ordnance factories converted to Defence PSUs (2021) |

### Key Missiles (APPSC-Relevant)

| Missile | Type | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Agni-V** | Ballistic (ICBM class) | Range **5,500+ km**; MIRV-capable (multiple warheads); completes India's nuclear triad |
| **BrahMos** | Supersonic cruise | World's fastest at **~2.8 Mach**; India–Russia joint development |
| **Akash** | Surface-to-air (SAM) | Air defence missile; replacing Russian SAMs indigenously |

### Key Platforms

- **Tejas (LCA)**: India's first **indigenously designed and developed** combat aircraft; inducted into IAF
- **INS Vikrant**: India's first **indigenous aircraft carrier**; commissioned **September 2022**
- **Nuclear Triad**: India has all three legs — land (Agni), air (Mirage/Jaguar), sea (INS Arihant SSBN)

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **BrahMos** is a **cruise missile** (flies at low altitude using air-breathing engine). **Agni** is a **ballistic missile** (arcs through space). Different flight profiles — a common exam trap.

## 3. IT & Digital Technology

### Digital India Programme (2015)

Three pillars:
1. **Digital Infrastructure** — BharatNet (broadband to all gram panchayats), Aadhaar-based identity
2. **Digital Services** — e-Governance, DigiLocker, e-Health, e-Education
3. **Digital Empowerment** — IT literacy, Common Service Centres

### India Stack

India's digital public infrastructure — a globally studied model:
- **Aadhaar** — Biometric identity (1.4 billion enrolled)
- **UPI** — Unified Payments Interface by **NPCI**; world's highest real-time payment volume
- **DPDP Act 2023** — India's first comprehensive data protection law; establishes Data Protection Board

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's **UPI** processed over 100 billion transactions in a year — more than the rest of the world combined. This was built on top of the Aadhaar identity stack. India now exports this model: UPI has gone live in UAE, Singapore, France, and others. The "India Stack" concept (Aadhaar + UPI + Data layer) is a development model being studied globally.

### Semiconductors

- **India Semiconductor Mission (2021)**: Make India a global chip manufacturing hub
- **First fab**: Tata Electronics plant in Dholera (Gujarat) + Assam facility announced; production expected from 2026
- **Strategic importance**: Chips = modern weapons, phones, satellites, cars — whoever makes chips controls the supply chain

## 4. Biotechnology

### Key Regulatory Bodies

| Body | Under | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **DBT** (Dept of Biotechnology) | MoS&T | Nodal government body for biotech policy |
| **BIRAC** | DBT | Funds biotech startups and innovation |
| **GEAC** | **MoEFCC** | Approves environmental release of GMOs |
| **RCGM** | DBT | Reviews confined field trials of GM crops |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** **GEAC is under MoEFCC**, NOT under DBT. This distinction is frequently tested.

### Key Applications

**Agriculture:**
- **Bt Cotton**: The **ONLY commercially approved GM crop in India** (2002). Uses Cry proteins from *Bacillus thuringiensis* to resist bollworm. All other GM crops (Bt Brinjal, GM Mustard) are under moratorium or restricted field trials.

**Medicine:**
- **Covaxin (BBV152)**: India's **indigenous COVID vaccine**; developed by Bharat Biotech with **ICMR**; whole-virion inactivated vaccine technology
- **CRISPR-Cas9**: "Gene editing scissors" — Nobel Prize 2020 (Doudna & Charpentier)
- **CAR-T Cell Therapy**: India approved first indigenous CAR-T therapy in 2024 (NexCAR19)

## 5. Nuclear Energy — 3-Stage Programme

Conceived by **Dr. Homi J. Bhabha** (Father of India's nuclear programme):

| Stage | Fuel | Reactor Type | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | Natural Uranium | Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWR) | **Operational** — multiple plants |
| **Stage 2** | Plutonium (from Stage 1 waste) | Fast Breeder Reactors (FBR) | Prototype FBR in **Kalpakkam** |
| **Stage 3** | **Thorium** | Advanced Reactors | Future — India's goal |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's 3-stage nuclear programme is driven by geography: India has **limited uranium** but the **world's largest thorium reserves** (Kerala coast). The strategy — use limited uranium in Stage 1 to breed plutonium → use plutonium in Stage 2 to eventually convert thorium to fissile U-233 in Stage 3 — is a long-term energy security play that no other country is pursuing at this scale.

**Nuclear Operators:**
- **DAE** (Department of Atomic Energy): Apex nuclear policy body
- **NPCIL** (Nuclear Power Corporation of India): Operates all nuclear power plants
