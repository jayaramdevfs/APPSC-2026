## The Big Picture

Climate change is no longer an abstract global problem — it is a present reality that already affects Indian farmers through erratic monsoons, coastal communities through rising sea levels, and urban residents through worsening air quality. India's response is shaped by a fundamental tension: India needs to grow its economy (still 800 million people without adequate income) while reducing carbon emissions that its own people barely contributed to creating.

For APPSC candidates, environmental questions span two dimensions: the **science** (what are greenhouse gases, how does climate change work) and the **policy** (what treaties, what laws, what India has committed to). The AP angle is significant — AP's coastline, cyclone exposure, Kolleru Lake, and Kurnool Solar Park all appear as AP-specific questions.

## 1. Climate Change — The Science

### Greenhouse Effect

- **Natural process**: GHGs trap heat in the atmosphere, keeping Earth warm enough for life (without it: -18°C average)
- **Enhanced greenhouse effect**: Burning fossil fuels adds GHGs beyond natural levels → global warming

### Key Greenhouse Gases

| Gas | GWP (100-yr) | Main Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **CO₂** | **1** (baseline) | Fossil fuel combustion, deforestation |
| **CH₄** (Methane) | **28–36** | Livestock, paddy fields, landfills, natural gas leaks |
| **N₂O** (Nitrous Oxide) | **265–298** | Fertilizers, livestock, combustion |
| **HFCs/PFCs/SF₆** | 100s–10,000s | Refrigerants, industrial processes |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** **CO₂ is the MOST ABUNDANT anthropogenic GHG** (by volume), but **methane has a MUCH HIGHER GWP per molecule**. Water vapor is the most abundant GHG overall but is not directly anthropogenic and cannot be controlled. Questions often confuse most abundant with highest warming potential.

### Consequences

- Global temperature: **+1.1°C** above pre-industrial levels (as of 2023)
- Rising sea levels (thermal expansion + glacial melt) — threatens AP's Krishna-Godavari delta
- Extreme weather events: more intense cyclones, droughts, floods
- **Coral Bleaching**: Ocean warming causes corals to expel symbiotic algae → death
- **Arctic Amplification**: Arctic warms **2–4x faster** than the global average

## 2. International Climate Agreements

### UNFCCC Framework

- **UNFCCC** (United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change): Signed at **Rio Earth Summit, 1992**
- **COP** (Conference of Parties): Annual meetings of all UNFCCC members

| Agreement | Year / COP | Key Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Kyoto Protocol** | 1997 (COP3, Kyoto) | Binding GHG reduction for **Annex I (developed) nations**; India exempt as developing country |
| **Paris Agreement** | 2015 (COP21, Paris) | 195 countries; limit warming to **1.5°C**; countries submit NDCs (voluntary, nationally determined) |
| **Glasgow Pact** | 2021 (COP26, Glasgow) | "Phase **down**" (not phase out) coal; Loss & Damage mechanism |
| **Sharm el-Sheikh** | 2022 (COP27) | Loss & Damage **fund** formally established for vulnerable nations |
| **Dubai Declaration** | 2023 (COP28) | First Global Stocktake; call to "**transition away**" from fossil fuels |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The shift from "phase out" to "phase **down**" coal at Glasgow was a major controversy — India and China pushed for "phase down." The difference matters: "phase out" means complete elimination with a date; "phase down" means gradual reduction with flexibility. India argued it cannot commit to coal phase-out while 300 million people still lack reliable electricity.

### India's NDC (Nationally Determined Contribution)

India's updated NDC under the Paris Agreement commits to:
1. Reduce **emission intensity of GDP by 45%** by 2030 (from 2005 base)
2. Achieve **50% cumulative electricity from non-fossil sources** by 2030
3. Create additional carbon sink of **2.5–3 billion tonnes CO₂ equivalent** via forest cover

## 3. Key Environmental Issues in India

### Air Pollution

- **AQI (Air Quality Index)**: 0–500 scale; measures PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, SO₂, CO, Ozone
- **PM2.5**: Particles < 2.5 microns — most dangerous; penetrate deep into lungs and bloodstream
- **NCAP (National Clean Air Programme, 2019)**: Target **40% reduction** in PM2.5/PM10 by 2026
- **Stubble burning** (Punjab/Haryana farmers) is a primary cause of winter smog in Delhi NCR

### Water Pollution

- **Namami Gange (2014)**: Integrated mission for Ganga rejuvenation; flagship programme
- **Ganga Action Plan (1985)**: Earlier Phase I programme focused on sewage treatment
- **Eutrophication**: Excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus from fertilisers) cause algal blooms → oxygen depletion → fish death

### Plastic Pollution

- India banned **Single Use Plastics (SUP)** from **1 July 2022** — covers straws, cutlery, plates, bags <75 micron thickness
- **Microplastics**: Particles < 5mm — found in oceans, food, fish, and human blood

### Soil Degradation

- India loses ~**5,334 million tonnes** of soil/year to erosion
- **UNCCD**: India committed to restoring **26 million hectares** of degraded land by 2030

## 4. Environmental Laws in India

| Act | Year | Key Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Wildlife Protection Act** | 1972 | Schedules list protected species; wildlife sanctuaries, national parks |
| **Water Prevention & Control of Pollution Act** | 1974 | Established PCBs (Pollution Control Boards); controls water pollution |
| **Forest Conservation Act** | 1980 | Central approval required before forest land diverted to non-forest use |
| **Air Prevention & Control of Pollution Act** | 1981 | Extended PCB mandate to air pollution |
| **Environment Protection Act** | **1986** | Umbrella legislation passed **post-Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984)**; empowers MoEFCC to issue notifications |
| **Biological Diversity Act** | 2002 | Implements CBD; establishes NBA (National), SBBs (State), BMCs (local) |
| **Forest Rights Act** | 2006 | Rights of forest-dwelling STs and traditional communities over forest land and produce |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Environment Protection Act 1986** was directly triggered by the **Bhopal Gas Tragedy of December 1984** — the world's worst industrial disaster (Union Carbide MIC gas leak, 3,000–15,000 deaths). India had no comprehensive environmental protection legislation at the time; the tragedy exposed this gap.

**Regulatory Bodies:**
- **CPCB** (Central Pollution Control Board): Under MoEFCC; sets national pollution standards
- **APPCB** (Andhra Pradesh Pollution Control Board): AP's state-level PCB

## 5. Ozone Layer — Montreal Protocol

- **Ozone Layer**: In the Stratosphere (15–35 km altitude); absorbs UV-B and UV-C radiation
- **Ozone Depletion**: Caused by **CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons)** from refrigerants and aerosols
- **Montreal Protocol (1987)**: Most successful international environmental treaty; phased out CFCs globally
- **Kigali Amendment (2016)**: Extends Montreal Protocol scope to include **HFCs** (not ozone-depleting, but powerful greenhouse gases)
- **Result**: Ozone hole over Antarctica is showing recovery; 2023 assessment showed gradual healing

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** Montreal Protocol targets **ozone-depleting substances (CFCs, HCFCs)** — NOT greenhouse gases directly. The **Kigali Amendment** adds HFCs (powerful GHGs but NOT ozone-depleting) to the treaty's scope. The Paris Agreement targets GHGs. They're different frameworks with overlapping coverage after Kigali.
