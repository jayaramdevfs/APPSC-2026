# Environmental Issues & Climate Change

## 1. Climate Change — Fundamentals

### The Greenhouse Effect:
- Natural process: Greenhouse gases (GHGs) trap heat from the sun in the Earth's atmosphere.
- **Key GHGs**: CO₂ (Carbon Dioxide), CH₄ (Methane), N₂O (Nitrous Oxide), H₂O (Water Vapor), O₃ (Ozone), and F-gases (HFCs, PFCs, SF₆).
- **Global Warming Potential (GWP)**: Measures how much heat a gas traps relative to CO₂ over 100 years.
  - CO₂ = 1 (baseline)
  - CH₄ = 28–36 (methane)
  - N₂O = 265–298

> 💡 **EXAM TRAP**: While CO₂ is the most **abundant** anthropogenic GHG, **Methane** has a much higher GWP per molecule. Water vapor is the most abundant GHG overall but is not directly controlled.

### Consequences of Climate Change:
- Rising global temperatures (1.1°C above pre-industrial levels as of 2023).
- Rising sea levels (glacial melting).
- Extreme weather events (cyclones, droughts, floods).
- **Coral Bleaching**: Due to ocean warming (corals expel symbiotic algae).
- **Arctic Amplification**: Arctic warming 2–4x faster than the global average.
- Disruption of monsoon patterns (critical for India).

## 2. International Climate Agreements

### UNFCCC (United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change):
- Signed at the **Rio Earth Summit, 1992**.
- Objective: Stabilize GHG concentrations at a safe level.
- **COPs (Conference of Parties)**: Annual meetings.

| Agreement | Year/COP | Key Provision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kyoto Protocol** | 1997 (COP3) | Binding GHG reduction targets for developed nations (Annex I). India exempt. |
| **Paris Agreement** | 2015 (COP21) | 195 countries. Limit global warming to **1.5°C** above pre-industrial levels. NDCs. |
| **Glasgow Pact** | 2021 (COP26) | "Phase down" (not phase out) coal. Loss & Damage mechanism. |
| **Sharm el-Sheikh** | 2022 (COP27) | Loss & Damage fund for vulnerable nations established. |
| **Dubai** | 2023 (COP28) | First Global Stocktake; call to "transition away" from fossil fuels. |

### India's NDC (Nationally Determined Contribution) under Paris Agreement:
- Reduce emission intensity of GDP by **45%** by 2030 (from 2005 levels).
- Achieve **50%** cumulative electric power from non-fossil sources by 2030.
- Create additional **carbon sink** of 2.5–3 billion tonnes of CO₂ equivalent through forest cover.

## 3. Key Environmental Issues in India

### Air Pollution:
- **AQI (Air Quality Index)**: 0–500 scale measuring PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, SO₂, CO, Ozone.
- **PM2.5**: Particles < 2.5 microns — most dangerous; penetrate deep into lungs.
- **NCAP (National Clean Air Programme, 2019)**: Target 40% reduction in PM2.5/PM10 by 2026.
- **Stubble Burning**: Major cause of winter air pollution in Delhi NCR (Punjab, Haryana farmers).

### Water Pollution:
- **Ganga Action Plan (1985)**: Phase I focused on sewage treatment.
- **Namami Gange (2014)**: Integrated Mission for rejuvenation and conservation of the Ganga.
- Industrial effluents, agricultural runoff (fertilisers, pesticides), and open defecation are main causes.
- **Eutrophication**: Excessive nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) cause algal blooms, depleting oxygen in water bodies.

### Soil Pollution & Land Degradation:
- **Soil Erosion**: Caused by deforestation, overgrazing, poor agricultural practices.
- India loses approximately **5,334 million tonnes** of soil annually to erosion.
- **Desertification**: Land degradation in arid/semi-arid areas. Rajasthan most affected.
- **UNCCD (UN Convention to Combat Desertification)**: India committed to restoring 26 million hectares of degraded land by 2030.

### Plastic Pollution:
- India banned **Single Use Plastics (SUP)** from **1 July 2022**.
- **Microplastics**: Particles < 5mm — found in oceans, food, and human blood.

## 4. Key Environmental Laws in India

| Act | Year | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wildlife Protection Act** | 1972 | Protects wildlife; schedules list protected species |
| **Forest Conservation Act** | 1980 | Requires central approval for diverting forest land to non-forest uses |
| **Water Prevention & Control of Pollution Act** | 1974 | Established PCBs (Pollution Control Boards) |
| **Air Prevention & Control of Pollution Act** | 1981 | Extends PCB mandate to air pollution |
| **Environment Protection Act** | 1986 | Umbrella legislation post-Bhopal disaster; enables specific notifications |
| **Forest Rights Act** | 2006 | Rights of forest-dwelling STs and traditional communities |
| **Biological Diversity Act** | 2002 | Implements CBD; establishes NBA, SBBs, BMCs |

### CPCB & SPCB:
- **CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board)**: Under MoEFCC. Apex body for pollution control standards.
- **SPCB (State Pollution Control Board)**: AP = **APPCB** (Andhra Pradesh Pollution Control Board).

## 5. Ozone Layer Depletion

- **Ozone Layer**: Located in the Stratosphere (15–35 km altitude). Absorbs harmful UV-B and UV-C radiation.
- **Ozone Depletion**: Caused by **CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons)** — used in refrigerants and aerosols.
- **Montreal Protocol (1987)**: International treaty to phase out ozone-depleting substances. **Most successful international environmental treaty**.
- **Kigali Amendment (2016)**: Extends Montreal Protocol to phase down **HFCs** (greenhouse gases).
- **Ozone Hole**: Largest over Antarctica during spring. 2023: Ozone hole showed signs of recovery.

> 💡 **EXAM TRAP**: The Montreal Protocol deals with **ozone-depleting substances**, NOT greenhouse gases directly. But the Kigali Amendment added HFCs (which are not ozone-depleting but ARE powerful GHGs) to the protocol's scope.

## 6. AP-Specific Environmental Concerns

- **AP Coastal Erosion**: Long coastline (974 km) faces erosion, especially near river deltas.
- **Cyclone Risk**: AP coast is highly vulnerable to cyclones from Bay of Bengal.
- **Nagarjuna Sagar**: Reservoir faces sedimentation challenges.
- **Kolleru Lake**: Declared a Wildlife Sanctuary; faces encroachment and pollution from aquaculture.
- **AP Solar**: Kurnool Solar Park (1,000 MW) is one of the world's largest.
