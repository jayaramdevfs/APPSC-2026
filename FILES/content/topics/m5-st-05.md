# Biotechnology & Nanotechnology

## 1. Biotechnology — Core Concepts

### Definition:
Biotechnology is the use of living organisms or their components to produce useful products and processes. The **Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)** defines it as: *"Any technological application that uses biological systems, living organisms, or derivatives thereof to make or modify products or processes for specific use."*

### Branches:
- **Red Biotechnology**: Medical applications (vaccines, therapeutic proteins, gene therapy).
- **Green Biotechnology**: Agricultural applications (GM crops, biopesticides, biofertilisers).
- **White Biotechnology**: Industrial applications (biofuels, enzymes, bioplastics).
- **Blue Biotechnology**: Marine applications (marine organisms for drugs and chemicals).

## 2. Recombinant DNA Technology (rDNA)

### Process:
```mermaid

    Gene[Target Gene] --> Restriction[Restriction Enzymes - cut]
    Restriction --> Vector[Insert into Vector - Plasmid/Virus]
    Vector --> Host[Introduce into Host Cell]
    Host --> Clone[Clone & Express Protein]
```

### Key Tools:
- **Restriction Endonucleases ("Molecular Scissors")**: Cut DNA at specific sequences (palindromic sequences).
- **Ligase ("Molecular Glue")**: Joins DNA fragments.
- **Vectors**: Plasmids, bacteriophages, cosmids — carriers for target genes.
- **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)**: Amplifies specific DNA sequences. Invented by **Kary Mullis** (Nobel 1993).
- **Gel Electrophoresis**: Separates DNA fragments by size.

## 3. Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs)

### Approved GM Crops in India:
- **Bt Cotton**: The ONLY commercially approved GM food/cash crop in India (approved 2002). Contains Cry proteins from *Bacillus thuringiensis* to resist bollworm.
- **All other GM crops**: Under field trial stage (e.g., Bt Brinjal — approved for commercial cultivation in Bangladesh in 2014 but still under moratorium in India).

### Regulation:
- **GEAC (Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee)**: Under **MoEFCC**. Final regulatory authority for environmental release of GMOs.
- **RCGM (Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation)**: Under DBT. Reviews confined field trials.

> 💡 **EXAM TRAP**: GEAC is under **MoEFCC**, not the Department of Biotechnology (DBT). RCGM is under DBT.

## 4. Key Biotechnology Applications

### Medicine:
| Application | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Insulin Production** | Recombinant human insulin (Humulin) — first biotech drug approved (1982). *E. coli* based. |
| **Vaccines** | Hepatitis B vaccine (recombinant); COVID-19 vaccines (mRNA, adenoviral vector). |
| **Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs)** | Targeted cancer therapy, autoimmune diseases. |
| **Gene Therapy** | Correcting faulty genes; approved for rare diseases (e.g., haemophilia). |
| **CAR-T Cell Therapy** | Engineering patient's T-cells to fight cancer. India approved first CAR-T therapy in 2024 (NexCAR19). |
| **CRISPR-Cas9** | "Gene editing scissors" — precise DNA editing. Nobel Prize 2020 (Doudna & Charpentier). |

### Agriculture:
- **Biofertilisers**: Contain microorganisms that fix nitrogen or solubilize phosphorus. Example: *Rhizobium*, *Azospirillum*, *Mycorrhiza*.
- **Biopesticides**: Microorganism-based pest control. Example: *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) toxin.
- **Tissue Culture (Micropropagation)**: Mass production of disease-free plants (banana, orchids, potato).

## 5. Nanotechnology

### Definition & Scale:
- **Nanotechnology**: Manipulation of matter at the **1–100 nanometre (nm)** scale.
- A nanometre = 10⁻⁹ metres. A human hair is ~80,000–100,000 nm wide.

### Why Nano-scale Matters:
At nanoscale, materials exhibit different properties than bulk matter:
- **Quantum effects** dominate (energy becomes quantized).
- **Surface area to volume ratio** dramatically increases — higher reactivity.
- Unique optical, electrical, and magnetic properties emerge.

### Key Nanomaterials:
| Nanomaterial | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs)** | Cylindrical carbon molecules; extremely strong, electrically conductive |
| **Graphene** | Single layer of carbon atoms; strongest known material; excellent conductor |
| **Gold Nanoparticles** | Used in drug delivery and diagnostic biosensors |
| **Quantum Dots** | Semiconductor nanocrystals; emit different colored light based on size; used in LEDs, medical imaging |
| **Titanium Dioxide (TiO₂) NPs** | Used in sunscreens, self-cleaning surfaces |

### Applications:

**Medicine (Nanomedicine)**:
- **Targeted Drug Delivery**: Nanoparticle "carriers" deliver drugs directly to cancer cells, reducing side effects.
- **Nano-Diagnostics**: Detect cancer biomarkers at extremely low concentrations.
- **Antimicrobial Nanoparticles**: Silver nanoparticles for wound dressings.

**Electronics**:
- **Nano-transistors**: Enable continued miniaturisation of chips (continuation of Moore's Law).
- **NAND Flash Memory**: Nano-scale storage in USB drives, SSDs.

**Energy**:
- **Nano-solar cells**: Perovskite solar cells with high efficiency.
- **Nanomaterials in batteries**: Graphene-based anodes for faster-charging lithium-ion batteries.

**Environment**:
- **Nano-filters**: Membranes for water purification; remove even nanoscale contaminants.
- **Nano-catalysts**: For pollution remediation (breaking down pollutants).

**Defence**:
- **Nano-coatings**: Stealth materials for aircraft.
- **Lightweight nano-composite armour**.
- **Nanosensors**: Chemical and biological agent detection.

### India's Nano Mission:
- **Launched**: 2007 by **DST (Department of Science & Technology)**.
- **Funding**: ~₹1,000 crore over five years.
- **Goals**: Build infrastructure, develop human resources, promote R&D and applications.
- **Key Centres**: CMET (Pune), NCL (Pune), JNCASR (Bengaluru), IISC (Bengaluru).

### Risks of Nanotechnology:
- **Toxicity**: Nanoparticles may penetrate biological barriers (cell membrane, blood-brain barrier).
- **Environmental Persistence**: Nano-silver can harm aquatic ecosystems.
- **Regulatory Gap**: Most countries lack specific nanotechnology regulations.
