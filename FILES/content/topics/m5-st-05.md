# Biotechnology & Nanotechnology

> *India approved the world's first commercially planted Bt Cotton in 2002. In 2024, it approved the first indigenous CAR-T cancer therapy. The distance between those two milestones is India's biotech journey in two facts.*

## The Big Picture

Biotechnology sits at the intersection of biology and technology — it uses living organisms to create medicines, improve crops, clean up pollution, and manufacture materials. India is one of the world's top three biotech hubs, with strengths in vaccine manufacturing (Serum Institute, Bharat Biotech), generic pharmaceuticals, and agricultural biotech.

Nanotechnology operates at a completely different scale — the nanometre, where individual atoms are manipulated and materials behave unlike their bulk counterparts. Both fields are converging in medicine: nanoparticles delivering biotech drugs directly to cancer cells is one of today's most active research areas.

For APPSC, the key facts are: regulatory bodies (GEAC vs. RCGM), the only approved GM crop (Bt Cotton), CRISPR Nobel, and India's Nano Mission.

## 1. Biotechnology — Core Concepts

### Definition

Biotechnology uses living organisms or their components to produce useful products and processes. The **CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity)** defines it as: *"Any technological application that uses biological systems, living organisms, or derivatives thereof to make or modify products or processes for specific use."*

### Colour-Coded Branches

| Colour | Application | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | Medical | Vaccines, insulin, gene therapy, mAbs |
| **Green** | Agriculture | GM crops, biopesticides, biofertilisers |
| **White** | Industrial | Biofuels, enzymes for textiles/paper, bioplastics |
| **Blue** | Marine | Marine organism-derived drugs and chemicals |

## 2. Recombinant DNA (rDNA) Technology

The core tool of modern biotechnology — allows genes from one organism to be inserted into another:

| Step | Tool | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Cut** | **Restriction Endonucleases** ("Molecular Scissors") | Cut DNA at specific palindromic sequences |
| **Join** | **Ligase** ("Molecular Glue") | Joins DNA fragments together |
| **Carry** | **Vectors** (Plasmids, Bacteriophages) | Carry the target gene into the host cell |
| **Amplify** | **PCR** (Polymerase Chain Reaction) | Amplifies specific DNA sequences; invented by **Kary Mullis** (Nobel 1993) |
| **Separate** | **Gel Electrophoresis** | Separates DNA fragments by size |

## 3. Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs)

### India's GMO Regulatory Framework

| Body | Under | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **GEAC** (Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee) | **MoEFCC** | Final authority for **environmental release** of GMOs in India |
| **RCGM** (Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation) | **DBT** | Reviews **confined field trials** of GM organisms |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** **GEAC is under MoEFCC**, NOT under the Department of Biotechnology (DBT). This is a very frequently tested distinction. RCGM (under DBT) handles research/field trials; GEAC (under MoEFCC) handles commercial release.

### Approved GM Crops in India

- **Bt Cotton**: The **ONLY commercially approved GM crop in India** (approved **2002**)
  - Contains **Cry proteins** from *Bacillus thuringiensis* — kills cotton bollworm
  - Reduced pesticide use in cotton significantly when first introduced
- All other GM crops: Under moratorium or field trial stage
  - Bt Brinjal: Approved in Bangladesh (2014); **moratorium in India** (GEAC approved but Environment Ministry imposed moratorium)
  - GM Mustard (DMH-11): Approved by GEAC in 2022; commercial cultivation pending govt decision

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's resistance to approving more GM crops is not purely scientific — it reflects political economy. The farmer movement (which drove the Farm Laws repeal in 2021) is deeply suspicious of corporate-controlled GM seeds. The Bt Cotton experience itself is contested: initial yield gains were followed by bollworm resistance and farmer indebtedness linked to higher seed costs.

## 4. Key Biotechnology Applications

### Medicine

| Application | Example / Detail |
|---|---|
| **Recombinant Insulin** | First biotech drug approved (1982); produced in *E. coli*; replaced pig/cow insulin |
| **Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs)** | Targeted cancer therapy; autoimmune diseases |
| **CRISPR-Cas9** | "Gene editing scissors" — precise DNA editing; **Nobel Prize 2020** (Doudna & Charpentier) |
| **CAR-T Cell Therapy** | Engineering patient's T-cells to fight cancer; India approved first indigenous CAR-T (**NexCAR19**) in **2024** |
| **Covaxin (BBV152)** | India's indigenous COVID-19 vaccine; Bharat Biotech + **ICMR**; whole-virion inactivated technology |

### Agriculture

- **Biofertilisers**: Microorganisms that fix nitrogen or solubilize phosphorus
  - Examples: *Rhizobium* (legume root nodules), *Azospirillum*, *Mycorrhiza*
- **Biopesticides**: Microorganism-based pest control; Example: *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) toxin
- **Tissue Culture (Micropropagation)**: Mass-produces disease-free plants (banana, orchids, potato)

## 5. Nanotechnology

### Scale and Properties

- **Nanotechnology**: Manipulation of matter at **1–100 nanometres (nm)** scale
- 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ metres; a human hair is ~80,000–100,000 nm wide
- At nanoscale, **quantum effects dominate** and **surface area:volume ratio** spikes → materials behave very differently from bulk matter

### Key Nanomaterials

| Nanomaterial | Properties / Use |
|---|---|
| **Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs)** | Extremely strong; electrically conductive; lightweight |
| **Graphene** | Single carbon atom layer; **strongest known material**; excellent conductor |
| **Gold Nanoparticles** | Drug delivery, diagnostic biosensors |
| **Quantum Dots** | Semiconductor nanocrystals; emit different colors based on size; used in LEDs, medical imaging |
| **TiO₂ Nanoparticles** | Sunscreens, self-cleaning surfaces |

### Applications

**Medicine (Nanomedicine):**
- **Targeted Drug Delivery**: Nanoparticle carriers deliver drugs directly to cancer cells, reducing side effects
- **Nano-diagnostics**: Detect cancer biomarkers at extremely low concentrations
- **Antimicrobial**: Silver nanoparticles for wound dressings

**Electronics:**
- Nano-transistors enable continued miniaturisation of chips (Moore's Law continuation)
- Graphene-based batteries for faster-charging lithium-ion

**Environment:**
- Nano-filters for water purification
- Nano-catalysts for breaking down pollutants

**Defence:**
- Nano-coatings for stealth aircraft
- Lightweight nano-composite armour
- Nanosensors for detecting chemical/biological agents

### Risks

- **Toxicity**: Nanoparticles may cross biological barriers (blood-brain barrier, cell membrane)
- **Environmental persistence**: Nano-silver harms aquatic ecosystems
- **Regulatory gap**: Most countries lack specific nanotechnology regulations

### India's Nano Mission

- **Launched**: 2007 by **DST** (Department of Science & Technology)
- **Funding**: ~₹1,000 crore over five years
- **Goals**: Build infrastructure, human resources, R&D, and applications
- **Key Centres**: CMET (Pune), NCL (Pune), JNCASR and IISc (Bengaluru)
