# Indian Constitution — Evolution & Features

> *The Constitution is not just a legal document — it is the story of how India chose to govern itself after centuries of colonial rule, and what kind of country it decided to become.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims Unit 4 | G1 Mains Paper 3 Unit 1 | G2 Paper 1 Section B
**Sources:** NCERT Class 11 (Constitution at Work), M. Laxmikanth (Indian Polity), Constitution of India (Text)

## The Big Picture

The Indian Constitution was not written in a vacuum. It was shaped by 150 years of British constitutional experiments in India, the independence movement's vision, and the framers' awareness of every major constitution in the world. Understanding it means understanding its context: Why did India choose parliamentary democracy over presidential? Why is it a federal system with a strong unitary bias? Why is it the longest written constitution in the world?

The story begins in 1773, winds through the Company era and Crown rule, reaches the Constituent Assembly in 1946, and concludes on 26 January 1950 — the day India truly became a republic. That journey is the foundation of every APPSC Polity question.

For APPSC candidates: the constitutional evolution section consistently produces 5–8 questions across papers. The key is pairing the Act/Year with what it did — especially the sequence from 1773 to 1950.

## 1. Evolution — From Company Rule to Constitution

### The Company Era (1773–1858)

Each Act in this period represents a gradual tightening of Parliamentary control over the East India Company:

| Act | Year | Key Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulating Act** | 1773 | First British Government control over Company; designated Bengal Governor as **Governor-General of Bengal** (first: Warren Hastings) |
| **Pitt's India Act** | 1784 | Separated commercial from political functions; created **Board of Control** (London) for political supervision |
| **Charter Act** | 1833 | Made Governor-General of Bengal the **Governor-General of India** (first: Lord William Bentinck); centralization complete |
| **Charter Act** | 1853 | Separated legislative and executive functions of Governor-General's Council; first law commission |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Warren Hastings = first Governor-General of **Bengal** (1773). Lord William Bentinck = first Governor-General of **India** (1833). These two are frequently confused in prelims questions.

### Crown Rule Era (1858–1947)

After the 1857 Revolt, governance transferred from the Company to the British Crown:

| Act | Year | Key Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Government of India Act** | 1858 | Power transferred from Company to Crown; created office of **Secretary of State for India** (London-based Cabinet minister) |
| **Indian Councils Act (Morley-Minto Reforms)** | 1909 | Introduced **separate electorates** for Muslims — the seed of communal politics |
| **Government of India Act (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms)** | 1919 | Introduced **Dyarchy** in provinces (some subjects transferred to elected Indian ministers, others retained by Governor) |
| **Government of India Act** | 1935 | Provided an All-India Federation; Dyarchy at Centre; **major source** for current Constitution (provincial autonomy, federal structure, judiciary) |
| **Indian Independence Act** | 1947 | Divided British India into India and Pakistan; declared both as independent sovereign states |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The Government of India Act **1935** is the single most important precursor to the Indian Constitution. Our federal structure, the three legislative lists (Union, State, Concurrent), the Federal Court (predecessor of Supreme Court), and provincial autonomy all trace directly to this Act. About 250 provisions were adopted from it.

## 2. Framing the Constitution — Key Dates

The Constituent Assembly was the body that wrote the Constitution. Know these dates and names:

| Event | Date | Person |
|---|---|---|
| Constituent Assembly formed | 1946 | Under **Cabinet Mission Plan** (British proposal) |
| First meeting | **9 December 1946** | **Sachchidananda Sinha** (interim President) |
| Permanent President elected | 11 December 1946 | **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** |
| Objective Resolution moved | **13 December 1946** | **Jawaharlal Nehru** (became basis of Preamble) |
| Drafting Committee formed | 29 August 1947 | **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** (Chairman) — "Father of the Constitution" |
| Constitution adopted | **26 November 1949** (Constitution Day) | — |
| Constitution came into force | **26 January 1950** (Republic Day) | — |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The Constitution was **adopted** on 26 November 1949 and **enforced** on 26 January 1950. The 26 January date was chosen because it marked the anniversary of the **Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) Declaration** of 1930 by the Indian National Congress.

## 3. Sources of the Constitution

The framers borrowed deliberately from the world's best constitutional models:

| Country | What India Borrowed |
|---|---|
| **UK** (Britain) | Parliamentary government, Rule of Law, Single citizenship, Cabinet system, Ministerial responsibility |
| **USA** | Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Impeachment of President, Independence of judiciary, Preamble concept |
| **Ireland** | **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)**, method of election of President, nomination to Rajya Sabha |
| **Canada** | Federation with a strong Centre, residuary powers with Centre, Advisory jurisdiction of Supreme Court |
| **Australia** | Concurrent List, joint sitting of Parliament, Freedom of trade between states |
| **Germany (Weimar)** | Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency |
| **South Africa** | Amendment procedure (for certain provisions), election of Rajya Sabha members |
| **Soviet Union (USSR)** | Fundamental Duties, socio-economic rights in Preamble |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The most-tested source in APPSC is **Ireland → DPSP**. The second most tested is **USA → Fundamental Rights and Judicial Review**. Memorize these two first, then the others.

## 4. Salient Features of the Constitution

The Indian Constitution is unique in the world for combining features that seem contradictory:

**1. Longest Written Constitution in the World**
Originally 395 Articles, 8 Schedules, and 22 Parts. Now 448 Articles, 12 Schedules, 25 Parts. The length reflects India's diversity — social complexity required detailed provisions.

**2. Drawn from Multiple Sources**
(See Sources table above)

**3. Blend of Rigidity and Flexibility**
- Some provisions need a 2/3rd special majority + ratification by half the states (e.g., federal provisions under Article 368)
- Some need only a simple majority of Parliament (ordinary legislation)

**4. Federal System with Unitary Bias**
K.C. Wheare called it "quasi-federal." It has federal features (written constitution, division of powers, independent judiciary) but also strong unitary features (Emergency provisions, residuary powers with Centre, single citizenship).

**5. Parliamentary Form of Government**
Executive is responsible to the legislature. PM and Council of Ministers remain in office as long as they enjoy Lok Sabha's confidence.

**6. Independent Judiciary**
The Supreme Court and High Courts have powers of judicial review — they can strike down laws that violate the Constitution.

**7. Secular State**
No state religion. The 42nd Amendment (1976) added "Secular" to the Preamble.

**8. Universal Adult Franchise**
Every citizen 18+ has the right to vote, regardless of caste, religion, gender, education, or property.

## 5. The Preamble

"WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens: JUSTICE, Social, Economic and Political; LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; EQUALITY of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation..."

**Five Key Characterizations of India:**
- **Sovereign**: India is fully independent — no external authority controls it
- **Socialist**: Added by 42nd Amendment (1976); commitment to reducing inequality, mixed economy
- **Secular**: Added by 42nd Amendment (1976); no state religion; equal treatment of all religions
- **Democratic**: Government by elected representatives; universal franchise
- **Republic**: Head of state (President) is elected, not hereditary

**The Three Values (SER → Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity):**
- **Justice**: Social, Economic, Political
- **Liberty**: Thought, expression, belief, faith, worship
- **Equality**: Status and opportunity
- **Fraternity**: Dignity of individual + unity and integrity of nation

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The Preamble was amended only **ONCE** — by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976)**, which added the words **Socialist**, **Secular**, and **Integrity**. The original words Sovereign, Democratic, Republic, Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity were in the original 1950 text. The Preamble is NOT justiciable (cannot be enforced in court), but it guides interpretation of the Constitution.
