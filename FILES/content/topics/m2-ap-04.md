# Andhra Movement & Formation of Andhra Pradesh

> *Potti Sriramulu died on December 15, 1952 after 58 days of fasting for a Telugu-speaking state. Within three months of his death, Andhra State was born. No single act of sacrifice in post-independence India produced faster political results.*

**Covers:** G2 Paper 1 (AP History) | G1 Mains Paper 2 (AP History & Culture)
**Sources:** K.V. Narayana Rao, *The Emergence of Andhra Pradesh* (1973); APPSC G2 Syllabus

---

## The Big Picture

The formation of Andhra Pradesh is a two-stage story. Stage one (1912–1953): the decades-long demand for a separate Telugu-speaking state carved out of the Madras Presidency, culminating in Potti Sriramulu's martyrdom and the creation of Andhra State in 1953. Stage two (1953–1956): the integration of Hyderabad's Telangana region into Andhra State, creating the linguistically unified Andhra Pradesh on November 1, 1956.

For APPSC, the critical facts are: the chronology of the Andhra agitation (Andhra Maha Sabha → JVP Committee → Potti Sriramulu → Andhra State), the Fazl Ali Commission's role, the Gentlemen's Agreement, and the exact dates of Andhra State (1953, capital Kurnool) vs. Andhra Pradesh (1956).

---

## 1. Pre-Independence Demand for Separate Andhra (1912–1947)

### Origin of the Demand

The demand for a separate Telugu-speaking province predates independence. Andhra was part of the Madras Presidency — a multilingual administrative unit where Telugu speakers felt dominated by Tamil speakers in administration, education, and commerce.

**Key milestones of the early movement:**

| Year | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1913 | **Andhra Maha Sabha** founded at Bapatla | First organized forum demanding separate Andhra province |
| 1913 | Congress session recognized Andhra demand | National-level legitimacy; Sub-committee formed |
| 1920 | Nagpur Congress session | Congress reorganized on linguistic lines; Andhra Pradesh Congress Committee formed separately |
| 1937–1939 | Andhra leaders' role in Congress ministries | Experience of governance sharpened state-formation aspirations |

**Leading figures:** Konda Venkatappaiah, Bulusu Sambamurthy, T. Prakasam (Andhra Kesari), Pattabhi Sitaramayya

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Andhra Maha Sabha** (1913, Bapatla) was the first organized body demanding a separate Telugu province. It was a cultural and political organization, NOT a political party. The Congress Working Committee formally recognized the principle of linguistic reorganization in 1920 at the Nagpur session.

## 2. Post-Independence: JVP Committee and Its Verdict

### The JVP Committee (1948–49)

After independence, the demand for linguistic reorganization intensified across India. Prime Minister Nehru appointed a three-member committee: **J**awaharlal Nehru, **V**allabhbhai Patel, and **P**attabhi Sitaramayya — hence the **JVP Committee**.

**JVP Committee's verdict (April 1949)**: Recommended **AGAINST** immediate linguistic reorganization. Reasons:
1. Newly independent India needed national integration over regional identities
2. Fear of cascading demands — if Andhra gets a state, everyone will want one
3. Risk of straining state finances (new smaller states = weaker tax bases)

**Impact:** The JVP verdict was a setback for the Andhra movement. It delayed but did not kill the demand. Telugu leaders intensified their pressure on the Congress government.

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The JVP Committee's rejection of linguistic reorganization in 1949 created the conditions that made Potti Sriramulu's sacrifice inevitable. Nehru had shut the door to a negotiated settlement. Only a dramatic act — martyrdom — was left to force a rethink. This is why Sriramulu's fast in 1952 was so consequential: it directly overturned the JVP verdict.

## 3. Hyderabad Liberation (1948) — Background Context

Before Andhra State could be formed, the Nizam's Hyderabad State had to be integrated into India. This matters because Telangana — the Telugu-speaking region of Hyderabad — later became part of AP.

| Event | Date | Detail |
|-------|------|--------|
| Nizam's Hyderabad | Pre-1948 | Largest princely state; Osman Ali Khan (last Nizam); refused accession to India |
| Operation Polo (Police Action) | **September 17–18, 1948** | Indian Army (Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri) integrated Hyderabad in 5 days; Sardar Patel's decisive action |
| Mir Laiq Ali's surrender | September 18, 1948 | Nizam signed instrument of accession; M.K. Vellodi became first CM of Hyderabad |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **Operation Polo** is also called the **Police Action** (euphemism used by Sardar Patel). It lasted just **5 days** (September 13–18, 1948). The Nizam had initially sought independence, then tried to approach the UN. Patel acted before any international intervention became possible.

## 4. Potti Sriramulu and the Andhra State (1952–53)

### Who Was Potti Sriramulu?

Potti Sriramulu (1901–1952) was a Gandhian activist from Krishna district who had already fasted for causes (including temple entry for untouchables). He was a devoted follower of Gandhi and believed in the power of satyagraha.

### The Fast-unto-Death

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| **October 19, 1952** | Sriramulu began his fast-unto-death in Madras, demanding immediate formation of Andhra State |
| November–December 1952 | Nehru's government remained unmoved; mass agitation grew across Telugu districts |
| **December 15, 1952** | Sriramulu died after **58 days** of fasting |
| December 16–17, 1952 | Massive violence and riots across Andhra — Vijayawada, Guntur, Nellore, Ongole; government buildings burned |
| **December 19, 1952** | Nehru announced the formation of a separate Andhra State |
| **October 1, 1953** | **Andhra State formally inaugurated** |

### Andhra State (1953–1956)

| Detail | Fact |
|--------|------|
| Formed | **October 1, 1953** |
| Capital | **Kurnool** |
| First Chief Minister | **Tanguturi Prakasam** (briefly); then **Bezawada Gopala Reddy** |
| Districts | 11 districts from the Madras Presidency's Telugu-speaking areas |
| Legislature | Andhra Legislative Assembly opened at Kurnool |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** Andhra State (1953) had **Kurnool** as capital — NOT Hyderabad and NOT Vijayawada. Hyderabad became the capital only after 1956 when the full Andhra Pradesh (including Telangana) was formed.

## 5. Fazl Ali Commission and States Reorganisation

### States Reorganisation Commission (1953)

The massive public response to Sriramulu's death forced Nehru to abandon the JVP position. In December 1953, the government appointed the **States Reorganisation Commission (SRC)**:

| Member | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Fazl Ali** | Chairman |
| **H.N. Kunzru** | Member |
| **K.M. Panikkar** | Member |

**The Commission toured the country and submitted its report in October 1955.**

**Key recommendations affecting Andhra:**
- The Telangana region of Hyderabad State should be merged with Andhra State → creating a unified Telugu state
- Recommended linguistic basis for all state reorganizations
- But expressed some reservations about the Andhra–Telangana merger: cultural and economic differences between coastal Andhra and Telangana were noted

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The Fazl Ali Commission endorsed linguistic reorganization — the very principle the JVP Committee had rejected just six years earlier. Sriramulu's martyrdom had shifted the political calculation entirely. The SRC report validated not just Andhra's demand but set the template for the 1956 reorganization that redrew India's entire state map.

## 6. Formation of Andhra Pradesh (1956)

### The Visalandhra Demand

**Visalandhra** (= "Greater Andhra") was the vision of a unified state comprising all Telugu-speaking people — coastal Andhra + Rayalaseema + Telangana. The **Visalandhra Mahasabha** was the organization that championed this cause.

**Arguments for Visalandhra:**
- Language = basis for nationhood; all Telugu speakers = one state
- Economic complementarity: coastal Andhra's agricultural surplus + Telangana's minerals and water
- Cultural unity through Telugu literature, cinema, music

**Arguments against (from Telangana side):**
- Telangana had higher per-capita revenue (Hyderabad State)
- Fear of being dominated by "Andhras" (coastal people)
- Economic interests would be diverted to coastal development

### The Gentlemen's Agreement (February 1956)

Before the merger, leaders of Andhra and Telangana regions signed the **Gentlemen's Agreement** in February 1956 to protect Telangana's interests:

| Provision | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **CM rotation** | If Andhra person is CM, Telangana person must be Deputy CM, and vice versa |
| **Employment** | Telangana people to get jobs in Telangana region; Mulki Rules to apply |
| **Education** | No domicile for Andhra students in Telangana educational institutions for jobs |
| **Budget** | Telangana to have a Regional Committee with advisory power over its development funds |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Gentlemen's Agreement of 1956** was NOT a constitutional guarantee — it was a political understanding between party leaders. Its non-implementation became the primary grievance driving Telangana separatist movements in 1969 and 2009.

### Andhra Pradesh — Formal Formation

| Detail | Fact |
|--------|------|
| Date | **November 1, 1956** |
| Composition | Andhra State (11 districts) + Telangana (9 districts of former Hyderabad State) |
| Capital | **Hyderabad** |
| First CM | **N. Sanjiva Reddy** |
| Legislature | AP Legislative Assembly, Hyderabad |
| **November 1** | Now celebrated as **AP Formation Day** |

**States Reorganisation Act, 1956** — the central legislation that reorganized all of India's states on linguistic lines, effective November 1, 1956.
