# Defence & Strategic Technologies

> *India became only the 4th country to shoot down a satellite in orbit in 2019. That same year, it commissioned its first indigenous aircraft carrier. These aren't coincidences — they're two data points in a deliberate strategy to build strategic autonomy.*

## The Big Picture

India's defence technology story is about escaping dependence. For decades after independence, India imported almost every major weapons system — MiG fighters from the USSR, tanks from Russia, ships from Britain. The Kargil War (1999) exposed how this dependence could constrain options during a crisis. Since then, two parallel strategies have run: **indigenisation** (make it in India) and **nuclear deterrence** (ensure no country would risk war with India).

For APPSC, the key knowledge areas are India's nuclear doctrine (No First Use, Civilian Control), missile technology (BrahMos vs. Agni), key indigenous platforms (INS Vikrant, Tejas), and the ASAT test (Mission Shakti 2019). The AP angle: Visakhapatnam hosts India's major naval base, submarine construction yard, and DRDO facilities.

## 1. Defence Policy Framework

### Key Institutions

| Body | Function |
|---|---|
| **Ministry of Defence (MoD)** | Apex body for defence policy and procurement |
| **CDS (Chief of Defence Staff)** | Created **January 2020** (Kargil Review Committee recommendation); integrates the three services; First CDS: Gen. Bipin Rawat |
| **DMA (Department of Military Affairs)** | Under CDS; manages inter-service coordination |
| **DRDO** | Defence R&D; ~50+ labs; HQ New Delhi |

### Tri-Services Commands

- **Andaman & Nicobar Command**: India's **only tri-services command** (Army + Navy + Air Force under one command); established 2001
- **Strategic Forces Command**: Controls India's nuclear weapons (both offensive and command-and-control)

## 2. India's Nuclear Programme

### Foundational Figures

| Figure | Role |
|---|---|
| **Homi J. Bhabha** | Father of India's nuclear programme; founded BARC (then AEET) in 1954 |
| **APJ Abdul Kalam** | Led missile programme; "Missile Man of India"; contributed to Pokhran-I preparations |
| **Dr. R. Chidambaram** | Scientific Adviser during Pokhran-II |

### Nuclear Tests

| Test | Year | Codename | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pokhran-I** | **1974** | Smiling Buddha / Operation Shakti | First nuclear test; India became 6th nuclear-capable state |
| **Pokhran-II** | **1998** | Operation Shakti | 5 tests in 2 days; India declared itself a nuclear weapon state |

### Nuclear Doctrine

Three key pillars:

1. **No First Use (NFU)**: India will NOT be the first to use nuclear weapons — only in retaliation
2. **Massive Retaliation**: Any nuclear attack on India will be met with massive nuclear retaliation
3. **Civilian Command**: Nuclear weapons under civilian Political Council of Nuclear Command Authority (chaired by the Prime Minister)

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's **No First Use policy** is significant and uncommon — most nuclear states (USA, Russia, China, Pakistan) either explicitly reject NFU or keep their options ambiguous. India's NFU commitment reassures neighbours but critics argue it creates deterrence uncertainty. There has been periodic debate about whether India should move away from NFU given the changing threat environment.

### Nuclear Treaties — India's Position

| Treaty | India's Position |
|---|---|
| **NPT** (Non-Proliferation Treaty, 1968) | **Not a signatory** — India says it discriminates between nuclear "haves" and "have-nots" |
| **CTBT** (Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty) | **Not ratified** — India says it won't sign until universal disarmament |
| **NSG waiver (2008)** | India–US Civil Nuclear Agreement enabled India to trade in nuclear materials despite not being NPT signatory |

## 3. Nuclear Triad

India is one of few countries with a **complete nuclear triad** (all three delivery platforms):

| Platform | System | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Land** | Agni missile series | Agni-V: ICBM-class, 5,500+ km range, MIRV-capable |
| **Air** | Mirage 2000H, Jaguar DARIN | Nuclear-capable aircraft |
| **Sea** | **INS Arihant** | India's first **domestically built nuclear submarine (SSBN)**; inducted **2016** |

## 4. Missile Technology

| Missile | Type | Range | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BrahMos** | **Cruise** (supersonic) | 300–500 km | India–Russia joint; **~2.8 Mach** (world's fastest operational cruise missile) |
| **Agni-V** | **Ballistic** (ICBM class) | 5,500+ km | MIRV-capable (multiple warheads); India's longest-range missile |
| **Prithvi** | Short-range ballistic | 150–250 km | Surface-to-surface; India's first indigenously built ballistic missile |
| **Akash** | Surface-to-air (SAM) | 25 km | Air defence; replacing imported Russian SAMs |
| **Nag** | Anti-tank guided | 4–7 km | "Fire and forget" ATGM |
| **K-15 (Sagarika)** | Submarine-launched (SLBM) | ~750 km | Fired from INS Arihant |
| **Shaurya** | Hypersonic | ~750 km | Land-equivalent of K-15; canister-launched |

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** **BrahMos** is a **cruise missile** — flies at low altitude using an air-breathing engine, like an aircraft. **Agni** is a **ballistic missile** — launches upward, follows a parabolic arc through space, then descends. Very different flight profiles and purposes. BrahMos is for precision strikes; Agni is for strategic nuclear deterrence.

## 5. Space Defence (ASAT & Military Satellites)

### Mission Shakti (2019)

- India became **only the 4th country** (after USA, Russia, China) to successfully test an **ASAT (Anti-Satellite) weapon**
- Shot down a live Indian satellite in **Low Earth Orbit** using a **Kinetic Kill Vehicle (KKV)**
- Designated operation: **Mission Shakti**
- Strategic significance: Demonstrates India can deny adversaries their satellite capabilities

### Key Military Satellites

| Satellite | Function |
|---|---|
| **RISAT** (Radar Imaging Satellite) | All-weather, day-night ground surveillance; can see through clouds |
| **EMISAT** | Electronic intelligence — detects and tracks enemy radar systems |
| **Cartosat** | High-resolution earth observation; border surveillance, mapping |
| **GSAT series** | Communications (some dedicated for military secure communications) |

## 7. Indigenisation — Atmanirbhar Bharat in Defence

### Policy Framework

- **DAP 2020** (Defence Acquisition Procedure 2020): Prioritises "Buy Indian — IDDM" (Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured) as first preference
- **Negative Import Lists**: Items that cannot be imported — must be procured domestically. 3 such lists issued; covers 300+ items including complex artillery, engines, sonars
- **Defence FDI**: Increased to **74% under automatic route** (100% under government route)

### Defence Industrial Corridors

| Corridor | States | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **UP Defence Corridor** | Lucknow–Agra axis | Small arms, ammunition, aerospace |
| **Tamil Nadu Defence Corridor** | Chennai–Coimbatore axis | Electronics, aeronautics, naval systems |

### Key Indigenous Platforms

| System | Status |
|---|---|
| **HAL Tejas (LCA)** | Inducted into IAF; Mk-2 (more powerful) under development |
| **INS Vikrant (IAC-1)** | India's first **indigenous aircraft carrier**; commissioned **September 2022** |
| **AMCA** (Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft) | India's first **5th generation stealth fighter**; design finalized; development ongoing |
| **FICV** (Future Infantry Combat Vehicle) | Under development; will replace Soviet-era BMP-2 IFVs |

**Nuclear Doctrine:**
- **No First Use (NFU)**: India will not strike first
- Civilian control: Political Council chaired by **PM**
- India is NOT in **NPT**; not ratified **CTBT**

**Key Tests:**
- Pokhran-I: **1974** (Smiling Buddha)
- Pokhran-II: **1998** (Operation Shakti)
- Mission Shakti (ASAT): **2019** — 4th country to test ASAT

- BrahMos: **Cruise**, **2.8 Mach**, India–Russia joint
- Agni-V: **Ballistic**, 5,500+ km, MIRV-capable
- Prithvi: First indigenous **ballistic** missile

**Key Institutions:**
- CDS created: **January 2020**; First CDS: **Bipin Rawat**
- BARC founded: **1954** by Homi Bhabha
- Only tri-services command: **Andaman & Nicobar** (est. 2001)

**Indigenous Platforms:**
- Tejas: India's first indigenous **combat aircraft**
- INS Vikrant: India's first indigenous **aircraft carrier** (**2022**)
- AMCA: India's **5th generation** stealth fighter (under development)
