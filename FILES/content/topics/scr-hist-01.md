## 1. Indus Valley Civilization (IVC) / Harappan Civilization

### Discovery & Timeline
- **Discovered:** 1921 — Daya Ram Sahni excavated Harappa; R.D. Banerjee excavated Mohenjo-daro (in Sindh)
- Large-scale excavations at Mohenjo-daro under J. Marshall (1931); Vats at Harappa (1940); Mortimer Wheeler (1946)
- **Dates:** Mature Harappan phase c. **2600–1900 BCE** (~4500–3900 years ago)
- This was the **first urbanization in India** (Bronze Age urban culture)

### Geographic Extent
- Covered: Punjab, Haryana, Sindh, Baluchistan, Gujarat, Rajasthan, fringes of western UP
- Extended from Siwaliks (north) → Arabian Sea (south); Makran coast (west) → Meerut (north-east)
- Area: ~1,299,600 sq. km — **larger than ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia**
- Nearly **2800 Harappan sites** identified; most important cities: **Harappa** (Punjab, Pakistan) and **Mohenjo-daro** (Sindh, Pakistan)

### Major Sites
| Site | Location | Special Feature |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| Harappa | Pakistani Punjab | 6 granaries; barracks; first discovered |
| Mohenjo-daro | Sindh, Pakistan | Great Bath; largest granary; ~500 ha (largest site) |
| Rakhigarhi | Ghaggar, Haryana | ~250 ha; larger than Dholavira |
| Dholavira | Kutch, Gujarat | 3-part town division; water reservoir; large tank; 50 ha |
| Lothal | Gulf of Cambay, Gujarat | Dockyard (Rao); rice cultivation evidence; ~1800 BCE |
| Kalibangan | N. Rajasthan | Black bangles; ploughed field; pre-Harappan + Harappan |
| Banawali | Hissar, Haryana | Two cultural phases; mud-brick platforms |
| Chanhu-daro | 130 km S of MJD | Only site with no citadel; bead-making factory |
| Surkotada | W. Gujarat | Citadel; horse bones (~2000 BCE, doubtful) |

### Town Planning
- **Grid system**: roads cutting at right angles
- **Two-part layout**: **Citadel** (western, higher — ruling class) + **Lower Town** (eastern — common people)
- Dholavira had **three parts** (citadel + middle town + lower town)
- **Great Bath, Mohenjo-daro**: 11.88 × 7.01 m, 2.43 m deep; burnt bricks; possibly for ritual bathing
- **Granaries**: Mohenjo-daro — 1 large granary (45.71 × 15.23 m); Harappa — 6 granaries, combined ~838 sq. m
- **Drainage system**: Each house had drains; covered drains in streets — unique in Bronze Age world
- Burnt bricks used extensively (unlike Egypt where dried bricks were used)

### Agriculture
- Crops: **wheat, barley, rai, peas, sesame, mustard**; Lothal — rice (~1800 BCE)
- No canal irrigation found (except Shortughai in Afghanistan); floodwater farming
- Ploughed fields found at Kalibangan (two crops simultaneously); terracotta plough model at Banawali
- Sowed in November (after floods); harvested April — like Nile-fed Egypt
- **First people to grow cotton** (Greeks called the region Sindon from Sindh)

### Technology & Crafts
- **Bronze tools**: axes, saws, knives, spears (copper from Khetri mines, Rajasthan + tin from Afghanistan)
- **Seals**: 2000+ seals found; steatite stone; undeciphered script (~400 pictographic signs)
- **Beads**: carnelian, jasper, lapis lazuli — Chanhudaro had bead factory; Lothal had bead-making evidence
- **Pottery**: wheel-made, glazed, characteristic black painted designs
- **Weights and measures**: standardized throughout the civilization (important for trade evidence)
- No iron tools (Bronze Age)

### Trade & Commerce
- Internal trade: stone, metal, shell, semi-precious stones
- **Trade with Mesopotamia** (Tigris-Euphrates): Harappan seals found in Mesopotamia
- Mesopotamian texts (~2350 BCE onwards) call Indus region **"Meluha"**; intermediate stations = Dilmun (Bahrain) and Makan
- Exported: carnelian beads, copper, cotton, lapis lazuli, ivory
- Imported: silver, tin, gold from Afghanistan and Iran
- No metal money — barter system

### Society, Polity, Religion
- **Social hierarchy**: citadel (rulers) → middle town (bureaucrats, merchants) → lower town (artisans, workers)
- Two-roomed houses at Harappa probably for artisans/labourers
- Probably ruled by a **merchant class** (no temples found; no evidence of priest-kings unlike Mesopotamia)
- **Mother Goddess** figurines (earth goddess/fertility); plant growing from embryo
- **Pashupati Seal**: three-horned deity in yogic posture, surrounded by elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, buffalo, deer — proto-Shiva (identification disputed)
- **Phallus and female organ symbols** found — proto-Shiva worship
- Pipal tree worship; humped bull worship; unicorn (one-horned animal) — most common seal motif
- **No temples** found at any Harappan site (unlike Egypt/Mesopotamia)

### Decline (~1900 BCE)
- Multiple theories: floods (Mohenjo-daro), ecological change/deforestation, drying of Sarasvati, Aryan invasion (discredited), epidemic
- Most likely: **combination of ecological factors** — reduced rainfall, silting of rivers
- Post-urban phase in Gujarat (Rangpur, Rojdi) continued after urban collapse

## 2. Vedic Age

### Origins & Early Vedic Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE)
- **Aryans migrated from Central Asia** (~1500 BCE) via Andronovo culture + BMAC (Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex, 1900–1500 BCE)
- Term **"arya"** occurs 36 times in Rig Veda — denotes cultural/linguistic community
- **Rig Veda**: earliest Indo-European text; 10 mandalas (books II–VII = oldest); collection of prayers to Agni, Indra, Mitra, Varuna etc.
- Settled in: eastern Afghanistan, NWFP, Punjab, fringes of western UP — the **Land of Seven Rivers (Sapta Sindhu)**

### Rig Vedic Society
- **Pastoral economy**: Cattle = primary wealth; wars fought for cows (gavishthi = search for cows)
- Three-tier society: warriors (kshatriyas), priests (brahmins), common people (vis/vaishyas); shudras appeared at the end of Rig Vedic period (Rig Veda Book X)
- **King (rajan)**: tribal chief, post becoming hereditary; protected tribe's cattle, fought wars
- **Assemblies**: sabha, samiti, vidatha, gana — deliberative, military, religious functions; women attended
- **Purohita** (priest) and **senani** (army chief) were key officials
- No standing army; no territorial administration; no taxes — voluntary offerings called **bali**
- **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)**: associated with Rig Vedic culture; found at Bhagwanpura (Haryana, 1600–1000 BCE)

### Rig Vedic Gods
- **Indra** (warlord, rain god, Purandara): 250 hymns — most important
- **Agni** (fire god, intermediary): 200 hymns — second
- **Varuna** (water/cosmic order god): third position
- **Soma** (plant god; intoxicating ritual drink)
- **Sarasvati**: important river goddess
- Nature gods predominant; no temples or idol worship

### Later Vedic Phase (c. 1000–500 BCE)
- Aryans expanded from Punjab → Upper Gangetic plain (Kurus-Panchalas combination)
- **Iron use** began; **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)** pottery emerged
- **Agriculture became dominant** (over pastoralism); land became property
- New texts: Sama Veda, Yajur Veda, Atharva Veda, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads
- **Varna system crystallized**: four varnas (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra) became rigid by birth
- **Ashvamedha** (horse sacrifice) and Rajasuya (coronation sacrifice) — king consolidated power
- **Upanishads**: philosophical texts on atman (individual soul) and Brahman (universal soul)
- Vedic religion = elaborate rituals; priest monopoly → gave rise to reform movements (Buddhism, Jainism)
- **Panini** composed Ashtadhyayi (grammar of Sanskrit) — ~500 BCE

### 4 Vedas Summary
| Veda | Content |
|------|---------|
| Rig Veda | Hymns to gods (oldest, ~1500 BCE) |
| Sama Veda | Musical recitation of Rig Veda hymns |
| Yajur Veda | Sacrificial formulas and rituals |
| Atharva Veda | Charms, spells, non-Aryan traditions |

## 3. Mahajanapadas & Rise of Magadha

### Mahajanapadas (~600 BCE)
- 16 Mahajanapadas mentioned in early Buddhist/Jaina texts (~500 BCE)
- Most important: **Vajji, Magadha, Koshala, Kuru, Panchala, Gandhara, Avanti**
- Each had fortified capital city; maintained armies and bureaucracies
- Two types: monarchies (ruled by kings) and **ganas/sanghas** (oligarchies — Vajji, Malla etc.)
- **Vajji sangha** (Vaishali) — world's first known republic; both Buddha and Mahavira belonged to sangha-type states

### Rise of Magadha
- Located in present-day Bihar; became dominant 6th–4th centuries BCE
- Advantages: fertile agriculture, iron mines (Jharkhand), elephants (forests), Ganga river network
- Key kings:
  - **Bimbisara** (~543–491 BCE): allied with Magadha; first expansionist Magadhan king
  - **Ajatasattu** (~491–461 BCE): killed father Bimbisara; conquered Vajji (Vaishali) after 16-year war
  - **Mahapadma Nanda**: last great Nanda ruler; vast empire but unpopular
- Capital shifted from **Rajagaha (Rajgir)** → **Pataliputra** (Patna) in 4th century BCE

### Alexander's Invasion (~327–325 BCE)
- Persian invasion preceded: Cyrus/Darius; Achaemenid empire controlled Gandhara — spread coins and satraps
- Alexander crossed Indus (326 BCE); defeated **Porus** (Battle of Hydaspes/Jhelum)
- Turned back at Beas river (soldiers refused to cross); left in 325 BCE
- Impact: opened Greek-Indian trade routes; Indo-Greek kingdoms later; Greek accounts (Megasthenes etc.)

## 4. Jainism & Buddhism

### Jainism
- **Vardhamana Mahavira** (599–527 BCE): 24th Tirthankara; born in Vaishali (Bihar)
- Attained enlightenment (Kaivalya) at age 42 at Jrimbhikagrama on Rijupalika river
- Core doctrines: **Ahimsa** (non-violence), **Satya** (truth), **Asteya** (non-stealing), **Brahmacharya** (celibacy), **Aparigraha** (non-possession)
- **Tri-ratna**: Right faith, Right knowledge, Right conduct
- Rejected Vedic authority and caste; attracted traders/merchants
- Two sects: **Digambara** (sky-clad) vs **Shvetambara** (white-clad) — split after Mahavira's death
- Texts: **Agamas** (Shvetambara); council at Valabhi (5th/6th c. CE) compiled Jaina texts
- Contribution: enriched Prakrit and Apabhramsha literature; banking/trade ethics

### Buddhism
- **Siddhartha Gautama Buddha** (563–483 BCE): born in Lumbini (Nepal); Kshatriya clan of Shakyas
- Enlightenment at **Bodh Gaya** (under Peepal/Bodhi tree); first sermon at **Sarnath** (Deer Park) — Dhammachakra Pravartan
- Died at **Kushinagar** (~483 BCE)

#### Four Noble Truths (Arya Satyas)
1. Life is full of suffering (dukkha)
2. Desire/craving (tanha/trishna) is cause of suffering
3. Cessation of desire leads to Nirvana
4. The Eightfold Path leads to cessation

#### Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga)
Right view, Right resolve, Right speech, Right conduct, Right livelihood, Right effort, Right mindfulness, Right meditation

#### Buddhist Councils
| Council | Location | Period | Key Event |
|---------|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1st | Rajagaha | 483 BCE (after Buddha's death) | Compiled Vinaya and Sutta Pitakas |
| 2nd | Vaishali | 383 BCE | Split into Sthaviravada and Mahasanghika |
| 3rd | Pataliputra | ~250 BCE (Ashoka's reign) | Compilation; missionaries sent abroad |
| 4th | Kashmir | ~1st c. CE (Kanishka) | Mahayana vs Hinayana formalized |

#### Buddhist Texts (Tripitaka — Three Baskets)
- **Vinaya Pitaka**: rules for monks/nuns
- **Sutta Pitaka**: Buddha's discourses (Dhammapada is part of this)
- **Abhidhamma Pitaka**: philosophical analysis

#### Two Major Schools
- **Hinayana (Theravada)**: original Buddhism; individual salvation; no idol worship; Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand
- **Mahayana**: Buddha as god; bodhisattvas; idol worship; China, Japan, Korea

#### Reasons for Spread
- Simple language (Pali); no caste discrimination; royal patronage (Ashoka, Kanishka); monasteries as centers of learning; practical ethical code

#### Decline in India
- Revival of Brahmanical Hinduism (Gupta period); Muslim invasions destroyed monasteries (Nalanda, Vikramashila); no lay base remained; absorption into Hinduism (Buddha as Vishnu avatar)

#### Nalanda University
- Greatest center of Buddhist learning; established ~5th century CE; patronized by Gupta kings; Xuan Zang studied here; destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khilji (1193 CE)

## 5. Mauryan Empire (321–185 BCE)

### Chandragupta Maurya (321–297 BCE)
- Founded dynasty with help of **Chanakya (Kautilya)** — brahmin strategist
- Origins disputed: brahmanical tradition = shudra-born (son of Mura); Buddhist tradition = from Gorakhpur republic (Pipphalivana)
- Overthrew the **Nandas** (~321 BCE); liberated north-west from Greek viceroy **Seleucus**
- Treaty with Seleucus: India got eastern Afghanistan, Baluchistan, area west of Indus; gave 500 war elephants
- Empire covered: Bihar, Bengal, Orissa, western/north-western India, Deccan (except Kerala, Tamil Nadu, NE India)

### Administrative System (from Arthashastra + Megasthenes's Indika)
- **Megasthenes**: Greek ambassador at Pataliputra; wrote Indika (fragments survive)
- **Arthashastra** of Kautilya: key administrative text (finally compiled a few centuries later)
- **Pataliputra** administration: 6 committees × 5 members each — sanitation, care of foreigners, births/deaths, weights/measures etc.
- City: 9.33 miles long × 1.75 miles broad; wooden wall + 570 towers + 64 gates + deep ditch
- Provincial administration: princes (scions of royal family) governed provinces
- Important cities: Taxila (NW), Ujjain (W), Pataliputra (capital), Kaushambi
- Army: **600,000 foot soldiers, 30,000 cavalry, 9000 elephants, 8000 chariots** (Pliny's account)
- **27 superintendents (adhyakshas)**: regulated agriculture, trade, weights, crafts, mining
- State monopoly: mining, liquor sale, arms manufacture
- Tax: 1/4 to 1/6 of produce from peasants
- Elaborate **espionage system** (various types of spies)

### Ashoka (273–232 BCE)
- Greatest Maurya ruler; son of Bindusara
- **Kalinga War (~261 BCE)**: fought and won but caused mass suffering
  - 100,000 killed, 150,000 deported (numbers as claimed in 13th Rock Edict — exaggerated)
  - Led to Ashoka's conversion to Buddhism and policy of Dhamma
  - **"Bherighosha" replaced by "Dhammaghosha"** (war drums → dharma drums)

#### Ashokan Inscriptions
- 39 inscriptions total; 182 versions at 47 places (India, Nepal, Pakistan, Afghanistan)
- Types: Major Rock Edicts (14), Minor Rock Edicts, Separate Rock Edicts, Major Pillar Edicts (7), Minor Pillar Edicts
- Language: **Prakrit**; Script: **Brahmi** (most of subcontinent), Kharoshthi (NW), Aramaic & Greek (Afghanistan)
- **First deciphered by James Prinsep in 1837**
- Title used: **"Devanampiya Piyadasi"** (Beloved of Gods, Good-looking)
- Name Ashoka appears in only 4 inscriptions (in Karnataka and MP)

#### Ashoka's Dhamma
- **Not sectarian Buddhism** — universal ethical code
- Key principles: compassion, ahimsa, obedience to parents, respect for brahmins/monks, mercy to slaves
- **Dhamma Mahamatras** (officers): propagated dharma among all sections
- **Rajukas**: officers for justice and administration
- Banned animal sacrifice; reduced killing of animals in royal kitchen
- Sent missionaries to Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Central Asia, Greek kingdoms
- Planted trees, dug wells, built rest houses, hospitals along highways

### Fall of Mauryan Empire
- After Ashoka (232 BCE), empire fragmented
- **Brahmanical reaction** (Pushyamitra Shunga killed last Maurya king Brihadratha, 185 BCE)
- Financial crisis (huge army, welfare expenses)
- North-west frontier neglect; Bactrian Greeks (Indo-Greeks) invaded
- Oppressive rule in later period; lack of strong successors

## 6. Post-Mauryan Period (185 BCE – 320 CE)

### Shungas (185–73 BCE)
- **Pushyamitra Shunga** killed Brihadratha (Maurya) in 185 BCE
- Brahmanical revival; performed ashvamedha; suppressed Buddhism
- Capital: Pataliputra; Vidisha also important
- Famous for: **Sanchi Stupa** (added gateways/railings during Shunga period)

### Indo-Greeks (~200–100 BCE)
- Ruled NW India and parts of Punjab after Alexander's successors
- First to issue **bilingual coins** (Greek + Kharoshthi)
- **Menander (Milinda)**: most famous Indo-Greek king; became Buddhist (Milindapanha)
- Introduced Greek elements into Indian art → **Gandhara School** of sculpture

### Shakas (~100 BCE – 400 CE)
- Central Asian people; kingdoms in NW, N, and western India
- Famous Shaka ruler: **Rudradaman I** (Junagarh inscription — oldest long Sanskrit inscription, 2nd c. CE)
- Defeated by Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) around 380–410 CE

### Kushanas (~1st–3rd c. CE)
- Central Asian dynasty; controlled NW India + Central Asia
- **Kanishka** (~1st/2nd c. CE): greatest Kushana king; capital at Peshawar; 2nd capital at Mathura
- Patron of Buddhism (4th Buddhist Council held in Kashmir); Mahayana Buddhism flourished
- **Silk Route**: Kushanas controlled and taxed it; connected Central Asia → Indus ports → Roman Empire
- Gold coins (copied Roman and Greek designs); excellent cavalry soldiers

### Satavahanas (~1st c. BCE – 3rd c. CE)
- Dominant in Deccan and western India; lasted ~400 years from ~2100 years ago
- Capital: Pratishthana (Paithan) on Godavari
- Most important ruler: **Gautamiputra Shri Satakarni** — called "lord of Dakshinapatha"
- Defeated Shakas in western India; promoted Brahmanical religion
- Inscriptions in Prakrit; known for vernacular literature in Maharashtri Prakrit
- Architecture: rock-cut caves at Ajanta, Ellora, Karle, Bhaja

### Sangam Kingdoms (South India, ~300 BCE – 300 CE)
- Three Tamil kingdoms: **Cholas** (capital Uraiyur/Kaveripattinam), **Cheras** (capital Vanji/Muzris), **Pandyas** (capital Madurai)
- Known from **Sangam Literature**: earliest Tamil texts (~2300 years ago) compiled at Madurai
- Important works: **Tolkappiyam** (grammar), **Silappadikaram**, **Manimekalai** (epics)
- **Arikamedu** (Puducherry): Roman trade port (2200–1900 years ago); Mediterranean pottery found
- Trade: pepper (called "black gold" by Romans), spices, gems, gold

## 7. Gupta Empire — The Golden Age (320–550 CE)

### Background
- After fall of Kushans (~230 CE), Murundas ruled till ~250 CE
- Guptas were probably of **vaishya origin** (gupta = hidden/protected — recommended title for vaishyas)
- Original kingdom: UP + Bihar (UP was primary base; Prayag was center)
- Guptas possibly started as feudatories of Kushans in UP

### Key Rulers
| Ruler | Period (CE) | Key Achievement |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Sri Gupta** | c. 240–280 | Founder; minor king |
| **Chandragupta I** | 319–334 | Married Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi; started **Gupta Era (319-20 CE)**; first to adopt title Maharajadhiraja |
| **Samudragupta** | 335–380 | Military genius; "Napoleon of India"; Allahabad Pillar inscription by poet Harishena; conquered 5 categories of rulers |
| **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)** | 380–412 | Peak of empire; defeated Shakas; controlled western coast; court of nine gems (Navaratnas) incl. Kalidasa; Fa Xian's visit (399–414) |
| **Kumaragupta** | 412–454 | Founded Nalanda University |
| **Skandagupta** | 455–467 | Repelled first Huna invasion; after him empire declined |

### Samudragupta's Conquests (from Allahabad Pillar Inscription)
- 5 groups of conquered territories:
  1. **Aryavarta kings** (9 rulers): defeated and kingdoms annexed
  2. **Dakshinapatha kings** (12 rulers): defeated then reinstated as feudatories
  3. **Frontier states** (Nepal, Assam, Bengal, Punjab republics): paid tribute
  4. **Outlying areas** (Shakas, Kushans, Sri Lanka): submitted voluntarily
  5. **Forest kingdoms** (Atavika rajyas in Vindhya region): brought under control

### Gupta Administration
- Titles: **Parameshvara, Maharajadhiraja, Paramabhattaraka**
- Key officers: **Kumaramatyas** (highest; appointed by king; paid in cash in core areas)
- Empire divided into: **Bhuktis** (divisions, under uparika) → **Vishayas** (districts, under vishayapati) → **Vithis** → villages
- Feudatory system: vassals paid homage, tribute, gave daughters in marriage; received Garuda-seal charters
- **Land grants** to priests/brahmins (brahma-deya): tax-free; peasants paid directly to beneficiaries — **beginnings of feudalism**
- Village headman gained power; local merchants/artisans had say in governance
- Less centralized than Mauryas; **decentralized administration**

### Economy
- Issued largest number of **gold coins (dinaras)** in ancient India
- Silver coins for local trade (after conquest of Gujarat)
- Trade with **eastern Roman (Byzantine) empire**: India exported silk
- ~550 CE: eastern Romans learnt silk-making from China → India's silk trade declined
- Forced labor (vishti) in central/western India
- Land grants brought virgin areas under cultivation

### Art, Literature & Science — "Golden Age"

#### Literature
- **Kalidasa**: Abhijnana Shakuntalam (play), Meghaduta, Raghuvamsha, Kumarasambhava — flourished under Chandragupta II
- **Vishakhadatta**: Mudrarakshasa (play on Chandragupta Maurya)
- **Puranas** compiled in final form during Gupta period: Vishnu, Shiva, Brahma Puranas etc.
- Mahabharata and Ramayana compiled in current form (~1500 years ago)
- **Amarasimha**: Amarakosha (Sanskrit dictionary)
- Fa Xian (Fa Hsien), Chinese pilgrim, visited 399–414 CE; described prosperous Magadha

#### Art & Architecture
- **Stupas**: Great Stupa at Sanchi (Ashoka's core; railings/gateways added later)
- **Temples**: first standalone Hindu temples built — **Bhitargaon** (Kanpur, baked brick, ~1500 years ago); Deogarh (stone, Dashavatara temple)
- Elements: **garbhagriha** (sanctum), **shikhara** (tower over garbhagriha), **mandapa** (hall for devotees)
- **Ajanta Caves**: Buddhist monastery caves; paintings done by torchlight; colors from plants/minerals; still vivid after 1500 years
- **Iron Pillar** at Mehrauli (Delhi): 7.2m high, 3 tonnes; ~1500 years old; no rust — Chandra = probably Chandragupta II
- Gupta coins: vividly portray kings; show horsemen, battle scenes, veena-playing

#### Science & Mathematics
- **Aryabhata** (476–550 CE): Aryabhatiyam (mathematical-astronomical text in Sanskrit)
  - Earth rotates on its axis (causes day/night); scientific explanation of eclipses
  - Calculated circumference of circle (π ≈ 3.1416)
  - Value of pi; place value system; **concept of zero**
- **Varahamihira**: Panchasiddhantika (astronomy); Brihat Samhita (encyclopedia)
- **Brahmagupta**: Brahmasphutasiddhanta (mathematics)
- **Charaka** (1st–2nd c. CE): Charaka Samhita — medicine/Ayurveda
- **Sushruta** (4th c. CE): Sushruta Samhita — surgery (elaborate surgical procedures described)
- **Nagarjuna**: Buddhist philosopher; founder of Madhyamaka school; also chemist

### Fall of Gupta Empire
- **Huna invasions** (second half of 5th century CE):
  - Skandagupta repelled initially; later Hunas occupied eastern Malwa and central India (by 485 CE)
  - **Yashodharman of Malwa** defeated Hunas but also challenged Guptas (532 CE pillar)
- Rise of **feudatories** (north Bengal, Maukharis in Bihar/UP, Valabhi in Gujarat)
- Decline of **trade** (silk export to Rome fell; guild of silk weavers migrated from Gujarat to Malwa ~473 CE)
- Debasement of gold coins (reduced pure gold content)
- By 550 CE: Bihar and UP lost; Gupta power restricted to eastern India
- Last Gupta rulers in Magadha as minor kings

## 8. Post-Gupta: Harshavardhana (606–647 CE)

### Background & Rise
- Kingdom of **Thanesar** (Haryana); Vardhana dynasty
- Father and elder brother died; Harsha succeeded to Thanesar throne
- Brother-in-law ruled Kanauj; was killed by ruler of Bengal → Harsha took over Kanauj
- Established large empire; **capital: Kanauj** (UP)

### Conquests
- Successful in east: conquered Magadha, Bengal
- **Failed to cross Narmada** — stopped by Chalukya king **Pulakeshin II** (~620 CE)
  - Pulakeshin's court poet Ravikirti: "After the defeat, Harsha was no longer Harsha" (pun — harsha = happiness)

### Administration & Legacy
- **Harshacharita** by Banabhatta: Sanskrit biography
- **Si-Yu-Ki** by Xuan Zang (Hiuen Tsang): Chinese pilgrim spent time at Harsha's court; detailed account
- Patronized Buddhism; built many stupas; held Buddhist councils
- **Nalanda University** flourished under Harsha
- After Harsha's death (647 CE): empire fragmented; no successor; India entered period of small kingdoms

## 9. AP / Andhra Pradesh Exam Angle (APPSC Focus)

### Key Andhra Connections
- **Satavahanas**: ruled Andhra/Deccan for ~400 years (c. 230 BCE – 220 CE); capital Amaravati and Pratishthana
  - **Amaravati Stupa** (Guntur): one of the greatest stupas of ancient India; magnificent sculptural school
  - Satavahana rulers were major patrons of Buddhism in the Deccan
  - **Nagarjunakonda** (Guntur district): important Buddhist site from post-Satavahana period (Ikshvaku dynasty)
- **Kalinga War**: Ashoka's conquest of Kalinga (Odisha) directly affected eastern Andhra
- **Ikshvaku dynasty** (~3rd–4th c. CE): succeeded Satavahanas in Andhra; capital Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda); Buddhist patrons
- **Vishnukundin dynasty** (~5th–6th c. CE): ruled parts of Andhra; known from inscriptions

### Important AP-Specific MCQ Facts
- Amaravati School of Art: limestone sculptures; characteristic "bending" posture (tribhanga)
- Nagarjuna (Buddhist philosopher) is associated with Andhra/Nagarjunakonda
- Satavahana coins: most found in Andhra Pradesh
- Buddhaprakasham district has Amaravati Stupa
- Kondapur (Medak): Satavahana-era site with silver punchmarked coins

## 11. Quick Revision Bullets

**IVC:**
- First discovery: 1921; Harappa (Daya Ram Sahni), Mohenjo-daro (R.D. Banerjee)
- Largest city: Mohenjo-daro (500 ha); Largest India site: Rakhigarhi (250 ha)
- Great Bath: Mohenjo-daro; Dockyard: Lothal
- No temples; no iron; no horses (IVC was not horse-centric)
- Undeciphered script; standardized weights and measures
- Called "Meluha" in Mesopotamian texts
- First to grow cotton

**Vedic Age:**
- Rig Veda: 10 mandalas; Indra (250 hymns), Agni (200 hymns), Varuna
- Battle of Ten Kings on river Parushni (Ravi) → Bharatas won → combined with Purus → Kurus
- PGW (Painted Grey Ware) = Rig Vedic culture; NBPW = post-600 BCE urbanization
- Later Vedic: varna system rigid; ashvamedha; Upanishads (atman-Brahman); Panini's grammar

**Mahajanapadas:**
- 16 Mahajanapadas; Magadha most powerful
- Bimbisara → Ajatasattu → Nandas → Chandragupta Maurya
- Vajji (Vaishali) = world's first republic (oligarchy)

**Buddhism:**
- Born Lumbini; Enlightened Bodh Gaya; First sermon Sarnath; Died Kushinagar
- 4 Noble Truths; 8-fold path; Tripitaka (3 pitakas)
- 4 Buddhist Councils (Rajagaha → Vaishali → Pataliputra → Kashmir)
- Hinayana = Theravada (individual salvation); Mahayana (Bodhisattvas, idol worship)

**Jainism:**
- Mahavira = 24th Tirthankara; born Vaishali; Panchvrata (5 vows)
- Digambara vs Shvetambara split; Tri-ratna
- Jains supported by merchants/traders

**Mauryas:**
- Chandragupta: with Kautilya; defeated Nandas; 321 BCE; treaty with Seleucus (500 elephants)
- Ashoka: Kalinga war 261 BCE; Dhamma policy; 14 Major Rock Edicts
- Brahmi deciphered by James Prinsep (1837); Ashokan script = Brahmi
- Arthashastra: 27 adhyakshas; 6 committees of Pataliputra

**Post-Mauryan:**
- Pushyamitra Shunga: killed last Maurya (185 BCE); brahmanical revival
- Indo-Greeks: bilingual coins; Menander/Milinda = Buddhist
- Kanishka: Kushana; controlled Silk Route; 4th Buddhist Council; Mathura + Peshawar capitals
- Satavahanas: ~400 years; Gautamiputra Shri Satakarni; Amaravati stupa
- Sangam kingdoms: Cholas, Cheras, Pandyas (~300 BCE–300 CE); Arikamedu = Roman trade

**Guptas:**
- Chandragupta I: 319 CE; married Lichchhavi princess; Gupta era
- Samudragupta: Napoleon of India; Allahabad Pillar (Harishena)
- Chandragupta II: Vikramaditya; Fa Xian 399–414; Kalidasa's patron; 9 gems court
- Fall: Hunas (485 CE) + feudatories + trade decline
- Aryabhata: earth rotates; eclipses; pi; value of zero
- Iron Pillar (Mehrauli): 1500 years old; no rust = Chandra inscription

**Harsha:**
- 606–647 CE; Kanauj; stopped by Pulakeshin II at Narmada
- Harshacharita (Banabhatta); Xuan Zang's account
- Nalanda University flourished
