## 1. Family, Marriage and Kinship

### Family
- **Foundation of Society:** Family is the primary unit of socialization.
- **Types of Family:**
  - **Nuclear Family:** Consists of husband, wife, and their unmarried children.
  - **Joint Family (Extended Family):** Comprises multiple generations living under one roof, sharing a common kitchen and property. Common in traditional agrarian Indian society.
- **Patriliny vs. Matriliny:**
  - **Patriliny:** Family lineage and property passed from father to son (dominant in most of India).
  - **Matriliny:** Lineage passed through the mother (e.g., Nayars of Kerala, Khasis of Meghalaya).
- **Patrilocal vs. Matrilocal:** Refers to where the couple resides after marriage.

### Marriage
- **Hindu Marriage as a Sacrament:** Traditionally viewed as an indissoluble bond.
- **Forms of Marriage (Ancient/Vedic):**
  - **Endogamy:** Marriage within one's own group (varna, caste).
  - **Exogamy:** Marriage outside one's own group (e.g., Gotra exogamy).
  - **Hypergamy (Anuloma):** Marriage of a man to a woman from a lower caste.
  - **Hypogamy (Pratiloma):** Marriage of a man to a woman from a higher caste (traditionally discouraged).
- **Marriage Customs:**
  - **Monogamy:** One man, one wife (most prevalent).
  - **Polygamy:** Includes **Polygyny** (one man, multiple wives) and **Polyandry** (one woman, multiple husbands - found in some tribes like Toda).

### Kinship
- **Definition:** Social relationship based on real or supposed genealogical ties.
- **Bonds:**
  - **Consanguineous:** Based on blood (e.g., parents, siblings).
  - **Affinal:** Based on marriage (e.g., in-laws).
- **Kinship Terms:** Describing relationships (e.g., Mama, Kaka, Anna).

## 2. Caste, Tribe and Ethnicity

### The Varna and Caste System
- **Four-fold Varna System:** Brahmana (Priest), Kshatriya (Warrior), Vaishya (Trader/Farmer), Shudra (Worker). 
- **Jati (Caste):** The localized, occupational groups that characterize the actual social hierarchy in India. 
- **Characteristics of Caste:**
  - Determined by **birth**.
  - **Endogamous** (marry within).
  - **Hierarchical** status.
  - Traditionally associated with **fixed occupations**.
  - Purity and Pollution concepts.

### Tribe (Scheduled Tribes)
- **Definition:** Historically called *Adivasis* (original inhabitants). Societies that did not follow the varna system or Hindu social norms.
- **Geographic Distribution:** Concentrated in Central India (MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha) and the North-East.
- **Constitutional Recognition:** Article 342 defines "Scheduled Tribes".
- **Ethnicity:** Refers to shared cultural heritage, including language, religion, and unique customs.

## 3. Religion and Women in Indian Society

### Religion in India
- **Secularism:** The Indian state maintains an "equidistance" from all religions while providing special protection to minorities.
- **Religious Demographics (1951 Census):** 12% Muslim population; also includes Sikhs, Christians, Jains, Buddhists, Parsis, and Jews.
- **Communalism:** The ideology that bases political identity on religion, often leading to conflict.

### Women in Indian Society
- **Patriarchal Structure:** Traditional society is predominantly male-dominated.
- **Challenges:** Crime against women, child marriage, lack of education, and gender pay gap.
- **Reform Movements:** In the 19th/20th century (Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Ambedkar) led to laws against Sati and support for widow remarriage.
- **Political Representation:** 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts provided **33% reservation for women** in Local Body elections (Panchayats/Municipalities).
- **Women's Movements:** Organizations like SEWA (Self-Employed Women's Association) and movements against dowry and domestic violence.

## 4. Social Dynamics & Change
- **Sanskritization:** (M.N. Srinivas) Process by which lower castes adopt the customs and rites of high-born castes to raise their social status.
- **Westernization:** Change resulting from British rule (education, technology, secular values).
- **Constitutional Protection:** Articles 14 (Equality), 15 (No discrimination), 16 (Opportunity in public employment), and 17 (Abolition of Untouchability) provide the legal framework for a just society.
