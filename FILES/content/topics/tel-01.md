# Telugu Language & Literature

> *Nannaya translated the Mahabharata into Telugu in the 11th century. A thousand years later, Telugu is the 3rd most spoken language in India, has Classical Language status, and has produced more literary forms — from prabandha to haiku — than most languages twice its size. The story of Telugu literature is the story of Andhra civilization.*

**Covers:** G2 Language Paper (Telugu) | G1 Language Paper | G1 Mains Paper 2 (AP History & Culture)
**Sources:** K. Veeresalingam Pantulu's literary history; APPSC Telugu Syllabus; Sahitya Akademi records

## The Big Picture

Telugu literature spans over 1,000 years — from Nannaya's Mahabharata (1022 CE) to contemporary novels and short stories. It developed through four distinct phases: the Classical period (1000–1500 CE) of royal patronage and epic poetry; the Bhakti/Prabandha period (1500–1800 CE) of devotional and ornamental poetry; the Modern period (1800–1950 CE) of social reform and prose; and the Contemporary period (1950–present) of diverse forms.

For APPSC, the key knowledge areas are: the three great poets (Kavitrayam — Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana), key works and their periods, the Telugu Script's origin, the Classical Language recognition in 2008, and the major modern writers (Vemana, Pothana, Gurajada).

## 1. Telugu Script and Language — Origins

### Linguistic Classification

- **Family**: Dravidian language family; closely related to Kannada
- **Script**: Derived from **Brahmi script** (via Bhattiprolu variant) — evolved through Grantha → Telugu-Kannada → Telugu script
- **Oldest inscription**: **Erragudi inscription** of Ashoka (3rd century BCE) — in Brahmi/Prakrit found in AP
- **First purely Telugu inscription**: **Kalamalla inscription** (6th century CE, Rayalaseema area)

### Classical Language Status

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Classical Language recognition** | **October 2008** — granted by Government of India |
| **Criteria** | High antiquity (>1,500 years); substantial literary tradition independent of other languages |
| **Effect** | Two chairs for Telugu studies in central universities; international awards; Centre for Classical Telugu at Tirupati |

**Other Classical Languages of India** (6 total, in order of recognition):
Tamil (2004), Sanskrit (2005), Kannada (2008), Telugu (2008), Malayalam (2013), Odia (2014)

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Telugu received Classical Language status in **2008**, the same year as Kannada. Tamil was the **first** Classical Language (2004). There are **6** Classical Languages total. Telugu's claim is based on the Kalamalla inscription (6th century CE) and the Nannaya tradition (11th century CE). Questions frequently ask: year, criteria, first language, and current count.

## 2. The Kavitrayam — The Three Great Poets

### The Mahabharat Translators

The **Kavitrayam** (Three Jewels of Telugu Poetry) translated the Mahabharata into Telugu — the foundational work of classical Telugu literature.

| Poet | Period | Contribution | Patron |
|------|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Nannaya Bhattaraka** | ~**1022–1063 CE** | First 2 books (Parvas) + part of 3rd book of Mahabharata | **Raja Raja Narendra** of Eastern Chalukyas (Rajahmundry) |
| **Tikkana Somayaji** | **13th century** | Last 15 books of Mahabharata | **Manuma Siddhi II** of Nellore; also worked under Ambadeva |
| **Errana (Yerrana)** | **14th century** | Completed Aranayaparva (3rd Parva) left by Nannaya | Prolaya Vema Reddy |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The Mahabharata translation took **over 300 years** and three poets — a remarkable collaboration across centuries. Nannaya wrote in **Champu style** (prose mixed with verse), which he invented for Telugu — this style became the model for Telugu classical poetry. Tikkana introduced the **dwipada** (couplet) metre. The gap between Nannaya and Tikkana (11th to 13th century) remains unexplained — a 150-year mystery in Telugu literary history.

### Nannaya — First Poet of Telugu

- Known as **"Adikavi"** (first poet) of Telugu
- Translated **Sabha Parva, Adi Parva**, and part of **Aranya Parva**
- Created the **champu style** — mixed prose and verse
- Established the grammar of classical Telugu through *Andhra Shabda Chintamani* (earliest Telugu grammar)
- Sanskrit scholar who made Sanskrit epic accessible in elegant Telugu

### Tikkana Somayaji — Greatest Telugu Poet

- Widely considered the **greatest of the three** — superior poetic skill
- Translated **15 of the 18 parvas** of Mahabharata
- Known for **psychological depth** of characters and dramatic dialogue
- **Title**: *Ubhaya Kavita Pitamaha* (grandfather of both Sanskrit and Telugu poetry)
- Famous for **Nirvachanottara Ramayanam** (original Telugu Ramayana, not translation)

### Errana — The Completer

- Real name **Yerrapragada**
- Completed the **Aranya Parva** left incomplete by Nannaya
- Also wrote **Ramayanamu** (Telugu Ramayana), **Narasimha Puranamu**
- Poetic style = elegant and graceful

## 3. Devotional/Bhakti Period (1300–1700 CE)

### Pothana — Bhagavatham

**Bammera Pothana** (15th century) is one of the most beloved Telugu poets:

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Work | **Maha Bhagavatamu** (Telugu translation of Srimad Bhagavatam, the Sanskrit devotional text) |
| Patron | Pothana is famous for **refusing to dedicate his work to any king** — dedicated it to Lord Rama alone |
| Quote | "Pothana will offer the fruits of his labour to Rama, not to any earthly king" (famous declaration) |
| Style | Pure devotional (*bhakti*) tone; accessible Telugu; emotional intensity |
| Significance | Bhagavatam = most widely read Telugu religious text |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Pothana's **refusal to dedicate to a human king** is his defining historical characteristic — he rejected the patronage system. This is heavily tested. The story goes that King Sarvajnasingama tried to appropriate the work; Pothana's response became legendary in Telugu culture.

### Vemana — Philosophical Poetry

**Yogi Vemana** (17th century) wrote **Vemana Shatakamu** — philosophical four-line verses (*shatakam* = a poem of 100 verses):

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Form | **Ataveladi metre** (specific 4-line verse form) |
| Content | Social criticism, spiritual wisdom, anti-superstition, human psychology |
| Famous ending | Each verse ends with **"Vishwadabhirama, Vinura Vema"** |
| Language | Simple, colloquial Telugu — accessible to all classes |
| Themes | Caste criticism, false worship, wisdom, impermanence, human nature |

**Famous Vemana verse (paraphrased):**
"A learned man who does not practice his learning is like a blind man carrying a lamp."

## 4. Social Reform Period (1800–1950)

### Kandukuri Veeresalingam Pantulu — Father of Modern Telugu Literature

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Period | 1848–1919 |
| Role | **Father of modern Telugu literature** and Telugu social reform movement |
| Social work | Pioneered widow remarriage; established first widow remarriage in AP (1881); established schools for girls |
| Literary works | *Rajashekhara Charitra* (first Telugu novel); plays; *Saraswati Naradam* (satirical journal) |
| Journalism | Founded *Viveka Vardhini* (first social reform journal in Telugu) |

### Gurajada Apparao — Kanyasulkam

**Gurajada Venkata Apparao** (1862–1915) is the most celebrated modern Telugu playwright:

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Work | ***Kanyasulkam*** (1892) — "The Bride Price" |
| Significance | **Greatest Telugu play**; satire on child marriage and social hypocrisy |
| Character | **Girisam** — charming hypocritical villain; one of Telugu literature's greatest characters |
| Language | Introduced **colloquial spoken Telugu** into literature (vs. formal literary Telugu) |
| Famous poem | **"Desa Bhakti"** — "Whatever is good for the country is a deed of worship" |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** **Kanyasulkam** (1892) was the first Telugu work to attack social customs using dramatic realism. Gurajada's choice to write in **spoken Telugu** (not the formal literary variety) was revolutionary — it democratized Telugu literature and influenced all subsequent prose writers. His *Desa Bhakti* poem is quoted in AP school curricula and is the closest Telugu literature has to a patriotic national poem.

### Sri Sri — Modern Revolutionary Poetry

**Srirangam Srinivasarao** ("Sri Sri", 1910–1983) is the most influential modern Telugu poet:

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Era | Progressive movement; 20th century |
| Work | ***Mahaprasthanam*** (1950) — epic revolutionary poetry collection |
| Style | Free verse (*Vachana Kavita*); broke from classical metres; influenced by Marxism |
| Theme | Class struggle, anti-colonialism, humanitarianism |
| Significance | Revolutionized Telugu poetry; the "people's poet" (*Prajaakavi*) |

## 5. Other Key Literary Works and Writers

| Writer | Period | Work/Contribution |
|--------|--------|------------------|
| **Srinatha** | 15th century | *Shringara Naishadham*; *Kasikhandam*; court poet at Vijayanagara |
| **Dhurjati** | 15th–16th century | *Sri Kalahasteeswara Mahatmyamu*; Shiva devotee; lyrical grace |
| **Allasani Peddana** | 16th century | *Manucharitra*; "Andhra Kavita Pitamaha" (grandfather of Telugu poetry) under Krishnadevaraya |
| **Krishnadevaraya** | 1509–1529 | *Amuktamalyada* — king himself wrote great Telugu poem; patron of Ashta Diggajas (8 poets) |
| **Tenali Rama** | 16th century | Court poet at Vijayanagara; wit and humour; *Panduranga Mahatyamu* |

### The Ashta Diggajas — Eight Poets of Krishnadevaraya

Krishnadevaraya's court had **8 great poets** (Ashta = 8, Diggaja = elephant of the 8 directions):
Allasani Peddana, Nandi Timmana, Madayyagari Mallana, Dhurjati, Ayyalaraju Ramabhadra Kavi, Pingali Surana, Ramaraja Bhushana, Tenali Ramakrishna

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **Allasani Peddana** was the **"Andhra Kavita Pitamaha"** (grandfather of Andhra poetry) — he was the most senior and respected among the Ashta Diggajas. His *Manucharitra* is considered the finest prabandha in Telugu. NOT to be confused with Kavitrayam's Nannaya (Adikavi) — Nannaya is the first poet; Peddana is the grandfather of the prabandha (ornate poetic) tradition.
