# Indian Administration — Centre, States & Local Government

> *The District Collector is the most powerful unelected person in India. A 24-year-old IAS officer can walk into a flood-hit village with authority over police, revenue, food supply, and public health — all at once. No democracy has replicated this instrument. It works — most of the time.*

## The Big Picture

India's administrative system is a three-tier structure: the Union (Centre), the States, and Local Bodies (Panchayati Raj + Urban Local Bodies). At every tier, a constitutional framework defines powers, a bureaucratic apparatus implements decisions, and political executives provide direction. The Indian Administrative Service (IAS) — the steel frame — links all three tiers through the system of cadre-based postings.

For APPSC, the key areas are: how the Union executive functions (President → PM → Cabinet → ministries), how state administration works (Governor → CM → Secretariat → Collector), how the IAS cadre system operates, and how local government functions under the 73rd/74th Amendments.

## 1. Union Executive — How the Centre is Administered

### The Constitutional Hierarchy

```
President of India (Constitutional Head)
    ↓ (acts on advice of)
Council of Ministers headed by Prime Minister
    ↓
Cabinet Committees
    ↓
Union Ministries (headed by Ministers)
    ↓
Departments (headed by Secretaries — IAS officers)
    ↓
Attached Offices / Subordinate Offices / PSUs
```

### President of India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Constitutional role | Head of State; executive power vested in him (Article 53) |
| In practice | Acts on the advice of Council of Ministers (Article 74) |
| **Discretionary power** | Limited: appointing PM when no clear majority; dismissal when no confidence |
| Emergency powers | Article 352 (National), 356 (President's Rule in states), 360 (Financial) |

### Prime Minister and Cabinet

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **PM** | Head of Government; chairs Cabinet; leader of majority in Lok Sabha |
| **Cabinet** | Senior ministers with full collective responsibility |
| **Cabinet Secretariat** | Administrative support to Cabinet; headed by **Cabinet Secretary** (senior-most IAS officer in India) |
| **PMO (Prime Minister's Office)** | PM's direct staff; Principal Secretary to PM; coordinates all ministries; growing influence |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **Cabinet Secretary** is the senior-most civil servant in India — the top of the IAS hierarchy. The **Cabinet Secretariat** (different from PMO) manages Cabinet meetings, Cabinet committees, and coordination between ministries. The distinction between PMO and Cabinet Secretariat is frequently tested: PMO = PM's personal staff office; Cabinet Secretariat = institutional administrative support.

### Union Ministries — How They Work

Each Ministry has:
- **Minister** (political head)
- **Secretary** (IAS officer — administrative head; provides policy advice and administrative coordination)
- **Additional Secretary, Joint Secretary, Director, Under Secretary** (hierarchy below Secretary)
- **Attached Offices** (e.g., CBDT under Finance Ministry)
- **Subordinate Offices** (field offices across states)

## 2. The IAS — The Steel Frame

### Civil Services Structure

India's bureaucracy has three layers:
1. **All India Services (AIS)**: IAS, IPS, IFoS — serve both Centre and states; recruited by UPSC
2. **Central Services**: Serve only the Centre (IRS, IFS, IRS-Customs, etc.)
3. **State Services**: Recruited by State PSCs; serve only in that state

### IAS Cadre System

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Cadre** | IAS officers are allotted to a state cadre (e.g., AP cadre, UP cadre) upon joining |
| **Deputation** | Officers can be deputed to Centre or other states |
| **UPSC examination** | Civil Services Examination (CSE) — Prelims + Mains + Interview |
| **Training** | Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie |
| **First posting** | SDO/Joint Collector in a district |
| **Career progression** | Collector → Commissioner → Secretary → Additional Secretary → Secretary |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **LBSNAA Mussoorie** is where IAS and other Group A Civil Services officers receive their foundational training (18 months). State-level training happens at **State Administrative Training Institutes (ATIs)** — in AP, the apex training institute is the **AP Academy of Rural Development (APARD)** for state civil servants and **Centre for Good Governance (CGG)** for senior officers.

## 3. State Administration

### Constitutional Framework

| Office | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Governor** | Constitutional head of state; appointed by President; acts on advice of CM in most matters |
| **Chief Minister** | Head of state government; leader of majority in Vidhan Sabha; chairs Cabinet |
| **State Cabinet** | Collective responsibility to state legislature |
| **Chief Secretary** | Senior-most IAS officer in state; head of state civil service; chairs DGP, coordinates departments |

### State Secretariat

The **State Secretariat** is the apex policy-making body of the state government:

| Level | Officer | Role |
|-------|---------|------|
| Secretary | IAS officer | Department head; advises minister; policy formulation |
| Special Secretary / Additional Secretary | Below Secretary | Assists Secretary |
| Joint Secretary / Deputy Secretary | Below Additional | Process files; coordinate |
| Under Secretary | Working level | Noting and drafting |

**AP Secretariat**: Located in **Velagapudi, Amaravati** (since 2017 relocation from Hyderabad)

## 4. District Administration — The Collector

### The District as Unit of Administration

The district is the basic unit of administration in India — inherited from the British East India Company model. The **Collector** (also called District Magistrate and District Collector) is the head of district administration.

**Functions of the District Collector:**

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Revenue** | Land revenue collection; land records; survey and settlement; natural calamity relief |
| **Law and Order** | District Magistrate powers — preventive detention, Section 144 CrPC, Curfew |
| **Development** | Chair of DISHA (District Development Coordination and Monitoring Committee) for all central schemes |
| **Elections** | District Election Officer — conduct of elections |
| **Disaster Management** | District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA) chairman |
| **Census** | District Census Officer |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The Collector is sometimes described as the **"eyes, ears and hands of the state government"**. The role was created by Lord Cornwallis in 1793 during the Permanent Settlement. In 225+ years, the title has barely changed — Collector — though functions have expanded massively. The Collector's power to invoke Section 144 CrPC (prohibitory orders) and to order preventive detention (as DM) makes this office unique in a democracy: extraordinary power vested in one unelected individual.

### Sub-District Administration

```
District (Collector / DM)
    ↓
Revenue Division (Revenue Divisional Officer / RDO)
    ↓
Mandal (Tahsildar / Mandal Revenue Officer - MRO) — AP specific term
    ↓
Village (Village Revenue Officer - VRO) — AP specific
```

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **"Mandal"** is specific to **AP and Telangana**. Most states use "Taluk" or "Tehsil". AP replaced the old taluk system with mandals in 1985. Each mandal has a **Mandal Revenue Officer (MRO)** and a **Mandal Development Officer (MDO)**. At village level, the **Village Revenue Officer (VRO)** handles land records. These are high-frequency AP-specific administrative terms for APPSC.

## 5. Panchayati Raj — Local Self-Government (Rural)

### Constitutional Foundation

**73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992)** — gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj:

| Feature | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Three-tier structure** | Gram Panchayat → Panchayat Samiti (Mandal level in AP) → Zilla Parishad |
| **11th Schedule** | 29 subjects for Panchayats (agriculture, education, social welfare, etc.) |
| **Reservation** | Not less than 1/3 seats reserved for women; reservation for SC/ST proportional to population |
| **SEAC** | State Election Commission — to conduct local body elections |
| **State Finance Commission** | To review financial position of Panchayats every 5 years |
| **Article 243** | All Panchayati Raj provisions |

### AP's Three-Tier Panchayat Structure

| Tier | Name | Level | Elected Body |
|------|------|-------|-------------|
| **Tier 1** (village) | **Gram Panchayat (GP)** | Village/hamlet cluster | Ward Members + Sarpanch |
| **Tier 2** (mandal) | **Mandal Praja Parishad (MPP)** | Mandal | MPTC members + President |
| **Tier 3** (district) | **Zilla Parishad (ZP)** | District | ZPTC members + ZP Chairperson |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** AP's mandal-level body is called **Mandal Praja Parishad (MPP)** — not Panchayat Samiti (as in Maharashtra) or Block Panchayat (as in Rajasthan). The elected representatives are **MPTC** (Mandal Praja Territorial Constituency) and **ZPTC** (Zilla Praja Territorial Constituency). State-specific nomenclature is heavily tested in APPSC.

### 74th Amendment — Urban Local Bodies

**74th Constitutional Amendment (1992)** — constitutionalized urban local bodies:

| Institution | For |
|------------|-----|
| **Gram Panchayat** | Rural areas under 10,000 population |
| **Nagar Panchayat** | Transitional area (urban–rural fringe) |
| **Municipal Council** | Smaller urban areas |
| **Municipal Corporation** | Larger cities (>5 lakh population typically) |

**12th Schedule** — 18 subjects for Urban Local Bodies (urban planning, regulation of land use, public health, etc.)

**AP Urban Local Bodies:** Greater Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation (GVMC), Vijayawada Municipal Corporation (VMC), Guntur Municipal Corporation (GMC), Tirupati Municipal Corporation (TMC)

## 6. UPSC and APPSC

### UPSC (Union Public Service Commission)

- Constitutional body (Article 315–323)
- Conducts Civil Services Examination (IAS, IPS, IFS, etc.)
- Advises on service matters, disciplinary cases
- Chairman appointed by President

### APPSC (Andhra Pradesh Public Service Commission)

- Constitutional body for AP (Article 315); counterpart of UPSC for state services
- Recruits **Group 1, Group 2, Group 3, Group 4** state civil servants
- **Group 1** = senior state services (Deputy Collector, DSP, etc.)
- **Group 2** = sub-collector level and equivalents
- Headquarters: **Vijayawada, AP**

## Quick Revision — High-Frequency APPSC Facts

**Centre:**
- Cabinet Secretary = senior-most IAS in India; heads Cabinet Secretariat
- PMO = PM's personal staff office (NOT Cabinet Secretariat)
- IAS training: LBSNAA, Mussoorie

**State:**
- Chief Secretary = senior-most IAS in state; head of state civil service
- AP Secretariat: Velagapudi, Amaravati

**District:**
- Collector = DM (District Magistrate) = DE (District Election Officer) = DDMA Chairman
- AP sub-district unit = **Mandal** (not taluk/tehsil) — introduced 1985
- AP village revenue officer = **VRO** (Village Revenue Officer)

**Panchayati Raj:**
- 73rd Amendment 1992; 29 subjects (11th Schedule); 1/3 reservation for women
- AP three tiers: Gram Panchayat → **Mandal Praja Parishad (MPP)** → **Zilla Parishad (ZP)**
- Elected members: **MPTC** (mandal level), **ZPTC** (district level)

**74th Amendment 1992:** Urban local bodies; 18 subjects (12th Schedule); Nagar Panchayat / Municipal Council / Municipal Corporation

**APPSC:** Vijayawada; recruits Group 1, 2, 3, 4 state services
