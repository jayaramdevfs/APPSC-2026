# Prehistoric Cultures of India

> *For 300,000 years before the first city was built, Indians lived as hunters and gatherers. The transition from stone tools to copper axes was not a single revolution — it was a series of slow transformations, each one building the foundation for what came next.*

**Covers:** G2 Paper 1 AP History | G1 Mains Paper 2 | G1 Prelims Ancient History
**Sources:** R.S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past* (Oxford, 2005), Chapters 7–9

---

## The Big Picture

The prehistoric era in India spans roughly 600,000 years — from the earliest stone tools to the emergence of the first metal-using cultures. It is studied through the material record alone (no written texts), which means every conclusion is inferred from tools, pottery, animal bones, and burial practices.

The arc of change is clear: Palaeolithic hunter-gatherers (dependent on nature) → Mesolithic herders (beginning animal domestication) → Neolithic farmers (settled agriculture, permanent villages) → Chalcolithic cultures (copper + stone, social stratification). Each transition was driven by technology and climate. For APPSC, know the characteristic tools of each phase, key sites, and the AP-specific sites.

## 1. Geological Background

| Epoch | Period | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Pleistocene** (Ice Age) | ~2 million BC – 12,000 BC | Entire Palaeolithic culture developed here; successive ice ages |
| **Holocene** (Post-Ice Age) | 12,000 BC – present | Warming climate → Mesolithic transition; agriculture begins |

**Human Evolution Chain:**
- **Australopithecus** (5.5–1.5 million years ago): Bipedal, small brain (~400 cc) — proto-human
- **Homo habilis** (2–1.5 million years ago): First true human; first stone tools; brain ~500–700 cc
- **Homo erectus** (1.8–1.6 million years ago): Made hand axes; **discovered fire**; brain ~800–1,200 cc; migrated out of Africa to Asia
- **Homo sapiens** (230,000–30,000 years ago): Neanderthal type; brain ~1,200–1,800 cc
- **Homo sapiens sapiens** (115,000 years ago – present): Fully modern; complex tools; language ~50,000 BC

**Early Man in India:**
- **Narmada Man (Hathnora, MP)**: Discovered 1982; archaic Homo sapiens skull — most significant find in India proper
- **Fa Hien Cave, Sri Lanka**: Homo sapiens sapiens fossils dated ~34,000 years ago

## 2. The Palaeolithic Age (Old Stone Age)

### Lower Palaeolithic (600,000 – 150,000 BC)

- **Characteristic tools**: Hand axes, cleavers, choppers — large, bifacially worked
- **Raw material**: Quartzite
- **Economy**: Food gathering, hunting, cave/rock shelter dwelling
- **Key sites**:
  - **Bori, Maharashtra**: Earliest Lower Palaeolithic site in India
  - **Belan Valley, UP**: Rich stone tools + Middle and Upper Palaeolithic layers
  - **Didwana, Rajasthan**: All three Palaeolithic phases found at same site
  - **Nagarjunakonda, AP**: Important Lower Palaeolithic site in AP

### Middle Palaeolithic (150,000 – 35,000 BC)

- **Characteristic tools**: Flakes — blades, points, borers, scrapers (smaller, lighter than Lower Palaeolithic)
- Technology shifted from large bifacial tools to small flakes struck from a prepared core
- Key sites along Narmada river and south of Tungabhadra

### Upper Palaeolithic (35,000 – 10,000 BC)

- **Characteristic tools**: Blades and **burins** (chisel-like engraving tools)
- **566 Upper Palaeolithic sites** identified in India
- Found in AP, Karnataka, Maharashtra, central MP, southern UP
- **Bhimbetka (MP)**: Most important rock art site — paintings from Upper Palaeolithic through Mesolithic; 500+ painted rock shelters in 10 sq km

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** **Bhimbetka rock shelters** (45 km south of Bhopal) are India's greatest prehistoric art site. The paintings show overwhelmingly animals — this is **hunting magic**: prehistoric people painted what they hunted to symbolically control it. Only food animals appear; grain-eating birds do not. This directly links the art to a hunting economy. Bhimbetka is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## 3. The Mesolithic Age (9,000 – 4,000 BC)

**Transition period**: End of Ice Age (warm, rainy climate) → new animal populations → new opportunities

- **Characteristic tools**: **Microliths** — tiny, geometrically shaped blades hafted onto wood/bone to make composite tools (saws, arrows, sickles)
- **Economy**: Still hunting + fishing + gathering, but **animal domestication begins** (new)
- Sites concentrated in **Rajasthan, southern UP, central and eastern India**, south of Krishna river

### Key Mesolithic Sites

| Site | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Bagor, Rajasthan** | Most extensively excavated Mesolithic site in India; 5,000+ years of continuous occupation |
| **Adamgarh, MP** | Together with Bagor, provides **earliest evidence of animal domestication** in India (~5,000 BC) |
| **Sambhar, Rajasthan** | Evidence suggesting **plant cultivation begun by 7,000–6,000 BC** |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** Animal domestication in India began in the **Mesolithic Age** (~5,000 BC) at Bagor and Adamgarh — BEFORE the full Neolithic revolution. This is an important nuance: domestication predates settled agriculture.

---

## 4. The Neolithic Age (7,000 – 1,000 BC)

The **Neolithic Revolution** = combination of **agriculture + animal domestication** = food-producing economy = settled village life.

- **Characteristic tools**: **Polished stone axes** (distinguishes from Palaeolithic's unpolished tools)
- Began in western Asia ~9,000 BC; India's earliest site: **Mehrgarh** (~7,000 BC); South India: ~2,500 BC

### Three Regional Groups

| Group | Location | Distinctive Axe |
|---|---|---|
| **North-Western** | Baluchistan, Kashmir | Rectangular with curved cutting edge |
| **North-Eastern** | Assam hills, Vindhyas, Bihar, UP | Rectangular butt; occasional shouldered hoes |
| **Southern** | Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, **AP** | Oval sides, **pointed butt** |

### Key Neolithic Sites

| Site | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| **Mehrgarh, Baluchistan** | Oldest known Neolithic site (~7,000 BC); wheat + barley + cattle; the "bread basket" |
| **Burzahom, Kashmir** | Pit dwellings; bone tools; coarse grey pottery; dogs buried with masters |
| **Gufkral, Kashmir** | Agriculture + animal husbandry; 41 km SW of Srinagar |
| **Chirand, Bihar** | 40 km west of Patna; antler bone implements; 4-river confluence site; ~2,000 BC |
| **Koldihwa + Mahagra, Allahabad** | **Rice cultivation evidence from 5th millennium BC** — among world's earliest |
| **Piklihal, Karnataka** | Cattle-herders; seasonal camps → ash mounds (characteristic of south Indian Neolithic) |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** South India has the **largest number of Neolithic settlements** in India — over 850, spread across AP, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu. This is because stone (needed for tools) was abundant in the Deccan. South Indian Neolithic people settled on **granite hilltops** and cultivated **ragi and horse gram** — not wheat and barley (which were the Mehrgarh crops). Different regions, different crops: this regional variation is a key exam point.

**Pottery**: First appeared in Neolithic age (storage/cooking need for agricultural communities). Types: black-burnished ware, grey ware, mat-impressed ware.

## 5. The Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Phase) (c. 3,000 – 700 BC)

**Chalcolithic** = Greek *chalcos* (copper) + *lithos* (stone) = **Copper-Stone Phase**

- First metal used by humans was copper
- Used alongside stone; occasionally low-grade bronze; rural communities (NOT urban)
- Concentrated in south-eastern Rajasthan, western MP, western Maharashtra, eastern India

### Major Chalcolithic Cultures

| Site / Culture | Location | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Ahar (Tambavati)** | Banas valley, SE Rajasthan | Original name = "place with copper"; copper smelting from the start; ~2,100–1,500 BC |
| **Malwa Culture (Kayatha, Eran)** | Western MP | **Malwa ware** = richest Chalcolithic pottery |
| **Jorwe Culture** | Western Maharashtra | ~1,400–700 BC; Daimabad (20 ha, ~4,000 people) + Inamgaon |
| **Navdatoli** | Narmada valley, MP | **Widest variety of food crops at any prehistoric site** (wheat, rice, lentil, cotton, bajra, millets) |
| **Ganeshwar** | Khetri copper belt, Rajasthan | Pre-Harappan (~2,800–2,200 BC); copper supplied to Harappa |
| **Pandu Rajar Dhibi** | West Bengal | Eastern Chalcolithic; ~1,500–700 BC |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **Inamgaon** (Pune district) is the most important Chalcolithic site for social evidence. A 5-room central house is believed to be the **chief's residence**. Craftsmen lived on the western fringe; common people in round huts; chief in rectangular house. This is the **earliest evidence of social stratification** in peninsular India visible in the settlement pattern.

### Chalcolithic Characteristics

**Pottery**: **Black-and-Red Ware** — most distinctive; wheel-thrown; painted with white linear designs; found from 2,000 BC across Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra, Bihar, West Bengal

**Burial**: Bodies buried under floors of own houses (Maharashtra); north-to-south orientation; pots and copper objects in graves = belief in afterlife

**Religion**: Mother goddess (terracotta figurines); bull cult in Malwa/Rajasthan

**Social Inequality**: Copper-based necklaces in children's graves at Chandoli/Nevasa show wealth differences even in childhood

### Copper Hoards

- Over **80 copper hoards** found across India (West Bengal to Gujarat; AP to UP)
- Contain rings, celts, hatchets, swords, harpoons, **anthropomorphic (human-shaped) copper figures**
- Largest hoard: **Gungeria, MP** — 424 copper tools/weapons + 102 silver objects
- **OCP (Ochre-Coloured Pottery) Culture** (~2,000–1,500 BC): associated with copper hoards in Ganga-Yamuna doab

### Chalcolithic Limitations

1. **Did not milk cattle** — slaughtered for food (could not use cattle as continuous resource)
2. **No iron plough** — only digging sticks; could not till hard black cotton soil
3. **No bronze making** — knew copper but not copper+tin alloy (which built Harappan civilization)
4. **No writing, no cities** — pre-urban, pre-literate

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** Chalcolithic cultures ultimately failed to become urban because **they couldn't make bronze**. Bronze = copper + tin. The Harappan civilization had tin (imported from Afghanistan) and could make bronze. Chalcolithic cultures only had copper. This single technological gap — the ability to create alloy metal — is what separated Bronze Age urban civilization (Harappa) from Chalcolithic rural cultures. The same agricultural land, the same crops — but one group built cities and the other didn't.

## Quick Revision — High-Frequency APPSC Facts

**Palaeolithic Sites:**
- Lower: **Bori (Maharashtra)** = earliest; **Nagarjunakonda (AP)**
- All three phases at same site: **Didwana (Rajasthan)** and **Belan Valley (UP)**
- Rock art: **Bhimbetka** (Vindhya Range, 45 km south of Bhopal)

**Mesolithic:**
- Tools: **Microliths** (tiny, geometric, composite)
- Animal domestication: **Bagor + Adamgarh** (~5,000 BC)
- Plant cultivation: **Sambhar** (~7,000–6,000 BC)

**Neolithic:**
- Earliest site: **Mehrgarh** (~7,000 BC)
- Largest number of sites: **South India** (850+ sites; AP, Karnataka, TN)
- Rice (earliest): **Koldihwa + Mahagra** (5th millennium BC)
- Unique: Dogs buried with masters — **Burzahom, Kashmir**
- Ash mounds: South Indian Neolithic (e.g., **Piklihal**)

**Chalcolithic:**
- Tools: Stone + Copper
- Pottery: **Black-and-Red Ware**
- Chief's residence evidence: **Inamgaon** (Pune)
- Largest copper hoard: **Gungeria, MP** (424 copper + 102 silver)
- OCP Culture: ~2,000–1,500 BC (Ganga-Yamuna doab)
- Jorwe culture (Maharashtra): lasted until **700 BC** (latest Chalcolithic)
