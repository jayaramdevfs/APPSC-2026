# AP Administration — Structure, Institutions & Programmes

> *The Gram Panchayat is where government becomes real — where the abstraction of the Constitution meets the concrete need of a family that cannot access a ration card. AP has 13,000 gram panchayats. Each one is a government. Each is the state's last point of contact with the citizen.*

## The Big Picture

AP's administrative structure post-2014 bifurcation has had to rebuild from scratch — a new capital (Amaravati), a relocated secretariat, a reconstituted cadre, and entirely new institutions. The administrative challenge of running a state without its former capital city (Hyderabad, shared with Telangana until 2024) has defined AP's governance story since 2014.

For APPSC, the key areas are: AP's government structure (Governor, CM, Secretariat), APPSC, the three-tier Panchayati Raj system with AP-specific terminology, key welfare schemes (SERP, MEPMA, Amma Vodi, YSR Rythu Bharosa), and AP's police/revenue administration structure.

## 1. AP Government Structure

### Constitutional Framework

| Office | Current Context (AP post-2014) |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| **Governor** | Raj Bhavan, Vijayawada; appointed by President; acts as constitutional head |
| **Chief Minister** | Leader of ruling party in AP Legislative Assembly |
| **Council of Ministers** | CM + Cabinet Ministers + Ministers of State |
| **State Legislature** | **AP is bicameral** — Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) + Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) |
| **AP High Court** | Located at **Amaravati** (moved from Hyderabad post-bifurcation) |

**AP Legislature:**
- **AP Legislative Assembly**: 175 seats (constituencies); 5-year term
- **AP Legislative Council**: Upper house; 58 seats; members from various constituencies
- AP is one of six states with a Legislative Council (others: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Bihar, Telangana)

> 📌 **Exam Note:** AP is **bicameral** — it has both Vidhan Sabha AND Vidhan Parishad. After bifurcation, Telangana initially had only a Vidhan Sabha (unicameral) but later created its own Legislative Council. The AP High Court was established at **Amaravati** — not Vijayawada. AP had to share Hyderabad High Court with Telangana until the new HC was set up.

## 2. AP Secretariat

**Location:** **Velagapudi, Amaravati** (near Guntur; relocated from Hyderabad in 2017)

### Structure

```
Chief Minister / Governor
    ↓
Chief Secretary (head of civil services; senior-most IAS)
    ↓
Principal Secretaries / Special Chief Secretaries (per department)
    ↓
Secretaries, Additional Secretaries, Joint Secretaries
    ↓
Under Secretaries / Section Officers
```

**Key Departments of AP Secretariat:**
- Revenue & Disaster Management
- Finance
- Agriculture & Cooperation
- Social Welfare (SC, ST, BC welfare)
- Municipal Administration & Urban Development
- Panchayati Raj & Rural Development
- School Education; Higher Education
- Energy; Irrigation; Roads & Buildings
- Home (Police)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Headquarters** | **Vijayawada** |
| Constitutional basis | Article 315 of Constitution |

| Key examinations | Group 1, Group 2, Group 3, Group 4 |

## 4. Revenue Administration in AP

### Hierarchy

```
State Level: Revenue Principal Secretary / Commissioner of Land Administration (CLA)
    ↓
District Level: Collector & District Magistrate (DM)
    ↓
Revenue Division: Revenue Divisional Officer (RDO) / Sub-Collector
    ↓
Mandal Level: Mandal Revenue Officer (MRO) / Tahsildar
    ↓
Village Level: Village Revenue Officer (VRO) / Village Revenue Assistant (VRA)
```

**Key roles:**

| Officer | Key Functions |
|---------|--------------|
| **Collector** | Land revenue; law & order; elections; disaster management; development coordination |
| **RDO / Sub-Collector** | Appeals in revenue matters; magisterial duties; subdivision development |
| **MRO (Mandal Revenue Officer)** | Land mutations; births/deaths records; welfare certificates (caste, income, residence) |
| **VRO (Village Revenue Officer)** | Maintains land records (FMB, pattadar passbooks); revenue collection; welfare certificates |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **Pattadar Passbook** — AP's system of giving farmers a bank-account-like document showing land ownership — was pioneered in AP and later adopted by other states. Digital land records (E-Pattadar) make land ownership visible and reduce land disputes. The VRO is the key custodian of this record at village level. This is one of AP's major contributions to governance innovation.

## 5. Three-Tier Panchayati Raj in AP

### Structure (post-73rd Amendment)

| Tier | Institution | Level | Key Official |
|------|-------------|-------|-------------|
| **Village** | **Gram Panchayat (GP)** | Village | Sarpanch (elected); Panchayat Secretary (appointed) |
| **Mandal** | **Mandal Praja Parishad (MPP)** | Mandal (block) | MPP President (elected); MPP CEO (IAS/APAS) |
| **District** | **Zilla Parishad (ZP)** | District | ZP Chairperson (elected); ZP CEO (IAS) |

**Numbers (approx.):**
- Gram Panchayats in AP: ~**13,000**
- Mandals in AP: ~**670** mandals
- Districts in AP: **26 districts** (after reorganization in 2022; was 13 before)

> 📌 **Exam Note:** AP was reorganized from **13 to 26 districts** in April 2022 (under YSRCP government). This major administrative change means the older "13 districts" figure is now outdated. Current exam papers use **26 districts**. Some old MCQs still say 13 — watch the year context of the question.

### Key Panchayat Functionaries

| Functionary | Role |
|-------------|------|
| **Sarpanch** | Elected head of Gram Panchayat; chairs GP meetings |
| **Upa-Sarpanch** | Deputy Sarpanch; elected by ward members |
| **Ward Members** | Elected representatives of each ward in the GP |
| **Panchayat Secretary** | Government-appointed; manages records, accounts, schemes |
| **MPTC (Mandal Praja Territorial Constituency)** | Elected members of MPP |
| **ZPTC (Zilla Praja Territorial Constituency)** | Elected members of Zilla Parishad |

## 7. Key AP Welfare Programmes

### Rural Development & Welfare

| Programme | Full Name | Focus |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| **SERP** | Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty | Self-help groups (SHGs) for rural women; credit, livelihoods, financial inclusion |
| **MEPMA** | Mission for Elimination of Poverty in Municipal Areas | Urban equivalent of SERP; urban SHGs; slum poverty |
| **DWCRA** | Development of Women and Children in Rural Areas | Earlier SHG programme (predecessor to SERP) |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** **SERP (Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty)** was created under the World Bank-aided **Velugu** / **Indira Kranthi Patham** programme in AP in 2000. It federated SHGs into **Village Organizations (VOs)** → **Mandal Samakhyas (MS)** → **Zilla Samakhyas (ZS)**. This three-tier SHG federation model became the template for **NRLM (National Rural Livelihoods Mission)** under the Central Government. AP's SHG movement is globally cited as a success model.

### Key YSR-era Schemes (post-2019)

| Scheme | Benefit |
|--------|---------|
| **YSR Rythu Bharosa** | ₹13,500/year to farmers; direct benefit transfer (landowner + tenant farmers) |
| **Amma Vodi** | ₹15,000/year to mothers of school-going children (to incentivize enrollment) |
| **YSR Arogyasri** | State health insurance; cashless treatment at empanelled hospitals |
| **YSR Pension Kanuka** | Pension to elderly, disabled, widows, fisherfolk |
| **Jagananna Thodu** | Working capital to street vendors and small traders |
| **Jagananna Vidya Deevena** | Fee reimbursement for SC/ST/BC/EWS students in technical education |
| **Navaratnalu** | 9-scheme social welfare package (2019 YSRCP manifesto commitments) |

## 8. AP Police Administration

```
Director General of Police (DGP) — State Police Head
    ↓
Inspector General of Police (IGP) — Range level
    ↓
Deputy Inspector General (DIG) — Range/Commissionerate
    ↓
Superintendent of Police (SP) — District
    ↓
Deputy SP / Circle Inspector
    ↓
Sub-Inspector (SI) → Assistant SI (ASI) → Constable
```

**Special Units:**
- **Greyhounds** — AP's anti-Naxal elite commando force; considered one of India's best counter-insurgency units
- **SIT (Special Investigation Teams)** — for specific serious cases
- **ACB (Anti-Corruption Bureau)** — investigates corruption by public servants
