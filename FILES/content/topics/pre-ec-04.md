# Financial Institutions & Fiscal Policy

> *The RBI is India's financial spine — it manages the currency, regulates banks, and balances growth against inflation. Understanding its tools is understanding how India's economy is governed day-to-day.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims Economy | G2 Screening Economy | G1 Mains Paper 3 | G2 Paper 2

## The Big Picture

India's financial system has three pillars: the **banking system** (RBI at the apex, commercial banks as the backbone), the **capital markets** (SEBI-regulated stock markets, mutual funds, insurance), and **fiscal policy** (government revenue and spending managed by the Finance Ministry). All three must work in coordination for economic stability.

Post-1991 reforms transformed all three: banking was liberalized (private banks allowed), capital markets were given a powerful regulator (SEBI got statutory powers in 1992), and fiscal discipline was eventually mandated by law (FRBM Act 2003). For APPSC, the most-tested elements are the specific institutions, their establishment years, their functions, and key numbers.

## 1. Reserve Bank of India — The Central Bank

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Established** | **1 April 1935** under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 |
| **Nationalised** | **1 January 1949** (transferred from private ownership to Government of India) |
| **Headquarters** | **Mumbai** (Central Office) |
| **Governor** | Appointed by the **Central Government** (currently: Sanjay Malhotra) |
| **Deputy Governors** | 4 Deputy Governors |

### Six Core Functions of the RBI

1. **Monetary Authority**: Formulates and implements monetary policy (via the MPC — Monetary Policy Committee)
2. **Issuer of Currency**: Issues all banknotes and coins **except ₹1 note and coins** (those are issued by Ministry of Finance — Government of India)
3. **Banker to Banks**: Maintains CRR deposits of commercial banks; acts as lender of last resort
4. **Banker to Government**: Manages accounts of Union and State governments; manages public debt; issues government securities
5. **Manager of Foreign Exchange**: Manages forex reserves; regulates forex transactions under **FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999)**
6. **Regulator of Banking System**: Issues banking licences; conducts inspection and supervision

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** **₹1 coins and ₹1 notes** are issued by the **Government of India (Ministry of Finance)**, NOT by the RBI. The RBI issues all other denominations (₹2, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500, ₹2,000). ₹2,000 notes were withdrawn from circulation in 2023 (but remain legal tender).

### Key Banking Committees

| Committee | Year | Key Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Narasimham I** | 1991 | 4-tier banking structure; reduce SLR/CRR; deregulate interest rates |
| **Narasimham II** | 1998 | NPA management; capital adequacy norms; bank mergers for stronger institutions |
| **Raghuram Rajan Committee** | 2008 | Financial sector reforms in global context; financial inclusion |
| **P.J. Nayak Committee** | 2014 | Bank governance reforms; recommended reducing government holding in PSBs below 50% |

## 2. Banking Structure in India

India's banking system is diverse — from giant nationalized banks to specialized small finance banks:

| Type | Examples | Regulator |
|---|---|---|
| **Public Sector Banks (PSBs)** | SBI, Punjab National Bank, Bank of Baroda (12 PSBs after mergers) | RBI |
| **Private Sector Banks** | HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Kotak Mahindra, Axis | RBI |
| **Foreign Banks** | Citibank, Standard Chartered, HSBC | RBI |
| **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)** | Andhra Pradesh Grameena Vikas Bank, etc. | RBI + NABARD |
| **Small Finance Banks** | AU Small Finance, Ujjivan, Jana | RBI |
| **Payments Banks** | Paytm Payments Bank, Airtel Payments Bank | RBI |
| **Cooperative Banks** | Urban + Rural cooperative credit societies | RBI + State Registrar |

**Key Banking Terms:**

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **NPA** (Non-Performing Asset) | Loan where interest/principal unpaid for **90+ days** | India's twin balance sheet problem; major drag on credit growth |
| **IBC** (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016) | Time-bound resolution for NPAs — 180 days + 90 day extension | Replaced slow court-based recovery; NCLT adjudicates |
| **SARFAESI Act (2002)** | Banks can seize and auction defaulters' assets without court intervention | Strengthens banks' hand in NPA recovery |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's **NPA crisis** (2013–2018) was caused by large corporate loans from the 2008–12 investment boom turning bad. At its peak, gross NPAs were ~12% of total bank credit. The IBC 2016 and a series of RBI clean-up directives helped reduce NPAs to ~3.5% by 2024. This recovery is a significant achievement.

## 3. Capital Markets

### Stock Exchanges

| Exchange | Established | Index | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BSE** (Bombay Stock Exchange) | **1875** | **SENSEX** (30 companies) | Oldest stock exchange in Asia |
| **NSE** (National Stock Exchange) | **1992** | **NIFTY 50** (50 companies) | Largest by trading volume in India |

**SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India):**
- Established as advisory body: 1988
- Became **statutory regulatory authority: 1992** (SEBI Act 1992)
- **Headquarters**: Mumbai
- Functions: Regulates IPOs (primary market), stock exchanges (secondary market), mutual funds, FIIs, credit rating agencies

> 📌 **Exam Trap:** SEBI existed since **1988** as a non-statutory advisory body. It became a **statutory authority** only in **1992**. Questions often state "SEBI was established in 1988 as a statutory body" — this is **wrong**. 1988 = formed. 1992 = statutory power.

### Financial Instruments

| Instrument | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity/Shares** | Ownership in company | Highest risk, highest potential return |
| **Bonds/Debentures** | Debt instruments | Fixed return; company's obligation |
| **Mutual Funds** | Pooled investment managed by AMCs | NAV (Net Asset Value) based pricing |
| **G-Secs** (Government Securities) | Sovereign debt | Risk-free; RBI manages auctions |
| **T-Bills** | Short-term G-Secs (91, 182, 364 days) | Money market instrument |

## 4. Fiscal Policy and Union Budget

### Budget Classification

| Type | Components |
|---|---|
| **Revenue Budget** | Revenue Receipts (taxes + non-tax) vs. Revenue Expenditure (salaries, subsidies, interest payments) |
| **Capital Budget** | Capital Receipts (borrowings, disinvestment proceeds) vs. Capital Expenditure (infrastructure, loans given to states) |

### Deficit Indicators (Most-Tested)

| Deficit | Formula | What It Tells |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue Deficit** | Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts | Government using borrowed money for current consumption (salaries, subsidies) — problematic |
| **Fiscal Deficit** | Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excl. borrowings) | Total borrowing requirement; the headline deficit number |
| **Primary Deficit** | Fiscal Deficit − Net Interest Payments | Shows whether new deficit is being created, separate from past debt burden |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** **Fiscal Deficit = Government's total borrowing requirement.** India's fiscal deficit was ~5.8% of GDP in 2020–21 (COVID year), brought down to ~5.6% in 2022–23, and targeted at ~4.9% in 2024–25. The FRBM Act 2003 originally targeted 3%, but this has been repeatedly missed. The **N.K. Singh Committee (2017)** recommended replacing the 3% fiscal deficit target with a **60% debt-to-GDP ratio target** as the anchor.

### FRBM Act, 2003

- **Goal**: Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management — enforce discipline
- **Target**: Fiscal Deficit ≤ 3% of GDP; Revenue Deficit = 0%
- **Escape Clause**: Government can deviate in "exceptional circumstances" (COVID, global recession)
- **N.K. Singh Committee Review (2017)**: Recommended fiscal council; debt-to-GDP target (Centre 40% + States 20% = 60%); medium-term fiscal consolidation

## 5. Key Financial Institutions (Development Finance)

| Institution | Year | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **NABARD** (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) | **1982** | Apex development bank for agriculture + rural development; refinances rural credit; supervises RRBs and cooperative banks |
| **SIDBI** (Small Industries Development Bank) | **1990** | Development finance for MSMEs; refinances banks lending to small industries |
| **NHB** (National Housing Bank) | **1988** | Apex body for housing finance; promotes housing finance institutions |
| **EXIM Bank** | **1982** | Finances and facilitates India's foreign trade; export credit |
| **MUDRA** (Micro Units Development & Refinance Agency) | **2015** | Microfinance — loans up to ₹20 lakh for micro enterprises |

**MUDRA Loan Categories:**
| Category | Loan Range |
|---|---|
| **Shishu** | Up to ₹50,000 |
| **Kishore** | ₹50,001 – ₹5 lakh |
| **Tarun** | ₹5 lakh – ₹10 lakh |
| **Tarun Plus** (added 2024) | ₹10 lakh – ₹20 lakh |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **NABARD** (1982) supervises **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)** and cooperative banks at the state level, while RBI is the apex regulator. NABARD is headquartered in **Mumbai**. It also manages the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) — a major source of rural investment credit.

## 6. Insurance Sector

| Body | Type | Year | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IRDAI** | Statutory Regulatory Body | **1999** | Regulates and develops insurance industry; protects policyholders |
| **LIC** | Public Sector | 1956 | Largest life insurer; IPO in 2022 (partial disinvestment) |
| **GIC** | Public Sector | 1972 | **Sole national reinsurer**; state-owned |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** **IRDAI is headquartered in Hyderabad** — one of the few national-level financial regulators outside Mumbai or Delhi. This is a frequently tested AP-specific fact. IRDAI was set up on the recommendation of the **Malhotra Committee (1993)** which recommended opening insurance to private and foreign players.

**Crop Insurance:**
- **PM Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), 2016**: Comprehensive crop insurance; premium: 2% (Kharif), 1.5% (Rabi), 5% (horticulture/commercial); rest of premium paid by Centre and State governments
