# Ethics in Public Administration & Governance

> *A civil servant who takes a bribe is not merely breaking a law — they are violating every citizen's trust in the idea that the state exists to serve them. Corruption is not a financial crime. It is a betrayal of the social contract.*

**Covers:** G1 Mains Paper 3 (Ethics, Integrity, Aptitude) | G2 Paper 3 (Governance & Ethics)

## The Big Picture

Ethics in public administration is about translating moral principles into institutional conduct. It is not enough for an individual official to be personally honest — the system must be designed so that honest behavior is rewarded, corrupt behavior is detected and punished, and citizens have tools to hold officials accountable. The two key instruments India has built for this are: the **RTI Act (transparency)** and the **Prevention of Corruption Act (accountability)**.

For APPSC, the key areas are: probity and integrity in governance, RTI Act provisions, the 2nd ARC's ethics framework, conflict of interest, whistleblower protection, and the Code of Conduct for civil servants. Case studies require applying these concepts to realistic scenarios.

## 1. Probity and Integrity in Governance

**Probity**: Complete and confirmed integrity; uprightness; honesty in all public conduct.
**Integrity**: Adherence to moral principles; internal consistency between values and actions.

### The Nolan Principles (Seven Principles of Public Life — UK, 1995)

Though British in origin, these principles are widely applied and tested in Indian civil services ethics:

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Selflessness** | Act only in the public interest; not for personal gain |
| **Integrity** | No financial or other obligation to outside individuals/organisations |
| **Objectivity** | Make choices based on merit only (appointments, contracts, recommendations) |
| **Accountability** | Subject to public scrutiny; cooperate with appropriate oversight |
| **Openness** | Transparent about decisions and actions; restrict only where justified |
| **Honesty** | Declare private interests; never deceive |
| **Leadership** | Promote and support these principles through leadership and example |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Nolan Principles** are **7 in number** (often tested as 5, 6, 7, or 8). The easiest way to remember them: **SIOAOHL** — or think of the sequence Selflessness → Integrity → Objectivity → Accountability → Openness → Honesty → Leadership.

## 2. Code of Conduct for Civil Servants

### All India Services (Conduct) Rules, 1968

The AIS (Conduct) Rules govern IAS, IPS, and IFoS officers:

**Key provisions:**

| Rule | Content |
|------|---------|
| No private employment | Cannot engage in trade, business, or profession outside service |
| Political neutrality | Cannot participate in politics or election campaigning |
| No criticism of government | Cannot make public statements criticizing government policy |
| Gifts | Cannot accept gifts above ₹25,000 from close relatives; much lower limits from strangers |
| Property declaration | Must declare assets annually |
| No lending/borrowing | Cannot borrow from subordinates or people under official influence |
| Outside employment | Spouse's business activities require intimation if creating conflict of interest |

### Central Civil Services (Conduct) Rules, 1964

Applicable to most central government employees:
- Similar provisions on gifts, outside employment, property declaration
- Key addition: **Social media guidance** — cannot share official information or make statements that could embarrass the government

## 3. Conflict of Interest

**Definition**: A conflict of interest arises when a public official's **personal interests** (financial, familial, or other) could **improperly influence** their official decisions.

### Types

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| **Financial conflict** | Officer awards contract to company in which they hold shares |
| **Familial conflict** | IAS officer approves application of spouse's business |
| **Post-employment** | Officer who joins private sector after retirement — "revolving door" conflict |
| **Information asymmetry** | Officer uses insider government information for personal financial gain |

### Managing Conflict of Interest

1. **Declaration**: Declare interests to superior; recuse from that decision
2. **Recusal**: Withdraw from the decision-making process
3. **Divestiture**: Sell the conflicting asset
4. **Blind trust**: Assets managed independently during tenure

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The **most common conflict of interest in Indian administration** is the post-retirement "revolving door" — senior officers joining the regulated industries or companies they previously oversaw. The **Cooling-Off Period** (typically 1 year prohibition on joining private entities related to previous role) exists to prevent this but is weakly enforced. The 2nd ARC recommended strengthening cooling-off provisions and creating an Independent Ethics Commission.

## 4. Right to Information Act, 2005

**RTI Act 2005** is India's most powerful transparency tool:

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Enacted** | **October 12, 2005** (came into force) |
| **Purpose** | Empower citizens to access information from public authorities |
| **Applicability** | All bodies under the Constitution or substantially government-funded |
| **Time limit** | **30 days** to respond (10 days if life/liberty is involved) |
| **First appeal** | To designated First Appellate Authority within department |
| **Second appeal** | To **Central Information Commission (CIC)** or **State Information Commission (SIC)** |
| **Fees** | ₹10 application fee; ₹2/page for copies; BPL applicants exempt |

**Exemptions under Section 8 (Information NOT required to be given):**
- National security and sovereignty
- Cabinet papers before decisions
- Commercial confidence/trade secrets
- Personal information with no public interest
- Information given in fiduciary relationship
- Information whose disclosure would endanger life or safety

> 📌 **Exam Note:** RTI applies to **all public authorities** — including high courts, Parliament (not internal proceedings), political parties (controversial but CIC has ruled in favor), private bodies receiving substantial government funding. **File notings** (how decisions were made) ARE covered by RTI. The **CIC** (Central Information Commission) — NOT CVC (Central Vigilance Commission) — handles central government second appeals.

### RTI and AP

- **AP Information Commission (APIC)** handles second appeals for AP government matters
- AP was one of the early states to implement transparency legislation before the central Act

## 5. Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (Amended 2018)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Original Act** | Prevention of Corruption Act, **1988** |
| **Amendment** | Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, **2018** — significant changes |
| **Key provision (2018 amendment)** | **Bribe-givers** are now also liable (previously only bribe-takers); exception for "coercion" victims who report within 7 days |
| **Corruption types** | Taking gratification, misuse of position, possession of disproportionate assets |
| **Disproportionate assets** | Presumption of guilt if assets exceed known income sources |
| **Prior sanction** | Prosecution of serving officers requires prior sanction from competent authority |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **2018 amendment** to the PC Act made **bribe-giving** an offense for the first time. Previously, only the public servant could be prosecuted for receiving bribes. The amendment was driven by India's UNCAC (UN Convention Against Corruption) obligations. Exception: a person coerced into paying a bribe is not guilty IF they report to police within **7 days**.

## 6. Whistleblower Protection

**Whistle-blowers Protection Act, 2014** (Passed but not fully notified in parts):

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Protect persons who disclose corruption or willful misuse of power |
| **Complaint to** | Competent Authority — Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) for central government; State Vigilance Commission for state |
| **Identity protection** | Identity of complainant to be kept secret |
| **Prohibition** | Victimization of whistleblower is an offense |
| **Limitation** | Does NOT protect disclosure of information related to national security, sovereign matters |

**Famous Whistleblower Cases in India:**
- **Satyendra Dubey** — IIT/IIM engineer; wrote to PM about corruption in NHAI Golden Quadrilateral; killed 2003
- **Shanmughan Manjunath** — Indian Oil officer; killed after sealing corrupt petrol pumps (Uttar Pradesh, 2005)

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** Both Dubey and Manjunath died before India had formal whistleblower protection legislation. Their deaths provided the moral urgency that drove the Whistle-blowers Protection Act 2014. The gap between their murders and the Act's passage — **11 years** — illustrates how slowly India institutionalizes ethical frameworks even when the need is obvious.

## 7. Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2nd ARC)

The **2nd ARC** (2005–2009), chaired by **Veerappa Moily**, produced 15 reports covering all aspects of governance reform:

**Key Ethics Recommendations (4th Report — Ethics in Governance, 2007):**

| Recommendation | Content |
|----------------|---------|
| **Ethics Commission** | Create an independent Ethics Commission for Parliament |
| **Lok Pal** | Independent Ombudsman at national level (implemented 2019) |
| **Lok Ayukta** | Ombudsman at state level; mandatory for all states |
| **Cooling-off period** | Civil servants should not join private entities in same sector for 2 years after retirement |
| **Declaration of assets** | Annual assets declaration by all public servants |
| **Citizens' Charter** | Legally enforceable service delivery commitments |
| **Social Audit** | Community-based audit of government programs |

**Other key 2nd ARC Reports:**
- **12th Report: Citizen Centric Administration** — RTI, Citizen Charter
- **13th Report: Organisational Structure** — Administrative reform
- **10th Report: Refurbishing of Personnel Administration** — Civil service reform

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013** was eventually enacted — implementing a long-standing 2nd ARC (and 1st ARC, 1966) recommendation. The first Lokpal was appointed in 2019. AP's Lok Ayukta is a state ombudsman that investigates corruption complaints against state officials.

## 8. Case Study Approach — How to Answer Ethics Questions

APPSC mains ethics case studies require structured analysis:

### Framework for Case Studies

**Step 1: Identify stakeholders** — who is affected?

**Step 2: Identify the ethical issues** — conflict of duty, corruption, fairness, loyalty, public interest vs. personal interest

**Step 3: Identify options** — what can the official do?

**Step 4: Evaluate each option** — using ethical theories (consequences, duty, virtue)

**Step 5: Choose and justify** — best option with reasons; acknowledge trade-offs

### Common Ethical Dilemmas in Civil Services

| Dilemma | Ethical Tension |
|---------|----------------|
| Following orders vs. conscience | Duty to superiors vs. moral duty |
| Loyalty to government vs. public interest | Institutional loyalty vs. citizen welfare |
| Rule-following vs. compassion | Legal correctness vs. human outcomes |
| Disclosure of wrongdoing vs. confidentiality | Whistleblowing vs. official secrecy |
| Efficiency vs. equity | Utilitarian calculus vs. rights-based protection |
