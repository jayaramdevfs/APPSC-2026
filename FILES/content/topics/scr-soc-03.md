# Welfare Mechanism in India

> *The Indian state made a promise in 1950: that independence would mean something for the poorest citizen. The welfare state is that promise in institutional form.*

**Covers:** G2 Screening Section 3 | Welfare Mechanism | G1 Mains Paper 3
**Sources:** Indian Constitution, AP SCERT Economics, Government Schemes 2025–26

---

## The Big Picture

The Indian welfare state rests on three foundations: the Constitution's Directive Principles, which mandate the state to secure a social order for people's welfare; legislative provisions that translate rights into enforceable entitlements; and government schemes that deliver benefits on the ground.

Understanding welfare mechanisms for APPSC means more than listing schemes. It means knowing the constitutional basis for welfare (which Article mandates what), the statutory framework (which Act creates which commission or benefit), and the current scheme names and numbers (₹6,000 under PM-KISAN, 100 days under MGNREGA).

Three categories of beneficiaries structure this note: **SCs and STs** (protected by Articles 17, 46, 330–338A, Atrocities Act); **minorities and backward classes** (Articles 29–30, 16(4), 338B); and **women, disabled, and children** (Articles 15(3), 21A, 24, 42).

## 1. Public Policies and Welfare Programmes

### National Level Flagship Schemes

India's welfare architecture since 1991 has shifted toward direct benefit transfers, rights-based entitlements, and insurance mechanisms. The key schemes by sector:

**Employment and Income:**
- **MGNREGA (2005)**: Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act. Guarantees **100 days of wage employment** per year to every rural household. Legal right, not charity — the state must provide work or pay unemployment allowance. Largest public works programme in the world.
- **PM-KISAN**: Income support of **₹6,000 per year** (in three installments of ₹2,000) to all landholding farmer families. Direct bank transfer.

**Health:**
- **Ayushman Bharat — PM-JAY**: World's largest government-funded health insurance scheme. Provides **₹5 lakh per family per year** for secondary and tertiary hospitalization. Covers approximately 50 crore beneficiaries.

**Food Security:**
- **National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013**: Provides **5 kg of subsidized foodgrain per person per month** to about 67% of India's population through the PDS (Public Distribution System). Rice at ₹3/kg, wheat at ₹2/kg, coarse grains at ₹1/kg.

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** MGNREGA is not just a poverty alleviation scheme — it is a **right** under law. The 2005 Act makes it justiciable. If the government fails to provide work within 15 days of demand, the person is entitled to an unemployment allowance. This legal enforceability makes it structurally different from ordinary schemes.

### Andhra Pradesh — Navaratnalu (Nine Gems)

AP's current welfare architecture under the Navaratnalu programme covers education, health, agriculture, housing, and social security:

| Scheme | Beneficiary | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Amma Vodi** | Mothers of school-going children | ₹15,000/year to mothers to keep children in school |
| **YSR Rythu Bharosa** | Farmers (including tenant farmers) | Investment support ₹13,500/year |
| **Jagananna Vidya Deevena** | College students (BC, SC, ST, Minority, EWS) | Full fee reimbursement directly to student's account |
| **Jagananna Vasathi Deevena** | Hostel-staying students | Maintenance allowance |
| **YSR Pension Kanuka** | Elderly, widows, disabled | ₹3,000/month pension |
| **Jagananna Amma Vodi** | EWS, BC, SC, ST, Minority mothers | Education support |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** APPSC frequently asks about AP-specific scheme names and their beneficiaries. **Amma Vodi** is specifically for mothers (not farmers, not students). **Rythu Bharosa** covers both landowner farmers AND **tenant farmers** — this is significant because tenant farmers were excluded from many previous schemes including PM-KISAN.

## 2. Constitutional Provisions for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes

India's Constitution includes both prohibitions (stopping discrimination) and positive provisions (enabling upliftment). For SCs and STs:

### Fundamental Rights (Prohibition)

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 15(4)** | State can make special provisions for advancement of socially and educationally backward classes or SCs/STs |
| **Article 16(4)** | Reservation in public employment for any backward class not adequately represented |
| **Article 17** | Abolition of Untouchability — practice prohibited; its enforcement is a punishable offence |
| **Article 19(5)** | Reasonable restrictions on movement/residence to protect interests of STs (tribal areas) |

### Directive Principles (Mandatory State Duty)

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 46** | State to promote educational and economic interests of weaker sections, especially SCs and STs; protect them from social injustice and exploitation |

### Representation and Protection

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 330** | Reservation of seats for SCs and STs in Lok Sabha |
| **Article 332** | Reservation of seats for SCs and STs in State Legislative Assemblies |
| **Article 335** | Claims of SC/ST to services and posts taken into consideration (consistent with maintaining efficiency) |
| **Article 338** | National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) |
| **Article 338A** | National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) |

### Key Legislation

- **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989**: Defines specific offences against SC/ST members; establishes special courts; shifts burden of proof; includes community offences (land grabbing, denial of water sources). Amended in 2015 and 2018.

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** The 102nd Constitutional Amendment (2018) gave constitutional status to the **National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)** under **Article 338B** — a new article. Before this, NCBC was only statutory (created by an ordinary Act). This is a high-frequency question distinguishing 338 (SC), 338A (ST), and 338B (BC).

## 3. Provisions for Minorities and Backward Classes

### Minorities — Constitutional Rights

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 29** | Protection of interests of minorities — right to conserve distinct language, script, or culture; no denial of admission to any state-aided institution on grounds of religion, race, caste, or language |
| **Article 30** | Right of minorities (religious and linguistic) to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice |

**Statutory Framework:**
- **National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992**: Established NCM to safeguard rights of religious minorities (currently: Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Zoroastrians, Jains — 6 notified groups)

### Backward Classes — OBC Welfare

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 16(4)** | Reservation in public employment for any backward class not adequately represented |
| **Article 340** | President can appoint a Commission to investigate conditions of backward classes and recommend steps (basis for Kaka Kalelkar Commission 1953 and Mandal Commission 1979) |
| **Article 338B** | National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) — added by 102nd Amendment, 2018 |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The **Mandal Commission (1979)** recommended **27% reservation** for OBCs in central government services. Implementation in 1990 under V.P. Singh. The Supreme Court in **Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)** upheld this but set the **50% ceiling** on total reservations (SC 15% + ST 7.5% + OBC 27% = 49.5%). Recently, the **103rd Amendment (2019)** added 10% EWS (Economically Weaker Sections) reservation for unreserved category — making total reservations 59.5% (this 50% cap exception was upheld by SC in 2022 in the *Janhit Abhiyan* case).

## 4. Provisions for Women, Disabled, and Children

### Women's Welfare — Constitutional Provisions

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 14** | Equality before law (includes women) |
| **Article 15(3)** | **State can make special provisions for women and children** — exception to non-discrimination rule |
| **Article 39(a)** | Equal right of men and women to livelihood |
| **Article 39(d)** | Equal pay for equal work for both men and women |
| **Article 42** | Just and humane conditions of work and **maternity relief** |
| **Article 243D** | **1/3rd reservation for women** in Panchayats |
| **Article 243T** | **1/3rd reservation for women** in Urban Local Bodies |

**Key Welfare Schemes:**
- **Beti Bachao Beti Padhao**: Address declining sex ratio; improve girls' enrollment
- **Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana**: Small savings scheme for the girl child; higher interest rate
- **Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act, 2017**: Extended paid maternity leave from 12 to **26 weeks** for first 2 children

### Disabled (Divyangjan) — Rights Framework

- **RPwD (Rights of Persons with Disabilities) Act, 2016**: Replaced Persons with Disabilities Act (1995). Key changes:
  - Increased identified disabilities from 7 to **21 categories**
  - Enhanced reservation in government jobs from 3% to **4%**
  - Introduced concept of "benchmark disability" (40%+ disability)
  - Mandated accessibility standards in public buildings

### Children — Constitutional and Legislative Framework

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 21A** | **Right to free and compulsory education** (6–14 years) — added by 86th Amendment, 2002 |
| **Article 24** | **Prohibition of child labour** — no child below 14 in factories, mines, or hazardous employment |
| **Article 45** | Early childhood care and education for children below 6 years (DPSP) |
| **Article 51A(k)** | Fundamental Duty of parents/guardians to provide educational opportunities to children aged 6–14 |

**Key Schemes:**
- **ICDS (Integrated Child Development Services)**: Nutrition, health, education for children under 6 and lactating mothers through **Anganwadi centres**
- **PM Poshan (renamed from Mid-Day Meal Scheme)**: Hot cooked meals to school children — aims to improve enrollment, attendance, nutrition
- **POCSO Act, 2012**: Protection from sexual offences (covered in Social Issues note)

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** Article **21A** was added by the **86th Constitutional Amendment (2002)**, making education a **Fundamental Right** (enforceable in court). Before that, it was only a DPSP (Article 45). The **Right to Education Act (2009)** gave operational force to Article 21A — mandating free schooling, no detention policy (later reversed), and 25% RTE seats in private schools.

## Quick Revision — High-Frequency APPSC Facts

**Key Articles (Number + Content):**
- **17**: Abolish Untouchability
- **15(3)**: Special provision for women and children (allows positive discrimination)
- **21A**: Free and compulsory education 6–14 years (86th Amendment, 2002)
- **24**: No child labour under 14 in hazardous work
- **46**: Promote interests of SC/ST and weaker sections (DPSP)
- **330/332**: SC/ST reservation in Lok Sabha/State Assemblies
- **338/338A/338B**: NCSC / NCST / NCBC respectively

**Scheme Numbers (All From APPSC Questions):**
- MGNREGA: **100 days** guaranteed
- PM-KISAN: **₹6,000/year** in 3 installments
- Ayushman Bharat: **₹5 lakh/family/year**
- YSR Pension Kanuka (AP): **₹3,000/month**
- Maternity leave: **26 weeks** (amended 2017)

**Reservation Percentages:**
- SC: **15%**; ST: **7.5%**; OBC: **27%** (Mandal, 1990)
- EWS: **10%** (103rd Amendment, 2019)
- Total: 59.5% (50% cap exceeded — upheld by SC 2022)

**Key Commissions:**
- **Mandal Commission (1979)**: OBC reservation → 27% in 1990
- **Kaka Kalelkar Commission (1953)**: First backward classes commission
- **NCW (1992)**: National Commission for Women
- **NCBC**: Statutory → Constitutional via **102nd Amendment (2018)**
