## 1. The Union and its Territory (Articles 1–4)
-   **Article 1:** Describes India, that is Bharat, as a **'Union of States'** rather than a 'Federation of States'. This signifies that the Indian Federation is not the result of an agreement (like the USA) and no state has the right to secede.
-   **Article 2:** Empowers Parliament to admit or establish new states (Outside the existing union).
-   **Article 3:** Empowers Parliament to form new states or alter boundaries of **existing** states (Separation of territory, uniting states, etc.).
-   **Article 4:** Declares that laws made under Art 2 & 3 are not considered amendments under Art 368 (can be passed by simple majority).

## 2. Reorganisation of States & Andhra Pradesh Case
The demand for states on a linguistic basis was a major challenge post-independence.

-   **Dhar Commission (1948) & JVP Committee (1948):** Rejected linguistic basis; recommended administrative convenience.
-   **Formation of Andhra State (1953):** First linguistic state formed after the death of **Potti Sreeramulu** (56-day hunger strike).
    *   **Andhra State Act 1953:** Created Andhra State with **Kurnool** as capital (separated from Madras).
-   **Fazal Ali Commission (SRC 1953-55):** Accepted language as the basis but rejected "one language, one state".
-   **States Reorganisation Act, 1956:** Formed 14 states and 6 UTs.
    *   **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956):** Led to the merger of Andhra State and Hyderabad State to form **Andhra Pradesh** (Nov 1, 1956) with Hyderabad as capital.

## 3. Federal Structure of India
The Indian Constitution provides for a **Federal System with a Unitary Bias**.

### Federal Features
-   Dual Polity (Centre and States).
-   Written Constitution.
-   Division of Powers (7th Schedule: Union, State, and Concurrent Lists).
-   Supremacy and Rigidity of Constitution.
-   Independent Judiciary.

### Unitary (Non-Federal) Features
-   **Strong Centre:** Residuary powers lie with the Centre.
-   **Single Citizenship.**
-   **Appointment of Governor:** Done by the President (Centre's agent).
-   **All-India Services (IAS/IPS).**
-   **Emergency Provisions (Art 352-360):** Can convert federal structure into a unitary one.

### Centre-State Relations Commissions
-   **Sarkaria Commission (1983):** Recommended that Art 356 (President's Rule) should be used sparingly.
-   **Punchhi Commission (2007):** Emphasized "Cooperative Federalism" and warned against arbitrary removal of Governors.

## 4. Fundamental Concept: The Parliamentary System
-   **Article 74 & 75:** Parliamentary system at the Centre.
-   **Article 163 & 164:** Parliamentary system in the States.
-   **Key Principle:** The Executive is responsible to the Legislature (Collective Responsibility).
