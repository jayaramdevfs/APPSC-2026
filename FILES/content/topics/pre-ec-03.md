# Agriculture, Industry & Economic Reforms

> *India feeds 1.4 billion people, manufactures everything from steel to smartphones, and transformed its economy in 1991 — yet its farmers still struggle. The gap between agricultural GDP and agricultural employment is the defining tension of Indian development.*

## The Big Picture

Three stories define Indian economic structure: the **agriculture story** (half the workers, one-sixth of GDP, still the backbone of rural life), the **industry story** (from License Raj to global manufacturing ambitions), and the **reform story** (1991's liberalization that broke open a closed economy).

Each story connects to APPSC questions — and each has an AP angle. AP grows the world's largest chilli supply and one-third of India's fish. AP's RINL is India's only shore-based steel plant. AP's agricultural corridors, industrial parks, and port connectivity are all direct outgrowths of post-1991 policy. Understanding these connections gives you the context to answer questions — not just recite facts.

## 1. Agriculture — India's Foundation

### Key Statistics

- Agriculture contributes **~17% of GDP** but employs **~45–50% of the workforce** — a productivity gap that drives poverty
- India is **world's largest producer** of: Milk, Pulses, Jute, Spices (banana, mango, ginger by some measures)
- India is **world's 2nd largest producer** of: Rice, Wheat, Sugarcane, Cotton, Fruits & Vegetables
- India is **world's largest exporter** of: Spices, Rice

### The Green Revolution (1960s–70s)

India faced famine-level food crises in the 1960s — importing grain under the humiliating "ship-to-mouth" US PL-480 arrangement. The Green Revolution changed that:

- **M.S. Swaminathan** (called Father of Green Revolution in India) introduced High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds
- Three inputs: HYV seeds + Assured irrigation + Chemical fertilizers
- **First covered**: Punjab, Haryana, and Western UP (where irrigation was available)
- **Outcome**: India achieved food self-sufficiency by the late 1970s; transformed from food importer to exporter

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The Green Revolution created **regional disparity** (only irrigated, already-developed states benefited) and **ecological problems** (soil salinity, groundwater depletion in Punjab, fertilizer run-off). MS Swaminathan himself became a critic of its approach in later years, advocating for an "Evergreen Revolution" — productivity without ecological destruction.

### Agricultural Reforms

| Reform | Content | Current Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Land Reforms (1950s–60s)** | Zamindari abolition, Land Ceiling Acts, tenancy protection | Partially implemented; land fragmentation continues |
| **APMC Acts** | Regulated agricultural markets (mandis) — states control | States have power to reform or abolish |
| **MSP (Minimum Support Price)** | Government price floor for 23 crops; announced by CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices) | NOT a legal guarantee — only notified |
| **Farm Laws (2020)** | Three laws to open agricultural markets to corporate buyers | **Repealed November 2021** following farmer protests |

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** **MSP is NOT a legal right.** The government announces MSP for 23 crops, but there is no legal obligation for anyone (including the government's own agencies) to purchase at MSP. A farmer can only receive MSP if a government procurement agency (FCI, NAFED, etc.) is actively procuring in their area. This is the central demand of recent farmer movements — a legal guarantee of MSP.

### Irrigation Patterns

India has three distinct irrigation systems, regionally determined:

| Method | Where Dominant | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Canal Irrigation** | Punjab, Haryana, Western UP, Rajasthan | Large river systems + flat plains |
| **Tank Irrigation** | **AP, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka** | Hard Deccan rock — cannot dig deep wells easily; tanks collect rainwater |
| **Well/Borewell** | Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, MP | Accessible groundwater; but over-exploitation is severe |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **AP has the highest density of irrigation tanks in India** (~1 lakh tanks). This makes tank irrigation a high-frequency AP-specific exam fact. Polavaram (Godavari dam, national project) will add major canal irrigation — expanding from tanks to canal in coastal AP.

## 2. Industrial Development — From Planning to Liberalization

### Industrial Policy Evolution

| Policy | Year | Philosophy |
|---|---|---|
| **Industrial Policy Resolution** | 1948 | Mixed economy; 4 categories of industries (state monopoly, state control, mixed, private) |
| **Industrial Policy Resolution** | 1956 | PSUs as "commanding heights"; large-scale heavy industry as priority; called "Economic Constitution" |
| **New Industrial Policy** | 1991 | Abolished industrial licensing (except 6 industries); opened FDI; reduced public sector reservation |

### Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)

PSUs were instruments of the Nehru-era strategy to build national industrial capacity without relying on foreign private capital.

- **Navaratna** (9): Large PSUs with autonomy for capital investments up to ₹1,000 crore (examples: ONGC, BHEL, RINL/Vizag Steel)
- **Maharatna** (12): Larger PSUs with greater autonomy (examples: NTPC, ONGC, Steel Authority, Coal India)
- **Miniratna**: Smaller but profitable PSUs

**Disinvestment:**
- Government sells equity stake in PSUs
- **Strategic Disinvestment**: Management control transferred (example: Air India → Tata Group 2022)
- **DIPAM** (Department of Investment and Public Asset Management): Nodal agency for disinvestment

### Make in India and PLI Schemes

- **Make in India (2014)**: Campaign to develop India as a global manufacturing hub; 25 focus sectors (Defence, Electronics, Pharma, Automobiles, Textiles)
- **PLI (Production Linked Incentive) Schemes**: Cash incentives tied to incremental production above a base year; 14 sectors; total outlay ~₹2 lakh crore
- **MSME Sector**: Contributes ~**30% to GDP** and ~**48% to exports**; employs ~110 million; redefined by Atma Nirbhar package 2020 based on investment + turnover criteria

### Industrial Corridors

| Corridor | States Covered | AP Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **DMIC** (Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor) | UP, Rajasthan, Haryana, Gujarat, Maharashtra | Anchor project for corridor model |
| **CBIC** (Chennai-Bengaluru Industrial Corridor) | AP (Krishnapatnam node), Tamil Nadu, Karnataka | **Krishnapatnam (Nellore) is AP's node** |
| **VCIC** (Vizag-Chennai Industrial Corridor) | AP coastal belt | **AP's flagship corridor**; ports, logistics, manufacturing |

## 3. Economic Reforms Since 1991 — The Pivot

### The 1991 Crisis That Changed India

By June 1991, India's situation was dire:
- Foreign exchange reserves: **$1.2 billion** — barely **2 weeks of import cover** (minimum safe level is 3 months)
- India pledged **67 tonnes of gold** to the Bank of England and Bank of Japan as collateral for IMF loan
- The IMF conditionality required structural reforms

**Finance Minister**: **Dr. Manmohan Singh** | **Prime Minister**: P.V. Narasimha Rao

The landmark Budget of July 1991 launched the New Economic Policy:

| Pillar | Key Measures |
|---|---|
| **Liberalization** | Abolished Industrial Licensing (except 6 industries); deregulated capital markets; reduced import tariffs from 150%+ to under 20% |
| **Privatization** | PSU disinvestment; private sector allowed in telecom, banking, civil aviation |
| **Globalization** | Rupee made convertible on current account (1994); FDI and FII allowed in most sectors; WTO membership (1995) |

### Special Economic Zones (SEZs)

- **SEZ Act, 2005**: Designated zones for export-oriented industries with tax concessions, customs duty waiver, and simplified regulations
- **Key SEZs**: Kandla (Gujarat), SEEPZ (Mumbai), Noida (UP), **Vizag/Visakhapatnam (AP)**, Srikakulam (AP)
- AP has multiple SEZs focused on pharmaceuticals, seafood, and electronics

### WTO (World Trade Organisation)

- India is a **founding member (1995)**
- **Food Security Exception ("Peace Clause")**: India negotiated the right to subsidize food procurement for PDS indefinitely — opposed by rich countries who consider it trade-distorting
- India uses WTO dispute settlement to challenge US/EU trade barriers on pharmaceuticals, solar panels, steel

> 🔑 **Key Insight:** India's **WTO "food security" fight** is central to agricultural politics. India's support prices (MSP-based procurement) subsidize food for poor people through the PDS. The USA and EU call this agricultural subsidy that distorts trade. India's position: feeding 800 million food-insecure people is non-negotiable. The ongoing "peace clause" is a temporary solution — the permanent solution has not been agreed.

## 4. Industry Structure

| Category | Contribution | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Organized Sector** | PSUs + large registered companies; formal contracts, labor laws apply | ~10% of workforce |
| **Unorganized/Informal Sector** | Small/unregistered enterprises, home-based work, agriculture | **~90% of workforce** |
| **MSMEs** | ~30% of GDP, 48% of exports | 6.3 crore enterprises; largest job creator |
