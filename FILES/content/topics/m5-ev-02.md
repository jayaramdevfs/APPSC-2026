# Conservation & Natural Resources

> *AP has the world's largest baryte deposits, India's largest tiger reserve, and the Krishna-Godavari basin that powers a tenth of India's offshore gas — yet its forests recede and groundwater tables fall. Resource wealth and resource pressure coexist here.*

**Covers:** G1 Prelims Environment | G2 Screening Environment | G1 Mains Paper 3 | G2 Paper 2 & 3
**Sources:** FSI State of Forest Report 2023, CGWB Reports, Ministry of Mines, MoEFCC Conservation Reports

## The Big Picture

India is one of the world's 17 Megadiverse countries — yet it has lost more than half its original forest cover, depleted its aquifers through the world's largest groundwater extraction, and pushed dozens of species to the edge of extinction. Conservation policy in India has evolved from fortress conservation (protect forests by keeping people out) to community-based approaches (Forest Rights Act 2006, community reserves). For APPSC, the AP-specific conservation facts — Nagarjunasagar Tiger Reserve, Coringa mangroves, Pulicat Lake, Tummalapalle uranium — are high-frequency exam points.

## 1. Forest Resources

### India's Forest Cover (FSI Report 2023)

- **Total Forest & Tree Cover**: ~80 million hectares (~24% of India's geographic area)
- **Forest Types by Canopy Density:**
  - Very Dense Forest (VDF): >70% canopy
  - Moderately Dense Forest (MDF): 40–70%
  - Open Forest: 10–40%

> 📌 **Exam Note:** The legal definition of "forest" in India is based on the **1996 Supreme Court judgement (Godavarman case)** — any land recorded as "forest" in any government record is treated as forest, regardless of actual canopy cover. This broad definition has both protected forests and complicated development projects.

### Forest Types in India (Champion & Seth Classification)

| Type | Location | Key Species |
|---|---|---|
| Tropical Wet Evergreen | Western Ghats, Andaman & Nicobar | Rosewood, Dipterocarp |
| Tropical Moist Deciduous | Most of India, including AP coastal | **Sal, Teak** |
| Tropical Dry Deciduous | Central India, AP interior | **Teak, Tendu** |
| Tropical Thorn Forest | Rajasthan, Gujarat, **AP's Rayalaseema** | Acacia, Euphorbia |
| Montane & Alpine | Himalayan region | Rhododendron, Oak, above treeline |

### AP Forest Cover

- AP has significant **Eastern Ghats** forest cover
- **Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve**: **3,568 sq km** — **largest tiger reserve in India** by area
- **Papikondalu Wildlife Sanctuary**: Dense forest along the Godavari gorge

## 2. Water Resources

### India's Water — Key Facts

- India is the **world's largest extractor of groundwater** — a critical and unsustainable position
- Average annual rainfall: ~**1,170 mm/year** (but highly uneven — NE India gets 10,000 mm, Rajasthan gets 100 mm)

### Major River Systems

| System | Key Rivers | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Himalayan** | Ganga, Yamuna, Brahmaputra, Indus | Perennial (glacier-fed + rain-fed) |
| **Peninsular** | Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Mahanadi | Seasonal (rain-fed only) |

### AP's River Systems

| River | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| **Godavari** | Largest river of AP; called **"Dakshin Ganga"**; Polavaram dam under construction |
| **Krishna** | 2nd major river; Nagarjunasagar Dam and Srisailam Dam on this river |
| **Penna (Pennar)** | Flows through Kadapa, Nellore; largely seasonal |
| **Vamsadhara & Nagavali** | Northern AP (Srikakulam, Vizianagaram) |

### Water Conservation Initiatives

| Scheme | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| **Jal Jeevan Mission (2019)** | "Har Ghar Jal" — piped water to every rural household; target 2024 |
| **Jal Shakti Abhiyan** | "Catch the Rain" campaign; water conservation focus |
| **Ken-Betwa Link** | First river interlinking project to be executed (MP rivers) |
| **Watershed Management** | Treating entire catchment areas; AP's tradition of check dams |

## 3. Mineral Resources in AP

AP's mineral wealth is a high-frequency APPSC topic:

| Mineral | Location in AP | India/World Rank |
|---|---|---|
| **Baryte (Barytes)** | Cuddapah basin | **World's largest producer** |
| **Limestone** | Guntur, Kurnool, Kadapa | Abundant; basis of cement industry |
| **Uranium** | **Tummalapalle, Kadapa** | One of **India's largest deposits** |
| **Mica (Sheet Mica)** | Nellore district | Major producer |
| **Manganese** | Srikakulam district | Significant reserves |
| **Graphite** | Srikakulam, Vizianagaram | Commercial deposits |
| **Granite** | Kurnool, Kadapa, Chittoor | India = **2nd largest granite exporter** globally |
| **Natural Gas (KG Basin)** | Krishna-Godavari basin, offshore | Reliance's D6 block; significant reserves |

> 📌 **Exam Note:** **Tummalapalle** in **YSR Kadapa** district has one of India's largest **uranium ore deposits**. Operated by **UCIL (Uranium Corporation of India Ltd.)**. This is critical to India's nuclear energy Stage 1 (natural uranium PHWRs). This is both an economy AND science/technology APPSC fact.

## 4. Energy Resources

### Conventional Sources

- **Coal**: India has 4th largest reserves; major states: Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh
- **Petroleum**: Major reserves at Bombay High (offshore), Rajasthan (Barmer), Gujarat
- **Natural Gas**: **KG Basin (AP)** — Reliance's D6 block; India's main domestic gas source

### Renewable Energy Status (2024)

| Source | Installed Capacity (~2024) | 2030 Target |
|---|---|---|
| Solar | ~80 GW | 280 GW |
| Wind | ~47 GW | 140 GW |
| Hydropower | ~47 GW | 60 GW |
| Biopower & Others | ~11 GW | 20 GW |

- **Green Hydrogen Mission (2023)**: India aims to produce **5 MMT/year** by 2030; **AP is a key state** for green hydrogen production due to its renewable capacity and port access

## 5. Conservation Projects

### Project Tiger (1973)

- India has **55 Tiger Reserves** (2024)
- India holds ~**70% of the world's wild tiger population**
- Tiger Census 2022: India has **3,167 tigers** — highest ever counted

### Project Elephant (1992)

- **33 Elephant Reserves** in India
- India has ~**60% of Asia's elephant population**

### Project Crocodile (1975)

- Three species: Mugger, Saltwater, **Gharial**
- **Gharial**: Critically Endangered; Chambal river

### Wetland Conservation — Ramsar Convention

- **Ramsar Convention (1971)**: Wetlands of international importance
- India has **75 Ramsar sites** (2023) — **highest number in the world**
- **Montreux Record** (threatened Ramsar sites): **Loktak Lake** (Manipur) and **Keoladeo Ghana** (Rajasthan)
- **AP Ramsar Sites**: Kolleru Lake, Coringa Wildlife Sanctuary, Pulicat Lake
