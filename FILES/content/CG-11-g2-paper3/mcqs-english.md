## MT-ECON-G2P3-4-001 — National Income (7 Questions)

1. **Question:** National Income of India is measured by:
   - A) GDP at market price
   - B) NNP at factor cost
   - C) GNP at market price
   - D) NDP at factor cost
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** National Income is technically defined as Net National Product (NNP) at factor cost — the sum of incomes earned by all factors of production in a year after deducting depreciation, valued at factor cost.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

2. **Question:** GNP is obtained from GDP by:
   - A) Adding depreciation
   - B) Subtracting indirect taxes
   - C) Adding Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)
   - D) Subtracting subsidies
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). NFIA is the difference between income earned by nationals abroad and income earned by foreigners domestically.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

3. **Question:** The expenditure method of calculating GDP uses the formula:
   - A) GDP = C + I + G + (X – M)
   - B) GDP = Rent + Wages + Interest + Profit
   - C) GDP = Sum of GVA of all sectors
   - D) GDP = NDP + Depreciation
   - **Answer:** A
   - **Explanation:** In the expenditure method, GDP = Consumption (C) + Investment (I) + Government Spending (G) + Net Exports (X – M). This measures total spending on final goods and services in the economy.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

4. **Question:** Which organization is responsible for compiling National Income statistics in India?
   - A) Reserve Bank of India
   - B) National Statistical Office (NSO), formerly CSO
   - C) Planning Commission
   - D) Finance Ministry directly
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The National Statistical Office (NSO), which subsumed the Central Statistics Office (CSO), under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) is responsible for compiling and publishing National Income estimates.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

5. **Question:** GDP Deflator is calculated as:
   - A) (Real GDP / Nominal GDP) × 100
   - B) (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100
   - C) (GDP – Depreciation) × 100
   - D) CPI / WPI × 100
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. It is the broadest measure of inflation as it covers all goods and services produced in the economy, unlike WPI or CPI which cover a basket of selected items.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

6. **Question:** The difference between GDP at market price and GDP at factor cost is:
   - A) Depreciation
   - B) Net Factor Income from Abroad
   - C) Net Indirect Taxes (Indirect Taxes – Subsidies)
   - D) Total government expenditure
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** GDP at factor cost = GDP at market price – Indirect Taxes + Subsidies. The difference is Net Indirect Taxes. Factor cost represents the actual income received by factors of production.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

7. **Question:** India changed the base year for GDP calculation from 2004-05 to 2011-12 and adopted:
   - A) GDP at factor cost as the primary measure
   - B) GVA at basic prices as the primary measure
   - C) NDP at market prices as the primary measure
   - D) NNP at current prices as the primary measure
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** In the new series (base year 2011-12), India adopted Gross Value Added (GVA) at basic prices as the primary measure, following the UN System of National Accounts (SNA) 2008 methodology. This replaced GDP at factor cost.
   - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-ECON-G2P3-2-001 — LPG Reforms (7 Questions)

8. **Question:** The immediate cause of India's New Economic Policy (1991) was:
   - A) Recommendations of the Planning Commission
   - B) Balance of Payments crisis and depleting foreign exchange reserves
   - C) World Bank mandate for all developing countries
   - D) A Supreme Court directive
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** In 1991, India faced a severe BoP crisis with forex reserves falling to barely 2 weeks of import cover. India had to mortgage gold to Bank of England. The IMF loan came with conditions that led to the launch of the New Economic Policy.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

9. **Question:** Which of the following was NOT a component of Liberalization in the 1991 reforms?
   - A) Industrial delicensing for most industries
   - B) Replacement of FERA with FEMA
   - C) Nationalization of commercial banks
   - D) Reduction of import tariffs
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** Nationalization of banks occurred in 1969 and 1980 — the opposite of liberalization. The 1991 reforms involved delicensing, FEMA replacing FERA (1999), tariff reduction, and opening up to FDI.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

10. **Question:** Strategic disinvestment means:
    - A) Selling up to 49% of government equity
    - B) Selling government shares in stock market
    - C) Selling 51% or more stake along with management control to a private entity
    - D) Merging two public sector companies
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Strategic disinvestment involves the sale of 51% or more government equity along with transfer of management control to a private buyer. Examples include BALCO, VSNL (now Tata Communications), and Hindustan Zinc.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

11. **Question:** MRTP Act (Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act) was replaced by:
    - A) Companies Act, 2013
    - B) Competition Act, 2002
    - C) FEMA, 1999
    - D) SEBI Act, 1992
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The MRTP Act (1969), which imposed restrictions on monopolistic business practices, was replaced by the Competition Act, 2002 (effective 2009). The Competition Commission of India (CCI) is the regulatory body under this Act.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

12. **Question:** Which of the following best describes 'Globalization' as part of the 1991 reforms?
    - A) Encouraging self-sufficiency in all sectors
    - B) Integrating the domestic economy with the world economy
    - C) Banning all foreign products
    - D) Nationalizing foreign companies operating in India
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Globalization refers to the integration of the Indian economy with the global economy through reduction of trade barriers, liberalization of FDI, capital account reforms, and participation in multilateral organizations like the WTO.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

13. **Question:** The current body handling disinvestment of PSEs in India is:
    - A) NITI Aayog
    - B) Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM)
    - C) Ministry of Corporate Affairs
    - D) Planning Commission
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM), under the Ministry of Finance, handles all matters related to disinvestment of Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs).
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

14. **Question:** Which of the following correctly represents the 'Generations of Economic Reforms' sequence?
    - A) First: Governance → Second: Trade → Third: Digital
    - B) First: Trade & Industry reform → Second: Factor Market & Governance → Third: Financial Inclusion & Digital
    - C) First: Agriculture → Second: Industry → Third: Services
    - D) First: Monetary policy → Second: Fiscal policy → Third: Trade policy
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** First Generation Reforms (1991–2000) focused on industrial delicensing, trade, and FDI. Second Generation (2000–2014) targeted factor markets, governance, and legal reforms. Third Generation (2014+) emphasizes financial inclusion, digital economy, and ease of doing business.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-ECON-G2P3-2-003 — Trade Policy (7 Questions)

15. **Question:** Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI) policy in India was characterized by:
    - A) Low import duties and open trade
    - B) High tariffs, quantitative restrictions, and import licensing
    - C) Export-led growth strategy
    - D) Free trade agreements with all countries
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** India's ISI strategy (1950s–1991) used high tariffs (150–300%), quantitative restrictions, and a complex import licensing system to protect domestic industries. This created the "License Raj" and fostered self-reliance but reduced competitiveness.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

16. **Question:** India removed remaining quantitative restrictions on imports by 2001 primarily because of:
    - A) Pressure from domestic industry
    - B) WTO dispute settlement ruling against India
    - C) Recommendations of the Planning Commission
    - D) Supreme Court order
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Following a WTO dispute (filed by the USA and EU), India was required to remove quantitative restrictions on imports as they violated WTO rules (GATT Article XI). India phased out QRs by April 2001.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

17. **Question:** Special Economic Zones (SEZs) in India were established under:
    - A) Industrial Disputes Act, 1947
    - B) SEZ Act, 2005
    - C) Foreign Trade (Development & Regulation) Act, 1992
    - D) Competition Act, 2002
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The SEZ Act, 2005 provided the legal framework for establishing SEZs in India. SEZs are duty-free enclaves designed for export-oriented manufacturing and services, offering tax incentives and simplified regulations.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

18. **Question:** India opted out of the RCEP (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership) trade agreement in:
    - A) 2017
    - B) 2018
    - C) 2019
    - D) 2020
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** India withdrew from RCEP negotiations in November 2019 at the Bangkok summit, citing concerns about the impact on domestic agriculture, dairy, and manufacturing sectors (especially vis-à-vis cheap Chinese imports).
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

19. **Question:** The Advance Authorization scheme in India's trade policy allows:
    - A) Duty-free import of capital goods for export production
    - B) Duty-free import of inputs used in export products
    - C) Free trade with neighboring countries
    - D) Tax-free sale within domestic market
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Advance Authorization allows duty-free import of raw materials and inputs that are physically incorporated in the export product. It is a key trade facilitation measure to make Indian exports price-competitive.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

20. **Question:** CEPA (Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement) signed by India with Japan became effective in:
    - A) 2005
    - B) 2008
    - C) 2011
    - D) 2015
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** India-Japan CEPA came into force on 1 August 2011. It is one of India's most comprehensive bilateral trade agreements covering trade in goods, services, investment, and economic cooperation.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

21. **Question:** India's current account convertibility means:
    - A) Indian rupee can be freely converted for trade and current transactions
    - B) Indian rupee can be freely converted for all purposes including investment
    - C) Only the RBI can convert rupees to foreign currency
    - D) Foreign currency cannot be held by Indian residents
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** India achieved full current account convertibility in 1994 (accepting Article VIII of IMF). This means the rupee is freely convertible for trade in goods/services, remittances, and interest payments. Capital account convertibility remains partial.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

## MT-ECON-G2P3-4-003 — Poverty (7 Questions)

22. **Question:** The first official poverty line in India was based on:
    - A) Monthly income
    - B) Minimum calorie intake
    - C) Per capita GDP
    - D) Housing quality
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Alagh Committee (1979/Lakdawala methodology) defined the poverty line based on minimum calorie intake: 2,400 calories per person per day in rural areas and 2,100 in urban areas, then estimated the corresponding expenditure level.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

23. **Question:** The Tendulkar Committee (2009) estimation showed that India's poverty rate in 2011-12 was approximately:
    - A) 37.2%
    - B) 29.5%
    - C) 21.9%
    - D) 11.3%
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Using the Tendulkar methodology, the poverty rate in India declined from 37.2% (2004-05) to 21.9% (2011-12), representing approximately 27 crore people living below the poverty line.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

24. **Question:** The Rangarajan Committee (2014) on poverty estimated the poverty line at:
    - A) ₹26/day (rural) and ₹32/day (urban)
    - B) ₹32/day (rural) and ₹47/day (urban)
    - C) ₹50/day (rural) and ₹70/day (urban)
    - D) ₹15/day (rural) and ₹20/day (urban)
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Rangarajan Committee set the poverty line at ₹32 per capita per day in rural areas and ₹47 in urban areas, estimating the poverty rate at 29.5% (about 36.3 crore people) for 2011-12 — higher than the Tendulkar estimate.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

25. **Question:** MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) guarantees:
    - A) 50 days of wage employment per household per year
    - B) 100 days of wage employment per household per year
    - C) 150 days of wage employment per household per year
    - D) Year-round employment to all rural residents
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** MGNREGA (2005, effective February 2006) guarantees at least 100 days of wage employment in a financial year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

26. **Question:** The Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) published by NITI Aayog measures poverty across:
    - 1. Health
    - 2. Education
    - 3. Standard of Living
    - A) 1 only
    - B) 1 and 2 only
    - C) All of the above
    - D) None of the above
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** India's national MPI (based on the global Alkire-Foster methodology) measures deprivation across three dimensions: Health (2 indicators), Education (2 indicators), and Standard of Living (8 indicators) — total 12 indicators.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

27. **Question:** Which committee first introduced state-specific poverty lines using different consumer price indices?
    - A) Alagh Committee
    - B) Lakdawala Committee
    - C) Tendulkar Committee
    - D) Rangarajan Committee
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Lakdawala Committee (1993) introduced state-specific poverty lines updated using CPI-IL (Industrial Labourers) for urban and CPI-AL (Agricultural Labourers) for rural areas, acknowledging inter-state price variations.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

28. **Question:** The National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013 aims to provide subsidized food grains to approximately:
    - A) 30% of the population
    - B) 50% of the population
    - C) 67% of the population
    - D) 100% of the population
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The NFSA, 2013 covers approximately 67% of the population (75% rural + 50% urban). It provides subsidized food grains at ₹1/kg (millets), ₹2/kg (wheat), and ₹3/kg (rice) to eligible households.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

## MT-ECON-G2P3-4-005 — Inflation (7 Questions)

29. **Question:** RBI's current inflation targeting framework targets:
    - A) WPI at 3% ± 1%
    - B) CPI (Combined) at 4% ± 2%
    - C) GDP Deflator at 5% ± 2%
    - D) CPI-IW at 4%
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Following the Urjit Patel Committee (2014) recommendation, the RBI adopted Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) in 2016. The target is CPI (Combined) inflation at 4% with a tolerance band of ±2% (i.e., 2%–6%).
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

30. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT included in the Wholesale Price Index (WPI)?
    - A) Primary articles
    - B) Fuel and power
    - C) Services
    - D) Manufactured products
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** WPI covers only goods in three categories: Primary Articles (22.6%), Fuel & Power (13.2%), and Manufactured Products (64.2%). Services are NOT included in WPI, which is a key limitation compared to CPI.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

31. **Question:** 'Stagflation' refers to a situation of:
    - A) High growth and low inflation
    - B) High inflation combined with high unemployment and stagnant economic growth
    - C) Deflation combined with high growth
    - D) Zero inflation and full employment
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Stagflation is an unusual economic condition where the economy simultaneously experiences stagnant output/employment AND rising prices (inflation). The term was coined during the 1970s oil crisis when many Western economies faced this situation.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

32. **Question:** Core inflation is calculated by excluding:
    - A) Service sector prices
    - B) Food and fuel prices from headline inflation
    - C) Government administered prices
    - D) Import prices
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Core inflation strips out volatile food and energy/fuel prices from headline inflation to reveal the underlying inflation trend. It is considered more stable and used by central banks to gauge persistent inflationary pressures.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

33. **Question:** The base year for the current CPI (Combined) series in India is:
    - A) 2004–05
    - B) 2010
    - C) 2012
    - D) 2016
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The current CPI (Combined) series uses 2012 as its base year and is compiled by the National Statistical Office (NSO). This series is used by the RBI for inflation targeting purposes.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

34. **Question:** WPI in India is published by:
    - A) Reserve Bank of India
    - B) National Statistical Office
    - C) Office of Economic Adviser, Ministry of Commerce & Industry
    - D) Ministry of Finance
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The Wholesale Price Index (WPI) is compiled and published by the Office of Economic Adviser (OEA) under the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce & Industry.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

35. **Question:** The Urjit Patel Committee (2014) recommended:
    - A) Abolishing CPI and using only WPI
    - B) CPI (Combined) as the nominal anchor for monetary policy with 4% ± 2% target
    - C) Fixing interest rates permanently at 6%
    - D) Replacing RBI with a new monetary authority
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Urjit Patel Committee recommended that the RBI adopt CPI (Combined) as the nominal anchor and target CPI inflation at 4% with a band of ±2%, transitioning to a Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) framework. This was formally adopted through the RBI Act amendment in 2016.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard
