## MT-ECON-G2P3-4-001 — National Income: Concepts, Measurement, GDP, GNP

### 1. Core Concepts
**Gross Domestic Product (GDP):**
- Total market value of all final goods and services produced *within* the domestic territory of a country during a given period (usually one year).
- Includes output by foreign nationals within the country; excludes output by nationals abroad.

**Gross National Product (GNP):**
- GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)
- GNP = GDP + (Income earned by nationals abroad – Income earned by foreigners domestically)

**Net Domestic Product (NDP):**
- NDP = GDP – Depreciation (consumption of fixed capital)
- Represents the net value of production after accounting for wear and tear of capital goods.

**Net National Product (NNP):**
- NNP = GNP – Depreciation
- NNP at factor cost = **National Income** (the most commonly referenced measure)

### 2. Market Price vs Factor Cost
- **Market Price:** Includes indirect taxes but subsidies are deducted
- **Factor Cost:** Income received by factors of production (rent, wages, interest, profit)
- **Relation:** Factor Cost = Market Price – Indirect Taxes + Subsidies

### 3. GDP Measurement Methods
1. **Production/Output Method:** Sum of Gross Value Added (GVA) by all sectors
2. **Income Method:** Sum of incomes earned by all factors of production
3. **Expenditure Method:** GDP = C + I + G + (X – M) where C = Consumption, I = Investment, G = Government Spending, X = Exports, M = Imports

### 4. New Series of National Accounts
- **Base Year Change (2015):** India shifted GDP base year from 2004-05 to 2011-12 (now further updated)
- **GVA at Basic Prices:** CSO adopted GVA at basic prices as the primary measure (replacing GDP at factor cost)
- **Methodology:** Follows the UN System of National Accounts (SNA) 2008 guidelines
- **MCA-21 Database:** Uses corporate filings data for more accurate private sector estimation

### 5. Key Indicators
| Indicator | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Per Capita Income** | NNP at factor cost / Total population |
| **Real GDP** | GDP adjusted for inflation (constant prices) |
| **Nominal GDP** | GDP at current market prices |
| **GDP Deflator** | (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100 |
| **GVA** | Gross Value Added = Output – Input (intermediate consumption) |

## MT-ECON-G2P3-2-001 — LPG Reforms: Liberalization, Privatization & Globalization

### 1. Background: Economic Crisis of 1991
- India faced a severe Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis
- Foreign exchange reserves fell to barely two weeks of imports
- India mortgaged 47 tonnes of gold to Bank of England and Bank of Japan
- IMF bailout came with conditions for structural reform → "New Economic Policy" announced (July 1991)

### 2. Liberalization
**Meaning:** Removing restrictions and barriers to allow free market functioning
- **Industrial Delicensing:** Abolished licensing for most industries (retained for 5 strategic sectors including defence, hazardous chemicals, alcohol)
- **MRTP Act Abolished:** Replaced by Competition Act, 2002
- **FERA → FEMA:** Foreign Exchange Regulation Act replaced by Foreign Exchange Management Act (1999) — from enforcement to management approach
- **FDI Liberalization:** Automatic route introduced for many sectors; FDI caps raised progressively
- **Trade Reforms:** Quantitative restrictions removed; tariffs reduced significantly

### 3. Privatization
**Meaning:** Transfer of ownership and management from public to private sector
- **Disinvestment:** Selling government equity in Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs)
- **Strategic Sale:** Selling 51% or more stake along with management control (e.g., Balco, VSNL, Hindustan Zinc)
- **Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM):** Handles disinvestment
- **National Investment Fund (NIF):** Created to pool disinvestment proceeds
- Recent policy: Government aims to retain no more than a bare minimum PSE presence in strategic sectors

### 4. Globalization
**Meaning:** Integration of domestic economy with the world economy
- **Reduction of Trade Barriers:** Import duties reduced from peak 150% (1991) to around 10-15% (current)
- **Capital Account Liberalization:** Progressive opening for FDI, FII, ECB
- **WTO Membership:** India is a founding member (1995); participates in multilateral trade negotiations
- **SEZs (Special Economic Zones):** Created for export-led growth; SEZ Act, 2005
- **Impact:** India's share in global trade increased; services sector (especially IT) became globally competitive

### 5. Generations of Reforms
| Generation | Period | Focus Areas |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| First Gen | 1991–2000 | Industrial delicensing, trade reform, FDI |
| Second Gen | 2000–2014 | Factor market reforms, governance, legal reforms |
| Third Gen | 2014+ | Financial inclusion, digital economy, ease of business |

## MT-ECON-G2P3-2-003 — Trade Policy: Import Substitution to Export Promotion

### 1. Import Substitution Policy (1950s–1991)
- Aimed at replacing foreign imports with domestically produced goods
- **Tools:** High tariffs, quantitative restrictions, import licensing (Canalized items)
- **Rationale:** Protect infant industries, save foreign exchange, self-reliance
- **Outcome:** Fostered domestic industrial base but led to inefficiency, lack of competition, and the "License Raj"

### 2. Shift to Export Promotion (Post-1991)
- LPG reforms triggered a shift from inward-looking to outward-oriented trade policy
- **Reduction in Tariffs:** From peak rates of 150–300% to 10–15%
- **Removal of QRs:** India removed remaining quantitative restrictions by 2001 (WTO compliance)
- **Convertibility:** Current account convertibility achieved; partial capital account convertibility

### 3. Key Trade Policy Instruments
- **Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG):** Allows duty-free import of capital goods for export production
- **Advance Authorization:** Duty-free import of inputs used in export products
- **Duty Drawback:** Refund of customs and excise duties on inputs used in export products
- **SEZs (Special Economic Zones):** Duty-free enclaves for export-oriented production
- **Foreign Trade Policy (FTP):** 5-year plan governing trade incentives and regulations (current: FTP 2023 onwards)

### 4. India's Trade Composition
**Exports:** Petroleum products, IT services, gems & jewellery, pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, textiles
**Imports:** Crude oil, electronic goods, gold, machinery, chemicals

### 5. Trade Agreements
- **WTO:** India is a member; participates in Doha Development Round
- **Regional:** RCEP (India opted out in 2019); SAFTA; India-ASEAN FTA
- **Bilateral:** India-Japan CEPA, India-UAE CEPA, India-Australia ECTA
- **Make in India:** Policy initiative to boost domestic manufacturing and exports

## MT-ECON-G2P3-4-003 — Poverty: Definitions, Trends & BPL Methodology

### 1. Defining Poverty
- **Absolute Poverty:** Based on minimum calorie intake
  - Alagh Committee (1979): 2,400 calories (rural), 2,100 calories (urban) per person per day
  - **Poverty Line:** The expenditure level required to achieve this calorie intake
- **Relative Poverty:** Measured relative to the median standard of living (used in developed countries)
- **Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI):** UNDP/Oxford measure covering health, education, and living standards

### 2. Key Committees on Poverty Estimation
| Committee | Year | Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Alagh Committee | 1979 | Calorie-based poverty line |
| Lakdawala Committee | 1993 | State-specific poverty lines using CPI-IL and CPI-UNME |
| Tendulkar Committee | 2009 | Uniform Reference Period (URP); mixed method; raised rural poverty line |
| Rangarajan Committee | 2014 | Revised upward; ₹32/day (rural), ₹47/day (urban); 29.5% poverty rate |

### 3. Poverty Trends in India
- 1993–94: 45.3% (Planning Commission, Lakdawala method)
- 2004–05: 37.2% (Tendulkar method)
- 2011–12: 21.9% (Tendulkar method)
- **NITI Aayog MPI (2023):** 11.28% multidimensional poverty; 13.5 crore people lifted out of poverty (2015–2021)

### 4. BPL (Below Poverty Line) Identification
- **BPL Census:** Periodic surveys to identify poor households for targeting welfare schemes
- Criteria include: housing type, food security, land ownership, means of livelihood, education, occupation
- Used for: PDS (ration cards), MGNREGA targeting, scholarships, housing schemes

### 5. Poverty Alleviation Programs
| Programme | Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| MGNREGA (2005) | Guaranteed 100 days of wage employment in rural areas |
| PMAY (2015) | Housing for all (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana) |
| NFSA (2013) | Food security — subsidized food grains to 67% population |
| PMJDY (2014) | Financial inclusion — bank accounts for all |
| DAY-NRLM | Self-help group based livelihood promotion |

## MT-ECON-G2P3-4-005 — Inflation: Types, Measurement, WPI & CPI

### 1. Definition & Types
**Inflation:** Sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services over time.
- **Demand-Pull Inflation:** Too much money chasing too few goods (excess aggregate demand)
- **Cost-Push Inflation:** Rising input costs (raw materials, wages) push up prices
- **Structural Inflation:** Supply bottlenecks, infrastructure constraints

**Variants:**
| Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Creeping | 1–3% annually; low and manageable |
| Walking | 3–10% annually; warning sign |
| Galloping | 10–100% annually; severe distortion |
| Hyperinflation | >100%; currency collapse (e.g., Zimbabwe, Venezuela) |

### 2. Wholesale Price Index (WPI)
- Measures price changes at the **wholesale/producer level** (first point of bulk sale)
- **Base Year:** 2011–12
- **Components:** Primary articles (22.6%), Fuel & Power (13.2%), Manufactured Products (64.2%)
- Published by **Office of Economic Adviser**, Ministry of Commerce & Industry
- Does NOT include services; weekly release
- Used by the government for policy purposes, deflation of nominal GDP

### 3. Consumer Price Index (CPI)
- Measures price changes at the **retail/consumer level**
- **Types of CPI:**
  - CPI (Combined) — Base Year: 2012; published by CSO/NSO
  - CPI-IW (Industrial Workers) — Base Year: 2016; published by Labour Bureau
  - CPI-AL (Agricultural Labourers) — published by Labour Bureau
  - CPI-RL (Rural Labourers)

- **CPI (Combined)** is the headline inflation measure used by the RBI for **Inflation Targeting**
- **RBI's Inflation Target:** 4% ± 2% (Flexible Inflation Targeting framework adopted in 2016 based on Urjit Patel Committee recommendation)

### 4. WPI vs CPI Comparison
| Parameter | WPI | CPI |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Level | Wholesale | Retail |
| Coverage | Only goods | Goods + Services |
| Users | Government policy | RBI monetary policy |
| Services | Not included | Included (housing, health, education) |
| Food weight | ~24% | ~46% |

### 5. Related Concepts
- **GDP Deflator:** (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100; broadest measure of inflation
- **Core Inflation:** Headline inflation minus food and fuel (volatile components)
- **Producer Price Index (PPI):** Measures prices received by domestic producers (India transitioning from WPI to PPI)
- **Phillips Curve:** Inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment (short run)
- **Stagflation:** High inflation + high unemployment + stagnant growth
