# Land Reforms and Land Ceiling in Andhra Pradesh
## Micro-Topic: Evolution of Land Ceiling, Tenancy Reforms, Zamindari Abolition, and Koneru Ranga Rao Committee

**Sources Used:**
- Ramesh Singh: Indian Economy — Chapter on Land Reforms
- Official AP Socio-Economic Survey

---

## 1. Timeline Overview / Key Framework
| Year | Act/Event | Objective / Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1948 | AP (Andhra Area) Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act | Abolishing the Zamindari and Inamdari systems |
| 1956 | AP (Andhra Area) Tenancy Act | Regulating relations between landlords and cultivating tenants |
| 1961 | AP Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings Act | First attempt at land ceiling; based on individual rather than family unit |
| 1973 | AP Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act | More stringent ceiling laws based on 'Family Unit', incorporating National Guidelines |
| 2004-2006 | Koneru Ranga Rao Committee | Comprehensive review of land assignments, tribal lands (1/70 Act), and ceiling surpluses |

## 2. Zamindari Abolition and Intermediaries
- **Features / Key Points**: The abolition of intermediaries (Zamindars, Jagirdars, Inamdars) was the first major step post-independence.
- It transformed tenants into direct titleholders (ryots) under the state. Over 14 million tenants acquired ownership rights nationally.
- **Exam Tips**: AP was a pioneer state. The *Madras Estates Act of 1948* applied to the Andhra region.

## 3. Tenancy Reforms
- **Features / Key Points**: Addressed rack-renting, insecurity of tenure, and conferred ownership rights to actual tillers.
- Maximum rents were fixed at **1/4th** to **1/5th** of the gross produce structurally, though implementation varied.
- Protective legislations included securing the rights of cultivating tenants against arbitrary eviction.

## 4. Land Ceiling Legislations
- **Features / Key Points**: Aimed at distributive justice by capping maximum land ownership and redistributing surplus land to the landless.
- **The 1961 Act** was largely ineffective because it treated 'individual' as the unit of application and allowed numerous exemptions (e.g., orchards, religious trusts).
- **The 1973 Act**: Fixed the 'Family' (husband, wife, and minor children up to 5 members) as the standard unit.
  - **Standard Holding Limits**:
    - **Wetland**: Ranges from 10 to 27 acres (depending on the class of land).
    - **Dryland**: Ranges from 35 to 54 acres.
- **Exam Tips**: The 1973 Act was included in the **Ninth Schedule** of the Indian Constitution to protect it from judicial review under Article 31B.

## 5. Koneru Ranga Rao Committee (2004-2006)
- **Features / Key Points**: Appointed by the AP Government to assess the implementation of land distribution programs and suggest measures to protect lands of the poor.
- **Major Recommendations**:
  - Speedy disposal of prolonged ceiling and inam land cases (fast-track courts).
  - Strict enforcement of the **AP Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act, 1977** (POT Act).
  - Effective implementation of the **1/70 Act** [AP Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation, 1959 (Amendment 1970)] prohibiting non-tribals from owning land in scheduled areas.

## 6. Thematic Understanding (for Mains)
- **Impact vs. Intent**: While intermediaries were successfully abolished, land ceilings found limited success due to benami transfers, poor land records, and political interference.
- **Socio-Economic Angle**: Lack of structural transformation; Dalits and marginalized communities remained largely landless laborers despite ceiling distributions.
- **Current Scenario**: Digitization of land records (e.g., 'Mee Bhoomi' in AP) and the issuance of e-passbooks aim to secure title accuracy and reduce litigation.

## 7. Prelims Revision Points (Quick Recall)
1. The **1961 Ceiling Act** failed mainly because the unit of application was the **individual**.
2. The **1973 Ceiling Act** succeeded it by changing the unit to a **five-member family**.
3. Ceiling limit under the 1973 Act for **wetland** was capped between **10 to 27 acres**.
4. Land ceiling acts were placed in the **Ninth Schedule** of the Constitution.
5. Benami transfers were the most prominent method used by landlords to evade ceiling limits.
6. The **AP Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act, 1977**, prevents the alienation of lands assigned to landless poor.
7. The **1/70 Act** is critical for safeguarding tribal land rights in Scheduled Areas from non-tribal alienation.
8. The **Koneru Ranga Rao Committee** (2006) is the landmark committee on land reforms in AP. (Exam trap: frequently asked to match committee name with domain).
9. 'Mee Bhoomi' is the digital land records portal of Andhra Pradesh.
10. The Bhoodan movement, initiated by Acharya Vinoba Bhave at Pochampally (then united AP, now Telangana), aimed at voluntary land donation, serving as a non-legislative precursor to ceilings.
