## 1. AP GSDP & Per Capita Income (Key Framework)

| Indicator | 2023-24 (FRE) | 2024-25 (FAE) | Growth Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| GSDP at Current Prices | ₹14.22 lakh crore | ₹16.06 lakh crore | 12.94% |
| Per Capita Income (PCI) | ₹2,37,951 | ₹2,68,653 | 12.9% |
| All India PCI | ₹1,84,205 | ₹2,00,162 | 8.7% |

- **Key Point (Source: AP Survey Ch.2):** AP's Per Capita Income (₹2,68,653) is **higher than** the national average (₹2,00,162) in 2024-25 — a frequently tested fact.
- **Exam Tip:** AP's PCI has consistently exceeded the national average since 2014-15. The gap has widened over the years.

### Sector-wise GVA Contribution (2024-25 FAE, Current Prices):
| Sector | Contribution (%) | GVA (₹ lakh crore) | Growth Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agriculture & Allied | 35.29% | 5.19 | 15.86% |
| Industry | 23.18% | 3.41 | 6.71% |
| Services | 41.53% | 6.11 | 11.70% |

- **Exam Tip:** Services sector is the largest contributor (41.53%), but agriculture shows highest growth (15.86%). Exam trap: students often confuse "largest contributor" with "fastest growing."

## 2. State Revenue Structure (FY 2024-25 RE)

### 2.1 Revenue Sources Overview
| Revenue Source | Amount (₹ Crores) |
|---|---|
| Own Tax Revenue | 94,967 |
| Own Non-Tax Revenue | 7,018 |
| Central Transfers | 89,157 |
| **Total Revenue** | **~1,91,142** |

### 2.2 Own Tax Revenue Composition (FY 2024-25 RE)
| Component | Share of Own Tax Revenue |
|---|---|
| GST (State's share) | 38% |
| State Excise | 22% |
| Sales Tax | ~20% |
| Stamps & Registration | ~10% |
| Motor Vehicle Tax | ~5% |
| Others | ~5% |

- **Key Point (Source: AP Survey Ch.3):** GST is the single largest component (38%) of the state's own tax revenue, followed by State Excise (22%).
- **Exam Tip:** State Excise (22%) is the second-largest own tax source — this is a common exam question about state finances.

### 2.3 Own Non-Tax Revenue (FY 2024-25 RE — ₹7,018 Crores)
| Component | Share |
|---|---|
| Mines and Minerals | 50% |
| Forests | 1% |
| Medical and Health | 4% |
| Others | 44% |

- **Key Point:** Mines and Minerals contribute **50%** of non-tax revenue — disproportionately high, reflecting AP's mineral wealth.

### 2.4 Central Transfers (FY 2024-25 RE — ₹89,157 Crores)
| Component | Amount (₹ Crores) |
|---|---|
| Finance Commission Allocation | 57,002 |
| Grants-in-Aid (other than FC & EAP) | 16,252 |
| EAP & Others | 5,037 |

- **Key Point (Source: AP Survey Ch.3):** Finance Commission allocation constitutes the major portion of Central Transfers — ₹55,352 Crores in FY 2023-24, rising to ₹57,002 Crores in FY 2024-25 (RE).

## 3. State Expenditure (FY 2024-25 RE)

| Expenditure Type | 2023-24 (₹ Crores) | 2024-25 RE (₹ Crores) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Expenditure | 2,12,450 | 2,24,343 |
| Capital Outlay | 23,331 | 24,072 |
| Loans & Advances | ~731 | ~1,003 |
| Public Debt Repayment | 13,070 | 21,102 |
| **Total Expenditure** | **2,36,512** | **2,49,418** |

- **Key Point:** Revenue expenditure dominates total spending. Capital outlay is relatively small (₹24,072 crore in 2024-25 RE) — spent mainly on irrigation, roads, and infrastructure.
- **Exam Tip:** Revenue expenditure includes welfare schemes like subsidy rice, power subsidy, old age pensions, and housing programs.

## 4. Debt and Fiscal Position

| Indicator | 2023-24 | 2024-25 (RE) |
|---|---|---|
| Total Outstanding Debt | ₹4,91,734 Cr | ₹5,64,488 Cr |
| Debt-to-GSDP Ratio | 34.58% | 35.15% |
| Market Borrowings | ₹3,66,565 Cr | ₹4,24,070 Cr |
| Interest Payments | ₹29,481 Cr | ₹32,944 Cr |
| Revenue Deficit | ₹38,683 Cr | ₹48,311 Cr |
| Fiscal Deficit | ₹62,720 Cr | ₹73,362 Cr |

- **Key Points (Source: AP Survey Ch.3):**
  - Market Loans form the **largest component** of state debt.
  - The **Debt-to-GSDP ratio** crossed 35% in 2024-25 — a sign of fiscal stress.
  - Both Revenue Deficit and Fiscal Deficit have **widened** from 2023-24 to 2024-25.
- **Exam Tip:** The total debt includes an **un-apportioned amount of ₹4,422.59 crores** under Public Account — related to post-bifurcation settlement. This is a unique AP-specific point.

### Historical Context (Source: AP Survey Ch.3):
- AP was bifurcated on **2nd June 2014** — the Union Government acknowledged adverse fiscal impact on residuary AP.
- PM gave assurances during Rajya Sabha discussions on AP Re-organization Bill — but GoI did not fulfill assurances in full.
- Fiscal stress caused by: shrinking revenue base, rising establishment costs, interest payments, welfare commitments, and loss of Hyderabad as capital/commercial hub.

## 5. Thematic Understanding (for Mains)

### Revenue vs. Expenditure Gap
- AP faces a structural fiscal problem: revenue growth (~10%) lags behind expenditure growth (~12-15%).
- Heavy dependence on Central Transfers (₹89,157 Cr = ~47% of total revenue) makes the state vulnerable to changes in Finance Commission allocations.
- Post-bifurcation loss of Hyderabad's tax base is a persistent drag on own-revenue generation.

### Debt Sustainability Concerns
- Debt-to-GSDP ratio at 35.15% is approaching the FRBM Act limit. Interest payments (₹32,944 Cr) consume a large share of revenue — crowding out developmental spending.
- Market borrowings dominate debt composition — exposing the state to interest rate risks.

### Capital Expenditure Quality
- Capital outlay (₹24,072 Cr) is small relative to total expenditure (₹2,49,418 Cr) — only ~9.7%.
- Bulk of capital spending goes to irrigation (Polavaram) and roads — essential for long-term growth but strains short-term finances.
