## 2. Palaeolithic Period (Old Stone Age)

### 2.1 Lower Palaeolithic (2.5 million – 100,000 BCE)

**Features (R.S. Sharma + Nitin Singhania):**
- Man has been living in India roughly from 500,000 B.C. (R.S. Sharma)
- Used tools of unpolished, undressed, rough stones — chips of stone were pebbles used for hunting, cutting and other purposes
- Pebble tools → Handaxes → Cleavers (Acheulean culture)
- Nomadic hunter-gatherers — no knowledge of cultivation or house building
- Lived in caves and rock shelters
- Quartzite widely used as raw material
- Phase generally continued till 8000 B.C. (R.S. Sharma)
- The Old Stone Age developed in the Pleistocene period (Ice Age), a geological period
- People moved constantly because: (a) resources got exhausted in one area, (b) followed animal herds, (c) plants bore fruit in different seasons, (d) needed water sources (NCERT Class 6)

**Important Sites:**

| Site | State | Importance |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Soan/Sohan river valley | Pakistan | Early Palaeolithic tools found here (R.S. Sharma) |
| South India | — | Unpolished rough stone tools found (R.S. Sharma) |
| Bhimbetka Rock Shelters | Madhya Pradesh | Continuous habitation from Lower Palaeolithic; early rock art |
| Hunsgi | Karnataka | Tool manufacturing centre |
| Attirampakkam | Tamil Nadu | One of the oldest Palaeolithic sites in India |
| Narmada Valley | Madhya Pradesh | Hominid fossil discovery (Narmada Man) |

**Key Term:** Acheulean Industry → Characterised by symmetrical bifacial handaxes and cleavers.

### 2.2 Middle Palaeolithic (100,000 – 40,000 BCE)

**Features (R.S. Sharma + Nitin Singhania):**
- Palaeolithic tools which could be as old as 100,000 B.C. found in Chotanagpur plateau (R.S. Sharma)
- Tools belonging to 25,000–10,000 B.C. found in Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh, about 55 km from Kurnool
- Bone implements and animal remains also discovered alongside
- Animal remains in Belan valley at Mirzapur district (UP) show goats, sheep and cattle domesticated around 25,000 B.C. (R.S. Sharma)
- Flake tools: Scrapers, points, borers
- Levallois Technique (prepared core method) — a significant cognitive advancement
- Smaller and lighter tools compared to Lower Palaeolithic

**Important Sites:**
- Chotanagpur plateau (Jharkhand) — tools as old as 100,000 B.C. (R.S. Sharma)
- Kurnool (Andhra Pradesh) — bone implements + fire evidence (R.S. Sharma)
- Belan Valley (Uttar Pradesh) — animal remains showing early domestication
- Nevasa (Maharashtra)
- Didwana (Rajasthan)
- Son River Valley

**Key fact (R.S. Sharma):** On the northern spurs of the Vindhyas in the Belan Valley, all three phases of the Palaeolithic followed by the Mesolithic and then the Neolithic have been found in sequence — a complete Stone Age sequence at one location.

**Exam Tip:** Levallois = Middle Palaeolithic (NOT Lower). This is a common prelims trap.

### 2.3 Upper Palaeolithic (40,000 – 10,000 BCE)

**Features:**
- Blade tools (long, narrow, parallel-sided)
- Bone tools (needles, harpoons)
- Beginning of cave art and symbolic thinking
- Homo sapiens sapiens dominance
- Evidence of fire use found at Kurnool Caves (traces of ash)

**Important Sites:**
- Bhimbetka Rock Shelters (Madhya Pradesh)
- Patne (Maharashtra)
- Baghor I (Madhya Pradesh)
- Kurnool Caves (Andhra Pradesh) — evidence of fire use

## 3. Mesolithic Period (10,000 – 7,000 BCE)

**Context:** Around 12,000 years ago, major climate changes — shift to warmer conditions. Grasslands developed → increase in deer, antelope, goat, sheep, cattle populations (NCERT Class 6). From 8000 B.C. began an intermediate stage in stone age culture, called the Mesolithic Age. It intervened as a transitional phase between the Palaeolithic Age and the Neolithic (R.S. Sharma). In India it started about 8000 B.C. and continued up to about 4000 B.C. (R.S. Sharma).

**Features:**
- Microliths (small stone tools) — the hallmark and characteristic tools of this period
- Composite tools (microliths hafted on wood or bone handles to make saws, sickles)
- Rock paintings flourish (hunting scenes, rituals)
- Early domestication of animals begins
- Dog was probably the first animal to be tamed (NCERT Class 6)
- Fishing becomes important

**Important Sites:**

| Site | State | Importance |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Bagor | Rajasthan | Largest Mesolithic site in India |
| Langhnaj | Gujarat | Animal bone evidence |
| Adamgarh | Madhya Pradesh | Rock shelters with paintings |
| Sarai Nahar Rai | Uttar Pradesh | Human burial evidence |
| Chotanagpur, central India | Jharkhand | Good numbers of Mesolithic sites (R.S. Sharma) |
| South of river Krishna | — | Mesolithic sites found (R.S. Sharma) |
| Birbhanpur | West Bengal | Mesolithic tools found (R.S. Sharma) |
| Tinnevelly | Tamil Nadu | Mesolithic tools found (R.S. Sharma) |

**Prelims Point:** Microliths = Mesolithic hallmark. This distinction is frequently tested.

## 4. Neolithic Period (7,000 – 2,000 BCE)

**Context:** In 7000 B.C., neolithic settlements in the Indian sub-continent are not older than 6000 B.C. Some settlements in south and eastern India are as late as 1000 B.C. (R.S. Sharma). People began growing plants — wheat, barley, rice grew naturally in different parts of the subcontinent (NCERT Class 6).

**Features (R.S. Sharma + NCERT Class 6 + Nitin Singhania):**
- Agriculture and animal domestication (sheep, goat, cattle, pig)
- Polished stone tools and implements — particularly stone axes, found in large numbers throughout the country
- In ancient legends, Parashurama became an important axe-wielding hero (R.S. Sharma)
- Mortars and pestles for grinding grain
- Pottery (grey ware, handmade, sometimes decorated)
- Permanent settlements with houses
- Pit dwellings in cold regions (e.g., Burzahom)
- Weaving of cloth (including cotton)
- Grain storage in large clay pots, woven baskets, or pits dug into ground

**Three Important Areas of Neolithic Settlements (R.S. Sharma):**
1. **North — Kashmir Valley:** Burzahom, about 20 km from Srinagar. People lived on a plateau in pits, probably had hunting and fishing along with agriculture/domestication. Used polished stone tools AND bone tools. Chirand (40 km west of Patna, on the Ganga) — only other place yielding considerable bone implements in a late neolithic setup. Burzahom people used coarse grey pottery. Domestic dogs were buried with masters in graves — unique practice not found elsewhere in India. Earliest date ~2400 B.C.
2. **South India:** Sites at Maski, Brahmagiri, Hallur, Kodekal, Sanganakallu, T. Narsipur and Tekkalakota in Karnataka, and Paiyampalli in Tamil Nadu. People used stone axes and blades, reared cattle and cultivated ragi and horse gram (kulithi). Settlements located on tops of granite hills or on plateaus near rivers. Earlier than 1600 B.C., possibly belonging to stone-copper phase.
3. **East — Allahabad region:** Several chalcolithic sites found due to proximity to Vindhyas. Chirand on the Ganga in Bihar; Pandu Rajar Dhibi in Burdwan district, West Bengal.

**Domestication (NCERT Class 6):** People selected plants for large grain size and strong stalks; selected gentle animals for breeding. Wild and domesticated animals differ — wild animals have larger teeth and horns.

**Important Sites:**

| Site | State/Region | Importance |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Mehrgarh | Balochistan (Pakistan) | Earliest evidence of farming in Indian subcontinent; barley, wheat cultivation; square/rectangular houses with compartments; burials with goats (NCERT Class 6) |
| Burzahom | Jammu & Kashmir | Pit dwellings; coarse grey pottery; dogs buried with masters; bone tools; ~2400 B.C. (R.S. Sharma) |
| Chirand | Bihar (40 km west of Patna) | Considerable bone implements; late neolithic setup on Ganga (R.S. Sharma) |
| Koldihwa | Uttar Pradesh | Evidence of rice cultivation |
| Maski | Karnataka | Southern Neolithic culture |
| Brahmagiri | Karnataka | Southern Neolithic (R.S. Sharma) |
| Tekkalakota | Karnataka | Ragi and horse gram cultivation (R.S. Sharma) |
| Hallur | Karnataka | Southern Neolithic (R.S. Sharma) |
| Paiyampalli | Tamil Nadu | Southern Neolithic (R.S. Sharma) |

**Mains Link:** Neolithic revolution = foundation of civilisation. Food surplus → social stratification → complex societies.

## 5. Chalcolithic Age / Stone-Copper Phase (R.S. Sharma Ch.5)

**Time:** 3,000 – 700 BCE

**Context (R.S. Sharma):** Towards the end of the neolithic period began the use of metals. The first metal to be used was copper. Several cultures were based on the use of stone and copper implements. Such a culture is called *chalcolithic*.

**Features:**
- Copper used alongside stone tools (first metal use)
- Rural agricultural settlements — located in semi-arid areas on brown-black soil with *ber* and *babul* vegetation, fell in riverine tracts
- Black-and-Red Ware pottery (thrown on wheel, occasionally painted with white linear designs) — distinctive marker. BUT it would be wrong to think all who used this pottery possessed the same culture; differences existed in forms of pottery and implements (R.S. Sharma)
- Agriculture-based economy — kept cows, sheep, goats, pigs, buffaloes, and hunted deer. Remains of the camel also found (R.S. Sharma)
- Cultivated foodgrains: wheat, rice, lentil (*masur*), black gram, green gram, grass pea. All found at Navdatoli on Narmada bank — perhaps no other place in India has so many cereals discovered (R.S. Sharma)
- Cotton was produced in the black cotton soil of the Deccan; *ragi*, *bajra* and several millets cultivated in the lower Deccan (R.S. Sharma)
- Navdatoli also produced *ber* and linseed (R.S. Sharma)
- Eastern India: fish hooks found in Bihar and West Bengal — fish and rice diet still popular in that part (R.S. Sharma)
- Anthropomorphic copper hoards found in UP region

**Chalcolithic Cultures (R.S. Sharma — detailed):**

| Culture/System | Region | Key Features |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Ahar-Gilund | South-eastern Rajasthan (Banas valley dry zones) | Copper tools at Ahar; stone-blade industry at Gilund; flat rectangular copper axes (R.S. Sharma) |
| Kayatha | Western Madhya Pradesh | Early Chalcolithic (NOT Gujarat — exam trap) |
| Malwa | Western MP, Rajasthan, Maharashtra | Most extensively excavated in western Maharashtra — Inamgaon, Jorwe, Nevasa, Daimabad, Chandoli, Songaon (R.S. Sharma) |
| Jorwe | Maharashtra (Pravara river) | Nucleated settlements with 35+ houses of different sizes, circular or rectangular; named after Jorwe village; village economy (R.S. Sharma) |
| Navdatoli | MP (Narmada bank) | Largest cereal diversity in India; wheat, rice, lentil, *bajra*, cotton, linseed (R.S. Sharma) |

**Chalcolithic people's limitations (R.S. Sharma):**
- Generally not acquainted with burnt bricks — houses made of mud bricks, wattle and daub, thatched
- At Inamgaon: large mud houses with ovens, circular pit houses discovered in later phase (1300–1000 B.C.)
- Could not settle on alluvial plains permanently — relied on stone blades (not heavy enough for clearing forests on Indo-Gangetic plains)
- Could not establish themselves on hard, rocky terrain or in thick forests — settled only on black cotton soil or in river valleys
- Needed copper supply — but could produce only limited surplus despite considerable effort (R.S. Sharma)

**Prelims Alert:** Copper anthropomorphic figures (UP) = Chalcolithic. Navdatoli = maximum cereals found. Jorwe = nucleated village settlements.

## 6. Thematic Understanding (for Mains)

### Evolution of Technology
Pebble → Handaxe → Flake → Blade → Microlith → Polished Stone → Copper

### Social Transformation
Nomadic → Semi-nomadic → Settled agriculture → Village communities

### Art Development
Rock engravings → Cave paintings (Bhimbetka) → Ritualistic symbols → Pottery decoration

### Climate Link
Pleistocene (Ice Age) → Holocene (warming, ~12,000 years ago) → Grasslands → Agriculture

## 7. Prelims Revision Points (Quick Recall)

1. Quartzite = common raw material in Palaeolithic
2. Acheulean = Lower Palaeolithic (handaxes)
3. Levallois technique = Middle Palaeolithic (NOT Lower)
4. Blade + bone tools = Upper Palaeolithic
5. Microliths = Mesolithic (NOT any other period)
6. Mesolithic in India: 8000 B.C. to 4000 B.C. (R.S. Sharma)
7. Pit dwellings = Burzahom (J&K)
8. Burzahom: dogs buried with masters in graves — unique in India (R.S. Sharma)
9. Chirand (Bihar) = bone implements in late neolithic, on Ganga (R.S. Sharma)
10. Earliest farming = Mehrgarh
11. Rice cultivation evidence = Koldihwa
12. Black-and-Red Ware = Chalcolithic (wheel-thrown, white linear designs)
13. Narmada Man fossil = Middle Pleistocene
14. First domesticated animal = Dog
15. Bagor = largest Mesolithic site (NOT Neolithic — exam trap)
16. Kayatha = Madhya Pradesh (NOT Gujarat — exam trap)
17. Fire evidence = Kurnool Caves (AP)
18. Navdatoli (Narmada bank) = maximum number of cereals found at any single site (R.S. Sharma)
19. Jorwe culture = nucleated village settlements, 35+ houses (R.S. Sharma)
20. Belan Valley (Vindhyas) = complete Stone Age sequence — all phases found at one location (R.S. Sharma)
21. Parashurama = legendary axe-hero linked to Neolithic stone axes (R.S. Sharma)
22. South Indian Neolithic: ragi + horse gram (*kulithi*) = main crops (R.S. Sharma)
