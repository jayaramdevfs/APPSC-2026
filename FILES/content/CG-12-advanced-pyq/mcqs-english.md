## MT-HIST-M2-2-016 — Buddhism (7 Questions)

1. **Question:** The First Buddhist Council was held at:
   - A) Vaishali
   - B) Rajagriha
   - C) Pataliputra
   - D) Kundalvana
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The First Buddhist Council was held at Rajagriha (Saptaparni Cave) in 483 BCE, shortly after Buddha's Mahaparinirvana. It was presided over by Mahakashyapa under the patronage of King Ajatashatru. The Vinaya Pitaka was recited by Upali and the Sutta Pitaka by Ananda.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

2. **Question:** Buddha delivered his first sermon (Dharmachakra Pravartana) at:
   - A) Bodh Gaya
   - B) Lumbini
   - C) Sarnath
   - D) Kushinagar
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** After attaining enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, Buddha gave his first sermon at Sarnath (Deer Park / Isipatana) near Varanasi, where he taught the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path to five ascetics. This event is called Dharmachakra Pravartana ("Turning the Wheel of Law").
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

3. **Question:** The Fourth Buddhist Council, which led to the formal split of Buddhism into Mahayana and Hinayana, was held during the reign of:
   - A) Ashoka
   - B) Kanishka
   - C) Harsha
   - D) Ajatashatru
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The Fourth Buddhist Council was held at Kundalvana (Kashmir) in the 1st century CE under the patronage of Kushan King Kanishka. It was presided by Vasumitra and Ashvaghosha. This council formalized the Mahayana and Hinayana (Theravada) division.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

4. **Question:** Which of the following concepts is central to Buddhist philosophy?
   - 1. Pratityasamutpada (Dependent Origination)
   - 2. Atman (Permanent Self)
   - 3. Anatman (No-Self)
   - A) 1 and 2 only
   - B) 1 and 3 only
   - C) 2 and 3 only
   - D) All of the above
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Buddhism rejects the concept of a permanent self (Atman) and instead teaches Anatman (No-Self). Pratityasamutpada (Dependent Origination) — that all phenomena arise in dependence upon causes and conditions — is a central doctrine. Statement 2 is incorrect as it belongs to Hindu philosophy.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

5. **Question:** The Gandhara School of Buddhist Art is characterized by:
   - A) Indigenous Indian style without any foreign influence
   - B) Greco-Roman influence on the depiction of Buddha
   - C) Abstract and non-figurative representations
   - D) Use of bronze exclusively
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The Gandhara School (present-day Pakistan/Afghanistan) was heavily influenced by Greco-Roman art traditions due to Alexander's conquests. It was the first to create human images of Buddha (previously represented by symbols). Features include wavy hair, muscular body, and toga-like robes.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

6. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT one of the three divisions (Tripitaka) of Buddhist canonical texts?
   - A) Vinaya Pitaka
   - B) Sutta Pitaka
   - C) Abhidhamma Pitaka
   - D) Jataka Pitaka
   - **Answer:** D
   - **Explanation:** The Tripitaka (Three Baskets) comprises: (1) Vinaya Pitaka (monastic rules), (2) Sutta Pitaka (Buddha's discourses), and (3) Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical analysis). Jataka tales (stories of Buddha's previous births) are part of the Sutta Pitaka, not a separate Pitaka.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

7. **Question:** Which of the following factors contributed to the decline of Buddhism in India?
   - 1. Rise of Hindu devotional movements (Bhakti)
   - 2. Loss of royal patronage after the Gupta period
   - 3. Destruction of monasteries like Nalanda by Turkish invaders
   - A) 1 and 2 only
   - B) 2 and 3 only
   - C) 1 and 3 only
   - D) All of the above
   - **Answer:** D
   - **Explanation:** Buddhism declined in India due to multiple factors: the revival of Hinduism through the Bhakti movement (especially Shankaracharya's Advaita philosophy), loss of royal patronage, corruption in monasteries, and the destruction of great centers of learning like Nalanda and Vikramashila by Bakhtiyar Khilji in 1193 CE.
   - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-AP_ECON-G2P3-5-002 — AP Agriculture (7 Questions)

8. **Question:** Which district of Andhra Pradesh is the largest producer of chillies in India?
   - A) Krishna
   - B) Guntur
   - C) Anantapur
   - D) Prakasam
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Guntur district is the largest chilli-producing district in India and hosts the largest chilli market in Asia. The famous "Guntur Sannam" variety is widely exported.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

9. **Question:** The Polavaram Project (Indira Sagar) is being built on which river?
   - A) Krishna
   - B) Godavari
   - C) Penna
   - D) Tungabhadra
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The Polavaram Project is a multi-purpose national project on the Godavari River in West Godavari and East Godavari districts. It aims to irrigate 2.91 lakh hectares, provide drinking water to 28.50 lakh people, and generate 960 MW of hydropower.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

10. **Question:** Andhra Pradesh pioneered which farming method at a large scale?
    - A) Precision Agriculture
    - B) Zero-Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF)
    - C) Slash-and-burn cultivation
    - D) Vertical farming
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Andhra Pradesh became the first state to adopt Zero-Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF) at a large scale, covering 6 lakh+ farmers. ZBNF promotes chemical-free farming using natural inputs like Beejamrutham, Jeevamrutham, and mulching, following Subhash Palekar's methods.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

11. **Question:** Rythu Bharosa Kendras (RBKs) in Andhra Pradesh are:
    - A) Agricultural universities
    - B) Wholesale produce markets
    - C) Village-level one-stop service centres for farmers
    - D) Micro-finance institutions for farmers
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Rythu Bharosa Kendras are village-level one-stop service centres providing farmers with seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, crop advisory, soil testing, and government scheme information. Over 10,000 RBKs have been established across AP.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

12. **Question:** The dominant crop grown in Anantapur district of Andhra Pradesh is:
    - A) Rice
    - B) Cotton
    - C) Groundnut
    - D) Sugarcane
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Anantapur is the largest groundnut-growing district in India. Due to its semi-arid climate and low rainfall, rain-fed groundnut cultivation is the primary agricultural activity in the district.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

13. **Question:** Which of the following is a key concern regarding irrigation in Rayalaseema region of AP?
    - A) Excessive canal water leading to waterlogging
    - B) Over-extraction of groundwater and aquifer depletion
    - C) Too much rainfall causing floods
    - D) Excess dam capacity going unused
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Rayalaseema (Anantapur, Kurnool, Kadapa, Chittoor) is the most drought-prone region of AP. Over 50% of irrigation depends on groundwater, and over-extraction has led to severe aquifer depletion, falling water tables, and bore-well failures.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

14. **Question:** Andhra Pradesh's aquaculture/shrimp farming industry is concentrated primarily in:
    - A) Anantapur and Kurnool
    - B) Nellore, Krishna, and West Godavari
    - C) Srikakulam and Vizianagaram
    - D) Chittoor and Kadapa
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Nellore district is the "Shrimp Capital of India," and along with Krishna and West Godavari districts, forms the core of AP's massive aquaculture industry. AP is the largest producer of brackish water shrimp in India.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-002 — Prime Minister & Council of Ministers (7 Questions)

15. **Question:** Under which article of the Constitution is the Prime Minister appointed?
    - A) Article 74
    - B) Article 75
    - C) Article 76
    - D) Article 78
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 75(1) states that the Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President, and the other Ministers shall be appointed by the President on the advice of the PM. Article 74 deals with the Council of Ministers aiding and advising the President.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

16. **Question:** The 91st Amendment Act (2003) provides that the total number of Ministers, including the PM, shall not exceed:
    - A) 10% of the total strength of Lok Sabha
    - B) 15% of the total strength of Lok Sabha
    - C) 20% of the total strength of Lok Sabha
    - D) 25% of the total strength of Lok Sabha
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act (2003) added Article 75(1A) providing that the total number of Ministers, including the PM, in the Council of Ministers shall not exceed 15% of the total number of members of the House of the People (Lok Sabha). Currently: max ~81 ministers.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

17. **Question:** Collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Lok Sabha is enshrined in:
    - A) Article 74
    - B) Article 75(3)
    - C) Article 78
    - D) Article 85
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 75(3) states: "The Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People." This means if the Lok Sabha passes a no-confidence motion, the entire Council of Ministers must resign, not just the individual minister.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

18. **Question:** Article 78 of the Indian Constitution deals with:
    - A) Appointment of the Prime Minister
    - B) Duties of the PM regarding furnishing information to the President
    - C) Removal procedure for the PM
    - D) PM's role in Rajya Sabha
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 78 prescribes three duties of the PM vis-à-vis the President: (a) communicate all decisions of the Council of Ministers, (b) furnish information on administration and legislation when asked, and (c) submit to the President matters on which a Minister has decided but the Council hasn't considered.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

19. **Question:** A Minister who is NOT a member of either House of Parliament:
    - A) Cannot be appointed as a Minister
    - B) Can remain a Minister indefinitely without being a member
    - C) Must become a member of either House within 6 months
    - D) Must become a member of Lok Sabha only within 6 months
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Article 75(5) states that a Minister who is not a member of either House of Parliament for a consecutive period of 6 months shall cease to be a Minister at the end of that period. They can get elected or nominated to either House.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

20. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT a function of the Cabinet?
    - A) Formulating national and international policies
    - B) Deciding major government appointments
    - C) Issuing writs for fundamental rights enforcement
    - D) Coordinating between various ministries
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Issuing writs is a judicial function performed by the Supreme Court (Article 32) and High Courts (Article 226), not the Cabinet. The Cabinet's functions include policy formulation, crisis management, coordination, and legislative agenda setting.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

21. **Question:** The concept of "Kitchen Cabinet" refers to:
    - A) A committee that manages government hospitality
    - B) An informal group of PM's trusted advisors outside the formal Cabinet structure
    - C) A Cabinet subcommittee on food policy
    - D) The official secretariat of the PM
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** "Kitchen Cabinet" is an informal term for the PM's inner circle of close, trusted advisors who may or may not be official Cabinet members but wield significant influence on decision-making. This is a political convention, not a constitutional body.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

## MT-ECON-G2P3-2-002 — Industrial Policy (7 Questions)

22. **Question:** The Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 divided industries into how many schedules?
    - A) 2
    - B) 3
    - C) 4
    - D) 5
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** IPR 1956 divided industries into three schedules: Schedule A (17 industries — exclusive state monopoly), Schedule B (12 industries — state progressively owns, private supplements), and Schedule C (remaining industries — open to private sector).
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

23. **Question:** The IPR 1956 was inspired by which economic model/plan?
    - A) Gandhian Plan
    - B) Bombay Plan
    - C) Mahalanobis Model (Second Five Year Plan)
    - D) Washington Consensus
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The IPR 1956 was closely aligned with the Mahalanobis Model which emphasized heavy industrialization under state control, forming the basis of the Second Five Year Plan. The stated goal was establishing a "Socialistic Pattern of Society."
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

24. **Question:** Under the New Industrial Policy, 1991, industrial licensing was retained for how many industries?
    - A) 3
    - B) 5
    - C) 8
    - D) 18
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The 1991 policy abolished licensing for all industries except 5 categories: (1) Distillation of alcoholic drinks, (2) Cigarettes/tobacco products, (3) Electronic aerospace and defence equipment, (4) Industrial explosives, and (5) Hazardous chemicals. This list has been further reduced over time.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

25. **Question:** The Make in India initiative was launched in:
    - A) 2010
    - B) 2014
    - C) 2016
    - D) 2020
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Make in India was launched on 25 September 2014 by PM Narendra Modi, identifying 25 key sectors for manufacturing and aimed at increasing manufacturing's share from ~15% to 25% of GDP. The logo features a lion made of cogs, symbolizing industrial strength.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

26. **Question:** The Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme was introduced to:
    - A) Subsidize agricultural production
    - B) Incentivize domestic manufacturing in key sectors through financial rewards based on incremental production
    - C) Provide free land for industrial setups
    - D) Guarantee minimum profit to manufacturers
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The PLI scheme (launched 2020 onwards) provides financial incentives (typically 4–6% of incremental sales) to eligible manufacturers in 14 key sectors including mobile phones, pharmaceuticals, electronics, textiles, automotive, and semiconductors to boost domestic manufacturing.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

27. **Question:** "Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyan" announced in 2020 had a total stimulus package of approximately:
    - A) ₹5 lakh crore
    - B) ₹10 lakh crore
    - C) ₹20 lakh crore
    - D) ₹50 lakh crore
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyan (Self-Reliant India Mission) announced in May 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic had a stimulus package of approximately ₹20 lakh crore (~10% of GDP), covering MSMEs, agriculture, industry, infrastructure, and social welfare measures.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

28. **Question:** The first Industrial Policy Resolution of 1948 placed how many industries under exclusive state monopoly?
    - A) 3
    - B) 6
    - C) 17
    - D) None — all industries were open to private sector
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** The IPR 1948 placed 3 industries under exclusive state monopoly: Arms & Ammunition, Atomic Energy, and Railways. Six more industries were listed for mixed (state + private) enterprise, while 18 were placed under regulated private sector.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-ENV-M5-6-004 — Environmental Clearance & EIA (7 Questions)

29. **Question:** The EIA Notification in India is issued under which parent legislation?
    - A) Wildlife Protection Act, 1972
    - B) Forest Conservation Act, 1980
    - C) Environment Protection Act, 1986
    - D) National Green Tribunal Act, 2010
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The EIA Notification, 2006 (and its subsequent amendments) is issued under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. This Act gives the central government powers to take measures for environmental protection, including setting up clearance procedures.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

30. **Question:** Under the EIA Notification, 2006, Category A projects require clearance from:
    - A) State Pollution Control Board
    - B) National Green Tribunal
    - C) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)
    - D) District Collector
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Category A projects (large-scale with potentially significant environmental impact) require Environmental Clearance from the MoEFCC at the central level, after appraisal by the Expert Appraisal Committee (EAC). Category B projects are cleared at the state level by SEIAA.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

31. **Question:** Public Hearing is mandatory for which category of projects under the EIA Notification?
    - A) Category A only
    - B) Category B2 only
    - C) Category A and Category B1
    - D) All categories including B2
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Public Hearing (also called Public Consultation) is mandatory for Category A and Category B1 projects. Category B2 projects are exempted from the requirement of EIA study and public hearing, though they still need environmental clearance.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

32. **Question:** The National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established under:
    - A) Environment Protection Act, 1986
    - B) Forest Conservation Act, 1980
    - C) National Green Tribunal Act, 2010
    - D) Wildlife Protection Act, 1972
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The NGT was established in 2010 under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 for effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to environmental protection and conservation of forests. It has the power to hear civil cases where a substantial question relating to environment is involved.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

33. **Question:** Article 48-A of the Indian Constitution, which directs the State to protect the environment, falls under:
    - A) Fundamental Rights
    - B) Directive Principles of State Policy
    - C) Fundamental Duties
    - D) Emergency Provisions
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 48-A (added by 42nd Amendment, 1976) falls under Part IV (DPSPs) and states: "The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country." The corresponding Fundamental Duty is Article 51A(g).
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

34. **Question:** The 2020 Draft EIA Notification was criticized mainly for proposing:
    - A) Stricter penalties for environmental violations
    - B) Ex-post-facto environmental clearance, which critics called "legalizing violations"
    - C) Complete ban on mining
    - D) Mandatory carbon tax
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The 2020 Draft EIA Notification was widely criticized for introducing ex-post-facto clearance (granting EC after violations), reducing public hearing requirements, extending compliance report periods from 6 months to 1 year, and exempting certain projects from public consultation.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

35. **Question:** Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA) differs from EIA because:
    - A) SEA is conducted at the individual project level
    - B) SEA is conducted at the policy, plan, or programme level rather than individual projects
    - C) SEA replaces the need for public hearings
    - D) SEA only applies to mining projects
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** While EIA assesses the environmental impact of individual projects, Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA) evaluates the environmental implications at a higher level — policies, plans, and programmes. SEA is not yet mandatory in India but is recommended for comprehensive environmental governance.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard
