## MT-HIST-M2-2-016 — Buddhism: Teachings, Spread & Decline

### 1. Life of the Buddha
- **Birth:** Lumbini (Nepal), 563 BCE; Siddhartha Gautama of the Shakya clan
- **The Great Renunciation (Mahabhinishkramana):** Left home at age 29
- **Enlightenment (Nirvana):** At Bodh Gaya under a Pipal (Bodhi) tree at age 35
- **First Sermon (Dharmachakra Pravartana):** At Sarnath (Deer Park) near Varanasi
- **Mahaparinirvana (Death):** At Kushinagar (UP), 483 BCE at age 80

### 2. Core Teachings
**Four Noble Truths (Arya Satya):**
1. **Dukkha:** Life is full of suffering
2. **Samudaya:** Suffering has a cause — desire/craving (Trishna)
3. **Nirodha:** Suffering can be ended
4. **Magga:** The path to end suffering is the Noble Eightfold Path

**Noble Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga):**
Right View, Right Intention, Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration

**Other Key Concepts:**
- **Middle Path:** Avoiding extremes of self-indulgence and self-mortification
- **Pratityasamutpada (Dependent Origination):** Everything arises in dependence upon conditions
- **Anatman (No-Self):** Rejection of permanent self/soul (differs from Hinduism's Atman)
- **Ahimsa:** Non-violence to all living beings

### 3. Buddhist Councils
| Council | Place | King | Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| First (483 BCE) | Rajagriha | Ajatashatru | Compilation of Vinaya Pitaka (Upali) & Sutta Pitaka (Ananda) |
| Second (383 BCE) | Vaishali | Kalashoka | Split into Sthaviravada & Mahasanghika |
| Third (250 BCE) | Pataliputra | Ashoka | Abhidhamma Pitaka compiled; missionaries sent abroad |
| Fourth (1st cent CE) | Kundalvana (Kashmir) | Kanishka | Buddhism split into Mahayana & Hinayana |

### 4. Schools of Buddhism
- **Theravada (Hinayana):** Original teachings; emphasis on individual salvation; Pali Canon; prevalent in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand
- **Mahayana:** Emphasis on universal salvation (Bodhisattva ideal); Sanskrit texts; China, Japan, Korea, Vietnam
- **Vajrayana:** Tantric Buddhism; rituals and mantras; Tibet, Mongolia

### 5. Spread & Decline in India
**Spread:** Ashoka sent missionaries to Sri Lanka (Mahinda), Central Asia, Southeast Asia
**Art:** Gandhara School (Greek influence, Buddha images), Mathura School (indigenous style), Amaravati School (dynamic reliefs)
**Decline Factors:** Rise of Hinduism (Shaivism, Vaishnavism), loss of royal patronage, absorption of Buddhist practices into Hinduism, Turkish invasions destroying monasteries (Nalanda, Vikramashila)

## MT-AP_ECON-G2P3-5-002 — AP Agriculture: Major Crops, Irrigation & Markets

### 1. Agricultural Profile of AP
- Agriculture contributes ~30% of AP's GSDP; employs ~60% of the workforce
- **Agro-climatic Zones:** AP has 6 agro-climatic zones — North Coastal, Godavari, Krishna, Southern, Rayalaseema, High-Altitude/Tribal
- **Net Sown Area:** ~60 lakh hectares; Gross Cropped Area: ~80 lakh hectares

### 2. Major Crops
| Crop | Key Districts/Areas | Season |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rice (Paddy)** | Krishna, Godavari, Nellore, Prakasam | Kharif & Rabi |
| **Groundnut** | Anantapur, Kurnool, Chittoor | Kharif |
| **Cotton** | Guntur, Prakasam, Kurnool | Kharif |
| **Chillies** | Guntur (largest producer in India) | Rabi |
| **Sugarcane** | Visakhapatnam, East Godavari | Perennial |
| **Tobacco** | Guntur, Prakasam | Rabi |
| **Turmeric** | Kadapa, Kurnool | — |
| **Mangoes** | Krishna, Chittoor (Banganapalle variety) | Summer |
| **Aquaculture** | Nellore, Krishna, West Godavari (shrimp) | Year-round |

### 3. Irrigation
- **Major Irrigation Sources:** Canals (Krishna, Godavari basins), Tanks, Borewells/Tubewells
- **Key Projects:**
  - **Polavaram (Indira Sagar) Project:** National project on Godavari river; aims to irrigate 2.91 lakh hectares; provides drinking water and hydropower
  - **Nagarjuna Sagar Dam:** On Krishna river; one of the largest in India; irrigates Krishna and Guntur
  - **Srisailam Dam:** On Krishna river; hydropower + irrigation
  - **Penna Ahobilam Balancing Reservoir (PABR):** For Rayalaseema irrigation
- **Micro-irrigation:** AP is a leader in drip and sprinkler irrigation adoption; government subsidizes through the AP Micro Irrigation Project (APMIP)
- **Groundwater Concerns:** Over-extraction in Rayalaseema; 50%+ of irrigation is groundwater-dependent

### 4. Agricultural Markets & Reforms
- **e-NAM (National Agriculture Market):** AP was one of the first adopters; online trading of agricultural commodities
- **Rythu Bharosa Kendras (RBKs):** One-stop service centres for farmers at village level; ~10,000+ RBKs established
- **Rythu Bharosa:** Direct benefit transfer of ₹13,500 per annum to farmers for input support
- **Market Yards (APMCs):** Government-regulated mandis; Guntur chilli market is the largest in Asia
- **Contract Farming:** Encouraged for horticulture and aquaculture; ITC e-Choupal model in AP

### 5. Key Issues & Initiatives
- **Climate Vulnerability:** Cyclones (coastal), drought (Rayalaseema), floods (Godavari delta)
- **Crop Insurance:** Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) coverage
- **AP AgriTech Initiative:** Use of drones, AI-based crop advisory, satellite monitoring
- **Zero-Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF):** AP pioneered large-scale adoption, now scaled to 6 lakh+ farmers

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-002 — Prime Minister & Council of Ministers

### 1. Appointment (Article 75)
- The President appoints the PM — typically the leader of the majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha
- Other Ministers are appointed by the President *on the advice of the PM*
- Ministers hold office "during the pleasure of the President" — in practice, this means during the PM's pleasure

### 2. Council of Ministers — Structure
- **Cabinet Ministers:** Attend Cabinet meetings; hold important portfolios (Defence, Finance, Home, External Affairs)
- **Ministers of State (Independent Charge):** Head a ministry without a Cabinet Minister above them
- **Ministers of State:** Assist Cabinet Ministers in their ministries
- **91st Amendment (2003):** Total number of Ministers (including PM) shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of Lok Sabha

### 3. Powers of the Prime Minister
- **Leader of the Cabinet:** Presides over Cabinet meetings; determines agenda
- **Link between President and Cabinet:** Communicates Cabinet decisions to the President (Article 78)
- **Allocates Portfolios:** Can reshuffle, create, or drop ministers
- **Leader of the House:** Leader of the Lok Sabha
- **Chairman of Key Bodies:** NITI Aayog, National Development Council, National Integration Council, Inter-State Council
- **Nuclear Button:** PM chairs the Nuclear Command Authority

### 4. Collective Responsibility (Article 75(3))
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
- If a No-Confidence Motion is passed, the entire Ministry must resign
- All ministers must publicly support Cabinet decisions (even if they personally disagree)
- A minister who disagrees must either toe the line or resign

### 5. Key Conventions
- PM should be from the Lok Sabha (convention, not constitutional mandate)
- Kitchen Cabinet: Inner circle of PM's trusted advisors (informal)
- PM can recommend dissolution of Lok Sabha to the President

## MT-ECON-G2P3-2-002 — Industrial Policy: 1948, 1956, 1991 & After

### 1. Industrial Policy Resolution, 1948
- First industrial policy of independent India
- Divided industries into 4 categories:
  1. **State Monopoly:** Arms, atomic energy, railways
  2. **State + Private (Mixed):** Coal, iron, aircraft, shipbuilding, telecom (6 industries)
  3. **Regulated Private Sector:** 18 industries of national importance
  4. **Open to Private Sector:** Remaining industries
- Established the role of the state as a major player in industrialization

### 2. Industrial Policy Resolution, 1956 (Most Important)
- Based on the Mahalanobis Model (Second Five Year Plan focus on heavy industries)
- Three categories (Schedules):
  - **Schedule A:** 17 industries — exclusive state monopoly
  - **Schedule B:** 12 industries — state progressively owns; private sector can supplement
  - **Schedule C:** Remaining — left to private sector
- "Socialistic Pattern of Society" as the stated goal
- Led to massive expansion of PSUs; established the "License-Permit Raj"

### 3. New Industrial Policy, 1991
**Context:** Economic crisis of 1991; IMF conditions
**Key Reforms:**
- **De-licensing:** Abolished industrial licensing for all but 5 strategic industries
- **De-reservation:** Reduced reserved areas for public sector from 17 to 8 (now just 2: atomic energy and railways)
- **MRTP Abolished:** No caps on asset size for companies
- **FDI Promotion:** Automatic approval for 51%+ FDI in many sectors
- **FERA → FEMA:** Permissive Foreign Exchange Management
- **Disinvestment:** Sale of government equity in PSEs began

### 4. Post-1991 Policy Evolution
- **National Manufacturing Policy (2011):** Aimed to increase manufacturing's GDP share to 25%
- **Make in India (2014):** 25 sectors identified; ease of doing business reforms
- **PLI Scheme (2020+):** Production-Linked Incentive for 14 sectors to boost domestic manufacturing
- **Startup India (2016):** Supporting innovation and entrepreneurship
- **Atmanirbhar Bharat (2020):** Self-reliant India initiative with ₹20 lakh crore stimulus

### 5. Current Industrial Scenario
- Services sector dominates (~55% of GDP); Manufacturing ~15–17%
- Key challenges: Infrastructure gaps, skill deficit, land acquisition, complex regulations
- Focus on: PLI scheme, semiconductor manufacturing, green energy manufacturing, defense production corridors

## MT-ENV-M5-6-004 — Environmental Clearance & EIA (Environmental Impact Assessment)

### 1. Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)
**Definition:** A systematic process of identifying, predicting, and evaluating the environmental effects of proposed projects before decisions are made.
- **Legal Basis:** EIA Notification, 2006 (under the Environment Protection Act, 1986)
- **Constitutional Basis:** Article 48-A (DPSP — protect environment) and Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty — protect environment)

### 2. EIA Process
1. **Screening:** Categorize project (Category A — Central clearance; Category B — State clearance)
2. **Scoping:** Define the terms of reference (ToR) for the EIA study
3. **Baseline Data Collection:** Study existing environmental conditions
4. **Impact Prediction:** Assess potential impacts on air, water, land, biodiversity, socio-economics
5. **Public Hearing:** Mandatory for Category A and B1 projects; affected communities participate
6. **EIA Report Preparation:** Comprehensive document with mitigation measures
7. **Expert Appraisal:** Expert Appraisal Committee (EAC) at central level; State Expert Appraisal Committee (SEAC) at state level
8. **Decision:** Grant or refuse Environmental Clearance (EC)

### 3. Categories of Projects
- **Category A:** Large-scale projects requiring central clearance from MoEFCC (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change). Examples: thermal power >500 MW, mining >50 hectares, large dams
- **Category B:** Smaller projects cleared at state level by State Environment Impact Assessment Authority (SEIAA)
  - **B1:** Require EIA study + public consultation
  - **B2:** Exempted from EIA study (but need clearance)

### 4. Key Features of EIA Notification, 2006
- Applies to 39 categories of developmental activities
- Validity of EC: 10 years for river valley/mining projects; 7 years for others (later extended to 10 years by 2020 amendment)
- EC conditions include: pollution monitoring, compensatory afforestation, rehabilitation plans
- **Post-clearance Monitoring:** Half-yearly compliance reports

### 5. Critical Issues & Reforms
- **2020 Draft EIA Notification (Controversial):** Proposed changes included ex-post-facto clearance (criticized as "legalizing violations"), reduced public hearing requirements, longer compliance report periods
- **Judicial Interventions:** National Green Tribunal (NGT) plays active role in enforcing EC conditions
- **Cumulative Impact Assessment:** Need for assessing combined impact of multiple projects in an area (currently weak)
- **Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA):** Assessment at policy/plan level (not yet mandatory in India)
- **Carbon Footprint Assessment:** Increasingly required for large industrial projects under climate commitments
