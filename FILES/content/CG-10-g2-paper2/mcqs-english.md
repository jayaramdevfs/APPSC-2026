## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I5-005 — AP Cultural Identity & Heritage (7 Questions)

1. **Question:** The Kavitrayam (Trinity of Telugu Poets) who translated the Mahabharata into Telugu are:
   - A) Krishnadevaraya, Peddana, Timmana
   - B) Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana
   - C) Vemana, Sumati, Yogi
   - D) Pothana, Srinatha, Dhurjati
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Nannaya Bhattaraka (11th century) started the Telugu translation of the Mahabharata, Tikkana (13th century) continued it, and Errana (14th century) completed the remaining portions. Together they are called the Kavitrayam.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

2. **Question:** Kalamkari, the traditional textile art form of Andhra Pradesh, is primarily associated with:
   - A) Machilipatnam and Srikalahasti
   - B) Tirupati and Vijayawada
   - C) Kurnool and Anantapur
   - D) Nellore and Ongole
   - **Answer:** A
   - **Explanation:** Kalamkari art has two distinct styles: Srikalahasti style (hand-painted using a pen/kalam) and Machilipatnam style (block-printed). Both have GI (Geographical Indication) tags.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

3. **Question:** Nagarjunakonda, an important Buddhist archaeological site in AP, was primarily built during the reign of:
   - A) Satavahana dynasty
   - B) Ikshvaku dynasty
   - C) Pallava dynasty
   - D) Kakatiya dynasty
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Nagarjunakonda flourished under the Ikshvaku dynasty (3rd–4th century CE) after the decline of the Satavahanas. It had several Buddhist monasteries, chaityas, and viharas. It is now partially submerged under the Nagarjuna Sagar dam.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

4. **Question:** Kuchipudi, the classical dance form of Andhra Pradesh, originated in:
   - A) Tirupati
   - B) Kuchipudi village, Krishna district
   - C) Amaravati
   - D) Kakinada
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Kuchipudi originated in the village of Kuchipudi (formerly Kuchelapuram) in Krishna district. Siddhendra Yogi (17th century) is credited with giving the dance form its present structure.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

5. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT a folk art form of Andhra Pradesh?
   - A) Burrakatha
   - B) Veedhi Bhagavatham
   - C) Yakshagana
   - D) Harikatha
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** Yakshagana is a folk art form primarily associated with Karnataka (Coastal Karnataka). Burrakatha, Veedhi Bhagavatham, and Harikatha are authentic AP folk/narrative art forms.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

6. **Question:** The Undavalli Caves, famous for their rock-cut architecture, are located near:
   - A) Vijayawada
   - B) Visakhapatnam
   - C) Amaravati
   - D) Kurnool
   - **Answer:** A
   - **Explanation:** The Undavalli Caves are located near Vijayawada in Guntur district. They are rock-cut caves dating to the 4th–5th century CE, featuring a large monolithic reclining Vishnu statue. They reflect Gupta-era architectural influence.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

7. **Question:** Which dynasty built the famous Amaravati Stupa, one of the largest in India?
   - A) Mauryas
   - B) Satavahanas
   - C) Pallavas
   - D) Kakatiyas
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The Amaravati Stupa (also called Mahachaitya) was constructed during the Satavahana period (2nd century BCE — 3rd century CE). It is known for its elaborate limestone relief panels depicting Jataka tales and scenes from Buddha's life.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I1-001 — Satavahana Contributions (7 Questions)

8. **Question:** The Satavahana dynasty is also known by which other name?
   - A) Pallavas
   - B) Andhras
   - C) Chalukyas
   - D) Rashtrakutas
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The Satavahanas are referred to as 'Andhras' in the Puranas, indicating their association with the Andhra region. They are considered the first significant dynasty of the Andhra-Deccan region.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

9. **Question:** Gautamiputra Satakarni is celebrated for:
   - A) Establishing trade routes to China
   - B) Defeating the Western Kshatrapas and re-establishing Satavahana power
   - C) Building the Sanchi Stupa
   - D) Converting to Buddhism
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Gautamiputra Satakarni (106–130 CE) is the greatest Satavahana ruler. His Nasik inscription (by his mother Gautami Balashri) describes him as the destroyer of the Shakas, Yavanas, and Pahlavas, and restorer of Satavahana prestige.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

10. **Question:** The Gathasaptashati, a famous collection of Prakrit poetry, was compiled by:
    - A) Nagarjuna
    - B) Ashoka
    - C) Hala
    - D) Simuka
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Hala, a Satavahana king (traditionally the 17th ruler), compiled the Gathasaptashati (Gaha Sattasai) — a collection of 700 verses in Prakrit dealing with love and courtly life. It is one of the earliest and finest collections of lyric poetry in India.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

11. **Question:** The Satavahanas significantly contributed to India's maritime trade. Which of the following ports was under their control?
    - A) Surat
    - B) Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam)
    - C) Calicut
    - D) Cochin
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Machilipatnam (ancient Masulipatnam) was an important port under Satavahana control. The finding of Roman coins and artefacts at Satavahana sites confirms their active participation in Indian Ocean trade with Rome.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

12. **Question:** The official language of the Satavahana administration was:
    - A) Sanskrit
    - B) Pali
    - C) Prakrit
    - D) Telugu
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The Satavahanas used Prakrit as their official language and inscribed their records in Brahmi script. Early traces of Telugu words have been found in some Satavahana-era inscriptions, but Prakrit was the primary administrative language.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

13. **Question:** Which of the following is a characteristic feature of the Amaravati School of Art?
    - A) Greco-Roman influence on Buddha images
    - B) Narrative limestone relief panels with dynamic compositions
    - C) Stone-cut cave temples with elaborate pillars
    - D) Bronze casting of Hindu deities
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Amaravati School is known for its naturalistic limestone relief sculptures with dynamic compositions depicting Jataka tales and events from Buddha's life. Unlike Gandhara, it has indigenous Indian stylistic influence.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

14. **Question:** The Bhattiprolu inscription is significant because:
    - A) It is the earliest known Sanskrit inscription in India
    - B) It contains early traces of Telugu-Brahmi script
    - C) It records the founding of the Satavahana dynasty
    - D) It is the oldest Buddhist text in India
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Bhattiprolu inscription (near Amaravati, Guntur district) is significant for containing an early form of Brahmi script with modifications that later evolved into the Telugu-Kannada script. It dates to the 2nd–3rd century BCE.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I4-002 — Role of AP in Freedom Struggle (7 Questions)

15. **Question:** The Chirala-Perala movement is unique because:
    - A) It was an armed tribal revolt
    - B) Residents abandoned their town as a form of protest against municipal taxes
    - C) It was led by a woman freedom fighter
    - D) It resulted in the first electoral victory for Congress in AP
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** In the Chirala-Perala movement (1921), residents of these twin towns in Prakasam district, led by Duggirala Gopalakrishna, abandoned their homes and lived outside municipal limits to protest against excessive municipal taxes — a unique Gandhian experiment.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

16. **Question:** Alluri Sitarama Raju is famous for leading the:
    - A) Salt Satyagraha in Machilipatnam
    - B) Rampa Rebellion (1922–24) against British in the tribal areas of East Godavari
    - C) Quit India Movement in Guntur
    - D) Non-Cooperation Movement in Rajahmundry
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Alluri Sitarama Raju led a tribal armed revolt in the Rampa/Manyam region of East Godavari (1922–24) against British forest policies and the muttadari system. He is called 'Manyam Veerudu' (Hero of the Forests) and was shot dead by the British in 1924.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

17. **Question:** Tanguturi Prakasam earned the title "Andhra Kesari" because:
    - A) He designed the Indian flag
    - B) He bared his chest before British guns during a protest
    - C) He led the Rampa revolt
    - D) He was the first CM of Andhra Pradesh
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** During a protest march in Madras, when British police opened fire, Tanguturi Prakasam reportedly bared his chest and dared them to shoot, earning the title 'Andhra Kesari' (Lion of Andhra). He later served as Chief Minister of the Madras Presidency.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

18. **Question:** Who designed the basic concept of the Indian National Flag (tricolour)?
    - A) Mahatma Gandhi
    - B) Pingali Venkayya
    - C) Jawaharlal Nehru
    - D) Sarojini Naidu
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Pingali Venkayya, a freedom fighter from Machilipatnam (Krishna district, AP), designed the basic concept of the Indian tricolour. He first presented his designs to Mahatma Gandhi at the Bezwada (Vijayawada) Congress Session in 1921.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

19. **Question:** The Vandemataram Movement in Andhra (1907–08) was strongest in:
    - A) Hyderabad and Secunderabad
    - B) Visakhapatnam and Srikakulam
    - C) Rajahmundry, Machilipatnam and Guntur
    - D) Tirupati and Kurnool
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The Vandemataram Movement found strong support in Rajahmundry, Machilipatnam, and Guntur, where local leaders organized public meetings, national schools, and boycott of foreign goods. Bipin Chandra Pal's visit further energized the movement.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

20. **Question:** The Rampa Rebellion of 1879 was a:
    - A) Peasant revolt against Ryotwari settlement
    - B) Tribal uprising against colonial muttadari system and forest restrictions
    - C) Landlord revolt against permanent settlement
    - D) Student agitation against British education policy
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The 1879 Rampa Rebellion was a tribal revolt in the hill areas of East Godavari against the British colonial muttadari system and restrictions on tribal access to forests. It preceded the more famous Alluri Sitarama Raju revolt of 1922–24.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

21. **Question:** Potti Sriramulu's fast unto death in 1952 was for the demand of:
    - A) Independence from British rule
    - B) A separate Telangana state
    - C) A separate Andhra state from Madras Presidency
    - D) Abolition of zamindari system
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Potti Sriramulu fasted unto death (16 October – 15 December 1952) demanding the creation of a separate state for Telugu-speaking people from the Madras Presidency. His death on 15 December 1952 led to widespread protests and the formation of Andhra State in 1953.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I2-001 — Vijayanagar Influence on AP Culture (7 Questions)

22. **Question:** Krishnadevaraya authored which famous Telugu literary work?
    - A) Manu Charitra
    - B) Ramayanam
    - C) Amuktamalyada
    - D) Bhagavatam
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Krishnadevaraya authored 'Amuktamalyada' (She Who Wears the Garland Already Offered to the Lord), a Telugu prabandha kavya (devotional poem) about the life of Andal/Goda Devi. It is considered one of the Pancha Kavyas (Five Great Telugu Poems).
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

23. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT one of the Ashtadiggajas (Eight Poets) of Krishnadevaraya's court?
    - A) Allasani Peddana
    - B) Tenali Ramakrishna
    - C) Nannaya Bhattaraka
    - D) Pingali Surana
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Nannaya Bhattaraka (11th century) predated Krishnadevaraya by several centuries and belonged to the Eastern Chalukya court. The Ashtadiggajas include Allasani Peddana, Nandi Timmana, Dhurjati, Tenali Ramakrishna, and others.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

24. **Question:** The Lepakshi Temple, famous for its hanging pillar, was built during the:
    - A) Chola period
    - B) Maurya period
    - C) Vijayanagar period
    - D) Satavahana period
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The Veerabhadra Temple at Lepakshi (Anantapur district) was built during the Vijayanagar period by Virupanna and Viranna, governors (Nayaks) under Achyutadevaraya. It is famous for its hanging pillar, monolithic Nandi, and beautiful frescoes.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

25. **Question:** Chandragiri Fort is historically significant for the Vijayanagar Empire because:
    - A) It was the birthplace of Krishnadevaraya
    - B) It was where the British obtained a land grant for Fort St. George (Madras)
    - C) It was the site of the Battle of Talikota
    - D) It was the largest fort in the Deccan
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Chandragiri Fort in Tirupati (Chittoor district) was the last capital of the Vijayanagar Empire under the Aravidu dynasty. In 1639, the local Vijayanagar governor granted the British East India Company permission to build Fort St. George (Chennai), from Chandragiri.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

26. **Question:** The Nayankara System of the Vijayanagar Empire was:
    - A) A revenue collection system based on land measurement
    - B) A system of military governors (Nayakas) who were granted territories
    - C) A judicial system based on Islamic law
    - D) A taxation system for foreign traders
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Under the Nayankara system, military commanders (Nayakas) were granted territorial units in return for military service and revenue collection. This feudal system was central to Vijayanagar's administration of its vast territories, including Andhra.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

27. **Question:** "Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa" (Among the languages of the land, Telugu is the finest) is attributed to:
    - A) Nannaya
    - B) Vemana
    - C) Krishnadevaraya
    - D) Tikkana
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** This famous declaration praising Telugu was made by Emperor Krishnadevaraya, reflecting his deep patronage and love for Telugu language and literature. He himself composed poetry in Telugu, Sanskrit, and Kannada.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

28. **Question:** Allasani Peddana, considered the foremost among the Ashtadiggajas, authored:
    - A) Amuktamalyada
    - B) Manu Charitra (Swarochisha Manu Sambhava)
    - C) Vasu Charitra
    - D) Kala Purnodayam
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Allasani Peddana, titled 'Andhra Kavita Pitamaha' (Grandfather of Telugu Poetry), wrote Manu Charitra (Swarochisha Manu Sambhava), which is considered the finest example of the Prabandha genre in Telugu literature.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I5-001 — States Reorganization Act, 1956 (7 Questions)

29. **Question:** The first state in India formed on a linguistic basis was:
    - A) Kerala
    - B) Karnataka
    - C) Andhra State
    - D) Tamil Nadu
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Andhra State was formed on 1 October 1953, carved out of the Telugu-speaking areas of the Madras Presidency, making it the first state in India formed on a linguistic basis. This was triggered by Potti Sriramulu's death during his fast.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

30. **Question:** The States Reorganisation Commission (Fazal Ali Commission) was established in:
    - A) 1948
    - B) 1950
    - C) 1953
    - D) 1956
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The States Reorganisation Commission was established in December 1953 under the chairmanship of Justice Fazal Ali, with K.M. Panikkar and Hridaynath Kunzru as members. It submitted its report in 1955, leading to the SRA, 1956.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

31. **Question:** The Gentlemen's Agreement (1956) was between:
    - A) India and Pakistan regarding Kashmir
    - B) Leaders of Andhra State and Telangana regarding safeguards for Telangana
    - C) Congress and Muslim League regarding partition
    - D) Centre and States regarding financial distribution
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Gentlemen's Agreement was signed on 20 February 1956 between the leaders of Andhra State and Telangana region, providing safeguards for Telangana in the proposed merger. Safeguards included proportional spending, representation, and a regional committee.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

32. **Question:** The States Reorganisation Act, 1956 reorganized India into:
    - A) 12 States and 4 Union Territories
    - B) 14 States and 6 Union Territories
    - C) 16 States and 8 Union Territories
    - D) 28 States and 7 Union Territories
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The SRA, 1956 reorganized India into 14 States and 6 Union Territories, abolishing the Part-A, Part-B, Part-C, and Part-D categorization of states that existed since 1950.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

33. **Question:** The Dhar Commission (1948) and JVP Committee (1948) both:
    - A) Strongly recommended linguistic reorganization of states
    - B) Opposed or cautioned against immediate linguistic reorganization
    - C) Recommended abolition of Princely States
    - D) Proposed the creation of Andhra Pradesh
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Both the Dhar Commission (headed by Justice S.K. Dhar) and the JVP Committee (Nehru-Patel-Sitaramayya) cautioned against or advised against the immediate reorganization of states on linguistic lines, citing threats to national unity and administrative disruption.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

34. **Question:** Andhra Pradesh was formed on 1 November 1956 by merging:
    - A) Coastal Andhra with Rayalaseema
    - B) Andhra State with Telugu-speaking areas of Hyderabad State
    - C) Madras Presidency with Hyderabad State
    - D) All Telugu-speaking areas of South India
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Andhra Pradesh was formed by merging the Andhra State (which included Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema, carved from Madras Presidency in 1953) with the Telugu-speaking Telangana region of the former Hyderabad State.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

35. **Question:** The Sri Bagh Pact (1937) was an agreement between:
    - A) Leaders of Coastal Andhra and Telangana
    - B) Leaders of Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema
    - C) India and Hyderabad State
    - D) Congress and the Nizam
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Sri Bagh Pact was signed in 1937 between leaders of Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema regions to address Rayalaseema's concerns about being dominated by the more developed Coastal Andhra region in a future united Telugu state.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard
