## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I5-005 — AP Cultural Identity & Heritage Preservation

### 1. Composite Cultural Heritage
Andhra Pradesh boasts a rich composite culture forged over millennia through the convergence of Hindu, Buddhist, Islamic, and tribal traditions.
- **Religious Pluralism:** Temples (Tirupati, Srisailam), Buddhist Stupas (Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda), Mosques (Qutb Shahi era), and Churches coexist.
- **Telugu Language & Literature:** One of the six classical languages of India; Nannaya, Tikkana, and Errana's Trinity (Kavitrayam) translated Mahabharata into Telugu.
- **Art Forms:** Kuchipudi dance (classical), Burrakatha (folk narrative), Harikatha, Veedhi Bhagavatham, Chindu Bhagavatham.

### 2. Architectural Heritage
- **Buddhist Architecture:** Amaravati Stupa (2nd century BCE), Nagarjunakonda (Ikshvaku dynasty), earliest examples of narrative art in India.
- **Hindu Temple Architecture:** Chalukyan and Pallava influences; Lepakshi temple, Srisailam temple, Ahobilam.
- **Islamic Architecture:** Qutb Shahi tombs, Golconda Fort, Charminar (though now in Telangana, historically shared heritage).
- **Kalamkari:** Traditional hand-painted or block-printed textile art from Srikalahasti and Machilipatnam.

### 3. Cultural Contributions by Different Dynasties
- **Satavahanas:** Promoted Prakrit and early Telugu; patronised Buddhism and Hinduism equally.
- **Ikshvakus:** Major Buddhist patrons at Nagarjunakonda; important Brahmanical Ashvamedha sacrifices.
- **Eastern Chalukyas:** Developed Vengi as a centre of Telugu literature; architects of early Telugu prose.
- **Kakatiyas:** Fostered Telugu identity; Rudrama Devi (one of India's earliest women rulers); Warangal Fort's distinctive gateway architecture.
- **Vijayanagar:** Golden age of Telugu literature and arts under Krishnadevaraya.

### 4. Heritage Preservation Efforts
- **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI):** Maintains protected monuments like Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda, Undavalli caves.
- **State Archaeology Department:** Documents and protects lesser-known heritage sites.
- **UNESCO Recognition:** Amaravati stupas under consideration; Buddhist heritage circuit promotion.
- **Intangible Heritage:** Kuchipudi, Burrakatha, and other folk arts receive government patronage and festival platforms.

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I1-001 — Satavahana Contributions to AP Culture

### 1. Origin and Political History
- **Also known as:** Andhras (mentioned in Puranas)
- **Period:** c. 230 BCE – 220 CE
- **Founder:** Simuka; most prominent ruler: Gautamiputra Satakarni (106–130 CE)
- **Capital:** Pratishthana (Paithan, Maharashtra); later centres at Amaravati and Dhanyakataka (Dharanikota)
- **Extent:** At peak, controlled the entire Deccan from Western to Eastern coast, including modern AP, Telangana, Maharashtra, Karnataka.

### 2. Administrative Contributions
- **Provincial Administration:** Kingdom divided into Aharas (districts) and Ratthikas
- Titles like Rajan, Maharathi, Mahasenapati indicate a feudal administrative structure
- **Trade & Commerce:** Major role in Indian Ocean trade; ports like Masulipatnam (Machilipatnam), Ghantasala facilitated trade with Rome
- Roman gold coins found at excavation sites confirm flourishing trade

### 3. Cultural & Religious Patronage
**Buddhism:**
- Patronised both Buddhism and Hinduism
- Constructed stupas at Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda
- Nagarjuna, the great Buddhist philosopher, lived during the Satavahana period
- Amaravati School of Art: Known for narrative panels depicting Jataka tales and Buddha's life

**Hinduism:**
- Performed Vedic sacrifices like Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice)
- Gautamiputra Satakarni described as a Brahmana who destroyed Kshatriyas (Nasik inscription)

### 4. Art & Architecture
- **Amaravati Stupa:** One of the largest stupas in India; elaborate limestone relief panels
- **Art Style:** Naturalistic sculpture with dynamic compositions; influence on Southeast Asian art
- **Nagarjunakonda:** Major archaeological site with remains of viharas, chaityas, and stupa complexes
- **Coinage:** Issued coins with bilingual (Prakrit + Telugu-Brahmi) legends; images of ships indicating maritime trade

### 5. Language & Literature
- **Official Language:** Prakrit; inscriptions in Brahmi script
- **Gathasaptashati (Gaha Sattasai):** Compiled by Hala, a Satavahana king; collection of 700 verses in Prakrit — one of the earliest works of Indian love poetry
- Early traces of Telugu language found in Satavahana-era inscriptions (like the Bhattiprolu inscription)

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I4-002 — Role of AP in Freedom Struggle

### 1. Early Resistance (Pre-1885)
- **Polygar Rebellions:** Local chieftains in Rayalaseema and coastal Andhra resisted British land revenue policies
- **Rampa Rebellion (1879):** Tribal uprising in Rampa (East Godavari) against colonial muttadari system
- **Impact of 1857:** Though limited in Andhra, some revolts in the Carnatic Nawab's territories

### 2. National Movement Phase (1885–1920)
- **Gadicherla Hari Sarvottama Rao:** One of the earliest Congress leaders from Andhra
- **Konda Venkatappayya:** Played a crucial role in establishing Congress presence in Andhra
- **Vandemataram Movement (1907–08):** Strong participation in Rajahmundry, Machilipatnam, and Guntur; Bipin Chandra Pal's visit galvanized the movement
- **Home Rule Movement:** Annie Besant's Home Rule League had active cells in Guntur and Machilipatnam

### 3. Gandhian Era (1920–1947)
**Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22):**
- Massive response in Chirala-Perala (Prakasam district) — residents refused to pay taxes and relocated outside municipal limits (unique peaceful protest led by Duggirala Gopalakrishna)
- Tanguturi Prakasam (later "Andhra Kesari") emerged as a prominent leader

**Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34):**
- Salt Satyagraha on the coast of Krishna and Guntur districts
- Women's significant participation, especially in picketing liquor shops

**Alluri Sitarama Raju & Rampa Revolt (1922–24):**
- Tribal leader who led armed resistance against British in the Manyam (hill) areas of East Godavari
- Combined tribal grievances with Gandhian ideals; became a folk hero

**Quit India Movement (1942):**
- Strong response across Andhra; underground activities, disruption of communications
- Potti Sriramulu, Nandamuri Taraka Rama Rao (NTR) generation were inspired

### 4. Key Leaders from AP
| Leader | Contribution |
| :--- | :--- |
| Tanguturi Prakasam | "Andhra Kesari"; baredhis chest to British bullets; CM of Madras Presidency |
| Alluri Sitarama Raju | Led Rampa tribal revolt against British |
| Pingali Venkayya | Designed the Indian National Flag (tricolour concept) |
| Potti Sriramulu | Fasted unto death for separate Andhra State (1952) |
| Duggirala Gopalakrishna | Led Chirala-Perala agitation |
| Konda Venkatappayya | Pioneer of Congress movement in Andhra |

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I2-001 — Vijayanagar Influence on AP Culture

### 1. Political Overview
- **Founded:** 1336 CE by Harihara I and Bukka Raya I (Sangama dynasty)
- **Location:** Capital at Hampi (Karnataka), but ruled over extensive AP territories
- **Peak:** Under Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529), the Tuluva dynasty; considered the greatest ruler
- **Decline:** After Battle of Talikota (1565); remnant Aravidu dynasty continued from Penukonda and Chandragiri in AP

### 2. Administration in AP
- The Vijayanagar Empire administered Andhra through Nayankara system (feudal governors called Nayakas)
- Major Nayak centres in AP: Madurai, Tanjore, Gingee, and importantly Penukonda, Chandragiri
- Revenue system: Land was classified and assessed; **Rayarekha** system used for revenue measurement

### 3. Telugu Language & Literature
**The Golden Age of Telugu Literature:**
- Krishnadevaraya himself was a Telugu poet: authored *Amuktamalyada* (one of the Pancha Kavyas)
- He declared: "Desa bhashalandu Telugu lessa" (Among the languages of the land, Telugu is the finest)
- **Ashtadiggajas (Eight Poets of Krishnadevaraya's Court):** Allasani Peddana (Manu Charitra), Nandi Timmana, Dhurjati, Madayyagari Mallana, Ayyalaraju Ramabhadrudu, Pingali Surana, Ramaraju Bhushanudu, Tenali Ramakrishna
- Prabandha style of Telugu poetry reached its peak during this period

### 4. Art, Architecture & Culture in AP
- **Lepakshi Temple (Anantapur):** Hanging pillar, monolithic Nandi, exquisite Vijayanagar-style frescoes
- **Penukonda Fort:** Second capital of Vijayanagar; architectural remains include temples and fortifications
- **Chandragiri Fort:** Last capital of the Vijayanagar Empire; where the British obtained the land grant for establishing Fort St. George (Madras)
- **Sculpture:** Dravidian style with elaborate gopurams, pillared halls (mandapas), and yali motifs
- **Music & Dance:** Flourishing of Carnatic music and dance forms; patronage of Kuchipudi

### 5. Economic Impact
- Greatly developed irrigation infrastructure in AP
- Promoted foreign trade through ports like Machilipatnam
- The empire's economic prosperity attracted travelers like Niccolò de' Conti, Abdur Razzaq, and Domingo Paes

## MT-AP_HIST-G2P2-I5-001 — States Reorganization Act, 1956

### 1. Background
- **Demand for Linguistic States:** The idea of reorganizing states on linguistic lines was a longstanding demand, rooted in the Indian National Congress resolution (Nagpur Session, 1920)
- **Andhra Movement:** The demand for a separate Telugu-speaking state gained momentum after independence; Potti Sriramulu's fast unto death (16 October – 15 December 1952) and subsequent death triggered public unrest
- **Formation of Andhra State (1953):** Carved out from the Madras Presidency; first state formed on a linguistic basis, with Kurnool as its capital

### 2. Commissions & Committees
- **Dhar Commission (1948):** Opposed linguistic reorganization on the grounds that it would threaten national unity
- **JVP Committee (1948):** Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya — also advised against immediate reorganization
- **Fazal Ali Commission (States Reorganisation Commission, 1953):**
  - Members: Justice Fazal Ali (Chairman), K.M. Panikkar, Hridaynath Kunzru
  - Recommended reorganization broadly on linguistic lines with due consideration for administrative convenience, financial viability, and national unity
  - Submitted report in 1955

### 3. States Reorganisation Act, 1956
- Enacted on 31 August 1956; came into force on 1 November 1956
- Combined with the 7th Constitutional Amendment Act
- **Key Outcomes:**
  - Merged Andhra State with Hyderabad State's Telugu-speaking areas → **Andhra Pradesh** (1 November 1956)
  - Reorganized India into 14 States and 6 Union Territories
  - Abolished Part-B (erstwhile Princely States) and Part-C (Commissioner's Provinces) states

### 4. Formation of Andhra Pradesh
- **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956):** Between leaders of Andhra State and Telangana region regarding safeguards for Telangana
  - Safeguards included: proportional representation, expenditure in Telangana region from its revenues, governance by a Chief Minister from Telangana (rotational), Regional Council for Telangana
- **Hyderabad as Capital:** Common capital for the new merged state
- **Visalandhra Movement:** The campaign for merging all Telugu-speaking areas into one state (Visalandhra = Greater Andhra)

### 5. Aftermath & Legacy
- The safeguards promised in the Gentlemen's Agreement were not fully implemented, sowing seeds for the separate Telangana movement
- Andhra Pradesh was eventually bifurcated in 2014 under the AP Reorganisation Act → creating Telangana and residuary AP
- The SRA, 1956 set the template for all future state reorganizations in India
