# CG-07: AP-Specific (History, Geography, Economy)
## Study Notes (English)

### 1. AP History & Culture — Dynasties, Satavahanas, Kakatiyas

*   **Satavahanas (c. 230 BCE – 220 CE):**
    *   One of the earliest ruling dynasties in the Andhra region (Deccan). Also known as Andhras in Puranic literature.
    *   **Simuka** was the founder of the dynasty.
    *   **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (106–130 CE) was the greatest ruler — defeated the Shakas, Yavanas, and Pahlavas; known as "destroyer of the pride of Kshatriyas."
    *   Capital: Pratishthana (Paithan); later shifted influence to Amaravati and Dharanikota.
    *   **Amaravati Stupa** — magnificent Buddhist site built during this era, showing advanced sculptural art.
    *   Land revenue was the main source of income; promoted trade through ports like Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam).
    *   Patronised both Buddhism and Hinduism; Prakrit was the official language.

*   **Ikshvakus (c. 225–340 CE):**
    *   Succeeded the Satavahanas in the Krishna-Guntur region.
    *   Capital: Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda).
    *   Patronised Buddhism — Nagarjunakonda is a major Buddhist archaeological site.

*   **Kakatiyas (c. 1083–1323 CE):**
    *   One of the most important dynasties in Telugu history; originally feudatories of the Late Chalukyas of Kalyani.
    *   Capital: **Orugallu (Warangal)**.
    *   **Prataparudra I (Rudradeva)** built the famous **Thousand Pillar Temple** at Hanamkonda (Warangal).
    *   **Ganapati Deva** (1199–1262) was the most powerful Kakatiya ruler; expanded the kingdom to its greatest extent.
    *   **Rudrama Devi** — one of the few women rulers in Indian history; succeeded Ganapati Deva.
    *   **Prataparudra II** — the last Kakatiya ruler; defeated by Ulugh Khan (Muhammad-bin-Tughlaq's general) in 1323.
    *   Kakatiyas built extensive **tank irrigation systems** (constructed over 1000 tanks), which became the backbone of agriculture in the Deccan.
    *   Promoted Telugu language and literature; the Kakatiya era is known as the golden age of Telugu culture.

*   **Qutb Shahis of Golconda (1518–1687 CE):**
    *   Arose from the ruins of the Bahmani Sultanate.
    *   **Sultan Quli Qutb Shah** (1518–1543) — founder; declared independence from the Bahmanis in 1518.
    *   **Ibrahim Qutb Shah** (1550–1580) — real architect of the kingdom; consolidated administration; participated in the Battle of Tallikota (1565) against Vijayanagara.
    *   **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** (1580–1612) — founded **Hyderabad** in 1591; built the iconic **Charminar**; great patron of Deccani Urdu poetry.
    *   **Abul Hasan** (1672–1687) — last Qutb Shahi ruler; kingdom annexed by Aurangzeb in 1687.
    *   Composite culture: blending of Persian, Telugu, and Deccani traditions.
    *   Diamond mines at Kolluru (Kohinoor diamond discovered here); Machilipatnam famous for muslin and trade.

*   **Vijayanagara Empire and AP:**
    *   The Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646) encompassed large parts of Andhra Pradesh including Rayalaseema and Penugonda.
    *   **Sri Krishnadevaraya** (1509–1529) — greatest ruler; patron of Telugu literature (Ashtadiggajas).
    *   After the Battle of Tallikota (1565), the empire declined; many territories came under Qutb Shahi and later Mughal control.

### 2. AP Bifurcation — Issues, Polavaram, Special Status

*   **Background — Movement for Separate Andhra State:**
    *   Telugu-speaking people were part of the Madras Presidency under British rule.
    *   **Andhra Mahasabha** (First session at Bapatla, 1913) — organised to demand a separate state for Telugu people.
    *   **Potti Sriramulu** — fasted unto death (Oct 19 – Dec 15, 1952) demanding a separate Andhra State.
    *   **Andhra State** formed on October 1, 1953 (carved out of Madras State), with Kurnool as the first capital.
    *   **Sri Bagh Pact (1937)** — agreement between Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema leaders to protect Rayalaseema's interests.
    *   **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** — between Andhra and Telangana leaders before formation of united AP.
    *   **Andhra Pradesh** formed on November 1, 1956 by merging Andhra State with the Telangana region of Hyderabad State, based on the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.

*   **AP Bifurcation (2014):**
    *   The **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014** divided the state into Telangana and residuary Andhra Pradesh on **June 2, 2014**.
    *   **Key Issues post-bifurcation:**
        *   Loss of Hyderabad (joint capital for 10 years until 2024).
        *   Revenue deficit — AP lost significant tax base and IT sector revenue.
        *   Need for new capital — **Amaravati** selected as the new capital city.
        *   Division of assets and liabilities — institutions, government properties, and employees.
        *   River water sharing disputes — particularly Krishna and Godavari waters.

*   **Polavaram Multi-Purpose Project:**
    *   **National Project** on the **Godavari River** in West Godavari district.
    *   Declared a national project under the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014.
    *   Objectives: Irrigation for 2.91 lakh hectares, drinking water for 28.50 lakh people, and 960 MW hydropower generation.
    *   The project will link the Godavari and Krishna rivers.
    *   One of the largest irrigation projects in India; considered the "lifeline of Andhra Pradesh."

*   **Special Category Status (SCS) Demand:**
    *   AP has been demanding SCS post-bifurcation to compensate for revenue losses.
    *   SCS provides 90:10 Centre-State funding (instead of normal 60:40 or 70:30).
    *   The 14th Finance Commission recommended that benefits equivalent to SCS be given through special packages.
    *   The demand remains a major political issue.

### 3. AP Development — Amaravati, Industrial Corridors

*   **Amaravati — New Capital City:**
    *   Located between Vijayawada and Guntur on the banks of the **Krishna River**.
    *   Area: Approximately 217 sq. km.
    *   Envisioned as a world-class capital with modern infrastructure.
    *   Named after the ancient Buddhist site of **Amaravati** (Satavahana era).

*   **Swarna Andhra@2047 Vision:**
    *   Goal to transform AP into a **$2.4 trillion economy** by 2047 with per capita income of $42,000.
    *   Requires an annual growth rate of 15%.
    *   Built on "Padi Sutralu" (Ten Guiding Principles) — "wealthy, healthy, and happy" state.

*   **Industrial Corridors:**
    *   **Vizag-Chennai Industrial Corridor (VCIC):** A key corridor connecting Visakhapatnam to Chennai, part of the larger East Coast Economic Corridor (ECEC). Funded with World Bank support.
    *   **Chennai-Bangalore Industrial Corridor:** Extends industrial growth into Rayalaseema.
    *   **Industrial Development Policy (4.0) 2024–29:** Latest policy focusing on sustainability, AI/Industry 4.0, and global value chain integration.

*   **Key Focus Sectors:**
    *   **Sustenance Sectors:** Automobiles, Chemicals, Pharma, Food Processing, Textiles, Blue Economy.
    *   **Propelling Sectors:** Aerospace, Defence, Electric Vehicles, Biotechnology, Semiconductors, Green Energy, Ports & Logistics.

*   **Manufacturing sector** accounts for about **11% of GSDP**.

### 4. AP Agriculture — Major Crops, Irrigation Projects

*   **Agricultural Profile:**
    *   AP is primarily an agrarian economy; agriculture is the backbone of the state's economy.
    *   **Major Crops:**
        *   **Rice (Paddy):** Principal food crop, extensively grown in Krishna and Godavari deltas.
        *   **Cotton:** Grown in black soil areas of Guntur, Kurnool, Anantapur.
        *   **Tobacco:** Guntur district is famous for flue-cured Virginia tobacco.
        *   **Chillies (Mirchi):** Guntur is the largest producer; traded nationally and internationally.
        *   **Groundnut:** Major oilseed crop in Rayalaseema districts (Anantapur, Kurnool).
        *   **Sugarcane:** Grown in East Godavari, Visakhapatnam, and Srikakulam.
        *   **Turmeric:** Grown in Kadapa, Kurnool, and Guntur.
    *   AP is the **largest producer of chillies, eggs, and aquaculture products** in India.
    *   **Horticulture:** Mango (Banganapalli variety), banana, citrus, oil palm.

*   **Major Irrigation Projects:**
    *   **Nagarjuna Sagar Dam:** On the Krishna River; one of the largest masonry dams in the world.
    *   **Srisailam Dam:** On the Krishna River; multipurpose — hydropower and irrigation.
    *   **Polavaram Project:** On the Godavari (National Project — see Section 2).
    *   **Tungabhadra Dam:** Shared between AP and Karnataka; irrigates parts of Rayalaseema.
    *   **Prakasam Barrage:** On the Krishna River at Vijayawada; diverts water for irrigation.
    *   **Somasila Dam:** On the Pennar River in Nellore district.
    *   AP Government emphasizes drip and sprinkler irrigation, and rainwater harvesting for water management.

*   **e-NAM (e-National Agriculture Market):**
    *   Electronic trading platform operating in 33 markets across AP.
    *   Commodities: Dry chillies, cotton, turmeric, lemons, groundnuts, etc.
    *   As of December 2024: 9.01 lakh MT of commodities traded worth Rs. 5,954.96 crores.

*   **Rythu Bazars:** 124 farmer markets operational, providing fair prices for both farmers and consumers.

### 5. AP Infrastructure — Ports, Highways, Corridors

*   **Ports:**
    *   AP has the **third-longest coastline in India** (~974 km along the Bay of Bengal).
    *   **Six operational ports:** Visakhapatnam (Major Port under GoI), Krishnapatnam, Gangavaram, Kakinada, Machilipatnam, and Rawa.
    *   **Visakhapatnam Port:** The only major port in AP; handles bulk cargo, containers, and petroleum products. Also houses the Eastern Naval Command of the Indian Navy.
    *   **Krishnapatnam Port:** One of the largest private ports in India (Nellore district).
    *   **Gangavaram Port:** Deep-water, all-weather port near Visakhapatnam; handles coal, iron ore, and fertilizers.
    *   AP's port infrastructure is critical for its "Gateway to the East" vision for Southeast Asian trade.

*   **Highways and Road Connectivity:**
    *   **NH-16 (formerly NH-5):** The main arterial highway running along the AP coast from Chennai to Kolkata via Nellore, Vijayawada, Rajahmundry, and Visakhapatnam.
    *   **NH-65:** Connects Hyderabad to Vijayawada (internal connectivity corridor).
    *   Significant investment in 4-lane and 6-lane expressways to improve connectivity.

*   **Industrial Infrastructure:**
    *   **VCIC (Vizag-Chennai Industrial Corridor):** Major corridor spanning ~800 km with industrial nodes at Visakhapatnam, Kakinada, Gannavaram, and Donakonda.
    *   **Special Economic Zones (SEZs):** Multiple SEZs operational, including Pharma, IT, and Petroleum SEZs.
    *   AP aims to provide **"Plug and Play" industrial infrastructure** through private industrial parks.

*   **Railways:**
    *   AP is served by the South Central Railway, East Coast Railway, and Southern Railway zones.
    *   Visakhapatnam is a major railway junction connecting Southern and Eastern India.

*   **Airports:**
    *   International airports at **Visakhapatnam (Visakhapatnam International Airport)** and **Tirupati (Sri Venkateswara International Airport)**.
    *   Vijayawada (Gannavaram Airport) also serves the capital region.
