# CG-05: Geography — India & AP
## Study Notes (English)

### 1. Physical Geography — Physiographic Divisions of India
India can be divided into six major physiographic divisions based on its geological structure and geomorphic processes:

*   **The Northern and North-Eastern Mountains (The Himalayas):**
    *   Young, structurally fold mountains stretching from the Indus to the Brahmaputra.
    *   **Main Ranges:** Great Himalayas (Himadri), Lesser Himalayas (Himachal), and Shiwaliks.
    *   **Regional Divisions:** Kashmir/North-western Himalayas, Himachal and Uttarakhand Himalayas, Darjiling and Sikkim Himalayas, Arunachal Himalayas, and Eastern Hills (Purvanchal).
*   **The Northern Plains:**
    *   Formed by the alluvial deposits of the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra systems.
    *   **Sub-divisions based on relief:** Bhabar (pebble-studded), Tarai (marshy), Bhangar (older alluvium), and Khadar (newer alluvium).
*   **The Peninsular Plateau:**
    *   Oldest and most stable landmass of India, composed of igneous and metamorphic rocks.
    *   **Major parts:** Deccan Plateau, Central Highlands, and North-eastern Plateau (Karbi-Anglong).
    *   **Western Ghats (Sahyadri):** Continuous, higher than Eastern Ghats.
    *   **Eastern Ghats:** Discontinuous and irregular, dissected by rivers.
*   **The Indian Desert (Thar Desert):**
    *   Located west of the Aravali hills. Low rainfall (below 150 mm/year) and arid climate.
    *   **Luni** is the only significant river in this region.
*   **The Coastal Plains:**
    *   **Western Coastal Plain:** Narrow plain between Western Ghats and Arabian Sea. Divisions: Konkan, Kannad, and Malabar.
    *   **Eastern Coastal Plain:** Wide and level plain. Divisions: Northern Circar and Coromandel Coast. Large deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri.
*   **The Islands:**
    *   **Andaman and Nicobar:** Volcanic origin, separated by Ten Degree Channel.
    *   **Lakshadweep:** Coral origin (Atolls), located in the Arabian Sea.

### 2. Climate of India — Monsoon Mechanism
India has a tropical monsoon climate. The Indian monsoon is a complex meteorological phenomenon.

*   **Classical (Thermal) Concept:** Seasonal reversal of winds due to differential heating and cooling of land and sea.
*   **Modern (Dynamic) Concept:** Shift of the ITCZ (Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone).
*   **Monsoon Phases:**
    *   **Onset (South-West Monsoon):** Usually arrives in Kerala by June 1st. Influenced by the Mascarene High and the Tibetan Plateau heating.
    *   **Branches:** Arabian Sea branch (Western Ghats, Rajasthan) and Bay of Bengal branch (Myanmar, North-East India, Ganga plains).
    *   **Withdrawal:** Retreating monsoon starts from September in the North-West.
*   **Factors influencing Monsoon:**
    *   **El Niño & La Niña:** El Niño generally leads to weak monsoons; La Niña strengthens it.
    *   **IED (Indian Ocean Dipole):** Positive IOD is favorable for Indian monsoon.
    *   **MJO (Madden-Julian Oscillation):** Periodic pulse of clouds and rain.
*   **Seasons in India:** 
    1.  Cold Weather Season (Dec-Feb) 
    2.  Hot Weather Season (March-May) 
    3.  South-West Monsoon Season (June-Sept) 
    4.  Retreating Monsoon Season (Oct-Nov)

### 3. Indian Rivers and Drainage Systems
India's drainage is broadly divided into Himalayan and Peninsular systems.

*   **Himalayan Rivers (Perennial):**
    *   **Indus System:** Originates near Mansarovar (Tibet). Tributaries in India: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj.
    *   **Ganga System:** Originates as Bhagirathi (Gaumukh). Meets Alaknanda at Devprayag. Tributaries: Yamuna (Right), Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi (Left).
    *   **Brahmaputra System:** Known as Tsangpo in Tibet. Enters India in Arunachal (Dihang). Forms the largest delta (Sundarbans) with Ganga.
*   **Peninsular Rivers (Seasonal):**
    *   **East-Flowing (Bay of Bengal):** Mahanadi, **Godavari** (Largest Peninsular river - Dakshin Ganga), Krishna, Kaveri. These rivers form large deltas.
    *   **West-Flowing (Arabian Sea):** Narmada and Tapi (flow through rift valleys), Mahi, Luni, Sabarmati. These usually form estuaries.
*   **Drainage Patterns:** Dendritic (tree-like), Radial (from a hill), Trellis (parallel), and Centripetal (into a depression).

### 4. AP Geography — Soils, Rivers, Districts
Andhra Pradesh is the eighth-largest state in India, located on the South-Eastern coast.

*   **Physiography of AP:** Divided into three regions: Coastal Andhra, Rayalaseema, and Uttarandhra (part of Coastal).
*   **Major Rivers in AP:**
    *   **Godavari:** Enters AP at Vvavihar (near Burgampad) and flows through Polavaram. Splitting into Vasishta, Gautami, etc., before joining the sea.
    *   **Krishna:** Enters at Muktyala. Important project: Srisailam and Nagarjuna Sagar.
    *   **Other Rivers:** Pennar, Vamsadhara, Nagavali, and Sarada.
*   **Soils of AP:**
    *   **Red Soils:** Most widespread (about 60% of area), poor in nitrogen and organic matter.
    *   **Black Soils (Regur):** Extensive in districts like Guntur, Kurnool, and Kadapa. Good for cotton and tobacco.
    *   **Alluvial Soils:** Found in the deltas of Godavari and Krishna. Highly fertile for paddy.
    *   **Laterite Soils:** Found in high rainfall areas like Agency tracts.
*   **Districts:** After the 2022 reorganization, AP has **26 districts**. (Important: Parvathipuram Manyam, Alluri Sitharama Raju, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar Konaseema are some of the newly formed/extended districts).

### 5. Population and Urbanization
Based on the 2011 Census:

*   **Distribution:** India is the second most populous country (now first). Highly concentrated in states like UP, Maharashtra, Bihar, and West Bengal.
*   **Density:** Average density is around 382 persons per sq km. Bihar has the highest density; Arunachal Pradesh the lowest.
*   **Population Growth:** Determined by Birth Rate, Death Rate, and Migration.
*   **Literacy Rate:** Significant gap remains between states (Kerala vs Bihar).
*   **Sex Ratio:** Number of females per 1000 males. Kerala has the highest; Haryana/UTs usually have lower ratios.
*   **Urbanization:** Process of population shift from rural to urban areas. India is experiencing rapid urbanization, leading to the growth of Mega Cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai).
*   **AP Population (2011):** Total population was approx. 4.93 crore (divided state). Literacy rate and sex ratio were slightly higher than the national average in some coastal districts.
