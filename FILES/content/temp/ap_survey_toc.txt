Preface 
The Government of India presents the Economic Survey annually in both houses of 
Parliament, during the Union Budget Session. This survey provides a detailed analysis of the 
national economy, reviewing economic trends over the past year, evaluating key 
development programs and outlining policy directions for the future. 
Similarly, the Government of Andhra Pradesh brings out the Socio-Economic Survey 
each year and presents it in both Houses of the State Legislature, alongside the Budget 
documents. The Survey is a comprehensive report that documents the socio-economic 
transformation of the state over the financial year, offering insights into macro economic 
trends, sectoral performance, and details of various government initiatives. The Socio-
Economic Survey 2024-25 is the result of collaborative efforts among government 
departments, compiled using official data. The report not only captures growth trends and 
developmental achievements but also identifies challenges and emerging opportunities to 
support evidence-based decision-making. 
This year's survey consists of multiple chapters covering key aspects of the state's 
development and also encompass a brief account on Swarna Andhra Vision 2047 outlining 
the long-term development goals for transforming Andhra Pradesh into a globally 
competitive state, Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) & KPI Monitoring and an 
overview of Policies that would influence socio-economic growth. 
I thank all government departments, research institutions and experts for their 
support in preparing this survey. Their efforts in providing accurate data and insights have 
helped compile this report. I also appreciate all stakeholders who contributed to making 
this document informative and useful.

1. GENERAL OVERVIEW 
1.1 GEOGRAPHIC, DEMOGRAPHIC AND 
SOCIO ECONOMIC PROFILE OF 
ANDHRA PRADESH 
 
ndhra Pradesh, the 8th largest state 
in india, spans 1,62,970 sq. Km 
across 26 districts. Located in a 
tropical region, it has the nation’s third-
longest coastline, stretching 974 km. As per 
the 2011 census, the state ranks 10thin 
population, contributing 4.09% of the 
national total. Population growth saw an 
upward trend from 18.88% (1961-71) to 
21.13% (1981-91) before declining to 
9.21% (2001-11), significantly lower than 
the national growth rate of 17.70%. 
With 
a 
population 
density 
of 
304 
individuals per sq. Km, Andhra Pradesh falls 
below the national average of 382. Krishna 
district records the highest density at 518, 
while ysrkadapa and prakasam have the 
lowest, below 200. The sex ratio improved 
from 983 (2001) to 997 (2011), surpassing 
the national average of 943. 
The state’s literacy rate rose from 62.07% 
(2001) to 67.35% (2011), though it remains 
below the national rate of 72.98%. Female 
literacy also improved from 52.72% to 
59.96% over the same period. Since 1981, 
overall literacy has increased by over 37 
percentage points. 
Urbanization has driven economic growth, 
with the urban population rising from 
24.13% 
(2001) 
to 
29.47% 
(2011). 
Visakhapatnam leads with 47.45% urban 
residents, followed by krishna (40.81%), 
while srikakulam (16.16%) and prakasam 
(19.56%) remain the least urbanized. 
The state’s soil composition varies due to 
climate, altitude, and bedrock, comprising 
sand, silt, and clay in different proportions. 
Rainfall disparities and high temperatures 
further influence soil characteristics. 
Land 
utilization 
patterns 
reveal 
that 
30.40% (49.55 lakh hectares) of the state’s 
land is cultivated. Forests cover 22.63% 
(36.88 lakh hectares), while 14.90% (24.29 
lakh hectares) is under current fallow lands. 
Non-agricultural use accounts for 12.84% 
(20.93 lakh hectares), and 8.19% (13.35 
lakh hectares) is barren or uncultivable. The 
remaining 10.09% (16.43 lakh hectares) 
includes other fallow lands, cultivable 
waste, grazing areas, and tree crops, with 
0.95% (1.54 lakh hectares) dedicated to 
miscellaneous groves.

sector remains a major contributor with a 
GVA of ₹6.11 lakh crore, growing at 11.70%.

1.3 PUBLIC FINANCE 
The revenue for the State in FY 2024-25 
(RE) comprised of ₹94,967 crores from 
Own Tax sources, ₹7,018 crores from Own 
Non-Tax sources, and ₹89,157 crores from 
Central Transfers. The Total Expenditure by 
the State, excluding ways and means 
advances and public debt repayments, was 
₹2,36,512 crores in FY 2023-24 and is 
expected to be ₹2,49,418 crores in FY 2024-
25 (RE). The Total Debt of the State in FY 
2023-24 was ₹4,91,734 crore and is 
expected to reach ₹5,64,488 crores at the 
end of FY 2024-25 (RE).  
In FY 2023-24, Andhra Pradesh faced a 
Revenue Deficit of ₹38,683 crores and a 
Fiscal Deficit of ₹62,720 crores. In FY 2024-
25 (RE), the Revenue Deficit is expected to 
be ₹48,311 crores, while the Fiscal Deficit is 
estimated at ₹73,362 crores. 
1.4 PRICES, WAGES, 
AND PUBLIC 
DISTRIBUTION 
IN 
ANDHRA 
PRADESH 
The state monitors the prices of essential 
commodities daily to track changes and 
implement price stabilization measures. For 
instance, the sharp increase in onion prices 
by 40.24% in 2024-25 was managed 
through government interventions such as 
direct procurement from farmers and 
investment in cold storage facilities. The 
Consumer Price Index (CPI) for Industrial 
Workers and Agricultural Labourers has 
shown an upward trend, reflecting the 
rising cost of living. The CPI for Industrial 
Workers in Andhra Pradesh increased by 
4.89% in 2024-25, while the CPI for 
Agricultural Labourers rose by 5.93%. 
Wages for agricultural laborers and artisans 
in Andhra Pradesh have seen varied growth. 
The 
average 
daily 
wage 
for 
male 
agricultural laborers increased by 5.65% to 
₹561 in 2024-25, while female laborers saw 
a modest rise of 2.58% to ₹398. Skilled 
trades such as carpentry and blacksmithing 
experienced faster wage growth, with 
carpenters' wages increasing by 7.74% to 
₹710. However, some occupations like 
cobblers saw a slight decline in wages. 
These wage trends highlight the varying 
demand for different occupations in the 
rural labor market. 
The Public Distribution System (PDS) in 
Andhra Pradesh plays a crucial role in 
ensuring food security for economically 
vulnerable populations. The state operates 
29,796 Fair Price Shops, covering 1.48 crore 
beneficiaries. The introduction of Aadhaar-
linked 
ration 
cards 
has 
enhanced 
transparency and reduced leakages. The 
monthly 
rice 
allocation 
under 
PDS 
increased to 2.35 lakh metric tonnes in 
2024-25, 
reflecting 
the 
government's 
commitment to food security.  
Initiatives like the Deepam-2 Scheme, which 
provides free LPG cylinders to eligible 
families, aim to improve health outcomes 
and reduce reliance on firewood. Despite 
these efforts, challenges such as logistical 
inefficiencies and subsidy burdens persist,

1.5 AGRICULTURE 
Agriculture is a vital sector in Andhra 
Pradesh, supporting 60% of the population. 
It remains the largest contributor to the 
rural economy, with 49.55 lakh hectares 
(30.4%) of the state's 162.97 lakh hectares 
under cultivation.  
The State received an average rainfall of 
960.3 mm from June to December 2024, 
compared to the normal rainfall of 860.0 
mm, resulting in an excess of 11.7% 
(Normal).  
According to the 2ndAdvance Estimates, the 
cultivated area of food grains in 2024-25 
increased to 37.51 lakh hectares, up 12.85% 
from 33.24 lakh hectares in 2023-24. Food 
grain production is estimated at 161.86 
lakh tonnes, a 12.94% increase from 143.31 
lakh tonnes in the previous year. Paddy 
production also saw significant growth, 
with the area under paddy increasing by 
9.16% and production rising by 12.95% % 
to 127.15 lakh tonnes. Improved rainfall 
patterns, better irrigation infrastructure, 
and crop diversification strategies have 
contributed to this growth. 
The state government aims to achieve high 
growth rate in agriculture and allied sectors 
over the next five years by enhancing 
infrastructure, 
providing 
interest-free 
loans, subsidized seeds, modern farming 
tools, and promoting Zero-budget Natural 
Farming (ZBNF).  
The APCNF agricultural schemes for 2024-
25 include support for 25,000 acres under 
natural farming, with 1,000 acres per 
Parliamentary 
constituency. 
Local 
consumption will be promoted to improve 
community health and nutrition. 
To 
safeguard 
tenant 
farmers, 
the 
government has focused on tenant loaning 
through RMG, JLG, and Crop Cultivator 
Rights Cards (CCRCS). As of January 3, 2025, 
CCRCS were issued to 9,13,283 tenant 
farmers, with Rs. 2,848.77 crores disbursed 
to 2,00,409 farmers. 
Horticulture: 
Andhra Pradesh, the largest fruit producer 
in India, has 18.23 lakh hectares under 
horticulture, producing 365.92 lakh MT, 
contributing 15.6% of the national output. 
The state excels in oil palm, papaya, lime, 
cocoa, tomato, and chillies productivity, and 
ranks 2nd in mango, sweet orange, and 
turmeric. In 2024-25, 1,43,329 acres were 
converted to remunerative horticulture 
crops, and micro-irrigation covered 2.05 
lakh acres, benefiting 75,035 farmers with a 
Rs. 793.67 crore subsidy. Micro-irrigation 
was implemented across 1,89,000 acres, 
and the oil palm area was expanded by 
39,502 acres.  
Animal Husbandry 
Inclusive growth through livestock in 
Andhra Pradesh primarily benefits poor 
communities, offering high returns, job 
creation, asset building, and a safety net 
against crop failure. Andhra Pradesh has 
some of the richest livestock resources in 
the country, with significant increases in 
milk and meat production. In 2023-24, 
Andhra 
Pradesh 
ranked 
1st 
in 
egg 
production (2548.74 lakh eggs), 5th in meat 
production (10.68 lakh metric tons), and 
7th in milk production (139.94 lakh metric 
tons). According to the 2019 Livestock 
Census, the state has 340.60 lakh livestock 
and 1078.63 lakh poultry, showing a 
15.79% increase over the 2012 census.

Fisheries Sector 
Andhra Pradesh contributes about 30% of 
national fish production and 31% of India's 
shrimp exports. In 2024-25, fish production 
reached 41.38 LMT (73.1%) against a target 
of 56.62 LMT. Key initiatives include a 61-
day marine fishing ban, diesel oil subsidies, 
ex-gratia payments to families of deceased 
fishermen, and the construction of fishing 
harbors and fish landing centers. The state 
also offers a power tariff concession for 
aqua service connections and promotes 
sustainable aquaculture through various 
projects and schemes. 
Forest 
Andhra Pradesh's forests cover 38,436.58 
sq. km, providing timber, bamboo, fuel 
wood, fodder, and non-timber products, 
along 
with 
ecological 
benefits. 
Nagaravanams (City Forests) aim to provide 
healthy urban environments, with 37 
completed and 7 Temple Eco-parks in 
development. The Nagar Van Yojana aims to 
create pollution-free, green cities, with 61 
Nagar Vans sanctioned. 
Sericulture 
Andhra Pradesh is the second-largest 
producer of raw silk in India, after 
Karnataka, and was awarded the "Best 
Bivoltine Practicing State in India" by the 
Ministry of Textiles. In 2024-25, sericulture 
farmers produced 61,394 MT of quality 
cocoons worth Rs. 2,701 crores and 8,458 
MT of quality raw silk worth Rs. 2,960 
crores. To extend sericulture activities, 621 
new 
silkworm 
rearing 
sheds 
were 
constructed in 2024-25.  
Agriculture Marketing 
The Agriculture Marketing sector in Andhra 
Pradesh is robust, with 1,052 godowns 
providing a total storage capacity of 
9,65,492 MTs, of which 626 godowns 
(capacity: 5,65,508 MTs) are operational. 
During 2024-25 (up to December 2024), 
Market Committees collected Rs. 433.39 
crores in market fees. The e-National 
Agriculture 
Market 
(e-NAM) 
platform 
integrates primary markets across the state, 
facilitating fair pricing for farmers. As of 
December 2024, e-NAM registered 1,452 
farmers, 69 traders, 9 commission agents, 
and 52 Farmer Producer Organizations 
(FPOs), with a total transaction value of Rs. 
5,954.96 crores.  
1.6 INDUSTRIAL DEVELOPMENT 
Andhra Pradesh is aiming to become  a 
premier 
destination 
for 
industrial 
investment in India, boasting the nation's 
third-longest 
coastline, 
six 
operational 
ports, abundant mineral resources, a strong 
agriculture base, and a surplus of electricity.  
Andhra Pradesh continues to make efforts 
to be a leading industrial hub in India, 
leveraging its strategic location, abundant 
resources, and investor-friendly policies. 
The state has introduced key policy 
frameworks 
such 
as 
the 
Industrial 
Development Policy (4.0) 2024-29, MSME & 
Entrepreneur Development Policy (4.0) 
2024-29, and the Electronics Manufacturing 
Policy to drive industrial growth. These 
policies prioritize key sectors, including 
automobiles, pharmaceuticals, textiles, food 
processing, green energy, electric vehicles, 
and semiconductors. 
The manufacturing sector remains key, 
contributing approximately 11% to the 
Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP). The 
state is commited to develop Micro, Small, 
and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs).

Handlooms and Textiles 
The Department of Handlooms and Textiles 
oversees handlooms and power looms in 
both the cooperative and decentralized 
sectors, as well as the establishment of 
apparel and textile parks in the garment 
sector. Andhra Pradesh plays a significant 
role in the national handloom industry, with 
various welfare schemes and progress 
initiatives being implemented to support 
weavers and artisans. 
1.7 ECONOMIC INFRASTRUCTURE 
The government is focusing on economic 
infrastructure with special emphasis on key 
sectors such as irrigation, power, roads, 
ports, airports, and tourism.  
The irrigation infrastructure, including 
canals, dams, and reservoirs, plays a crucial 
role in the state's agricultural productivity. 
Polavaram Irrigation Project, a National 
Project has now picked up pace with Head

Canal at 73%, Right Main Canal at 93% and 
the total project (works) at 78% completion 
rate. 
Number 
of 
other

like

being focused.  
In the power sector, Andhra Pradesh has an

renewable 
energy,

a
