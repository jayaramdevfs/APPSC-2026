## MT-POLT-M3-5-001 — Federalism (7 Questions)

1. **Question:** Which article of the Indian Constitution describes India as a "Union of States"?
   - A) Article 1
   - B) Article 2
   - C) Article 3
   - D) Article 4
   - **Answer:** A
   - **Explanation:** Article 1 of the Constitution states that "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." The term "Union" was preferred over "Federation."
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

2. **Question:** In which of the following cases can the Union Parliament legislate on subjects in the State List?
   - 1. During a Proclamation of National Emergency
   - 2. If the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution with two-thirds majority in national interest
   - 3. To implement international treaties
   - 4. When two or more States make a request
   - A) 1 and 2 only
   - B) 3 and 4 only
   - C) 1, 2, and 3 only
   - D) All of the above
   - **Answer:** D
   - **Explanation:** The Constitution allows Parliament to legislate on State subjects under several circumstances: Emergency (Art 250), Rajya Sabha resolution (Art 249), international treaties (Art 253), and request by states (Art 252).
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

3. **Question:** Residuary powers in India are vested in:
   - A) The States
   - B) The Union Parliament
   - C) Both Union and States
   - D) The President
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Unlike the US where residuary powers rest with States, Article 248 of the Indian Constitution vests them exclusively in the Union Parliament.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

4. **Question:** The Sarkaria Commission (1983) was primarily concerned with:
   - A) Judicial reforms
   - B) Centre-State relations
   - C) Electoral reforms
   - D) Local government
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** The Sarkaria Commission was appointed in 1983 to examine the relationship and balance of power between the Centre and the States.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

5. **Question:** Which of the following features of the Indian Constitution is considered "Unitary" in nature?
   - 1. Written Constitution
   - 2. Single Citizenship
   - 3. All-India Services
   - 4. Independent Judiciary
   - A) 1 and 4
   - B) 2 and 3
   - C) 1, 2, and 4
   - D) 2, 3, and 4
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Single Citizenship and All-India Services are unitary features. Written Constitution and Independent Judiciary are federal features.
   - **Difficulty:** Hard

6. **Question:** The power of the Parliament to alter the boundaries or change the name of any State under Article 3 is:
   - A) Dependent on the consent of the concerned State legislature
   - B) Subject to a special majority in Parliament
   - C) Independent of the consent of the concerned State legislature
   - D) Only possible during an Emergency
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** While the President must refer the bill to the State legislature for views, the views are not binding on the Parliament.
   - **Difficulty:** Medium

7. **Question:** Inter-State Water Disputes are resolved by:
   - A) The Supreme Court under its Original Jurisdiction
   - B) The Inter-State Council
   - C) Tribunals established under Article 262
   - D) The Zonal Councils
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** Article 262 empowers Parliament to provide for the adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-state rivers/valleys. Parliament has excluded the jurisdiction of courts in such matters via the Inter-State Water Disputes Act.
   - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-003 — Parliament (7 Questions)

8. **Question:** Who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?
   - A) The President of India
   - B) The Vice-President of India
   - C) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
   - D) The Prime Minister
   - **Answer:** B
   - **Explanation:** Under Article 64, the Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha).
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

9. **Question:** A Money Bill can be introduced in:
   - A) Rajya Sabha only
   - B) Either House of Parliament
   - C) Lok Sabha only
   - D) Joint sitting of both Houses
   - **Answer:** C
   - **Explanation:** Per Article 109, a Money Bill (defined in Art 110) cannot be introduced in the Rajya Sabha; it must originate in the Lok Sabha.
   - **Difficulty:** Easy

10. **Question:** What is the maximum period within which the Rajya Sabha must return a Money Bill to the Lok Sabha?
    - A) 10 days
    - B) 14 days
    - C) 30 days
    - D) 6 months
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** After a Money Bill is passed by the Lok Sabha, the Rajya Sabha has only 14 days to recommend changes or approve it.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

11. **Question:** Who presides over a Joint Sitting of the Parliament?
    - A) The President
    - B) The Vice-President
    - C) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
    - D) The Prime Minister
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Article 118(4) specifies that the Speaker of the Lok Sabha (or in their absence, the Deputy Speaker) presides over joint sittings.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

12. **Question:** Which House of Parliament can alone initiate the procedure for the removal of the Vice-President?
    - A) Lok Sabha
    - B) Rajya Sabha
    - C) Both Houses simultaneously
    - D) Either House
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The Vice-President can be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by an effective majority and agreed to by the Lok Sabha.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

13. **Question:** The 'No-Confidence Motion' against the Council of Ministers can be moved only in:
    - A) Rajya Sabha
    - B) Lok Sabha
    - C) Joint Sitting
    - D) The President's presence
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Since the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75), the motion of no-confidence can only be moved in the Lok Sabha.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

14. **Question:** Which of the following are the exclusive powers of the Rajya Sabha?
    - 1. Authorization of Parliament to make laws on State subjects
    - 2. Creation of new All-India Services
    - 3. Approval of Proclamation of Emergency
    - 4. Election of the President
    - A) 1 and 2 only
    - B) 3 and 4 only
    - C) 1, 2, and 4 only
    - D) All of the above
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** Articles 249 (State List) and 312 (All-India Services) are special powers exclusive to the Rajya Sabha.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-POLT-M3-4-006 — Judicial Review (7 Questions)

15. **Question:** The concept of 'Judicial Review' in India is based on:
    - A) Procedure established by law
    - B) Due process of law
    - C) Rule of law
    - D) Precedents and Conventions
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** While the concept has evolved significantly, the Indian Constitution specifically uses the phrase "procedure established by law" (Art 21), though the SC has moved closer to "due process" since the Maneka Gandhi case.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

16. **Question:** Which article of the Constitution declares all laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights to be void?
    - A) Article 12
    - B) Article 13
    - C) Article 14
    - D) Article 15
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 13 provides the constitutional basis for judicial review by declaring that any law in force that is inconsistent with FRs shall be void.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

17. **Question:** Public Interest Litigation (PIL) was pioneered in India by:
    - A) Justice M.H. Beg
    - B) Justice P.N. Bhagwati
    - C) Justice Y.V. Chandrachud
    - D) Justice H.R. Khanna
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Justice P.N. Bhagwati and Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer are considered the pioneers of PIL in India (Sunil Batra case, Hussainara Khatoon case).
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

18. **Question:** A person can move the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights under:
    - A) Article 226
    - B) Article 136
    - C) Article 32
    - D) Article 131
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) allows citizens to approach the Supreme Court directly for FR enforcement. Dr. Ambedkar called it the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

19. **Question:** The 'Doctrine of Basic Structure' was first propounded by the Supreme Court in the:
    - A) Golaknath case
    - B) Kesavananda Bharati case
    - C) Minerva Mills case
    - D) Maneka Gandhi case
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** In the landmark Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), the SC ruled that Article 368 does not enable Parliament to alter the 'basic structure' of the Constitution.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

20. **Question:** Judicial Review power of the High Courts is wider than that of the Supreme Court because:
    - A) High Courts can review State laws only
    - B) High Courts can issue writs for purposes other than Fundamental Rights
    - C) High Courts have more judges
    - D) Supreme Court is an appellate court only
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Under Article 226, HCs can issue writs for the enforcement of FRs *and* for "any other purpose," whereas the SC's writ jurisdiction under Art 32 is limited to FR enforcement.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

21. **Question:** Which article empowers the Supreme Court to review any judgment pronounced or order made by it?
    - A) Article 137
    - B) Article 141
    - C) Article 143
    - D) Article 145
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** Article 137 gives the SC the power to review its own judgments to rectify errors.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

## MT-POLT-M3-4-005 — Privileges of State Legislature (7 Questions)

22. **Question:** Which article of the Indian Constitution deals with the privileges of the State Legislature?
    - A) Article 105
    - B) Article 194
    - C) Article 212
    - D) Article 114
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 194 defines the powers, privileges, and immunities of State Legislatures and their members (comparable to Art 105 for Parliament).
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

23. **Question:** A member of the State Legislature cannot be arrested in a civil case:
    - A) At any time
    - B) During the session of the House only
    - C) During the session and 40 days before and after the session
    - D) Only when inside the House premises
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** This is an individual privilege to ensure members can attend sessions without interference in civil matters.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

24. **Question:** No member of the State Legislature is liable to any proceedings in any court in respect of anything said in the House. This is called:
    - A) Freedom of expression
    - B) Freedom of speech in the House
    - C) Absolute immunity
    - D) Legislative sovereignty
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 194(1) guarantees freedom of speech in the legislature, and 194(2) provides immunity from court proceedings for such speech.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

25. **Question:** Who has the power to punish a person for "Breach of Privilege" or "Contempt of the House"?
    - A) The Governor
    - B) The High Court
    - C) The House itself
    - D) The Advocate General
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Each House has the inherent power to protect its dignity and punish any person (member or outsider) for breach of privilege.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

26. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT a collective privilege of the State Legislature?
    - A) Right to exclude strangers from proceedings
    - B) Right to publish reports and prohibit others from doing so
    - C) Right to be exempted from serving as a juror
    - D) Right to regulate its own internal procedure
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** Exemption from jury service is an *individual* privilege of a member, not a collective privilege of the House.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

27. **Question:** Can the privileges of the State Legislature be codified by a law?
    - A) Yes, by the State Legislature
    - B) No, they are fixed by the Constitution
    - C) Only by the Parliament
    - D) Only with the President's prior permission
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** Article 194(3) allows the State Legislature to define its privileges by law. Until then, they remain the same as those of the UK House of Commons as of 1950.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

28. **Question:** The freedom of speech of a member in the State Legislature is:
    - A) Absolute and not subject to any restriction
    - B) Subject only to the provisions of the Constitution and the rules of the House
    - C) Same as the general freedom of speech under Article 19(1)(a)
    - D) Subject to the prior approval of the Governor
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** While broader than Art 19(1)(a), it is still subject to constitutional provisions (like Art 211 - restriction on discussing conduct of judges) and the Speaker's rules of procedure.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

## MT-POLT-M3-5-008 — 73rd & 74th Amendment Acts (7 Questions)

29. **Question:** The 11th Schedule of the Constitution, added by the 73rd Amendment, contains:
    - A) 18 subjects
    - B) 22 subjects
    - C) 29 subjects
    - D) 33 subjects
    - **Answer:** C
    - **Explanation:** The 11th Schedule lists 29 functional items that may be transferred to Panchayati Raj Institutions.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

30. **Question:** What is the minimum age prescribed for contesting elections to a Panchayat?
    - A) 18 years
    - B) 21 years
    - C) 25 years
    - D) 30 years
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 243-F states that a person is qualified to be a member of a Panchayat if they have attained the age of 21 years.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

31. **Question:** Which of the following is the "Gram Sabha"?
    - A) All adult residents of the village
    - B) All persons whose names are registered in the electoral rolls of the village
    - C) The elected members of the Panchayat
    - D) The village elders committee
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Article 243(b) defines Gram Sabha as a body consisting of persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village comprised within the area of Panchayat at the village level.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

32. **Question:** The State Finance Commission is appointed by:
    - A) The President
    - B) The Governor
    - C) The State Legislature
    - D) The Chief Minister
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** Under Article 243-I, the Governor of a State appoints a Finance Commission every five years to review the financial position of local bodies.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium

33. **Question:** Reservation of seats for SCs and STs in Panchayats is:
    - A) Compulsory and based on their proportion in total population
    - B) Discretionary and at the will of the State Government
    - C) Limited to the village level only
    - D) Fixed at 33% for all categories
    - **Answer:** A
    - **Explanation:** Article 243-D makes reservation of seats for SCs/STs mandatory in every Panchayat in proportion to their population.
    - **Difficulty:** Hard

34. **Question:** The 'Nagarpalikas' (Municipalities) were given constitutional status by which amendment?
    - A) 71st Amendment
    - B) 72nd Amendment
    - C) 73rd Amendment
    - D) 74th Amendment
    - **Answer:** D
    - **Explanation:** The 74th Amendment Act of 1992 (effective 1993) provided constitutional status to urban local bodies.
    - **Difficulty:** Easy

35. **Question:** Who is responsible for the delimitation of constituencies and the conduct of elections for Panchayats?
    - A) Election Commission of India
    - B) State Election Commission
    - C) District Collector
    - D) State Legislative Assembly
    - **Answer:** B
    - **Explanation:** The State Election Commission (Article 243-K) is solely responsible for the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of all elections to the Panchayats.
    - **Difficulty:** Medium
