## MT-POLT-M3-5-001 — Federalism

### 1. Framework & Evolution
Federalism in India is a system of "Union of States" (Article 1) rather than a "Federation of States." It was designed to accommodate India's immense diversity while maintaining a strong center to ensure national unity.
- **Nature:** "Unitary Bias" or "Quasi-federal" (K.C. Wheare).
- **Key Principle:** Cooperation between the Centre and States (Cooperative Federalism).

### 2. Division of Powers (Seventh Schedule)
The Constitution demarcates powers into three lists:
- **Union List (97+ subjects):** Exclusive power to Union Parliament (e.g., Defence, Foreign Affairs, Banking, Railways).
- **State List (66- subjects):** Exclusive power to State Legislatures under normal circumstances (e.g., Police, Agriculture, Local Government).
- **Concurrent List (47+ subjects):** Both can make laws; Union law prevails in case of conflict (e.g., Education, Forests, Trade Unions).
- **Residuary Powers:** Vested in the Union Parliament (Article 248).

### 3. Federalism with a Strong Centre
- **Territorial Integrity:** Parliament can form new states or alter boundaries/names of existing states (Article 3) without state consent.
- **Emergency Provisions:** Part XVIII allows the Union to assume all legislative and executive powers.
- **All-India Services:** IAS and IPS officers are under the ultimate control of the Central Government.
- **Article 257:** The executive power of the State must not impede the executive power of the Union.

### 4. Centre-State Relations & Conflicts
- **Governor's Role:** Appointed by the Centre; can reserve state bills for President's assent.
- **Article 356:** President's Rule (dismissal of state government) has been a major source of tension.
- **Financial Autonomy:** States are heavily dependent on Central grants and the Finance Commission's recommendations for revenue sharing.
- **River Water Disputes:** Resolved through Tribunals (Article 262) but often lead to prolonged legal/political battles.

## MT-POLT-G2P2-II4-003 — Parliament

### 1. Composition
The Indian Parliament consists of the President and two Houses:
- **Lok Sabha (House of the People):** Lower House, directly elected by the people for a 5-year term. Max strength: 550.
- **Rajya Sabha (Council of States):** Upper House, indirectly elected by state MLAs. Permanent house (1/3rd members retire every 2 years). Max strength: 250 (including 12 nominated).

### 2. Functions & Powers
- **Legislative:** Chief law-making body for the Union.
- **Executive Control:** The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Parliament uses Question Hour, Zero Hour, and No-Confidence Motions to hold the government accountable.
- **Financial:** No tax can be levied or expenditure incurred without Parliamentary approval (Budget).
- **Constituent:** Power to amend the Constitution (Article 368).

### 3. Law-Making Procedure
A Bill goes through several stages:
1. **Introduction:** First Reading.
2. **Scrutiny:** Second Reading (often referred to a Committee).
3. **Voting:** Third Reading.
4. **Second House:** Same procedure.
5. **Joint Sitting (Article 108):** To resolve deadlocks between houses (not applicable to Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills).
6. **Presidential Assent:** Bill becomes an Act.

## MT-POLT-M3-4-006 — Judicial Review

### 1. Concept & Scope
Judicial Review is the power of the Judiciary to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders.
- **Constitutional Basis:** Though the term is not mentioned, Articles 13, 32, 131-136, 143, 226, and 246 provide the foundation.
- **Purpose:** To protect Fundamental Rights and maintain the federal balance.

### 2. Key Provisions
- **Article 13:** Any law that violates Fundamental Rights shall be void.
- **Article 32 & 226:** Power of Supreme Court and High Courts to issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, etc.).
- **Article 137:** Supreme Court has the power to review its own judgments.

### 3. Judicial Activism & PIL
- **PIL (Public Interest Litigation):** Allows any public-spirited citizen to move the court on behalf of the poor/deprived.
- **Evolution:** Emerged around 1979 (Hussainara Khatoon case).
- **Judicial Activism:** Proactive role of the judiciary in ensuring executive accountability and social justice.

## MT-POLT-M3-4-005 — Privileges of State Legislature

### 1. Definition (Article 194)
Privileges are special rights, immunities, and exemptions enjoyed by the Houses of the State Legislature, their committees, and their members.
- **Purpose:** To ensure the effective discharge of their legislative duties without interference.

### 2. Collective Privileges
- Right to publish reports/proceedings (and right to prohibit others from publishing).
- Right to exclude strangers from the House.
- Right to punish for breach of privilege or contempt of the House.

### 3. Individual Privileges
- **Freedom of Speech:** No member is liable for anything said or any vote given in the House.
- **Freedom from Arrest:** No member can be arrested in civil cases 40 days before, during, or 40 days after a session.
- **Exemption from Jury Service:** Members cannot be summoned as witnesses when the House is in session.

## MT-POLT-M3-5-008 — 73rd & 74th Amendment Acts

### 1. Evolution of Local Government
- **73rd CAA (1992):** Constitutional status to Rural Local Governments (Panchayati Raj). Added Part IX and 11th Schedule (29 subjects).
- **74th CAA (1992):** Constitutional status to Urban Local Governments (Municipalities). Added Part IX-A and 12th Schedule (18 subjects).

### 2. Key Features (73rd Amendment)
- **Gram Sabha:** Foundation of the Panchayati Raj system.
- **Three-Tier Structure:** Village, Intermediate (Mandal/Block), and District levels.
- **Reservations:** 1/3rd seats for women; proportional seats for SCs and STs.
- **Term:** Fixed 5-year tenure; elections must be held within 6 months of dissolution.

### 3. Institutional Framework
- **State Election Commission:** Autonomous body to conduct local body elections.
- **State Finance Commission:** Appointed every 5 years to review the financial position of local bodies and recommend resource sharing.
- **Subject Transfer:** 29 subjects (e.g., Agriculture, Primary Education, Health) listed in the 11th Schedule to be devolved to Panchayats.
