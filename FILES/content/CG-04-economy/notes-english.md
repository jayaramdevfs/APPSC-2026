## MT-ECON-G2P3-3-001: RBI Functions & Monetary Policy
**Source:** Ramesh Singh (Ch 12: Banking in India), NCERT Class 12 (Macroeconomics Ch 3)

### Timeline / Framework
- **1926:** Hilton-Young Commission recommends a central bank.
- **1934:** RBI Act passed.
- **1935 (April 1):** RBI established in Calcutta (later moved to Bombay in 1937).
- **1949 (January 1):** RBI nationalised.
- **2016:** Adoption of flexible inflation-targeting framework (4% ± 2%) and establishment of Monetary Policy Committee (MPC).

### Content Sections
#### 1. Core Functions of RBI
- **Issuer of Currency:** Sole authority to issue banknotes (except one-rupee notes/coins).
- **Banker to the Government:** Manages public debt, provides short-term credit (Ways and Means Advances).
- **Banker’s Bank:** Maintains cash reserves of scheduled banks and acts as 'Lender of Last Resort'.
- **Controller of Credit:** Regulates money supply through quantitative and qualitative tools.
- **Custodian of Foreign Exchange:** Manages the 'Foreign Exchange Management Act' (FEMA) and maintains forex reserves.

#### 2. Monetary Policy Instruments
- **Quantitative Tools (General):**
  - **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio):** Percentage of NDTL (Net Demand and Time Liabilities) banks must keep with RBI (no interest earned).
  - **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio):** Percentage of NDTL to be kept in liquid assets (Gold, G-Secs, Cash) by banks themselves.
  - **Repo Rate:** Rate at which RBI lends short-term money to banks against collateral.
  - **Reverse Repo Rate:** Rate at which RBI absorbs liquidity from banks.
  - **MSF (Marginal Standing Facility):** Penalty rate at which banks can borrow overnight beyond Repo.
  - **LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility):** Includes Repo and Reverse Repo.
- **Qualitative Tools (Selective):**
  - Moral Suasion, Margin Requirements, Rationing of Credit.

### Thematic Focus: MPC vs Traditional Setting
The **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** is a 6-member body (3 from RBI, 3 from Govt) chaired by the Governor. Decisions are by majority; Governor has a casting vote. This shifted the focus to a more transparent, consensus-driven inflation targeting.

### Prelims Revision Points
- RBI does not manage the fiscal deficit; it manages the monetary side.
- Printing of one-rupee notes is with the Ministry of Finance.
- RBI serves as 'Lender of Last Resort' to stabilize banks during a run.

## MT-ECON-G2P3-2-004: FDI Policy, Sectors & Limits
**Source:** Ramesh Singh (Ch 15: External Sector), NCERT (Economic Development)

### Timeline / Framework
- **1991:** LPG Reforms open India to Foreign Investment.
- **2017:** Abolition of FIPB (Foreign Investment Promotion Board); replaced by individual ministries via DPIIT portal.
- **Recent:** Consistently high FDI in Services, Computer Hardware/Software, and Telecommunications.

### Content Sections
#### 1. Components of Foreign Investment
- **FDI (Foreign Direct Investment):** Investment for a long-term interest in a foreign enterprise. Generally involving control/management.
- **FII (Foreign Institutional Investor):** Short-term 'Hot Money' in stock markets (now categorized under RFPI - Registered Foreign Portfolio Investors).
- **QFI (Qualified Foreign Investor):** Direct access for foreign individuals to Indian markets.

#### 2. Entry Routes
- **Automatic Route:** No prior approval required from RBI or Govt.
- **Government Route:** Prior approval required from the concerned Ministry/Department.

#### 3. Sectoral Limits (Sample)
- **100% Automatic:** Agriculture, Mining (coal/metals), Civil Aviation (greenfield), E-commerce (Marketplace model), Single Brand Retail.
- **Caps:** 74% in Banking (Private), 74% in Insurance (formerly 49%), 74% in Defence (up to 100% under Govt route for modern tech).
- **Prohibited:** Gambling, Lottery, Tobacco manufacturing, Atomic Energy.

### Thematic Focus: FDI vs FPI
FDI is considered stable and brings technology + management skills. FPI (Portfolio) is volatile and can lead to sudden outflows ('Sudden Stop' or 'Capital Flight').

### Prelims Revision Points
- FDI is allowed in Multi-Brand Retail (subject to state caveats).
- P-Notes (Participatory Notes) are instruments used by foreign investors not registered with SEBI.
- Mauritius and Singapore have historically been the largest sources of FDI due to DTAAs (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements).

## MT-ECON-G2P3-3-004: Capital Market, SEBI & Stock Exchanges
**Source:** Ramesh Singh (Ch 13: Financial Market)

### Timeline / Framework
- **1875:** Establishment of BSE (oldest in Asia).
- **1988:** SEBI set up as a non-statutory body.
- **1992:** SEBI Act passed; SEBI becomes a statutory body with quasi-legislative/judicial powers.

### Content Sections
#### 1. Segments of Capital Market
- **Primary Market:** Dealing with new securities (IPO - Initial Public Offering, FPO, Rights Issue).
- **Secondary Market:** The stock market where existing securities are traded (BSE, NSE).

#### 2. Role of SEBI
- **Protector:** Protects the interests of investors in securities.
- **Regulater:** Regulates the business in stock exchanges and other securities markets.
- **Promoter:** Promotes the development of the market and guides intermediaries (brokers, underwriters).

#### 3. Market Instruments
- **Equity Shares:** Units of ownership.
- **Bonds/Debentures:** Debt instruments for raising capital.
- **Derivatives:** Futures and Options whose value is derived from underlying assets.

### Thematic Focus: Stock Exchange Reforms
Shift from 'Outcry System' to 'Electronic Screen-Based Trading'. Dematerialization (Demat) of shares through NSDL and CDSL.

### Prelims Revision Points
- SEBI is an autonomous body; its orders can be challenged in the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT).
- Insider Trading is a prohibited practice monitored by SEBI.
- SENSEX consists of 30 stocks; NIFTY consists of 50 stocks.

## MT-ECON-G2P3-4-002: Per Capita Income & HDI
**Source:** Ramesh Singh (Ch 2: Growth, Development & Happiness), NCERT Class 12 (Macroeconomics)

### Framework
- **National Income (NI):** Defined as Net National Product at Factor Cost (NNP at FC). 
- **Base Year:** 2011-12 (Recently shifted for better accuracy).
- **GVA (Gross Value Added):** Adopted in 2015 to measure sector-specific performance.

### Content Sections
#### 1. Per Capita Income (PCI)
- **Formula:** PCI = Net National Product (NNP) / Total Population.
- **Significance:** Measures the standard of living, though it hides income inequalities.

#### 2. Human Development Index (HDI)
- **Origin:** Created by Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen (UNDP).
- **Three Dimensions:**
  1. **Long and Healthy Life:** Life expectancy at birth.
  2. **Knowledge:** Mean years of schooling (adults) & Expected years of schooling (children).
  3. **Decent Standard of Living:** GNI per capita (PPP $).
- **Classification:** Countries ranked from 0 to 1. 

#### 3. Gross National Happiness (GNH)
- Concept pioneered by Bhutan (1971). Focuses on mental well-being over material growth.

### Prelims Revision Points
- GVA at basic prices = GVA at factor cost + (Production taxes - Production subsidies).
- India is a "Medium Human Development" country (verify latest rank: 134 in 2022 HDR).
- Nominal GDP (at current prices) vs Real GDP (at constant base year prices).

## MT-ECON-G2P3-1-005: Regional Planning & Balanced Development
**Source:** Ramesh Singh (Ch 4/5: Planning in India)

### Framework
- **Centralized Planning:** Top-down approach (Soviet model).
- **Decentralized Planning:** Bottom-up approach (Multi-level planning).
- **Institutional Shift:** PC (Planning Commission) abolished in 2014; NITI Aayog established in 2015.

### Content Sections
#### 1. Multi-Level Planning in India
- **National Level:** Five-Year Plans (1st to 12th).
- **State Level:** State plans.
- **District Level:** District Planning Committees (DPCs) - Constitutional under 74th Amendment.
- **Block/Local Level:** Gram Sabhas and Village Panchayats.

#### 2. Strategies for Balanced Development
- **Backward Area Programs:** Special Category Status (Historical), Hilly Area Development Program.
- **Indicator based allocation:** Using finance commission criteria to bridge gaps between developed and backward states.
- **SEZ (Special Economic Zones):** To promote regional hubs of export growth.

#### 3. Role of NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)
- Functions as a 'Think Tank'.
- Focus on **Cooperative Federalism** and **Competitive Federalism**.
- Aspirational Districts Program (ADP) to target the most backward districts.

### Prelims Revision Points
- NITI Aayog is a non-statutory, non-constitutional body.
- The 12th Five Year Plan (2012-17) was the last official plan.
- Balanced development aims to reduce regional disparities (e.g., BIMARU states vs Frontrunners).
