## 2. Recombinant DNA Technology (rDNA)
- **Features / Key Points** (Source: NCERT 12 Ch.11):
  - **Restriction Enzymes**: Chemical "molecular scissors". e.g., EcoRI. Cuts DNA at specific palindromic nucleotide sequences.
  - **Cloning Vectors**: DNA molecules that can carry a foreign DNA fragment and replicate in a host cell (e.g., Plasmids, Bacteriophages).
  - **DNA Ligase**: Joins the foreign DNA to the vector.
- **Important Key Terms**:
  - **Palindromic sequence**: DNA sequence that reads the same on the two strands when reading orientation is kept the same (e.g., 5'-GAATTC-3').
  - **Competent Host**: Treating bacteria with specific divalent cations (like calcium) creates pores to help them take up recombinant DNA.
- **Exam Tips**: Often there is a trap regarding whether restriction enzymes cut just one strand or both. They cut **both strands** of the DNA double helix.

## 3. Biotechnology Applications
- **Features / Key Points** (Source: NCERT 12 Ch.12):
  - **Transgenic Plants**: e.g., Bt Cotton. The *cry* gene from *Bacillus thuringiensis* produces toxic crystals that kill specific insects (lepidopterans, coleopterans).
  - **Pest Resistant Plants**: RNA interference (RNAi) used in tobacco plants to resist the nematode *Meloidegyne incognita*.
  - **Medical Applications**: Recombinant therapeutics such as Genetically Engineered Insulin (Eli Lilly recombined A and B chains in 1983).
  - **Gene Therapy**: Used to treat diseases like ADA deficiency.
- **Exam Tips**: Remember that Bt toxin is **inactive** in the bacterium. It converts into an active form in the insect's **alkaline** gut logic. (Exam trap: often stated as acidic gut).

## 4. Thematic Understanding (for Mains)
- **Evolution of Agriculture**: Green Revolution (chemicals) $\rightarrow$ Biotech Revolution (GM crops), reducing reliance on chemical pesticides but raising ecological and ethical concerns.
- **Ethical and Regulatory Dimensions**: GEAC (Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee) in India takes decisions regarding the validity of GM research and the safety of introducing GM organisms for public services.
- **Biopiracy**: Unauthorized use of bio-resources by multinational companies (e.g., patenting of Basmati rice or Neem by Western firms).

## 5. Prelims Revision Points (Quick Recall)
1. **EcoRI** is isolated from *Escherichia coli* RY 13.
2. The sequence recognized by EcoRI is **5'-GAATTC-3'**.
3. **Gel Electrophoresis** separates DNA fragments based on their **size** (smallest moves farthest).
4. DNA is **negatively charged**, therefore moves towards the **anode** under an electric field.
5. In gel electrophoresis, DNA is visualized after staining with **Ethidium Bromide (EtBr)** and exposure to **UV radiation**.
6. **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)** involves 3 steps: Denaturation, Annealing, and Extension.
7. *Taq polymerase*, used in PCR, is isolated from the thermophilic bacterium **Thermus aquaticus** (exam trap: NOT from E. coli).
8. **Downstream Processing** includes separation and purification of the product before it is brought to market.
9. **Bt toxin** targets the insect gut lining, creating pores that cause cell swelling and lysis.
10. **Pro-insulin** contains an extra stretch called the **C-peptide**, which is removed during maturation. Recombinant insulin skips this and directly synthesizes A and B chains.
11. **RNA interference (RNAi)** silences a specific mRNA due to a complementary dsRNA molecule.
12. **ELISA** is based on the principle of antigen-antibody interaction.
13. **Transgenic mice** are extensively used to test the safety of the polio vaccine.
14. Alpha-1-antitrypsin is used to treat **Emphysema**.
15. **GEAC** regulates genetic engineering approvals in India. (Exam trap: NOT ICAR or MoEFCC directly deciding without GEAC).
